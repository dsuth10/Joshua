# Lesson design standards

## Quality gate

Score each category from 0–3. A strong lesson scores at least 2 everywhere and 3 in Learning, Arc, Thinking, and Model/transfer.

| Dimension | 0 — absent | 1 — weak | 2 — sound | 3 — strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Learning | No cognitive outcome | Topic/activity goal | Assessable outcome | Precise outcome with visible evidence |
| Arc | Disconnected tasks | Loose sequence | Logical progression | Each phase creates a need for the next |
| Thinking | Passive reception | Recall/guessing | Classification/application | Justification, transfer, revision, or evaluation |
| Model/transfer | No model or final use | Example without analysis | Analysed model and practice | Model decisions transfer into an authentic product |
| Formative assessment | None | Generic checking | Misconception observable | Evidence shapes feedback or next teaching |
| Inclusion | Add-on worksheet | Lower-volume version | Accessible route to core goal | Dignified access and meaningful extension |
| Visual communication | Dense/plain/unreadable | Decorated worksheet | Clear and consistent | Visual storytelling supports cognition |
| Teacher usability | Requires improvisation | Directions scattered | Notes/timing usable | Fallbacks, resets, and transitions ready |
| Technical reliability | Broken/untested | Main path only | Main and retry paths work | Media, interactions, navigation, and assets verified |

## Failure patterns

### Worksheet-on-a-screen

Symptoms: projected headings, paragraphs, boxes, and bullet lists duplicate a handout.

Response: put students in a decision before explaining the rule. Split content into a visual or narrative sequence. Move extended writing to a handout or build screen.

### Activity carousel

Symptoms: video, sort, quiz, and writing provide variety but do not build one another.

Response: identify the final product. Remove or redesign anything that does not supply content, criteria, modelling, practice, or feedback for it.

### False interactivity

Symptoms: obvious matching, click-to-reveal exposition, or guessing until green.

Response: require evidence, criteria, explanation, comparison, or revision. Make feedback address the misconception.

### One-right-answer judgement

Symptoms: contextual choices such as hooks or interpretations are treated as universally correct.

Response: define audience and purpose, publish criteria, accept defensible choices, and require justification.

### Engagement without learning

Symptoms: media creates emotion but does not become disciplinary knowledge.

Response: add a noticing frame, evidence capture, explicit model, and transfer task.

### Unsupported claims

Symptoms: invented statistics, decontextualised quotations, or advocacy treated as neutral authority.

Response: use traceable sources, teach perspective and corroboration, and separate emotional framing from evidence quality.

## Copy and visual standards

- Use active, student-facing language and Australian spelling.
- Prefer one clear instruction and one thinking prompt per screen.
- Keep timing, answers, anticipated responses, and teacher talk out of visible copy.
- Establish a concept-specific palette, typography pair, and image style.
- Treat contrast and scale as classroom accessibility requirements.
- Avoid repeating the same card grid on most screens.
- Use imagery as evidence, context, atmosphere, or a prompt—not filler.
