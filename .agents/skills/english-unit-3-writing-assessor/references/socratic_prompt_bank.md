# Socratic Prompting & Diagnostic Question Bank — Year 5 Persuasive Writing

This reference bank supplies targeted, inquiry-based Socratic coaching questions for Year 5 persuasive writing. Assessors and coaches must use these prompts to help students identify their own revision steps without ghostwriting or providing solutions.

---

## 🛑 P0 Safety Guardrail: Strict Zero-Ghostwriting Policy

* **NEVER** write sample sentences, replacement clauses, or model phrases for the student to copy.
* **NEVER** invent metaphors, similes, rhetorical questions, statistics, quotes, or vocabulary substitutions for them.
* **ALWAYS** quote the student's exact phrase from their text as the anchor point.
* **ALWAYS** formulate questions that prompt the student to think, research, or apply the grammatical device themselves.

| Target Dimension | ❌ FORBIDDEN GHOSTWRITING (Never do this) | ✅ PERMITTED SOCRATIC COACHING (Always do this) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Figurative Language** | *"Change your sentence to: 'The bulldozers roared like hungry monsters.'*" | *"In your sentence, 'The machines cut down the trees loudly,' how could you use a simile to compare the roar of the machines to something fierce or destructive?"* |
| **Authoritative Evidence** | *"You should write: 'According to the WWF, 80% of forests are gone.'*" | *"In your sentence, 'Lots of orangutans are losing their homes,' you make a compelling point, but it lacks evidence. Which wildlife organisation or research group could you cite here to prove this fact?"* |
| **Complex Sentences** | *"Rewrite it as: 'Although palm oil is cheap, it destroys ancient ecosystems.'*" | *"Look at your two short sentences: 'Palm oil is cheap. It ruins forests.' How could you combine these into one complex sentence starting with a conjunction like 'Although' or 'Even though'?"* |
| **High Modality** | *"Replace 'maybe we should' with 'we must urgently'."* | *"In your sentence, 'Maybe we should think about stopping this,' your words sound hesitant. What high-modality verb could replace 'maybe we should' to show your reader that immediate action is essential?"* |
| **Topic-Specific Vocabulary** | *"Add words like deforestation, biodiversity, and monoculture."* | *"In your sentence, 'Cutting trees hurts animals and plants,' what scientific terms have we studied in our unit that would make your explanation sound more authoritative?"* |

## 🏗️ 1. Text Structure & Paragraph Stages

### A. Introduction (Hook, Contention, Roadmap)
* **Missing or Weak Hook:**
  * *"Read your opening sentence: '[Quote]'. How could you open with a startling fact, a vivid descriptive scene, or a rhetorical question to grab your reader's attention right from the start?"*
* **Unclear Stance/Contention:**
  * *"After reading your first paragraph, a reader might ask: 'Is the writer for or against this change?' Which sentence can you edit so your point of view is 100% clear?"*
* **Missing Argument Roadmap:**
  * *"An effective persuasive introduction previews your main reasons. What are the two or three key arguments you plan to explore in your body paragraphs? How can you introduce them briefly in your opening?"*

### B. Body Paragraphs (PEEL / TEEL Structure)
* **Missing Topic Sentence:**
  * *"Your paragraph jumps straight into details about [topic]. What opening sentence could you write for this paragraph to clearly signpost the single main argument you are discussing?"*
* **Thin Elaboration:**
  * *"In your sentence: '[Quote]', you state that [claim], but you haven't explained *how* or *why*. What real-world consequence can you describe to explain this to your reader?"*
* **Weak Linking Sentence:**
  * *"How does the argument in this paragraph link back to your main opinion? What concluding sentence could you add to wrap up this point before moving to the next paragraph?"*

### C. Counterargument & Rebuttal
* **Unacknowledged Opposing View:**
  * *"Strong persuaders don't ignore the other side; they address it. What might someone who disagrees with your opinion say? How could you begin a sentence with: 'Some people may argue that...'?"*
* **Incomplete Rebuttal:**
  * *"You mentioned that the opposing side thinks '[Quote]'. Now, how can you prove them wrong or show that your reasons are much more important? What fact or reason disproves their concern?"*

### D. Conclusion (Restatement, Summary, Call to Action)
* **Repetitive Restatement:**
  * *"Look at your final paragraph and compare it to your introduction. You've used almost identical words. How can you restate your opinion using fresh, powerful vocabulary rather than repeating yourself?"*
* **Missing Call to Action:**
  * *"Persuasive texts aim to inspire change. What specific action do you want your audience to take right now? How could you conclude with an inspiring call to action or a thought-provoking challenge?"*

## 🔬 2. Supporting Details & Authoritative Evidence

* **Bare Assertions without Evidence:**
  * *"In your sentence: '[Quote]', you make a bold statement. What proof or evidence can you provide so the reader knows this is true and not just your personal feeling?"*
* **Informal or Unnamed Sources:**
  * *"You wrote: 'Experts say...' or 'Studies show...'. Who are these experts? Can you name a specific university, scientific organisation (like CSIRO or WWF), or community leader to make your evidence authoritative?"*
* **Missing Numerical or Survey Data:**
  * *"Where could you incorporate a percentage, survey finding, or statistic to demonstrate that this issue affects many people or animals?"*

---

## ✍️ 3. Sentence Variety & Grammar

* **Run-on Sentences:**
  * *"Read your sentence aloud: '[Quote]'. Notice where you naturally need to breathe. Where could you place a full stop to divide this long sentence into two clear, impactful sentences?"*
* **Over-reliance on Simple Sentences:**
  * *"You have several short sentences in a row: '[Quote 1]' and '[Quote 2]'. How could you connect them into a complex sentence using a conjunction like *because, whereas, although,* or *since*?"*
* **Underdeveloped Noun Groups:**
  * *"Look at the noun '[simple noun]' in your sentence: '[Quote]'. What adjectives or describing phrases could you add before or after the noun to paint a precise, vivid picture in the reader's mind?"*
* **Inconsistent Verb Tense:**
  * *"In Paragraph [X], you shift between present tense ('[Quote]') and past tense ('[Quote]'). Since this is an ongoing issue you want to change now, how can you adjust your verbs so they stay consistently in the present tense?"*

---

## 🎭 4. Persuasive & Literary Devices

* **Low Modality Language:**
  * *"In your sentence: '[Quote]', you used words like 'maybe' and 'could'. What high-modality words (such as *must, certainly, essential, undeniably*) could you use to show absolute conviction?"*
* **Missing Rhetorical Questions:**
  * *"Where in this paragraph could you speak directly to your reader by asking a rhetorical question that makes them stop and reflect on the consequences?"*
* **Rule of Three (Tricolon):**
  * *"Persuasive speeches often use three powerful descriptors or actions together. How could you expand your description of [issue] into a set of three compelling points?"*
* **Figurative Language (Metaphor / Simile / Personification):**
  * *"In your sentence: '[Quote]', you describe the environment plainly. How could you use a metaphor or personification to give the subject living characteristics and evoke an emotional reaction?"*

---

## 🔗 5. Cohesion & Text Signposting

* **Abrupt Paragraph Transitions:**
  * *"Your transition from Paragraph [X] to Paragraph [Y] feels abrupt. What cohesive linking word (for example: *Furthermore, In addition, Consequently, Conversely*) could you use at the start of your new paragraph to show how the ideas connect?"*
* **Sentence Starting Points (Theme/Topic Prominence):**
  * *"Notice how many of your sentences begin with 'I think' or 'The'. How can you vary your sentence starters by beginning with a dependent clause, a prepositional phrase, or an adverbial connective?"*

---

## 📝 6. Punctuation, Mechanics & Spelling

* **Direct Quotation Punctuation:**
  * *"You cited evidence in Paragraph [X]: '[Quote]'. Have you enclosed the expert's exact spoken words inside quotation marks, with the comma placed inside the quotation mark?"*
* **Commas in Complex Sentences:**
  * *"When you begin a sentence with a dependent clause (starting with 'Although' or 'Because'), where should you place the comma to separate the clauses for your reader?"*
* **Spelling Self-Correction:**
  * *"Check the spelling of '[word]' in Paragraph [X]. Break the word into its syllables or root word plus suffix. Does it match the pattern you know?"*
