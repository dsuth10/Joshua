# Lesson: The Three Levels of Australian Government

## 🎯 Learning Intentions
- We are learning about the structure of the Australian Government.
- We are learning to identify the roles and responsibilities of the Federal, State/Territory, and Local governments.

## ✅ Success Criteria
- I can name the three levels of the Australian Government.
- I can explain at least two responsibilities for each level of government.
- I can correctly categorize different government services into their respective levels.

## 📚 Curriculum Alignment (AC v9)
- **Civics and Citizenship (Year 6)**: The roles and responsibilities of the three levels of government, including the provision of services.

## 🧰 Resources Required
- `Presentation.pptx` (Interactive slides on the three levels of government)
- `Student_Handout.docx` (Mapping responsibilities worksheet)
- `Assessment.docx` (10-question Microsoft Forms quiz)
- Whiteboard and markers

---

## 🧑‍🏫 Teaching Sequence

### 1. Hook (10 minutes)
- **Activity**: "Who is responsible?" 
- Ask students: *If the road outside your house has a massive pothole, who do you call to fix it? What if the whole country is being invaded? What if your local hospital needs more doctors?*
- Write their answers on the board.
- **Explain**: Australia is a big country. To make sure everything runs smoothly, we don't just have one big government trying to do everything. We divide the power and responsibilities into three different levels.

### 2. I Do: Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
- **Presentation**: Open `Presentation.pptx`.
- **Federal Government**: Explain that they handle big-picture things that affect the whole country (e.g., Defence, Immigration, Currency, Post, Telecommunications). Based in Canberra. Led by the Prime Minister.
- **State/Territory Government**: Explain that they handle things that affect people within state borders (e.g., Schools, Hospitals, Police, Public Transport, Main Roads). Led by the Premier (or Chief Minister).
- **Local Government**: Explain that they handle local community needs (e.g., Rubbish collection, Local parks, Libraries, Pet registration, Local roads/potholes). Led by the Mayor.
- *Check for Understanding*: Pause and ask students to recall one responsibility for each level.

### 3. We Do: Guided Practice (15 minutes)
- **Activity**: "Sort the Services"
- On the whiteboard or using an interactive slide, display a list of 10 services (e.g., *Buying submarines, Hiring teachers, Fixing a broken swing at the park, Printing new $10 notes, Running a vaccination clinic, Collecting the recycling bins*).
- As a class, discuss and categorize each service under Federal, State, or Local government.
- Discuss *why* a particular level handles that responsibility (e.g., why wouldn't the Federal government collect our rubbish?).

### 4. You Do: Independent Practice (15 minutes)
- **Handout**: Distribute `Student_Handout.docx`.
- **Task**: Students must independently map a list of scenarios and services to the correct level of government. They will also write a short justification for one of their choices.
- **Differentiation**: 
  - *Support*: Provide a word bank and visual cues matching the presentation.
  - *Extension*: Ask students to research or propose a scenario where two levels of government might have to work together (e.g., during a natural disaster or building a major highway).

### 5. Plenary / Assessment (5 minutes)
- **Review**: Quickly go over the answers to the handout.
- **Exit Ticket**: Students complete the 10-question multiple-choice `Assessment.docx` (which can be administered via Microsoft Forms) to check their understanding of the lesson's core concepts.
