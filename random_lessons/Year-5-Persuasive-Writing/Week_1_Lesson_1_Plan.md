# Lesson Plan: Persuasive Essay Writing (PEEL Structure)

## Learning Intention

Students will understand the structural components of a persuasive essay and learn how to construct powerful paragraphs using the **PEEL** method.

## Success Criteria

- I can identify the parts of a persuasive essay (Introduction, Body Paragraphs, Conclusion).
- I can identify and use persuasive techniques (Rhetorical Questions, Emotive Language, Rule of Three).
- I can use the PEEL structure (Point, Elaboration, Evidence, Link) to write a persuasive paragraph.

---

## 1. Introduction (Warm-up) - 10 Minutes

- **Hook**: Ask students: "Should we have mobile phones in the classroom?" Quick poll of the class.
- **Introduction to Persuasion**: Discuss what it means to "persuade" (convince someone to agree with your point of view).
- **Techniques Check**: Introduce the "Persuasive Toolkit":
  - **Alliteration**: "Make my mind move."
  - **Rhetorical Questions**: "Who wouldn't want more fun?"
  - **Emotive Language**: "Unthinkable," "wonderful," "terrible."
  - **Rule of Three**: "Fast, fun, and fantastic."
  - **Statistics**: "8 out of 10 students agree..."

## 2. Core Activity 1: The Whole Essay Structure - 15 Minutes

- **The Introduction**: Signposts the arguments (Intro -> Point 1 -> Point 2 -> Point 3).
- **Body Paragraphs**: The meat of the argument. Each paragraph uses PEEL.
- **The Conclusion**: Summarises the main points and finishes with a **Call to Action**.

## 3. Core Activity 2: Deep Dive into PEEL - 20 Minutes

- **P - Point (Blue)**: State your main argument clearly.
- **E - Elaboration (Green)**: Explain your point in more detail.
- **E - Evidence (Orange)**: Give a fact, statistic, or example to prove it.
- **L - Link (Red)**: Link back to the main topic or the next paragraph.

---

### Example Paragraph (Topic: Mobile Phones in Schools)

- **(Point - Blue)** Firstly, mobile phones are powerful educational tools that can help students learn more effectively.
- **(Elaboration - Green)** They allow us to access information instantly, use educational apps, and research topics we are curious about right in the middle of a lesson.
- **(Evidence - Orange)** In fact, a recent survey found that 75% of teachers believe technology helps students stay more engaged with their work.
- **(Link - Red)** Therefore, allowing phones would turn our classrooms into modern, high-tech learning environments.

---

## 4. Conclusion (Consolidation) - 10 Minutes

- **Review**: Revisit the success criteria.
- **Exit Ticket**: Students write one "Point" and one "Elaboration" for an argument against mobile phones.

## 5. Differentiation

- **Support**: Provide a "PEEL Sentence Starter" sheet for students who need help beginning each sentence.
- **Extension**: Challenge students to include at least three different persuasive techniques within a single PEEL paragraph.
