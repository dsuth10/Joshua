# Book Week Song Challenge: Teacher Run Sheet

## The outcome by 2:30 pm

Each class submits one original, school-appropriate lyric and one production brief. The lyric celebrates the books, characters, places and story moments represented in that class's Book Week costumes. It includes the class/bus mascot, **the dingo**.

The producer will turn the lyric into a track for the Book Week Disco in the undercover area from **2:40 pm to 3:00 pm**.

### Non-negotiable submission format

- **Years 5-6:** Verse 1 -> Chorus -> Verse 2 -> Chorus. Write four short lines in each section; the chorus may repeat.
- Lyrics are original classroom writing. Referencing a book or costume is welcome, but do not copy published lyrics, poems or song melodies.
- The song names or clearly shouts out **the dingo** at least once.
- The producer brief has one genre, one speed and one mood. Do not request a living artist's voice or a copy of a specific song.

## Before the lesson (5-10 minutes)

1. Start [Book_Week_Song_Challenge_Presentation.html](Book_Week_Song_Challenge_Presentation.html) in a browser and use Fullscreen. It contains speaker notes, timers and slide controls.
2. Print the Year 5-6 songwriting page from `Book_Week_Song_Challenge_Student_Pack.docx`, plus a copy of the final hand-in page.
3. Nominate a class scribe, or let students draft collectively while the teacher controls the final wording.
4. Have a photographed or typed hand-in route ready. The final page is deliberately designed as the exact information the music producer needs.
5. Begin no later than **2:00 pm**. A 1:55 pm start gives a useful five-minute collection buffer.

## Learning intention and visible success

**We are learning to turn a shared story world into an original song that an audience can sing and dance to.**

By the end, students can point to:

- a character, place or action from their costume/story world;
- a repeating chorus that holds the class idea together;
- one dingo shout-out; and
- a clear choice about how the music should sound.

The finished sheet, rather than participation alone, is the exit evidence.

## The 30-minute lesson sequence

### 1. Dramatic hook and mission (0:00-0:03)

Say: **"Today, the Book Parade is not finishing when the costumes come off. We are going to turn our stories into music."**

Explain that every class will make one lyric. AI will turn the words into a professionally produced track in time for the disco. Keep the distinction clear: students are the songwriters; the AI is the music producer.

Project the song shape: `VERSE -> CHORUS -> VERSE -> CHORUS`.

**Check:** Ask, "Which part comes back so people can join in?" Expected idea: the chorus.

### 2. WHO / WHERE / WHAT word storm (0:03-0:08)

Project the three prompts and record only vivid, usable ideas.

- **WHO?** Characters, creatures and costume identities in the room.
- **WHERE?** Story places, real places transformed by the costumes, or a shared imagined setting.
- **WHAT?** Actions, problems, powers, journeys and celebrations.

Ask for details instead of a long costume list: "What would we see? What would we hear? What is moving?" Keep 8-12 strong words or phrases.

**Check:** Circle one item from each column. If a column is empty, prompt once more before moving on.

### 3. Choose the class picture (0:08-0:11)

Invite a fast class choice: Which moment deserves the song? Frame it as a scene, not a list: "A dingo bus rolls into a story-world parade" is stronger than naming twenty costumes.

Use this sentence orally: **"Our song is about ___, where ___, and everyone is ___."**

Invite two candidate scenes, then vote.

### 4. Build the chorus first (0:11-0:16)

Tell students: the chorus is the bit everyone can sing at the disco. It needs a repeated phrase, a strong verb and the dingo.

Model the shape without giving them words to copy:

> We are ___, we are ___ / Dingo ___, ___ / [repeat the key phrase] / [a final invitation to dance, sing or join]

Ask students to clap the rhythm of the strongest class line. If it runs out of breath, trim it. Write only one final chorus together.

Ask for one repeated phrase and one deliberate sound/rhyme choice. Reject forced rhyme that makes the image unclear.

### 5. Write Part/Verse 1: arrive in the story world (0:16-0:20)

Use the WHO and WHERE words. The job of the first section is to place the listener inside the scene.

- Draft four short lines; count or clap the beats and revise one clunky line.

### 6. Write Part/Verse 2: make something happen (0:20-0:24)

Use the WHAT words. The job of the second section is action, surprise, courage or celebration.

Prompt: "What changes? What do our characters do together?"

Avoid plot summaries. Keep one memorable action and a class feeling.

### 7. Rehearse, improve and choose the sound (0:24-0:28)

Read/sing the complete lyric with a steady clap. Pause after the chorus: Can the whole class say it together? Does the dingo appear? Does every line add an image or action?

Then vote, with one vote only for each choice:

| Decision | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Genre | Pop, Rock, K-Pop, Hip-Hop, Dance/EDM, Musical Theatre, Country |
| Speed | Slow, Medium, Fast |
| Mood | Happy, Funny, Cool, Epic, Magical, Spooky |

If votes split, the teacher makes the quick final call. Momentum is more valuable than a second ballot.

### 8. Final hand-in (0:28-0:30)

The scribe reads the lyric word-for-word. Students give a thumbs-up for every check:

1. It is one original class song.
2. The chorus repeats.
3. The dingo appears.
4. Genre, speed and mood are selected.
5. The class and teacher names are written legibly.

Photograph or deliver the **Music Producer Hand-in** page by 2:30 pm. If time is tight, submit a clear photo of that page first; the producer can work from it.

## Years 5-6 songwriting studio

- Assign two quick roles: detail hunters (images/actions) and rhythm keepers (clap/read lines aloud). Rotate or pool their ideas after two minutes.
- Invite rhyme, rhythm, repetition, contrast or strong imagery, but preserve meaning first.
- Require one visible revision after a whole-class read-through: shorten, sharpen a verb, improve an image or strengthen the repeat.
- Extension: use a purposeful contrast in Verse 2 (quiet/loud, lost/found, shadow/light) that earns the final chorus.

## Inclusion and behaviour supports

- Keep the same destination: a class-owned lyric and production brief. Reduce language load or the number of initial choices, not the ownership.
- Students can contribute through a word bank, speaking to a partner, drawing a scene, gesture, sound effects, speech-to-text or scribing.
- Use a predictable turn structure: think for five seconds, partner whisper, then class share.
- Do not press students to disclose a personal connection to a character or story. Costume and book choices are enough.
- If a costume/book is unfamiliar, ask what the character does or what colour/object/sound belongs in its world.

## Quality check for the music producer

Use this quick triage after collecting sheets:

| Check | Green | Quick repair if needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singable chorus | Repeats a short, clear phrase | Repeat the best class phrase as the last line |
| Dingo link | Dingo is named in the lyric | Add a spoken/shouted dingo call in the chorus |
| Story image | Listener can picture a place/action | Add one concrete noun or verb from the word storm |
| Usable brief | Genre, speed, mood all selected | Default to Pop / Medium / Happy only with teacher approval |
| Safety/copyright | Original words; no artist imitation request | Remove copied lyrics, song titles or artist names; retain the class's original idea |

## Contingencies

- **Class is late:** start with the word storm, dictate the shared scene, write a two-line chorus with dingo, then add one line for each part. Submit the short form rather than an unfinished long song.
- **No unanimous sound vote:** teacher chooses the sound that matches the chorus energy.
- **A line is too long:** keep the most vivid words and cut the rest.
- **No dingo idea:** use a refrain such as "Dingo, dingo, lead the way" and let the class adapt it.
- **Technology fails:** use the printed resource only. The projected deck is a support, not a dependency.
