# Journal and Accessibility Standards

## 1. Journal Standards

Every JN (Journal) resource is a cumulative evidence record, not a disposable worksheet. It must contain:
1. Lesson question and success criteria.
2. Retrieval or prediction section.
3. A partially completed organiser or representation (avoid copying-heavy notes).
4. Practical plan, variables, or proposed circuit.
5. Risk/safety check where relevant.
6. Field for screenshot, photograph, data table, or conventional circuit diagram.
7. Scientific explanation using claim-evidence-reasoning (CER) or equivalent structure.
8. Debugging, limitation, or improvement field.
9. Exit reflection.
10. Accessible response choices for formative evidence.
11. One extension activity that deepens reasoning.

The journal must be produced in two formats:
- **OneNote-ready DOCX** (editable, large fields for responses/images).
- **Markdown/HTML source**.

When students work in pairs, the journal must still prompt each student to retain individual predictions, their own screenshot/photo, a personal explanation, and their specific role/contribution.

## 2. Accessibility Gates (P1 Validation)

The lesson resources must pass these accessibility checks:
- **Operability**: Presentation and website controls must be keyboard operable.
- **Alternatives**: Essential interactions must have non-drag alternatives.
- **Colour**: Colour must never be the sole carrier of meaning (e.g. terminals, energy forms).
- **Semantics**: Images and diagrams require meaningful alternative text.
- **Media**: Videos must have captions and transcripts (a storyboard/transcript is the minimum guaranteed output).
- **Document Structure**: OneNote/DOCX resources must use headings, short directions, and accessible tables.
- **Fallback**: Printable or low-bandwidth alternatives must exist for essential website activities.
- **Cognitive Demand**: Formative response choices must not reduce the scientific demand of the task.
