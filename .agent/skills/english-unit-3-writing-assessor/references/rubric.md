## 🎯 Official A–E Achievement Standards (Writing and Creating)

### Standard A
* **W1 (Text Structure & Audience):** Creates a detailed, sequenced and cohesive written persuasive text for an identified audience on a topic, developing and expanding on ideas, providing supporting details and drawing information from authoritative sources.
* **W2 (Paragraphs & Cohesion):** Uses a range of well-structured, sequenced paragraphs and cohesive devices to effectively organise, develop and link ideas.
* **W3 (Language Features):** Uses a wide range of language features creatively, including more complex sentences with expanded noun groups, relevant verb tense for effect, literary devices and topic-specific vocabulary, that engage and persuade.

### Standard B
* **W1 (Text Structure & Audience):** Creates a sequenced written persuasive text for an identified audience on a topic, developing and expanding on ideas and providing supporting details that draw information from sources.
* **W2 (Paragraphs & Cohesion):** Uses sequenced paragraphs and cohesive devices to organise ideas with topic sentences to guide the reader through the text.
* **W3 (Language Features):** Uses language features including complex sentences that have expanded noun groups, tenses, topic-specific vocabulary, and literary devices.

### Standard C
* **W1 (Text Structure & Audience):** Creates a written persuasive text for an identified audience, developing and expanding on ideas with supporting details on a topic.
* **W2 (Paragraphs & Cohesion):** Uses paragraphs to organise, develop and link ideas.
* **W3 (Language Features):** Uses language features including complex sentences, tenses, topic-specific vocabulary and literary devices.

### Standard D
* **W1 (Text Structure & Audience):** Creates a written persuasive text for an audience that develops ideas about a topic.
* **W2 & W3 Combined:** Uses paragraphs and language features such as simple and compound sentences and topic-specific vocabulary.

### Standard E
* **W1 (Text Structure & Audience):** Creates a written persuasive text that provides ideas about a topic.
* **W2 & W3 Combined:** Uses paragraphs, simple and compound sentences or language features such as topic-specific vocabulary.

## 🗺️ Component Coverage Map

When evaluating student writing, evaluate every component within the three analytical elements:

### Element W1: Text, Audience and Development
1. **Persuasive Purpose & Stance:** Clear point of view and opinion maintained consistently.
2. **Identified Audience & Adaptation:** Language, tone, and framing consciously adapted for the intended reader.
3. **Whole-Text Structure & Sequence:** Deliberate stages of argument (introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion / call to action).
4. **Development & Expansion of Ideas:** Elaborating on reasons through explanations, justifications, and real-world impacts.
5. **Supporting Details & Evidence:** Moving beyond bare assertions; citing facts, statistics, examples, or surveys.
6. **Information Drawn from Sources:** Mentioning sources (Standard B) vs drawing from authoritative sources such as WWF, CSIRO, universities, or recognized experts (Standard A).
7. **Whole-Text Cohesion:** Sustained unity of purpose across the entire piece (Standard A).

### Element W2: Paragraphs and Cohesion
1. **Paragraph Structure:** Clear division into distinct paragraphs with appropriate spacing/indentation.
2. **Topic Sentences:** Distinct topic sentences at the start of body paragraphs to signpost arguments for the reader (Standard B requirement).
3. **Internal Paragraph Development:** Sentences within paragraphs logically develop the specific claim of that paragraph.
4. **Paragraph Sequencing:** Logical flow and progression from one argument to the next.
5. **Cohesive Devices & Transitional Links:** Conjunctions and text connectives (*Furthermore, Consequently, On the other hand, In addition, Ultimately*) linking ideas between sentences and across paragraphs.
6. **Range & Effectiveness:** A variety of well-structured paragraphs operating seamlessly to guide the reader (Standard A).

### Element W3: Language Features & Devices
1. **Sentence Variety & Complexity:** Balance of simple, compound, and complex sentences with subordinate clauses.
2. **Expanded Noun Groups:** Noun groups with pre-modifiers and post-modifiers providing precise description (Standard B/A).
3. **Verb Tense Control:** Accurate past/present/future tense usage, deployed intentionally for rhetorical effect at Standard A.
4. **Topic-Specific Vocabulary:** Precise technical terms related to the chosen topic (e.g. *biodiversity, deforestation, renewable energy, nutritional value*).
5. **Literary & Persuasive Devices:** Rhetorical questions, high modality words (*must, essential, undeniably*), tricolon (rule of three), emotive language, and figurative language (metaphor, simile, personification).
6. **Creative & Persuasive Impact:** Language features crafted deliberately to engage, influence, and captivate the reader (Standard A).

## 📋 Supplementary Assessment Guidance

* **Suggested Length:** 200–400 words (informed by QCAA guidelines). This is a flexible guideline, not a punitive ceiling. Excessive length should be assessed in terms of repetition, focus, and audience engagement rather than an automatic grade deduction.
* **Drafting Conditions:** Individual work completed over multiple lessons; drafting with teacher conferencing and peer feedback is explicitly encouraged by the curriculum unit plan.
* **Spelling Monitoring Strategy:** Evaluated as a separate diagnostic monitoring observation (*"Spell using phonic, morphemic and grammatical knowledge"* — Demonstrating / Not yet demonstrating / Comment). Final written submissions show orthographic accuracy patterns but cannot definitively reveal real-time cognitive strategies.
* **Speaking & Listening Scope Boundary:** Part B (oral presentation to an audience using voice features like pitch, pace, tone, and volume) is assessed independently and must **never** be aggregated into or confused with the Part A written mark.
