# Lesson Plan: Quantum Quest - Mastering Long Multiplication

**Subject:** Mathematics  
**Year Level:** 5  
**Topic:** Multiplication by 2-digit numbers (Long Multiplication)  
**Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Curriculum Mapping:** [AC9M5N06] - Solve problems involving multiplication of larger numbers by one- or two-digit numbers, choosing efficient calculation strategies.

---

## 🎯 Learning Intentions (WALT)
- We are learning to multiply 2-digit numbers by other 2-digit numbers using the long multiplication algorithm.
- We are learning to accurately place the 'placeholder zero' when multiplying by tens.

## ✅ Success Criteria (WILO)
- I can partition the multiplier into units and tens.
- I can multiply the top number by the units digit.
- I can place a zero before multiplying by the tens digit.
- I can add partial products to find the final answer.

---

## 🚀 The Hook: "Quantum Quest" Opening Day (10 mins)
*Teacher Note: Display Slide 2 with the Theme Park Logo.*
**Scenario:** You have been hired as the Lead Engineer for **Quantum Quest**, the world’s first anti-gravity theme park. Before we can open the gates, we need to calculate the capacity of our main attractions. If we get the math wrong, the rides won't have enough power, or worse—too many people will be in the anti-gravity chamber at once!

**Quick Challenge:** The 'Plasma Pulsar' ride holds 32 people. If it runs 14 times an hour, how many people can ride it in one hour? (Mental check: Is it more than 300? More than 400?)

---

## 🛠️ I Do: The Algorithm Blueprint (15 mins)
*Focus on procedural accuracy.*

1.  **Step 1: Partitioning.** Explain that multiplying $36 \times 72$ is just $(36 \times 2) + (36 \times 70)$.
2.  **Step 2: Units First.** Multiply the top number by the ones digit of the bottom number.
3.  **Step 3: The Magic Zero.** Crucial step! Before multiplying by the tens digit, we MUST put down the placeholder zero because we are actually multiplying by 70, not 7.
4.  **Step 4: Tens Multiplication.** Multiply the top number by the tens digit.
5.  **Step 5: Recombination.** Add the two partial products together.

*Demonstrate $36 \times 72$ on the board/slide, highlighting the zero in a different colour (Australian spelling).*

---

## 🤝 We Do: Engineering Check (15 mins)
**Problem 1: The Zero-G Cafe.** 
Each table seats 24 people. There are 53 tables. How many people can eat at once?
- *Step 1:* Partition 53 into 3 and 50.
- *Step 2:* $24 \times 3 =$ ?
- *Step 3:* Drop the Magic Zero.
- *Step 4:* $24 \times 5 =$ ? (making it $24 \times 50$).
- *Step 5:* Add them up.

**Problem 2: Cosmic Coaster.** 
The coaster has 43 carriages. Each carriage holds 31 riders.
- Students work on mini-whiteboards/notebooks simultaneously with the teacher.

---

## 🏗️ You Do: Differentiated Roles (15 mins)

### Tier 1: Junior Builders (Support)
- Use guided calculation grids with the "Magic Zero" already printed.
- Focus on smaller 2-digit multipliers (e.g., $12, 13, 14$).
- Context: Ordering supplies for the Gift Shop (Stickers, Pins).

### Tier 2: Senior Engineers (Core)
- Complete the standard long multiplication algorithm independently.
- Context: Ride capacity and ticket revenue for "Quantum Quest".
- Challenge: 4 problems + 1 word problem.

### Tier 3: Project Directors (Extension)
- Multi-step problems involving budget constraints.
- Multipliers up to 99.
- Context: "If tickets cost $15 and we sell 72 tickets for 24 different sessions, how much revenue do we generate?"

---

## 🏁 Wrap Up & Reflection (5 mins)
**The Error Audit:** Show an example on the board where the placeholder zero was forgotten. Ask students: "Why is this answer $432$ instead of $2592$? What did the engineer forget?"

**Exit Ticket:** Solve $12 \times 89$ before leaving.
