# Lesson Plan: Finding Missing Numbers

**Subject**: Mathematics  
**Year Level**: 5  
**Topic**: Number and Algebra - Finding unknown values in numerical equations  
**Duration**: 60 minutes  

---

## 📋 Learning Intention
Students will identify and apply inverse operations to find unknown values (missing numbers) in single-step numerical equations involving addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

## ✅ Success Criteria
- I can identify the inverse of addition (+), subtraction (-), multiplication (×), and division (÷).
- I can rewrite an equation using its inverse operation to find a missing number.
- I can solve for unknown values in single-step equations.
- I can check my answer by substituting the missing number back into the original equation.

---

## 🎓 Curriculum Mapping (AC v9)
- **AC9M5A01**: Recognise and explain the connection between multiplication and division as inverse operations and use this to develop families of number facts.
- **AC9M5A02**: Find unknown values in numerical equations involving multiplication and division using the properties of numbers and operations.

---

## 🚀 Introduction (10 mins) - Warm-up
### Fact Family Flash
- Quick-fire round: Display a set of three numbers (e.g., 3, 4, 12).
- Ask students to shout out the 4 related facts (3 × 4 = 12, 4 × 3 = 12, 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 12 ÷ 4 = 3).
- Repeat with addition/subtraction families (e.g., 7, 8, 15).
- **Socratic Question**: "If I know that $15 - 7 = 8$, what other facts do I automatically know? How do these numbers 'work' together?"

---

## 💡 Core Activities (40 mins)

### Part 1: Direct Instruction (I Do) - The Balance Scale
- Explain that an equation is like a balance scale. To find a missing piece, we can "undo" the operation using its inverse.
- **Addition/Subtraction Inverse**:
  - Example: $\Box + 37 = 40$
  - Model: "To undo $+ 37$, I subtract $37$ from $40$." $\rightarrow 40 - 37 = 3$.
  - Example: $16 - \Box = 7$
  - Model: "This one is tricky! If I have $16$ and end with $7$, how much did I lose? $16 - 7 = 9$."
- **Multiplication/Division Inverse**:
  - Example: $\Box \times 8 = 24$
  - Model: "To undo $\times 8$, I divide $24$ by $8$." $\rightarrow 24 \div 8 = 3$.
  - Example: $56 = \Box - 37$ (Wait, this is subtraction, model the move).
  - Model: "To find the starting number, I add $37$ to $56$." $\rightarrow 56 + 37 = 93$.

### Part 2: Guided Practice (We Do) - Interactive Whiteboards
- Display equations on the board from the worksheet (e.g., $\Box \times 7 = 42$, $12 \div \Box = 3$).
- Students solve on mini-whiteboards and show their "inverse step".
- **Think-Pair-Share**: "Why does dividing help us find a missing number in a multiplication problem?"

### Part 3: Independent Practice (You Do) - Handout
- Students work through the **Finding Missing Numbers Handout**.
- **Support Group**: Focus on Section 1 & 3 (visual cues and basic facts).
- **Core Group**: Focus on Sections 1-5 (variety of operations).
- **Extension Group**: Focus on Section 6 and create their own "Missing Number" challenge for a partner.

---

## 🏁 Conclusion (10 mins) - Review
### The Calculator Check
- Model how to use a calculator to verify the "missing number" by plugging it back into the original equation.
- **Exit Ticket**: "What is the inverse of division? Give an example of how you used it today."

---

## 🪜 Differentiation

### 🆘 Support
- Provide "Inverse Operation Cheat Sheets" (e.g., $+$ pairs with $-$, $\times$ pairs with $\div$).
- Use concrete materials (counters or MAB blocks) for smaller numbers.
- Provide equations with visual placeholders (boxes/pictures).

### 🚀 Extension
- Introduce multi-step equations (e.g., $2 \times \Box + 5 = 15$).
- Challenge students to write their own word problems involving missing values (e.g., "I had some coins, I gave away 15, and now I have 78. How many did I start with?").
