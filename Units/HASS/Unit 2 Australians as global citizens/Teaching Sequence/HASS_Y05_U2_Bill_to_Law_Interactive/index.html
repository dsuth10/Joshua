AUSTRALIAN PARLIAMENTARY SIMULATION

Have you ever wondered how an idea turns into a real law in Australia?

In our democracy, laws are not made by one single person. Instead, representatives elected by the community gather in Federal Parliament to propose, debate, check, and vote on laws to keep our community safe and fair.

As an MP (Member of Parliament), you are about to sponsor a new bill representing your community. Step through the green House, the red Senate, and the Governor-General's desk to see if your idea can become an Act of Parliament.

THE 7 STAGES:
  1. Community recognises an issue
  2. Written as a Bill & introduced
  3. Debated in House of Reps
  4. Vote passed by the House
  5. Debated and passed by the Senate
  6. Governor-General gives Royal Assent
  7. Act of Parliament (a law is born!)

Glossary of Terms

  • Bill: A draft or proposal for a new law before it has been voted on and approved.
  • House of Representatives: The "Lower House" (green room). The government is formed here, and most bills start here.
  • Senate: The "Upper House" (red room). Checks laws proposed by the House of Representatives.
  • Debate: A formal discussion where MPs give speeches sharing reasons for and against a bill.
  • Consensus: A general agreement reached by members with different viewpoints.
  • Royal Assent: The signature of the Governor-General that makes the bill a legal Act of Parliament.
  • Act of Parliament: A bill that has passed all stages and is now a law of Australia.
