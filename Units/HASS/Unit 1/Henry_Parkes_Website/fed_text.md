Context of Source 1: This is a black-and-white pen-and-ink drawing by cartoonist and illustrator Herbert Cotton from 1900, of the New South Wales politician, Sir Henry Parkes, which appeared in the Daily Telegraph. Beneath the drawing is a quote from Parkes in 1889, in which he speaks of a vision for a federation of the Australian colonies.

‘I do see very clearly that there may come a time and that time not very remote, when the Australian Colonies may be brought more into the position of one great and united people. We should have an outline of empire, such as we could never hope for as isolated colonies, and our place would be admitted in the rank of nations — Sir Henry Parkes. August 1889

Source 2

Henry Parkes’s key arguments for Federation

Henry Parkes argued that Australia needed:

a national defence force to protect the borders from foreign attack 

a central government with executive powers to control the defence force and make decisions without the involvement of Great Britain 

a uniform rail gauge to aid the movement of the national defence around the country. 

He also argued that: 

America had between three and four million people when it became a Federation and gained independence from Britain. America achieved Federation through war.

Australia had around three and a half million people by the 1880s and surely could achieve Federation and independence from Britain through peaceful means, not war. 

Each of the separate colonies should send a representation (delegate) to attend a convention to write a constitution for a Federal government. 

The delegates from the colonies should be appointed by Parliament to fully represent the different needs of each colony.
