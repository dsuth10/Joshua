# Lesson Plan — Lesson 15: Converting Metric Units of Length

**Unit:** Maths Unit 2: Measurement & Geometry  
**Year Level:** Year 5  
**Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Curriculum Mapping:** 
* **AC9M5M01**: Choose appropriate metric units when measuring the length, mass, and capacity of objects; use smaller units or a combination of units to obtain a more accurate measure.
* **AC9M6M01** (Bridging): Convert between common metric units of length, mass, and capacity; choose and use decimal representations of metric measurements.

---

## Learning Intentions
* We are learning to identify the most appropriate metric unit to measure length.
* We are learning to convert between millimetres (mm), centimetres (cm), metres (m), and kilometres (km) using place value and scaling factors of 10, 100, and 1000.

## Success Criteria
* **I can** select the most appropriate unit (mm, cm, m, km) for measuring real-world objects.
* **I can** state the scaling relationships between units of length ($10\text{ mm} = 1\text{ cm}$, $100\text{ cm} = 1\text{ m}$, $1000\text{ m} = 1\text{ km}$).
* **I can** convert from a larger unit to a smaller unit by multiplying ($\times 10$, $\times 100$, $\times 1000$).
* **I can** convert from a smaller unit to a larger unit by dividing ($\div 10$, $\div 100$, $\div 1000$).
* **I can** apply conversion strategies to solve multi-step word problems involving length.

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Warm-up: Naming & Visualising Units (10 mins)
* **Real-world Context**: Introduce the four main metric units of length:
  1. **Millimetre (mm)**: About the thickness of a plastic bank card or coin.
  2. **Centimetre (cm)**: About the width of a standard fingernail.
  3. **Metre (m)**: About one large stride of an adult.
  4. **Kilometre (km)**: About a 10-minute walk.
* **Australian Spelling Standard**: Emphasise the spelling ending in **-tre** (metres, centimetres, millimetres, kilometres).
* **Whiteboard Check (CFU)**: Call out items and have students write down the most appropriate unit to measure them:
  * A ladybug's length (mm)
  * The height of a classroom door (m)
  * The distance from Brisbane to Gold Coast (km)
  * The length of a school textbook (cm)

### 2. Explicit Instruction: The Conversion Chart (15 mins)
* **Model the Chart** (projecting Slide 3):
  * Show that converting from a **larger unit to a smaller unit** makes the number bigger $\rightarrow$ we **multiply**.
  * Show that converting from a **smaller unit to a larger unit** makes the number smaller $\rightarrow$ we **divide**.
  * Show the multipliers:
    * $\text{km} \xrightarrow{\times 1000} \text{m} \xrightarrow{\times 100} \text{cm} \xrightarrow{\times 10} \text{mm}$
    * $\text{mm} \xrightarrow{\div 10} \text{cm} \xrightarrow{\div 100} \text{m} \xrightarrow{\div 1000} \text{km}$
* **I Do (Multiplying - Larger to Smaller)**:
  * **Example 1**: Convert $5\text{ m}$ to $\text{cm}$. Since $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$, we multiply by 100. $5 \times 100 = 500\text{ cm}$.
  * **Example 2**: Convert $2.5\text{ km}$ to $\text{m}$. Since $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$, we multiply by 1000. $2.5 \times 1000 = 2500\text{ m}$.
* **I Do (Dividing - Smaller to Larger)**:
  * **Example 3**: Convert $60\text{ mm}$ to $\text{cm}$. Since $10\text{ mm} = 1\text{ cm}$, we divide by 10. $60 \div 10 = 6\text{ cm}$.
  * **Example 4**: Convert $450\text{ m}$ to $\text{km}$. Since $1000\text{ m} = 1\text{ km}$, we divide by 1000. Move the decimal point three places to the left: $450.0 \rightarrow 0.45\text{ km}$.

### 3. Guided Practice: Whiteboard CFU Check (15 mins)
* **Activity**: Project Slide 6 (We Do). Have students work individually or in pairs to write calculations on their mini-whiteboards.
* **Whiteboard Exercises**:
  * **Check 1**: Convert $8.2\text{ cm}$ to $\text{mm}$ ($8.2 \times 10 = 82\text{ mm}$).
  * **Check 2**: Convert $750\text{ cm}$ to $\text{m}$ ($750 \div 100 = 7.5\text{ m}$).
  * **Check 3**: Compare $1.5\text{ m}$ and $145\text{ cm}$. Convert $1.5\text{ m} \rightarrow 150\text{ cm}$. Since $150\text{ cm} > 145\text{ cm}$, $1.5\text{ m}$ is longer.
* **Whiteboard Protocol**: Direct students to hide their answers until the teacher calls "Show Boards". Review and address decimal placement errors before revealing the slide's digital solution.

### 4. Guided & Independent Practice: Word Problems (15 mins)
* **Real-world Challenges (Slide 7)**:
  * **Challenge A (Rope Lengths)**: Sarah has a rope that is $3.2\text{ m}$ long. She cuts off a piece that is $80\text{ cm}$ long. How many centimetres of rope are left?
    * *Working*: Convert $3.2\text{ m}$ to $\text{cm}: 3.2 \times 100 = 320\text{ cm}$. Subtraction: $320 - 80 = 240\text{ cm}$.
  * **Challenge B (Athlete Runs)**: An athlete runs on a track. They complete $3\text{ km}$, then run another $850\text{ m}$. What is the total distance they ran in metres?
    * *Working*: Convert $3\text{ km}$ to $\text{m}: 3 \times 1000 = 3000\text{ m}$. Addition: $3000 + 850 = 3850\text{ m}$.
  * **Challenge C (Soccer Field Perimeter)**: A small rectangular school playground has a length of $400\text{ cm}$ and a width of $3.5\text{ m}$. What is the perimeter of the playground in metres?
    * *Working*: Convert length to metres: $400\text{ cm} \div 100 = 4\text{ m}$. Perimeter: $2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width}) = 2 \times (4\text{ m} + 3.5\text{ m}) = 2 \times 7.5\text{ m} = 15\text{ m}$.

### 5. Plenary / Wrap-up & Forms Assessment (5 mins)
* **Exit Ticket**: Ask students to write down:
  1. One conversion where you multiply.
  2. One conversion where you divide.
* **Assessment**: Direct students to log into their devices and complete the 15-question **Assessment_Forms.docx** multiple-choice quiz to collect diagnostic data.

## Differentiated Support Strategies

### Standard Pathway
* Convert fluently between non-adjacent units (e.g. millimetres to metres).
* Solves multi-step word problems involving conversions and decimals.

### Support Pathway (Lucas ICP)
* Focus on single-step conversions between adjacent units (e.g., cm to mm, m to cm) using whole numbers (e.g., $4\text{ cm} = 40\text{ mm}$, $6\text{ m} = 600\text{ cm}$).
* Scaffold multi-step problems by breaking them into explicit questions (e.g. Step 1: convert, Step 2: subtract).
