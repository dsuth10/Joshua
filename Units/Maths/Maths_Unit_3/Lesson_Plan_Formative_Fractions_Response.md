## 🎯 Target Misconceptions & Diagnostic Breakdown

```
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                       CLASS MISCONCEPTION HEATMAP                       │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 1. Simplifying Mixed Sums (1 7/10 + 3/10 = 2)      [27.8% PASS] 🔴 CRITICAL│
 │ 2. Hundredths Decimal Place Value (6 8/100 = 6.08)  [33.3% PASS] 🔴 CRITICAL│
 │ 3. Adding Related Denominators (1/4 + 3/8 = 5/8)   [44.4% PASS] 🟠 HIGH    │
 │ 4. Golden Rule Application                         [44.4% PASS] 🟠 HIGH    │
 │ 5. Full Simplification to Lowest Terms (8/12=2/3)   [50.0% PASS] 🟡 MODERATE│
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Misconception 1: Stopping at Unsimplified Whole Fraction Parts ($27.8\%$ Accuracy)**
   - *Question:* What is $1\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10}$?
   - *Data:* Only $5$ students answered $2$. $5$ students selected "Both $1\frac{10}{10}$ and $2$ are correct", and $3$ selected $1\frac{10}{10}$.
   - *Root Cause:* Students treat the whole number and fractional part in isolation. They calculate $1 + \frac{10}{10} = 1\frac{10}{10}$ but fail to recognise that $\frac{10}{10} = 1$ whole, requiring renaming to $1 + 1 = 2$.

2. **Misconception 2: Decimal Place Value Confusion in Hundredths ($33.3\%$ Accuracy)**
   - *Question:* Which of the following represents $6\frac{8}{100}$ as a decimal?
   - *Data:* $5$ students chose $6.8$ (confusing hundredths $\frac{8}{100}$ with tenths $\frac{8}{10}$), $4$ chose $0.68$, and $2$ chose $68.0$.
   - *Root Cause:* Lack of place value zero-placeholder understanding. Students link the digit $8$ directly to the first decimal position regardless of whether the denominator is $10$ or $100$.

3. **Misconception 3: Adding Across Numerators & Denominators in Related Fractions ($44.4\%$ Accuracy)**
   - *Question:* Calculate $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8}$ by first converting $\frac{1}{4}$ into eighths.
   - *Data:* $5$ students chose $\frac{4}{8}$ (added numerators $1+3=4$ without converting $\frac{1}{4}$ to $\frac{2}{8}$ first), $3$ students chose $\frac{4}{12}$ (added across top $1+3=4$ AND bottom $4+8=12$).
   - *Root Cause:* Over-reliance on basic addition patterns without establishing equal slice sizes (common denominators).

4. **Misconception 4: Incomplete Simplification ($50.0\%$ Accuracy)**
   - *Question:* Which fraction is equivalent to $\frac{8}{12}$ when simplified by dividing numerator and denominator by $4$?
   - *Data:* $6$ students selected $\frac{4}{6}$ (divided by $2$ instead of $4$, or stopped at an intermediate equivalent form).
   - *Root Cause:* Rushing or failing to identify the Highest Common Factor (HCF).

## 🎯 Learning Intentions & Success Criteria

### WALT (We Are Learning To)
- Identify and repair key fraction misconceptions involving place value decimal conversions, renaming unsimplified mixed numbers, applying the Golden Rule for related denominators, and simplifying fractions to lowest terms.

### WILF (What I'm Looking For)
1. **Place Value Precision:** Correctly write hundredths fractions as decimals using zero placeholders (e.g. $6\frac{8}{100} = 6.08$, NOT $6.8$).
2. **Renaming Whole Fractions:** Rename fractions where numerator equals denominator as $1$ whole and add to existing whole numbers (e.g. $1\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = 1\frac{10}{10} = 2$).
3. **Golden Rule Conversion:** Double or multiply the smaller denominator to create matching denominators BEFORE adding or subtracting (e.g. $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{8} \rightarrow \frac{2}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$).
4. **Simplification Mastery:** Divide both numerator and denominator by their Highest Common Factor to reach simplest form (e.g. $\frac{8 \div 4}{12 \div 4} = \frac{2}{3}$).

### Step 2: Clinic 1 — Decimal Place Value: Tenths vs Hundredths (10 Mins)

- **Slide:** Slide 2 (Place Value Grid: $6\frac{8}{100}$ vs $6\frac{8}{10}$)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"Look at Question 2 from yesterday. Only 33% of us got this right! The question asked: What is $6\frac{8}{100}$ as a decimal?*
  > *Many people chose 6.8. But let's look at our Place Value Chart!*
  > *The first column after the decimal point is TENTHS ($\frac{1}{10}$). The second column is HUNDREDTHS ($\frac{1}{100}$).*
  > *If you write 6.8, that means 6 whole units and 8 TENTHS ($6\frac{8}{10}$ or $6\frac{80}{100}$)!*
  > *To show 8 HUNDREDTHS, we MUST put a 0 in the tenths column as a placeholder: 6.08!*
  > *Let's test this: How do we write $1.05$ as a fraction over 100? $1\frac{5}{100}$! If it were $1.5$, it would be $1\frac{50}{100}$."*

#### Logistics & Teacher Notes (Notes Drawer)
- **DO:** Project interactive place value chart on Slide 2. Show 100-grid visualizer showing 8 shaded squares ($0.08$) vs 80 shaded squares ($0.80$).
- **WORK:** Students sketch a 3-column place value table (Ones | Tenths | Hundredths) in their books.
- **RECORD:** Write down contrast pairs:
  - $6\frac{8}{100} = 6.08$ (8 hundredths)
  - $6\frac{8}{10} = 6.80 = 6.8$ (8 tenths)
- **CHECK:** Quick poll: Write $4\frac{3}{100}$ on board. Have students chant the decimal response ("4 point 0 3!").

### Step 3: Clinic 2 — Renaming $\frac{10}{10}$ & Simplifying Mixed Sums (10 Mins)

- **Slide:** Slide 3 (Visual Bar Model: $1\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10}$)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"This was our biggest class trap—only 27% correct! When asked: What is $1\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10}$?, many wrote $1\frac{10}{10}$.*
  > *Is $1\frac{10}{10}$ wrong? It's an incomplete answer! Imagine you have 1 whole pizza and 7 slices out of 10. Someone hands you 3 more tenths. You now have 10 slices, which fills a SECOND complete pizza!*
  > *In mathematics, whenever your numerator equals your denominator ($\frac{10}{10}$), it equals 1 WHOLE.*
  > *So $1\frac{10}{10} = 1 + 1 = 2$! Never leave $\frac{n}{n}$ hanging inside a mixed number!"*

#### Logistics & Teacher Notes (Notes Drawer)
- **DO:** Demonstrate bar model joining on Slide 3. Show 1 full bar + 7 tenths bar, then add 3 tenths bar to complete the 2nd full bar.
- **WORK:** Students draw two 10-frame rectangles. Shade 1 full bar + 7 tenths. Draw + 3 tenths arrow completing the 2nd bar.
- **RECORD:** Write step-by-step rule:
  1. Add numerators: $1\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = 1\frac{10}{10}$
  2. Rename whole fraction: $\frac{10}{10} = 1$
  3. Combine wholes: $1 + 1 = 2$
- **CHECK:** Mini-whiteboard quick check: Evaluate $2\frac{4}{5} + \frac{1}{5}$? (Students must write $= 2\frac{5}{5} = 3$).

---

### Step 4: Clinic 3 — The Golden Rule & Related Denominators (10 Mins)

- **Slide:** Slide 4 (Equivalent Fraction Multiplier & Stacked Number Lines)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"Look at this sum: $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8}$. Can we just add $1 + 3 = 4$ and $4 + 8 = 12$ to get $\frac{4}{12}$? ABSOLUTELY NOT! That breaks the Golden Rule of Fractions!*
  > *You cannot add quarters and eighths directly because the slice sizes are different.*
  > *The Golden Rule says: Convert to matching denominators FIRST!*
  > *Look at $\frac{1}{4}$. How do we convert quarters into eighths? Multiply top and bottom by 2!*
  > *$\frac{1 \times 2}{4 \times 2} = \frac{2}{8}$.*
  > *NOW we can add: $\frac{2}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$! Notice how 5 eighths is completely different from $\frac{4}{8}$ or $\frac{4}{12}$!"*

#### Logistics & Teacher Notes (Notes Drawer)
- **DO:** Project aligned fraction bars showing $\frac{1}{4}$ matching exactly with $\frac{2}{8}$. Show multiplier arrows ($\times 2$).
- **WORK:** Students write out the Golden Rule conversion steps for $\frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{10}$.
- **RECORD:** Write conversion steps:
  1. Convert $\frac{3}{5} \rightarrow \frac{3 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = \frac{6}{10}$
  2. Add converted fractions: $\frac{6}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{9}{10}$
- **CHECK:** Ask Tier 2 student: *"Why did we multiply by 2?"* (Because $5 \times 2 = 10$, giving matching denominators!).
