Equivalent Fractions

Exploring equal fractional values through Area Models, Aligned Number Lines, and Multiplication Rules

🎯 WALT (We Are Learning To)

Represent and create equivalent fractions using subdivided blocks, aligned equal-length number lines, and multiplying top and bottom numbers.

⭐ WILF (What I'm Looking For)

  • Explain why two fractions with different numbers can have the same value.
  • Draw a horizontal cut through an area block to convert thirds into sixths or ninths.
  • Find matching points on aligned number lines (13 = 26 = 39).
  • Multiply numerator and denominator by the same number to generate equivalent fractions.

Model 1: Subdivided Block (Area Model)

When we slice a block into equal parts, we can cut across it horizontally to create smaller equal parts without changing the total shaded area!

Base Columns:
Shaded Columns: 2
Horizontal Cuts (Rows):
Original Fraction (Columns):
23
Subdivided Equivalent Fraction:
46

🟩 Subdivided Block Support:

• Start with 2 thirds (23) shaded blue.
• Draw 1 line straight across the middle.
• Now you have 6 equal pieces in total, and 4 of them are shaded!
• So 23 is exactly equal to 46.

Model 3: The Multiplication Rule

The Golden Multiplication Rule:
"Multiply the numerator AND denominator by the SAME number!"
× Top & Bottom ✖️
Interactive Rule Stepper:
13 → 1 × 23 × 2 = 26
Multiplying by 22 is the exact same as multiplying by 1 whole!

Equivalent Fraction Chains:

12 = 24 = 36 = 48 = 510
13 = 26 = 39 = 412 = 515

Teacher Notes — Slide 4

DO: Click the multiplier buttons (×2, ×3, ×4, ×5) to show how top and bottom scale together.

WORK: Explain Signpost Math 1:19 concept: "Why does multiplying numerator and denominator by 5 give an equivalent fraction? Because 5/5 equals 1! Any number multiplied by 1 stays equal in value!"

RECORD: Have students copy: 1/3 × 2/2 = 2/6 and 1/3 × 5/5 = 5/15 into their books.

FINISH: 4 minutes.

CHECK: Ask: "If I multiply the numerator by 3, what MUST I do to the denominator? Multiply by 3!"

Class Practice: Reasoning & Discussion Check 💬 (Set 2)

Discuss these 4 reasoning questions with a partner. Write your answer on your mini-whiteboard, then tap to check!

Q1. Area Model Reasoning Tap to Reveal
If you have 12 of a block and cut it horizontally into 3 rows, what fraction is shaded? Did the amount change?
12 = 36! The shaded amount stays the same because we only sliced existing pieces into smaller parts! 🍫
Q2. Spot the Mistake! Tap to Reveal
Sam says 13 = 24 because he added 1 to top and bottom. Is Sam correct?
INCORRECT! ❌ Adding 1 changes the value (13 is 33%, 24 is 50%). You must MULTIPLY top and bottom!
Q3. Number Line Equivalence Tap to Reveal
On an aligned number line, which fraction in twelfths lines up at the exact same point as 34?
34 = 912! 🎯 (Top & Bottom × 3)
Q4. Real-World Pizza Sharing Tap to Reveal
Maya ate 25 of a pizza. Ben ate 410 of an identical pizza. Who ate more?
They ate the EXACT SAME AMOUNT! 🍕 25 = 410. Ben just had smaller slices!

Teacher Notes — Slide 6

DO: Give students 60 seconds to discuss each question with a shoulder partner before recording their whiteboards.

WORK: Tap cards to reveal answers. Ask 1-2 pairs to explain why adding to top and bottom (Q2 addition fallacy) breaks equivalence.

RECORD: Have students record key takeaway: "Multiply top and bottom, NEVER add!" in exercise books.

FINISH: 6 minutes class check.

CHECK: Thumbs up if students agree Maya and Ben ate the same amount of pizza in Q4.
