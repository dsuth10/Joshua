## 🎯 Learning Intentions & Success Criteria

### WALT (We Are Learning To)
- Identify and repair key fraction misconceptions involving place value decimal conversions, renaming unsimplified mixed numbers, applying the Golden Rule for related denominators, and simplifying fractions to lowest terms.

### WILF (What I'm Looking For)
1. **Place Value Precision:** Write hundredths fractions as decimals using zero placeholders (e.g. 6 8/100 = 6.08, NOT 6.8).
2. **Renaming Whole Fractions:** Rename fractions where numerator equals denominator as 1 whole and combine with existing whole numbers (e.g. 1 7/10 + 3/10 = 1 10/10 = 2).
3. **Golden Rule Conversion:** Multiply the smaller denominator to create matching denominators BEFORE adding or subtracting (e.g. 1/4 = 2/8 ➔ 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8).
4. **Simplification Mastery:** Divide both numerator and denominator by their Highest Common Factor to reach simplest form (e.g. 8/12 = 2/3).

### Step 2: Clinic 1 — Decimal Place Value: Tenths vs Hundredths (10 Mins)
- **Slide:** Slide 2 (Place Value Grid: 6 8/100 vs 6 8/10)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"Look at Question 2 from yesterday. Only 33% of us got this right! The question asked: What is 6 8/100 as a decimal?*
  > *Many people chose 6.8. But let me show you on our Place Value Chart! The first column after the decimal point is TENTHS (1/10). The second column is HUNDREDTHS (1/100).*
  > *Writing 6.8 means 6 wholes and 8 TENTHS (6 80/100)! To show 8 HUNDREDTHS, we MUST put a 0 in the tenths column as a placeholder: 6.08!"*
- **Logistics (Notes Drawer):**
  - **DO:** Project interactive place value chart on Slide 2. Contrast 8 shaded hundredths (0.08) vs 80 shaded hundredths (0.80).
  - **WORK:** Students sketch a 3-column place value table (Ones | Tenths | Hundredths) in their books.
  - **RECORD:** Copy contrast pairs: 6 8/100 = 6.08 vs 6 8/10 = 6.80.
  - **CHECK:** Write 4 3/100 on board ➔ class chants "4 point 0 3!".

---

### Step 3: Clinic 2 — Renaming n/n & Simplifying Mixed Sums (10 Mins)
- **Slide:** Slide 3 (Visual Bar Model: 1 7/10 + 3/10)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"This was our biggest class trap—only 27% correct! When asked: What is 1 7/10 + 3/10?, many wrote 1 10/10.*
  > *Imagine you have 1 whole pizza and 7 slices out of 10. Someone hands you 3 more tenths. You now have 10 slices, which fills a SECOND complete pizza!*
  > *In mathematics, whenever your numerator equals your denominator (10/10), it equals 1 WHOLE. So 1 10/10 = 1 + 1 = 2!"*
- **Logistics (Notes Drawer):**
  - **DO:** Demonstrate bar model joining on Slide 3. Show 1 full bar + 7 tenths bar, then add 3 tenths bar to complete 2nd full bar.
  - **WORK:** Students draw two 10-frame rectangles. Shade 1 full bar + 7 tenths. Draw + 3 tenths arrow completing 2nd bar.
  - **RECORD:** Write steps: 1. Add numerators (1 10/10). 2. Rename 10/10 = 1. 3. Combine wholes (1 + 1 = 2).
  - **CHECK:** Mini-whiteboard quick check: 2 4/5 + 1/5 = ? (= 3).

---

### Step 4: Clinic 3 — The Golden Rule & Related Denominators (10 Mins)
- **Slide:** Slide 4 (Equivalent Fraction Multiplier & Stacked Number Lines)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"Look at this sum: 1/4 + 3/8. Can we just add 1 + 3 = 4 and 4 + 8 = 12 to get 4/12? ABSOLUTELY NOT! That breaks the Golden Rule of Fractions!*
  > *You cannot add quarters and eighths directly because the slice sizes are different. The Golden Rule says: Convert to matching denominators FIRST!*
  > *Look at 1/4. Multiply top and bottom by 2: (1 x 2) / (4 x 2) = 2/8. NOW add: 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8!"*
- **Logistics (Notes Drawer):**
  - **DO:** Project aligned fraction bars showing 1/4 matching exactly with 2/8. Show multiplier arrows (x 2).
  - **WORK:** Students write out conversion steps for 3/5 + 3/10.
  - **RECORD:** Solution: 3/5 = (3 x 2)/(5 x 2) = 6/10 ➔ 6/10 + 3/10 = 9/10.
  - **CHECK:** Cold-call student: *"Why did we multiply by 2?"* (Because 5 x 2 = 10).

### Step 5: Guided CFU & Mini-Whiteboard Challenge (8 Mins)
- **Slide:** Slide 5 (Rapid-Fire Misconception CFU)
- **Teacher Script:**
  > *"Mini-whiteboards ready! I will display 4 problems on screen. Each tests one of our mystery mistakes. Write your final answer in big bold text and hold it up when I say SHOW!"*
- **Prompts:**
  1. Express 3 4/100 as a decimal. (3.04)
  2. Calculate 4 5/8 + 3/8. (5)
  3. Calculate 5/8 - 1/4 by converting 1/4 to eighths. (3/8)
  4. Simplify 8/12 completely by dividing top and bottom by 4. (2/3)
- **Logistics (Notes Drawer):**
  - **DO:** Reveal prompts one by one. Scannable check of class readiness.
  - **WORK:** Students solve on mini-whiteboards and hold up.
  - **RECORD:** Tally accuracy on teacher sheet (Target >= 85%).
  - **CHECK:** Direct struggling students to Tier 3 group.
