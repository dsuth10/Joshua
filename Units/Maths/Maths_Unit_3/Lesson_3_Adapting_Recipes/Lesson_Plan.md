# Lesson Plan — Lesson 3: Adapting a Recipe for the Class

**Unit:** Maths Unit 3: Number, Algebra & Measurement  
**Year Level:** Year 5 / Year 6  
**Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Focus:** Recipe Multipliers, Scaling Fractions & Whole Numbers, Metric Unit Conversions ($\text{g} \leftrightarrow \text{kg}$, $\text{mL} \leftrightarrow \text{L}$)  
**Curriculum Mapping (Australian Curriculum v9):**
* **AC9M5N04**: Solve problems involving multiplication using efficient mental and written strategies.
* **AC9M5N06**: Find simple fractions of a quantity where the result is a whole number.
* **AC9M6N05**: Multiply fractions by whole numbers using simple, visual strategies.
* **AC9M5M01**: Convert between smaller and larger metric units ($\text{g} \rightarrow \text{kg}$, $\text{mL} \rightarrow \text{L}$).

---

## Learning Intentions
* **We are learning to** find a recipe multiplier ($\text{Multiplier} = \text{People Needed} \div \text{Recipe Makes}$).
* **We are learning to** multiply ingredient quantities (whole numbers and simple fractions) by a multiplier.
* **We are learning to** convert large metric measurements into kilograms ($\text{kg}$) and litres ($\text{L}$) for shopping.
* **We are learning to** explain our working clearly using the 4-Phase Problem Solving Framework.

## Success Criteria
* **I can** find the multiplier by dividing the number of students needed by the recipe's original serves.
* **I can** write a clear number sentence for each ingredient ($\text{Original Amount} \times \text{Multiplier} = \text{New Amount}$).
* **I can** change large measurements to kilograms or litres ($1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$; $1000\text{mL} = 1\text{L}$).
* **I can** explain why my strategy was easy to use and show how to scale a recipe for a whole school assembly (120 people).

## Lesson Sequence (60 Minutes)

### Phase 1: UNDERSTAND the Problem (10 mins)

#### 1. Hook & Problem Introduction
* **Teacher Prompt**: *"Your teacher has asked you to choose a recipe to share with the class. How can you adapt a recipe to make enough for all 24 students in our class?"*
* **Notice & Wonder Routine**:
  * Project Slide 2 ("UNDERSTAND the Problem").
  * Show a sample Anzac Biscuits recipe (makes 12 biscuits).
  * **Ask Students**: *"What do you notice about this recipe? What do you wonder about making enough for our class?"*
  * **Key Discussion Points**:
    * *Notice*: The recipe makes 12 biscuits. We have 24 students in our class.
    * *Wonder*: 12 is less than 24. We need to double the recipe ($12 \times 2 = 24$)!
    * *Maths Connection*: Maths helps us find the exact multiplier so everyone gets a fair share.

#### 2. Mini-Whiteboard Check (CFU)
* **Prompt**: *"If a recipe makes 6 serves, how many times bigger does it need to be for 24 students? Write the number sentence on your whiteboard."*
* **Expected Student Response**: $24 \div 6 = 4$ times bigger (Multiplier = 4).

---

### Phase 2: PLAN How to Solve the Problem (15 mins)

#### 1. Finding the Recipe Multiplier
* **Step 1: Choose a Recipe**: Select a recipe (Anzac Biscuits, Guacamole, Berry Smoothie, or Pizza Bites).
* **Step 2: Note the Recipe Makes**: Identify original serving size (e.g. 12 biscuits).
* **Step 3: Set Your Class Target**: Identify people needed (e.g. 24 students).
* **Step 4: Calculate the Multiplier**:
  $$\text{Recipe Multiplier} = \frac{\text{People Needed}}{\text{Recipe Makes}} = \text{People Needed} \div \text{Recipe Makes}$$
  * *Example 1 (Friendly Doubling)*: Recipe makes 12, Class needs 24 $\rightarrow \text{Multiplier} = 24 \div 12 = 2$.
  * *Example 2 (Friendly Quadrupling)*: Recipe makes 6, Class needs 24 $\rightarrow \text{Multiplier} = 24 \div 6 = 4$.
  * *Example 3 (2.5× Scale)*: Recipe makes 12, Target is 30 $\rightarrow \text{Multiplier} = 30 \div 12 = 2.5$.

#### 2. Choosing a Strategy
* Discuss two student-friendly strategies:
  1. **Exact Multiplier**: Multiply every ingredient by the exact multiplier (e.g. $\times 2$).
  2. **Full Batches**: Make full extra batches (e.g. 3 batches of 12 = 36 biscuits) so you don't have to split eggs or half-packets.

---

### Phase 3: DO (Solve) the Problem (20 mins)

#### 1. Explicit Modeling (I Do / We Do)
* **Recipe Example: Party Guacamole (Makes 6 Serves — Target: 24 Students)**
  * Multiplier = $24 \div 6 = 4$.
* **Simple Number Sentences**:
  * Avocados: $3 \text{ avocados} \times 4 = 12 \text{ avocados}$.
  * Red Onion: $\frac{1}{2} \text{ onion} \times 4 = 2 \text{ onions}$.
  * Lime Juice: $30\text{mL} \times 4 = 120\text{mL}$.
  * Cherry Tomatoes: $150\text{g} \times 4 = 600\text{g}$.

#### 2. Changing to Supermarket Shopping Units ($\text{g} \rightarrow \text{kg}$, $\text{mL} \rightarrow \text{L}$)
* When ingredient totals reach $1000\text{g}$ or $1000\text{mL}$, change them to kilograms ($\text{kg}$) or litres ($\text{L}$):
  * $1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg} \rightarrow 1500\text{g} = 1.5\text{kg}$
  * $1000\text{mL} = 1\text{L} \rightarrow 2000\text{mL} = 2\text{L}$

#### 3. Student Investigation (Interactive Web App)
* Students open `interactive_app/index.html` on their laptops/tablets:
  1. Select a recipe.
  2. Set class size (24 students).
  3. Fill out the scaling table and click "Check My Answers".

---

### Phase 4: CONSIDER Findings & SHARE Solution (15 mins)

#### 1. Student Reflection
* **Questions to Discuss**:
  * *What is your solution?* Share new ingredient totals for your class.
  * *Was your strategy easy and efficient? Why?* (e.g. "Doubling was easy because $12 \times 2 = 24$. Changing 1200g to 1.2kg makes it easier to buy at the shop.")

#### 2. Challenge: School Assembly Scale-Up (120 People)
* **Problem**: *"How would you make enough for a whole school assembly of 120 people?"*
* **Working**:
  * Original recipe = 12 biscuits. Assembly = 120 people.
  * Multiplier = $120 \div 12 = 10$ times bigger!
  * Butter: $100\text{g} \times 10 = 1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$ butter!

## Differentiated Support Strategies

### Standard Pathway
* Calculates whole number and clean decimal multipliers fluently ($2\times, 2.5\times, 4\times$).
* Converts metric units ($\text{g} \rightarrow \text{kg}$, $\text{mL} \rightarrow \text{L}$) using division by 1000.
* Explains why their strategy was easy and efficient.

### Support Pathway (Lucas ICP)
* Focuses on friendly whole-number multipliers ($2\times, 3\times, 4\times$).
* Uses visual fraction addition for half cups ($\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$).
* Uses visual unit conversion prompts ($1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$).
