1. UNDERSTAND the Problem

The Classroom Challenge

Your teacher has asked you to choose a recipe to share with the class.

How can you change the recipe to make enough for all 24 students in our class?

Classic Anzac Biscuits

Original Recipe Makes: 12 biscuits

  • 1 cup rolled oats
  • 100g butter
  • 2 tbsp (30mL) golden syrup
  • 12 cup sugar

The Challenge (Support Scaffold)

We want to share biscuits with our class!

The recipe makes 12 biscuits. We have 24 students.

Recipe Facts

• 1 recipe = 12 biscuits (not enough for 24!)

• 2 recipes = 24 biscuits (exact match!)

• We need to double the recipe!

Notice & Wonder

What do you NOTICE? (Recipe makes 12, class needs 24)

What do you WONDER? (How many times bigger must the recipe be?)

Teacher Notes — Slide 2 (UNDERSTAND)

DO: Lead Notice & Wonder discussion. Ask "How many biscuits does 1 batch make?" (12).

WORK: 5 minutes discussion.

RECORD: Note student answers on whiteboard.

FINISH: Emphasise that 24 is double 12.

CHECK: Ask "How many batches do we need for 24 students?" (2 batches).

2. PLAN: Finding the Multiplier

Recipe Multiplier = People Needed ÷ Recipe Makes

Option A: Exact Multiplier

People Needed = 24, Recipe Makes = 12

24 ÷ 12 = 2  (Multiplier = 2)

Multiply every ingredient amount by 2!

Option B: Full Extra Batches

• Batch 1: 12 biscuits

• Batch 2: 24 biscuits (exact match!)

• Batch 3: 36 biscuits (if you want leftovers!)

Easy to bake in full baking trays.

Multiplier = People Needed ÷ Recipe Makes

Step-by-Step Plan

1. People needed: 24 students

2. Recipe makes: 12 biscuits

3. 24 ÷ 12 = 2. Multiply every ingredient by 2!

Teacher Notes — Slide 3 (PLAN)

DO: Explicitly show how to calculate the multiplier: People Needed ÷ Recipe Makes.

WORK: 7 minutes instruction.

RECORD: Have students write "Multiplier = People Needed ÷ Recipe Makes" on their boards.

FINISH: Confirm students understand multiplying by 2 means doubling.

CHECK: CFU: "If recipe makes 6 and we need 24, what is the multiplier?" (24 ÷ 6 = 4).

3. DO: Changing to Shopping Units (kg and L)

Supermarket Units

When ingredient amounts reach 1000g or 1000mL, change them to kilograms (kg) or litres (L)!

Grams to Kilograms

1000 g = 1 kg

• 1500 g = 1.5 kg

• 2000 g = 2 kg

Millilitres to Litres

1000 mL = 1 L

• 1800 mL = 1.8 L

• 2000 mL = 2 L

Shopping Rule

1000 g = 1 kg   |   1000 mL = 1 L

• 1000 g → 1 kg

• 2000 g → 2 kg

Teacher Notes — Slide 5 (Metric Units)

DO: Explain that supermarkets label flour and butter in kg, and milk in L.

WORK: 5 minutes whiteboard CFU.

RECORD: Whiteboard check: "Convert 2000mL to Litres" (2L).

FINISH: Move to student investigation.

CHECK: CFU: "How many grams are in 1kg?" (1000g).

Challenge: School Assembly

How would you scale your recipe for a school assembly of 120 people?

120 people ÷ 12 biscuits = 10 times bigger!

Multiply all original ingredients by 10:

  • 100g butter × 10 = 1000g = 1kg butter
  • 1 cup oats × 10 = 10 cups oats
  • 30mL syrup × 10 = 300mL = 0.3L syrup
