# PowerPoint Lesson Manual Content: Lesson 10 Examples

*The following examples are designed to be copied directly into your PowerPoint lesson manual. They mirror the structure and complexity of the student worksheet but use completely different scenarios for explicit teaching.*

---

## Slide 1: Example 1 (I Do)
**Topic:** Finding the total, then finding the difference.
**Focus:** Highlighting keywords and identifying a multi-step problem.

**The Problem:**
A local primary school is building a new community garden and needs $1,500 for the project. The P&C raised $425 from a bake sale, $615 from a school disco, and $230 from a free dress day. 
How much more money do they need to raise to reach their goal?

**Teacher Notes / Walkthrough:**
1. **CUBES - Box the keywords:** "raised" (addition), "how much more" (subtraction), "goal" (target).
2. **Step 1 (Addition):** We need to find the *total* amount raised so far.
   * $425 + $615 + $230 = $1,270
3. **Step 2 (Subtraction):** We need to find the *difference* between the goal and what they have.
   * $1,500 - $1,270 = $230
4. **Answer:** They need to raise **$230 dollars** more.

## Slide 2: Example 2 (I Do)
**Topic:** Multi-step with extraneous information.
**Focus:** Filtering out numbers we don't need to use.

**The Problem:**
A fruit shop owner buys 15 boxes of apples for the week. Each box contains 24 apples. While unpacking the boxes on Tuesday morning, the owner notices that 18 apples are badly bruised and have to be thrown away. 
How many good apples does the owner have left to sell?

**Teacher Notes / Walkthrough:**
1. **CUBES - Circle the numbers:** 15, 24, "Tuesday" (not a math number!), 18.
2. **Step 1 (Multiplication):** Find the total number of apples bought. 
   * 15 boxes × 24 apples per box = 360 apples total.
3. **Step 2 (Subtraction):** Take away the spoiled apples ("thrown away" means subtract).
   * 360 total apples - 18 bruised apples = 342
4. **Answer:** The owner has **342 apples** left to sell.

## Slide 3: Example 3 (We Do)
**Topic:** Division followed by Subtraction.
**Focus:** Breaking down logical steps together as a class.

**The Problem:**
A baker makes a massive batch of 450 cookies. He wants to pack them evenly into large boxes that hold 12 cookies each. 
How many full boxes can he make, and how many cookies will be left over?

**Teacher Notes / Walkthrough:**
1. **CUBES - Box the keywords:** "pack them evenly" (division), "each" (per group), "left over" (remainder/subtraction).
2. **Step 1 (Division):** Divide the total cookies by the box capacity.
   * 450 ÷ 12 
   * *Mental strategy: 12 x 10 = 120. 12 x 30 = 360. We need 90 more. 12 x 7 = 84. Total: 37 boxes.*
   * 450 ÷ 12 = 37 with a remainder.
3. **Step 2 (Multiplication & Subtraction for the remainder):** How many cookies are *in* those 37 boxes?
   * 37 boxes × 12 cookies = 444 cookies packed.
   * 450 total - 444 packed = 6 cookies left over.
4. **Answer:** He can make **37 boxes** and there will be **6 cookies** left over.

## Slide 4: Example 4 (We Do / You Do)
**Topic:** Complex Multi-step (Division then Multiplication).
**Focus:** Mirrors Question 8 on the worksheet (Distance/Fuel/Cost).

**The Problem:**
My family car can travel 8 km on a single litre of fuel. 
How much fuel would I use to travel to a holiday park that is 104 km away?
If the fuel costs 150 cents per litre, what would be the total cost of the fuel used for the trip?

**Teacher Notes / Walkthrough:**
1. **CUBES - Underline the questions:** There are *two* distinct questions here. We must answer them in order.
2. **Step 1 (Division):** Find out how many litres we need for the trip.
   * 104 km total ÷ 8 km per litre = 13 litres.
   * *Answer 1: 13 litres.*
3. **Step 2 (Multiplication):** Calculate the total cost of those litres.
   * 13 litres × 150 cents = 1,950 cents.
4. **Step 3 (Conversion):** The answer is in cents, but we usually talk about dollars.
   * 1,950 cents ÷ 100 = $19.50
5. **Answer:** I would use **13 litres**, and it would cost **$19.50 dollars**.
