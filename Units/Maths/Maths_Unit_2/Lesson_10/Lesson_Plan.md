# Lesson 10: Using Operations to Solve Problems

## Overview
**Unit:** Maths Unit 2
**Lesson:** 10
**Topic:** Using Operations to Solve Problems
**Duration:** 60 minutes

## Learning Intentions
- We are learning to decode word problems to identify the correct mathematical operation(s).
- We are learning to solve multi-step mathematical problems logically.

## Success Criteria
- I can identify keyword clues that indicate addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division.
- I can use the **CUBES** strategy to break down a complex word problem.
- I can solve multi-step problems and include the correct units in my final answer.
- I can check my final answer to ensure it is reasonable.

## Materials Required
- PowerPoint Lesson Manual (Lesson 10)
- Student Workbooks
- Student Worksheet ("2:53 Using operations to solve problems" - Page 79)
- Whiteboards and markers (for guided practice)

---

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Warm-up: Keyword Match (10 mins)
* **Activity:** On the board, write the four mathematical symbols (+, -, x, ÷) in four quadrants.
* **Execution:** Read out common math vocabulary words (e.g., *total, difference, share equally, product, combined, less than, per, times, altogether, half*). Have students write the matching symbol on their mini-whiteboards and hold them up.
* **Discussion:** Emphasise that in Year 5, many problems won't just say "add" or "subtract"; they will use these hidden keywords, and some problems will have *more than one* step.

### 2. Explicit Instruction (I Do) (15 mins)
* **Concept Introduction:** Introduce the focus of today's lesson: not just doing the math, but figuring out *what* math to do.
* **The CUBES Strategy:** Quickly review the problem-solving framework.
    * **C** - Circle the numbers
    * **U** - Underline the question
    * **B** - Box the math action words (keywords)
    * **E** - Evaluate and draw (What steps do I take?)
    * **S** - Solve and Check
* **Modeled Example 1 (Addition & Subtraction):** Use the PowerPoint to display Example 1. Model the CUBES strategy out loud. Show how you find the total first, then subtract to find the difference.
* **Modeled Example 2 (Division & Multiplication):** Use the PowerPoint to display Example 2. Model how to draw a simple picture or bar model to visualise the problem. Emphasise writing the **unit** at the end (e.g., "$", "km", "apples").

### 3. Guided Practice (We Do) (15 mins)
* **Interactive Problem Solving:** Present Example 3 from the PowerPoint.
* **Execution:** Ask the students to guide *you* through the CUBES method. 
    * "What numbers should I circle?"
    * "What is the actual question asking us?"
    * "What is the hidden first step?"
* Have students solve the calculation on their whiteboards.
* **Progress to Example 4:** Have students attempt Example 4 entirely on their whiteboards in pairs. Walk the room to check for understanding and correct operation selection. Have a pair explain their logic to the class.

### 4. Independent Practice (You Do) (15 mins)
* **Activity:** Distribute the worksheet (Page 79: "Using operations to solve problems").
* **Instructions:** 
    * Students are to complete the 10 word problems independently.
    * Remind them that answers **must include units** (as stated at the top of the worksheet).
    * Encourage students to use highlighters to box keywords if they get stuck.
* **Differentiation:** 
    * *Support:* Have students struggling with multi-step problems focus on identifying the operations for questions 1-5 before doing any calculating.
    * *Extension:* For early finishers, direct them to the unit digit pattern activity (Question 11) at the bottom of the page, or have them write their own multi-step word problem for a peer to solve.

### 5. Plenary / Wrap-up (5 mins)
* **Review:** Bring the class back together. Select one of the more challenging multi-step problems from the worksheet (e.g., Question 4 with the soil truck, or Question 8 with the fuel).
* **Discussion:** Ask a student to explain their reasoning step-by-step. 
* **Reflection:** Ask the class: "Why is it important to check if our answer makes sense?" (e.g., If we added instead of subtracted in Question 10, the farmer would end up with more money than he started with after paying fees, which is impossible).
