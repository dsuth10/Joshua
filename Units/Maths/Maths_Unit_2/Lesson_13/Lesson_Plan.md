# Lesson 13: Time Conversions and Word Problems

## Overview
**Unit:** Maths Unit 2
**Lesson:** 13
**Topic:** Converting 12-Hour and 24-Hour Time & Elapsed Time Word Problems
**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Curriculum Alignment:** **AC9M5T03** — Recognise and use 12-hour and 24-hour time systems and solve practical problems involving elapsed time.

## Learning Intentions
* We are learning to convert between 12-hour (a.m./p.m.) time and 24-hour time notation.
* We are learning to calculate elapsed time and duration using the "timeline jump" method.

## Success Criteria
* I can write times using both 12-hour notation (with a.m./p.m.) and 24-hour notation (four digits, no a.m./p.m.).
* I can convert time notation back and forth accurately, including noon and midnight edge cases.
* I can use the 3-step timeline jump method to calculate elapsed time:
  1. Jump the **minutes to the next hour**.
  2. Jump the **hours to the destination hour**.
  3. Jump the **minutes to the final destination time**.
* I can add hours and minutes together to find the total duration of an event or journey.

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Warm-up: Time Systems & Vocabulary (10 mins)
* **Activity:** Explain that we use two different ways to write time. One is 12-hour time, and the other is 24-hour time.
* **Vocabulary check:**
  1. **12-Hour Time:** Uses numbers from 1 to 12. It must always include **a.m.** (before noon) or **p.m.** (after noon).
  2. **24-Hour Time:** Uses four-digit notation. It counts hours from 0000 (midnight) up to 2359. It never uses a.m. or p.m.
* **Explicit Key Rules:**
  * **Midnight:** 12:00 a.m. = `0000` hours.
  * **Morning (a.m.):** Keep the numbers the same but format to 4 digits (e.g., 8:15 a.m. = `0815`).
  * **Noon:** 12:00 p.m. = `1200` hours.
  * **Afternoon/Evening (p.m.):** Add 12 to the hours (e.g., 4:30 p.m. $\rightarrow$ 4 + 12 = 16 $\rightarrow$ `1630`).
* **Active Whiteboard Check:** Call out times (e.g., "7:30 a.m.", "9:15 p.m.", "1545") and have students write the converted times on their mini-whiteboards.

### 2. Interactive Time Conversion Quiz (15 mins)
* **Activity:** Project Slides 3–7 of `Lesson_13_Presentation.html` onto the interactive whiteboard at the front of the classroom.
* **Interactive Mode:** 
  * A time conversion question appears on the slide.
  * Have a student volunteer come up to the interactive whiteboard and press the correct option button.
  * The system provides immediate visual feedback (green overlays for correct, red/orange for incorrect) with micro-animations.
  * **Standard Pathway:** Covers complex conversions (including midnight/noon edge cases).
  * **Lucas Pathway:** Offers simplified prompts and concrete scaffolding for students requiring additional visual cues.

### 3. Explicit Instruction: The Timeline Jump Method (15 mins)
* **Introduction to the Timeline:** Introduce the `12-hour 24-hour timeline.png` graphic. Explain that calculating elapsed time across morning and evening is much easier using horizontal "jumping" than trying to subtract vertically.
* **Modelled Example (I Do):** *A train leaves Brisbane at 1:30 a.m. and arrives in Cairns at 10:30 p.m. How long did the journey take?*
* Demonstrate the 3 steps on the board (projecting Slide 8):
  1. **Jump 1 (Minutes to next hour):** Start at 1:30 a.m. Jump 30 minutes to reach the neat hour of 2:00 a.m. Write down **30 minutes**.
  2. **Jump 2 (Hours to destination hour):** From 2:00 a.m., jump in hours to the nearest destination hour of 10:00 p.m. (2:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon = 10 hours; 12:00 noon to 10:00 p.m. = 10 hours. Total = 20 hours). Write down **20 hours**.
  3. **Jump 3 (Minutes to final time):** From 10:00 p.m., do a small jump of 30 minutes to reach 10:30 p.m. Write down **30 minutes**.
  * **Add together:** 20 hours + 30 minutes + 30 minutes = 21 hours.
* **Check for understanding:** Walk through a second flight example (Perth to Sydney, Slide 9) as a class.

### 4. Guided & Independent Practice: Word Problem Drawing (15 mins)
* ** putih-whiteboard Jumps (Slides 10–14):**
  * Present real-world Australian word problems (Outback mail runs, reef cruises, Sydney to Hobart yacht race, Indian Pacific train, The Ghan).
  * Students use their printed **Lesson_13_Handout.docx** which has the timeline graphic directly below each question.
  * Volunteers come up to the front and draw their jumps on the interactive whiteboard using the presentation's pen/highlighter annotation overlay tools.
  * **First Three Slides (10, 11, 12):** Include the teacher **"Reveal Solution Jumps"** button to overlay the correct mathematical jumps and help consolidate the strategy.
  * **Last Two Slides (13, 14):** Do NOT include the overlay button. Students must execute independently on their worksheets and write down their calculations.
* **Lucas ICP Pathway Support:**
  * Lucas's worksheet focuses on neat hour boundaries first, with simplified scenarios (e.g., Adelaide to Melbourne train leaving at 8:00 a.m. and arriving at 4:00 p.m.) with clear visual icons.

### 5. Plenary / Wrap-up & Forms Assessment (5 mins)
* **Exit Ticket / Assessment:** Assign the **Assessment_Forms.docx** quiz.
* Ask: "Why do we jump to the next hour first, instead of jumping all the hours in one go?" (It avoids errors when crossing morning and afternoon boundaries).
* Review the noon/midnight rules one final time.

## Differentiated Support Strategies

### Standard Pathway
* Encouraged to calculate time differences that cross multiple days (e.g., Monday 2:30 p.m. to Tuesday 7:15 p.m.).
* Independent calculations without overlay support on the final two slides.

### Support Pathway (Lucas ICP)
* Focus on time jumps that stay within the same block (all a.m. or all p.m.) or neat whole-hour boundaries.
* High-volume visual scaffolding on the printed handout with simplified timelines.
* Uses direct, clear, non-contact real-world associations.
