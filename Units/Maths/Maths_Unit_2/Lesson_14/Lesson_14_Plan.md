# Lesson Plan — Lesson 14: Time Conversions & Elapsed Time Practice

**Unit:** Maths Unit 2: Time Calculations  
**Year Level:** Year 5  
**Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Curriculum Mapping:** Australian Curriculum v9 Mathematics — Content Descriptor **AC9M5T03**  
*Solve problems involving elapsed time and convert between 12-hour and 24-hour time systems.*

---

## Learning Intention
We are learning to turn 12-hour times into 24-hour times (and vice versa) and use a step-by-step timeline jump strategy to calculate the elapsed time between two different times.

## Success Criteria
* **I can** convert any 12-hour morning (a.m.) or afternoon (p.m.) time into 4-digit 24-hour time notation.
* **I can** convert 24-hour times back into 12-hour notation with colons and correct a.m./p.m. labels.
* **I can** calculate the time difference spanning across a.m. and p.m. boundaries by breaking it down into three simple timeline jumps:
  1. Jump minutes to the next hour.
  2. Jump full hours.
  3. Jump remaining minutes.
* **I can** add the individual jumps together to select the correct total elapsed time from multiple-choice choices.
* **I can** apply these skills in both 12-hour and 24-hour time environments.

---

## 1. Introduction (Warm-up & Hook) — 10 Minutes
* **Hook:** Remind students of their previous time conversions from Lesson 13. Write a few times on the whiteboard: *8:30 a.m.*, *1615*, *11:05 p.m.*, and *0045*. Have students quickly call out the converted formats.
* **Midday / Midnight Focus:** Explicitly reinforce the edge cases:
  * Noon (*12:00 p.m.*) is **1200**.
  * Midnight (*12:00 a.m.*) is **0000**.
* **Problem Framing:** Introduce a real-life scenario: *"If a movie starts at 10:45 a.m. and finishes at 1:15 p.m., how do we calculate exactly how long it ran without getting confused by crossing the 12 o'clock midday mark?"* Introduce the **Timeline Jump Strategy**!

---

## 2. Core Activities — 40 Minutes

### Part 1: Interactive Time Conversions (15 Minutes)
* **Presentation Slide Sequence (Slides 3-8):** Show a series of 6 interactive multiple-choice questions.
* **Explicit Instruction:** Focus on the mechanical rules:
  * *For a.m. times:* Keep hours the same, ensure 4 digits (e.g., 8:30 a.m. → 0830).
  * *For p.m. times:* Add 12 to the hours (e.g., 4:15 p.m. → 1615).
  * *From 24-hour back to 12-hour:* Subtract 12 if hours are 13 or greater, add "p.m.". Keep same if 12 or less, add "a.m." (with 12:00 exceptions).
* **Interactive Modelling:** Call students to the board to tap options on the slides. Have students explain why wrong answers (e.g. 2030 instead of 0830) are incorrect.

### Part 2: 12-Hour Elapsed Time Differences (15 Minutes)
* **Presentation Slides (Slides 9-14):** 
  * The first 3 slides present elapsed time calculations with an **Interactive Timeline Jump Calculator** containing a brand new **4th Step (Total Duration Multiple Choice)**.
  * The last 3 slides are multiple-choice problems.
* **Direct Instruction (Timeline Jumps & Summation):** Teach the 4-step sequence:
  1. **Jump 1 (Minutes):** From the starting time (e.g., 9:45 a.m.) to the next whole hour (10:00 a.m.) → **+15 minutes**.
  2. **Jump 2 (Hours):** From that whole hour (10:00 a.m.) to the target hour (2:00 p.m.) → **+4 hours**.
  3. **Jump 3 (Minutes):** From that hour (2:00 p.m.) to the final target minutes (2:10 p.m.) → **+10 minutes**.
  4. **Step 4 (Total Time Multiple Choice):** Add the three jumps together: 4 hours + 15 mins + 10 mins = **4 hours and 25 minutes**. Choose the correct option from the 4-choice button grid.
* **Interactive Tooling:** Invite students to use the slide's button interface to construct these jumps step-by-step. The interactive timeline will dynamically animate the arrow jumps based on their clicks! Once the three jumps are selected, a 4th step will dynamically uncollapse, requiring them to click the correct total time.

### Part 3: 24-Hour Elapsed Time Differences (10 Minutes)
* **Presentation Slides (Slides 15-20):**
  * The first 3 slides feature the interactive step-by-step jump calculator configured for 24-hour notation, including the 4th step total calculation.
  * The last 3 slides are multiple-choice problems.
* **Explicit Modelling:** Demonstrate that the exact same 4-step logic applies:
  * *Example:* 0940 to 1420.
  * Jump 1: 0940 to 1000 = **+20 mins**.
  * Jump 2: 1000 to 1400 = **+4 hours**.
  * Jump 3: 1400 to 1420 = **+20 mins**.
  * Step 4: Sum the jumps to get **4 hours and 40 minutes**, selecting the correct answer on the card.
  * Total = 4 hours and 40 minutes.

## 3. Conclusion (Consolidation & Summary) — 10 Minutes
* **Slide 21 (Lesson Summary):** Review key rules and summary cards.
* **Exit Ticket / Independent Work:** Inform students that it is now time to test their skills individually on their tablets or laptops using the **Microsoft Forms Assessment**. Write the link/code on the board.
* **Self-Assessment Reflection:** Ask students to raise a thumb:
  * *Up:* I can confidently convert times and calculate time differences.
  * *Sideways:* I can do the conversions but need a bit of support drawing/calculating the jumps.
  * *Down:* I need more practice with the jumps.

---

## 4. Differentiation Strategies

### Support (Scaffolding)
* **Visual Handout:** Provide students with standard analogue clock faces to manually count hours if they struggle with the jump transitions.
* **Simplified Jumps:** Prompt students: *"What is the next hour after 9:45 a.m.?"* and *"How many minutes are in a full hour?"* to guide their calculations.
* **Mini-Whiteboard Drawings:** Encourage students to draw manual line segment arrows under the teacher's guidance.

### Extension (Challenge)
* **Day Boundary Extensions:** Challenge students to calculate durations that cross midnight (e.g., leaving at 10:30 p.m. Monday and arriving at 6:15 a.m. Tuesday).
* **Speed Runs:** Have students convert 24-hour times back and forth at speed to build automaticity.
* **Multi-Segment Flights:** Have students calculate the total travel duration of multi-leg flights crossing Australian time zones (e.g. Brisbane to Perth, accounting for daylight saving offsets).
