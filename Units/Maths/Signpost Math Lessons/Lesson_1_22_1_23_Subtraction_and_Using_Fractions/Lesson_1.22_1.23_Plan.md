# Lesson Plan: Signpost Mathematics — Lessons 1:22 & 1:23
## Subtraction from Whole Numbers & Using Fractions (Related Denominators)

---

### Pedagogical Contemplation

1. **Cognitive Goal**: Students analyze and operate on fractional quantities by (a) decomposing whole numbers into equivalent fractional units (e.g. $1 = \frac{4}{4}$, $3 = 2 + \frac{4}{4}$), (b) identifying equivalent fractions using visual models, and (c) renaming fractions to related denominators (e.g. halves, quarters, eighths; thirds, sixths, twelfths; fifths, tenths) to order, add, and subtract them.
2. **Interactive Alignment**: Interactive drag-and-drop fraction walls, sequencing strips, and fill-in-the-blank cloze fields force students to visualize equivalence and commit to intermediate steps (renaming numerators) before performing final addition or subtraction.
3. **Surfacing Student Thinking**: Interactive checks for understanding (CFU) require physical mini-whiteboard responses before revealing digital solutions. Misconceptions—such as adding denominators ($\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4}{12}$) or failing to rename whole numbers—are surfaced and corrected during guided practice.
4. **Pedagogical vs. Engagement Goal**: The primary pedagogical aim is mastering common denominator conversion and whole-number fractional renaming. The engagement design utilizes visual fraction bar models and responsive digital feedback to make abstract fraction relationships tactile and immediate.

## 2. Learning Intentions & Success Criteria

### WALT (We Are Learning To)
- Subtract fractions from 1 whole and whole numbers greater than 1.
- Use equivalent fractions to order fractions with related denominators.
- Add and subtract fractions with related denominators by converting them to a common denominator.

### WILF (What I'm Looking For)
1. **Renaming Wholes**: I can rename a whole number into a whole number and a fraction (e.g., $1 = \frac{8}{8}$, $3 = 2\frac{4}{4}$).
2. **Equivalent Fractions**: I can find equivalent fractions using fraction walls or multiplication/division rules (e.g., $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{5}{10}$).
3. **Ordering**: I can convert fractions to the same denominator before ordering them from smallest to largest.
4. **Operations**: I can change one fraction so both denominators match before adding or subtracting numerators.

## 5. Detailed Lesson Sequence

### Phase 1: Hook & Warm-Up (10 Mins)
- **Focus**: Decomposition of whole numbers into fractional parts.
- **Teacher Action**: Project Slide 2. Display a pizza divided into 4 equal slices ($4/4$) and 8 equal slices ($8/8$). Ask: *"If we eat 1 slice out of 4, how much is left? What if we have 3 whole pizzas and eat 1 quarter of one pizza?"*
- **Student Action**: Mini-whiteboard quick response:
  - $1 - \frac{1}{3} = \underline{\hspace{1cm}}$
  - $1 - \frac{1}{8} = \underline{\hspace{1cm}}$
  - $3 - \frac{1}{4} = \underline{\hspace{1cm}}$
- **Logistics (Notes Drawer)**: `DO`: Prompt mini-whiteboards. `CHECK`: Ensure students subtract numerator from total fractional parts.

### Phase 2: Explicit Teaching — Subtraction from Whole Numbers & Equivalent Fractions (15 Mins)
- **Focus**: Signpost Page 1:22 concepts.
- **Step 1: Subtraction from 1 Whole**
  - Model: $1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$.
  - Model: $1 - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{8}{8} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{7}{8}$.
- **Step 2: Subtraction from Whole Numbers $> 1$**
  - Model: $3 - \frac{1}{4}$. Decompose $3$ into $2 + 1 = 2 + \frac{4}{4}$.
  - Subtract: $2\frac{4}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}$.
- **Step 3: Fraction Wall Equivalence (Page 1:22 Q4–5)**
  - Display interactive Fraction Wall ($1$ whole, $\frac{1}{3}$s, $\frac{1}{6}$s, $\frac{1}{12}$s).
  - Demonstrate: $\frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}$, $\frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$, $\frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$.
- **CFU Check**: Project Slide 6 (CFU Badge). Mini-whiteboard check: $4 - \frac{1}{10} = ?$.

### Phase 3: Explicit Teaching — Using Fractions with Related Denominators (15 Mins)
- **Focus**: Signpost Page 1:23 concepts.
- **Step 1: Ordering Fractions**
  - Problem: Order $\frac{3}{8}, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{2}, 1\frac{1}{8}$ smallest first.
  - Strategy: Find common denominator ($8$).
    - $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{8}$, $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{8}$.
    - Compare numerators: $\frac{3}{8}, \frac{4}{8} (\frac{1}{2}), \frac{6}{8} (\frac{3}{4}), 1\frac{1}{8}$.
- **Step 2: Addition with Related Denominators**
  - Problem: $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8}$.
  - Convert: $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{2}{2} = \frac{2}{8}$.
  - Add: $\frac{2}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$.
- **Step 3: Subtraction with Related Denominators**
  - Problem: $\frac{5}{8} - \frac{1}{4}$.
  - Convert: $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{8}$.
  - Subtract: $\frac{5}{8} - \frac{2}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$.

### Phase 4: Independent Practice & Rotations (20 Mins)
Students complete `Lesson_1_22_1_23_Worksheet.docx`.

| Group / Tier | Focus Task | Scaffolding / Extension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1 (Support)** | Worksheet Part 1 & Part 2 | Use visual fraction strips and fraction wall reference sheet. Focus on 1 whole subtraction and like denominators. |
| **Tier 2 (Core)** | Worksheet Parts 1–5 | Complete whole number subtraction, ordering, and related denominator addition/subtraction ($4/5 + 7/10$, $9/10 - 1/2$). |
| **Tier 3 (Extension)** | Worksheet Part 6 & Challenge | Solve multi-step word problems involving mixed number operations with unlike/unrelated denominators (e.g., $3\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{3}{4}$). |

### Phase 5: Plenary & Formative Assessment (10 Mins)
- Review key answers as a class using the interactive presentation.
- Administer the 10-question MS Forms assessment or paper exit ticket.

## 6. Differentiation Strategies

- **Support (Lucas Pathway / Tier 1)**:
  - Provide printed fraction wall visual strips.
  - Scaffold subtraction from whole numbers by drawing fraction bars and crossing off parts.
  - Focus on denominator families of $(2, 4, 8)$ before moving to $(5, 10)$ or $(3, 6, 12)$.
- **Extension (High Achievers / Tier 3)**:
  - Transition from related denominators to unrelated denominators (e.g., $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$ finding LCM = 12).
  - Challenge with improper fraction conversions and mixed numeral reduction ($15/10 = 1\frac{5}{10} = 1\frac{1}{2}$).
