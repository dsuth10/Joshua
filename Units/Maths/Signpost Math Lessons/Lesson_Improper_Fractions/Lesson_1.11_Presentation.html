Improper Fractions & Mixed Numbers

Exploring, visualising, and converting fractions greater than 1

🎯 WALT (We Are Learning To)

Understand, visualise, and convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions using concrete representations and numerical steps.

⭐ WILF (What I'm Looking For)

  • Identify mixed numbers vs improper fractions.
  • Convert mixed numbers → improper ("Multiply then add").
  • Convert improper fractions → mixed ("Divide bottom into top").

Concept Discovery: Pizza Fraction Visualiser

Imagine ordering pizzas cut into equal slices. When we have more slices than one whole pizza, we can name the amount in two different ways!

Number of Slices: 5
As an Improper Fraction:
54
(Total Slices over Denominator)
As a Mixed Number:
114
(Whole Pizzas + Remaining Slices)

🍕 Pizza Slices Activity: Slices make whole pizzas!

• 4 slices make 1 whole pizza.
• If you have 5 slices, you have 1 whole pizza and 1 slice left over!
• Written as a fraction: 5 quarters (54) = 114.

Method 1: Mixed Number → Improper Fraction

The Golden Rule:
"Multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator!"
Multiply then Add ✖️ ➕
Worked Formula Model:
314 = (3 × 4) + 14 = 134
3 wholes of 4 quarters = 12 quarters + 1 extra quarter = 13 quarters!
1
Multiply the whole number by the bottom number (denominator).
2
Add the top number (numerator) to your result.
3
Write the total over the same denominator!

Teacher Notes — Slide 4

DO: Chant "Multiply the whole by the bottom, add the top!" with the class twice.

WORK: Model 3 1/4: 3 x 4 = 12, 12 + 1 = 13, result is 13/4. Note that the denominator NEVER changes!

RECORD: Have students copy the golden rule and example 3 1/4 = 13/4 into their books.

FINISH: 4 minutes.

CHECK: Ask: "Why does the denominator stay 4? Because the size of the pieces hasn't changed!"

Method 2: Improper Fraction → Mixed Number

The Division Rule:
"Divide the top by the bottom. The answer is the whole, remainder is the fraction!"
Divide Bottom into Top ➗
Worked Division Model:
114 = 11 ÷ 4 = 2 rem 3
= 234
4 goes into 11 two whole times (8), leaving 3 quarters remaining!
1
Divide the numerator (top) by the denominator (bottom).
2
The whole number answer becomes your whole number.
3
The remainder becomes the new top fraction number!

Teacher Notes — Slide 5

DO: Walk through 11/4. Ask students: "What is 11 divided by 4?" (2 remainder 3).

WORK: Emphasise that the quotient (2) is the whole number, and the remainder (3) sits on top of the original denominator (4).

RECORD: Have students write 11/4 = 11 ÷ 4 = 2 3/4 in their books.

FINISH: 4 minutes.

CHECK: Ask: "What happens if there is no remainder? It's just a whole number!" (e.g. 12/4 = 3).
