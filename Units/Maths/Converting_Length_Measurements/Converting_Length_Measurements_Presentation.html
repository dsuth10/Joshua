Understanding Metric Length Units

Millimetre (mm)

Used for tiny things. Think of the thickness of a small coin or a pencil lead.
10 mm = 1 cm

Centimetre (cm)

Used for everyday items. Think of the length of a pen or the width of a phone.
100 cm = 1 m

Metre (m)

Used for larger scale spaces. Think of classroom height or the width of a street.
1000 m = 1 km

Kilometre (km)

Used for long distances. Think of distances between towns or the length of a river.

Direct Instruction

Explicitly point out the scale differences. Highlight that the metric system is base 10, meaning conversions involve multiplying or dividing by 10, 100, or 1000.

Converting mm to cm (Divide by 10)

Since there are 10 millimetres in 1 centimetre, we can convert by dividing by 10.

To convert mm to cm:
Divide the number of millimetres by 10.
Rule: Move the decimal point one place to the left.
Examples:
• 34 mm → 34 ÷ 10 = 3.4 cm
• 26 mm → 26 ÷ 10 = 2.6 cm
👑
Mascot Rule

"Just like the king says, 34 mm splits into 3.4 cm!"

34 ÷ 10 = 3.4

Dividing by 10

Explain that dividing by 10 shifts place value. Every digit moves one place to the right, which visually looks like shifting the decimal point one place to the left.

Converting cm to m (Divide by 100)

Since there are 100 centimetres in 1 metre, we divide by 100 to convert.

To convert cm to m:
Divide the number of centimetres by 100.
Rule: Move the decimal point two places to the left.
Examples:
• 251 cm → 251 ÷ 100 = 2.51 m
• 829 cm → 829 ÷ 100 = 8.29 m
• 1165 cm → 1165 ÷ 100 = 11.65 m
💡
Decimals Shift

"Dividing by 100 means shifting the decimal point 2 positions left."

100 cm = 1 m

Dividing by 100

Remind students that dividing by 100 is equivalent to two steps of dividing by 10. The decimal point shifts two places left.

Ask: "How many metres are in 500 cm?" to check the baseline.

Converting m to cm (Multiply by 100)

To convert from a larger unit (metres) to a smaller unit (centimetres), we multiply by 100.

To convert m to cm:
Multiply the number of metres by 100.
Rule: Move the decimal point two places to the right.
Examples:
• 3.16 m → 3.16 × 100 = 316 cm
• 9.54 m → 9.54 × 100 = 954 cm
• 10.75 m → 10.75 × 100 = 1075 cm
⚡
Inverse Operation

"Going from large unit to small unit? We multiply!"

× 100

Multiplying by 100

Discuss the visual shift. When multiplying, the number becomes larger, so the decimal point moves to the right.

Give simple mental checks: "What is 2m in cm? What about 2.5m?"
