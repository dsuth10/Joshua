# Week 2, Lesson 7: Informal Language & Character

**Learning Intention:** Examine the use of informal language to establish character.

## Overview

In this lesson, students explore the informal language and Australian idioms used by Dylan and his community in _Paper Planes_. By contrasting Dylan's everyday voice with the formal expectations of school, students understand how authors use dialogue to shape a character's identity and background.

## Introduction (10 mins)

- **Discuss:** What is "informal language"? How do you speak differently to your friends at the park compared to a teacher or the principal at school?
- **Define:** 'Idioms' and 'Slang'. Why do Australians have so many unique slang words?
- **Visualise:** Display **Slide 2 & 3** to review definitions and initial examples.

## Activity 1: The Slang Hunt (15 mins)

- **Read:** Have students revisit Chapters 9-10 of _Paper Planes_.
- **Identify:** Ask students to find at least three examples of Australian slang or idioms.
- **Complete:** Using **Part 1 of the Student Handout**, students build a glossary by matching slang terms like 'mate', 'esky', and 'fair dinkum' to their definitions.
- **Discuss:** Why does the author use 'mate' so often in Waleup? What does it tell us about the community? (It establishes a casual, rural, egalitarian setting). Display **Slide 4 & 5**.

## Activity 2: Character Voice (15 mins)

- **Analyse:** How does Dylan's speech reflect his personality? Look closely at how he talks to his Dad. Is he disrespectful, or just informal?
- **Complete:** Students answer the short response questions in **Part 2 of the Student Handout**.
- **Share:** Have students share their reflections with a partner.

## Activity 3: Code-Switching (15 mins)

- **Compare:** Look at the official speech from a teacher or principal at the school. How is it different from Dylan? (Longer sentences, formal vocabulary, rigid structure).
- **Translate:** Using **Part 3 of the Student Handout** (Code-Switching), students will take quotes of Dylan's informal slang and rewrite them into precise, formal, "school-appropriate" language. Display **Slide 6**.
- **Reflect:** Does Dylan sound like the same person when he uses formal language? How important is voice to a character?

## Conclusion (5 mins)

- **Wrap-up:** Review the answers to the code-switching activity.
- **Checkpoint:** Ensure all students have completed their Glossary and Analysis.
- **Exit Ticket:** Have students write one formal sentence and one informal sentence describing their lunch on a sticky note.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a glossary of Australian slang used as an appendix or a pre-filled matching activity.
- **Extension:** Challenge students to write a short paragraph of dialogue entirely in formal language, and then rewrite it entirely in slang to see the differing impact on the reader.
