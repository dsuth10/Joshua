# Lesson 9: Character Relationships

**Unit:** Year 5 English - _Paper Planes_  
**Lesson Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Learning Intention:** Analyse the relationship between Dylan and his father (Jack) and compare it to the relationship between Dylan and his grandfather.

---

## Learning Context

Students have read up to the Sydney competition of _Paper Planes_. They are familiar with Dylan's home life in Waleup and the emotional distance of his father following the loss of Dylan's mother.

## Teaching and Learning Sequence

### 1. Introduction (10 Mins)

- **Recall:** Briefly discuss Dylan’s life in Waleup.
- **Hook:** Look at the visual of a lone paper plane vs. a flock of birds. How does this reflect Dylan’s feelings at home?
- **Goal:** Set the stage for analysing character dynamics.

### 2. Analysis: Dylan and Jack (Father) (20 Mins)

- **Discussion:** Focus on the "early tension."
  - Why is Jack often in the dark?
  - What are the "unspoken rules" in their house?
- **Evidence:** Identify 2-3 moments where Jack is emotionally unavailable (e.g., the microwave meals, the lack of conversation).
- **Symbolism:** Discuss the "eagle video" that Jack keeps watching—what does it represent for him?

### 3. Comparison: Dylan and Grandfather (20 Mins)

- **Transition:** How does the atmosphere change when Grandpa is around?
- **Independent Task:** Students write a paragraph response to the following prompt:
  > "Grandfather provides Dylan with the 'wind' he needs to fly, whereas his father is stuck on the ground." Compare how these two men influence Dylan’s journey so far.
- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for students needing assistance.

### 4. Conclusion & Reflection (10 Mins)

- **Share:** 2-3 students share their paragraph reflections.
- **Predict:** Based on the Sydney competition, do we think Jack’s relationship with Dylan will change? Why or why not?

---

## Differentiation

### Support (including Lucas)

- Provide an "Emotional Thermometer" to track Jack's vs. Grandpa's warmth.
- **Lucas:** Match pictures of Jack (sad/distant) and Grandpa (happy/nurturing) to Dylan. Write: "Grandpa helps Dylan."

### Extension

- Identify specific figurative language Worland uses to describe the "silence" in Dylan's house.
- Analyse the contrast between the _Waleup_ setting and the _Sydney_ setting in relation to Jack's mood.
