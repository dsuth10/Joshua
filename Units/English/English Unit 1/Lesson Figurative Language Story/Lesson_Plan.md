# Lesson Plan: Painting Pictures with Words - Crafting a Narrative with Figurative Language

## Context

**Year Level:** Year 5
**Subject:** English
**Unit Context:** _Paper Planes_ novel study
**Duration:** 60 minutes

## Learning Intentions

- **We are learning to** structure a narrative with a clear orientation, complication, climax, and resolution.
- **We are learning to** enhance our writing using descriptive language (noun and verb groups).
- **We are learning to** use figurative language (similes, metaphors, personification, and idioms) to paint a picture for the reader.
- **We are learning to** correctly punctuate spoken text (dialogue) between characters.

## Success Criteria

- I can plan a short story with a clear beginning, middle, and end.
- I can include at least two descriptive noun or verb groups.
- I can write at least one simile, metaphor, or example of personification.
- I can correctly use speech marks when my characters talk.

- PowerPoint Presentation (`Presentation.pptx`)

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Introduction: The Shape of a Story (10 mins)

- **Hook:** Present the concept of a story as a rollercoaster (Slide 2). Discuss how a story needs a structure to keep the reader engaged.
- **Direct Instruction:** Review the Narrative Structure (Slide 3-4):
  - _Orientation:_ Setting the scene (e.g., Dylan's dusty backyard).
  - _Complication:_ The problem arises (e.g., Jason destroying the plane).
  - _Climax:_ The point of highest tension (e.g., The final throw at the World Championships).
  - _Resolution:_ How things settle (e.g., Dylan making peace with Jason/Grandpa's pride).
- **Student Action:** Students begin filling out the "Narrative Arc" section of their graphic organiser handout, brainstorming a short story idea based on the _Paper Planes_ universe (e.g., A side adventure with Kimi, or Dylan inventing a new plane).

### 2. Descriptive Language: Noun & Verb Groups (10 mins)

- **Direct Instruction:** Explain that structure is the skeleton, but description is the skin (Slide 5-6).
  - _Noun Groups:_ Expanding a noun with adjectives (e.g., "The plane" -> "The crisp, perfectly folded paper plane").
  - _Verb Groups:_ Expanding a verb with adverbs or auxiliary verbs (e.g., "It flew" -> "It soared gracefully upwards").
- **Collaborative Practice:** Show a basic sentence on the board: "The boy threw the plane." As a class, upgrade it using _Paper Planes_ context. (e.g., "The determined young boy aggressively hurled the crumpled paper plane.")

### 3. Figurative Language: Painting with Words (15 mins)

- **Direct Instruction:** Introduce Figurative Language as the "special effects" of writing (Slides 7-11). Define and provide _Paper Planes_ examples for:
  - _Simile:_ Comparing two things using 'like' or 'as' (e.g., The plane shot forward like a silver bullet).
  - _Metaphor:_ Saying one thing _is_ another (e.g., Jason was a thunderstorm of anger).
  - _Personification:_ Giving human qualities to objects (e.g., The wind snatched the paper from his hands).
  - _Idioms:_ Common phrases with hidden meanings (e.g., Dylan was _on cloud nine_).
- **Student Action:** Students complete the "Figurative Language Bank" section of their handout, drafting one of each type for their own story.

### 4. Dialogue: Bringing Characters to Life (10 mins)

- **Direct Instruction:** Explain how spoken text reveals character personality and advances the plot (Slide 12-13).
- **Explicit Teaching:** Review punctuation rules for speech marks ("66 and 99").
  - Rule 1: New speaker, new line.
  - Rule 2: Punctuation goes _inside_ the closing speech marks (e.g., "Watch this!" yelled Dylan.)
- **Checking for Understanding:** Display an unpunctuated sentence from the book. Ask students to copy it onto whiteboards and punctuate it correctly.

### 5. Independent Practice: Drafting (10 mins)

- **Student Action:** Students use their completed graphic organiser to begin drafting their short story.
- **Teacher Role:** Circulate and provide targeted feedback. Check that students are attempting to include the figurative language they planned.

### 6. Plenary: Author's Chair (5 mins)

- **Share:** Invite 2-3 students to read a sentence from their draft that includes a strong noun group, a simile, or correctly punctuated dialogue.
- **Review:** Revisit the Success Criteria.

## Differentiation

- **Support (Below Level):**
  - Provide pre-filled noun/verb group options on their handout.
  - Allow students to rewrite a specific scene directly from the book rather than inventing a new plot.
  - Focus on mastering _Similes_ before attempting Metaphors or Personification.
- **Extension (Above Level):**
  - Challenge students to use an extended metaphor throughout a whole paragraph.
  - Require compound/complex sentences when crafting descriptive segments.
  - Focus on _Show, Don't Tell_ when writing dialogue (e.g., pairing speech with an action verb instead of just "said").
