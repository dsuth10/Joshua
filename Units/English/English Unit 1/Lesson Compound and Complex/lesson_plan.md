# Lesson Plan: Soaring with Sentences (Compound & Complex)

## General Information

- **Subject:** English
- **Year Level:** Year 5
- **Unit:** Unit 1 (_Paper Planes_ Novel Study)
- **Duration:** 60 Minutes
- **Topic:** Sentence Structures (Simple, Compound, Complex)

## Australian Curriculum Alignment

- **Content Descriptor:** [AC9E5LA05] Understand that the structure of a complex sentence represents a primary idea and one or more supporting ideas; and how writers can use this structure for effect.
- **Learning Intention:** We are learning to identify and create simple, compound, and complex sentences to improve our narrative writing.
- **Success Criteria:**
  - I can identify the difference between an independent clause and a dependent clause.
  - I can use FANBOYS to create compound sentences.
  - I can use subordinating conjunctions to create complex sentences.
  - I can create sentences that reflect the characters and settings of _Paper Planes_.

## Resources Required

- Novel: _Paper Planes_ by Steve Worland
- PowerPoint Presentation: _Soaring with Sentences_
- Student Worksheet: _Sentence Flight Manual_
- Microsoft Forms Assessment: _Sentence Flight Check_

---

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Hook (5 Minutes)

- **Activity:** "The Short Flight".
- **Action:** Display a series of three very short simple sentences on the board: "Dylan threw the plane. It flew high. Jason watched."
- **Discussion:** Do these sentences sound smooth? How do they feel to read? (Staccato, choppy). Explain that today we are going to learn how to make our writing "soar" like Dylan’s planes.

### 2. Review: Simple Sentences (5 Minutes)

- **Definition:** A simple sentence has one independent clause (subject + verb + complete thought).
- **Example:** "Dylan lives in Waleup."
- **Check:** Ask students to identify the subject and verb.

### 3. Input: Compound Sentences & FANBOYS (15 Minutes)

- **Definition:** Two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction.
- **FANBOYS:** For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.
- **Visual:** Display the FANBOYS acronym.
- **Example:** "Dylan wanted to win the regional competition, **so** he practiced every day after school."
- **Activity:** Students practice joining two simple sentences using a FANBOYS conjunction from a list provided on the slide.

### 4. Input: Complex Sentences & Clauses (15 Minutes)

- **Definition:** An independent clause joined with one or more dependent clauses.
- **Dependent Clause:** A group of words that has a subject and verb but does NOT make sense on its own.
- **Subordinating Conjunctions:** Use examples like _because, although, if, since, when, while_.
- **Example:** "**Although** Dad was sad, he still helped Dylan find some paper."
- **Relationship:** Explain that the dependent clause provides "extra information" or "reasons" for the main idea.

### 5. Guided Practice (10 Minutes)

- **Action:** Use characters from _Paper Planes_ (Dylan, Dad, Grandpa, Jason) to create complex sentences on the board.
- **Prompt:** "Because Jason was jealous..." (Students complete the sentence).
- **Prompt:** "...when the wind picked up." (Students provide the main clause).

### 6. Independent Practice (10 Minutes)

- **Task:** Students complete the _Sentence Flight Manual_ worksheet.
- **Activities:** Sorting clauses, combining sentences, and writing their own _Paper Planes_ sentences.

---

## Differentiation

### Support (Lucas & Support Group)

- **Scaffolding:** Provide sentence starters and a "Conjunction Cheat Sheet".
- **Simplified Task:** Focus on using 'and' for compound and 'because' for complex sentences.
- **Visuals:** Use pictures of characters to prompt sentence ideas.

### Extension

- **Challenge:** Experiment with placing the dependent clause at the start of the sentence (using a comma) versus the end.
- **Effect:** Discuss how complex sentences can build tension or describe a character's internal feelings.

## Evaluation & Assessment

- Collect worksheets for formative assessment.
- Assign the Microsoft Forms _Sentence Flight Check_ for individual data tracking.
