Jaguar

Apex Ambush-Hunter on a Precipice

With a coat the colour of sun-dappled forest floor and jaws strong enough to pierce a caiman's skull, the jaguar (*Panthera onca*) ranks among nature's most formidable predators. Yet even this muscular marvel now teeters on the edge of decline. In less than a century the cat has vanished from half of its historical range and faces mounting threats inside its last great refuge—the Amazon rainforest. Meet the Apex Hunter

Discover the fascinating characteristics of these magnificent big cats

Status & Population

Near Threatened (IUCN) with roughly 170,000–180,000 individuals left worldwide. These magnificent cats have vanished from half of their historical range and now face mounting threats in their last great refuge. Learn more about their conservation status and population trends.

Size & Physical Power

Males average 80 kg; females around 56 kg, but some males exceed 100 kg. Their stocky, barrel-chested build generates sprint power and swimming thrust, making them one of the most powerful big cats. Learn about their physical characteristics.

Longevity & Life Cycle

Jaguars live 12–15 years in the wild; up to 20 in managed care. Females give birth year-round after a 100-day gestation, raising two to four cubs that stay with their mother for nearly two years. Learn about their life cycle.

Habitat & Lifestyle

From riverbank stalking to territorial defense in the Amazon ecosystem

Jaguars once roamed from Arizona to Argentina, but today nearly 90 percent of the global population is bottled up in Amazonian forest and wetland mosaics, especially in Brazil's Pará and Mato Grosso states. These cats thrive near water, happily stalking along riverbanks before slipping in to swim after fish, turtles or even 2-metre-long caimans. Learn about their habitat preferences.

Diet & Killer Toolkit

The jaguar's specialized hunting strategy and powerful adaptations

A jaguar's menu reads like a rainforest inventory: more than 85 prey species ranging from 1 kg armadillos to 100 kg tapirs. Powerful limb muscles launch the cat in a short, explosive pounce, but its signature weapon is a 1,500 newton bite—the strongest of any big cat relative to size—that it drives straight through the skull or cervical vertebrae for an instant kill. Ecological Role

The vital role of jaguars as apex predators and keystone species

As an apex predator—and arguably a keystone species—the jaguar regulates populations of large herbivores and seed-eating rodents, preventing over-browsing and maintaining plant diversity. Camera-trap surveys show that landscapes retaining viable jaguar numbers also shelter higher densities of tapirs, monkeys and even understory birds, underscoring the cat's umbrella effect for Amazonian biodiversity.

Rising Threats

Multiple challenges facing jaguar populations

Deforestation & Fires

More than 27 million hectares of Brazilian jaguar habitat were converted to pasture or soy between 2000 and 2023, with 89 percent of clearing lacking legal permits. Learn about habitat destruction and fire impacts.

Livestock Conflict

As forest edges expand, cats raid cattle, prompting ranchers to retaliate with firearms or poisoned carcasses—killing up to 150 jaguars and pumas annually in some Amazon frontiers. Learn about human-wildlife conflict.

Illegal Trade

Teeth and bones fetch high prices on the black market, fuelling poaching networks that have shifted from Asian big cats to American ones. Learn about illegal trade.

Roads & Fragmentation

Planned highway extensions across the southern Amazon could slice migration corridors, increasing vehicle collisions and isolating small sub-populations. Learn about fragmentation impacts.

Hope on the Horizon

Conservation efforts and positive developments

Conservation biologists have mapped 51 Jaguar Conservation Units and 2.6 million km² of corridors that could keep breeding populations connected if deforestation is curbed. Studies show Indigenous territories host 63 percent of Amazonian jaguars while exhibiting the lowest threat indices, highlighting the pivotal role local communities play in safeguarding rainforest giants.
