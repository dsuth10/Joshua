## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to identify how authors introduce characters and settings at the beginning of a story
- **Writing:** I am learning to draft the beginning of my narrative, introducing my character and setting
- **Spelling:** I am revising consonant digraph spellings

## Success Criteria

- I can identify how the author introduces the character and setting in today's book
- I can draft a beginning for my story that tells the reader WHO, WHERE, and WHEN
- I can spell words with sh, ch, th, ck, and ng correctly

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Introduction (5 min)
- Show the new book. "Today while we read, pay special attention to the BEGINNING of the story."
- "How does the author introduce the character? How do we know where the story is happening?"

### Guided Reading (10 min)
- Lucas reads the book aloud.
- After the first page or two, pause: "The author has already told us a lot. What do we know about the character? What do we know about where this is happening?"
- Continue reading. Note any descriptive language or interesting sentence structures.

### Comprehension — Reading Like a Writer (5 min)
- Go back to the first page. Highlight:
  - How the author introduced the character (name, description, what they were doing)
  - How the author described the setting (place words, time words, sensory details)
- "We're going to use the same tricks in OUR writing today!"

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Modelled Writing: Story Beginning (5 min)
- Using a simple story map (NOT Lucas's), model writing a beginning:
  - Story map: Character = a brave cat named Milo / Setting = a dark forest at night
  - Draft: *"Milo was a small, brave cat with orange fur. One dark night, he crept into the forest. The trees were tall and the wind was cold."*
- Point out: "In three sentences, the reader knows the character, the setting, and can imagine the scene."

### Guided Drafting (10 min)
- Lucas retrieves his story map from Lesson 13.
- Together, draft the beginning of HIS story:
  - **Sentence 1:** Introduce the character (who + describing word)
  - **Sentence 2:** Introduce the setting (where + when)
  - **Sentence 3:** Add a sensory detail or what the character was doing
- Teacher aide scribes on the whiteboard while Lucas composes orally, then Lucas writes it in his book.

### Independent Drafting (10 min)
- Lucas continues drafting, adding 1–2 more sentences to his story beginning.
- Encourage: "What else can you tell the reader about the character or setting?"
- Remind: capitals, full stops, finger spaces.
- **Save this draft** — it continues in Lessons 15 and 16.

---

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

### Digraph Revision (5 min)
- Quick-fire: Teacher aide says a word, Lucas identifies the digraph:
  - *shop* (sh), *much* (ch), *thin* (th), *kick* (ck), *ring* (ng)
  - *catch* (tch), *which* (wh), *thing* (th + ng)

### Dictation Sentences (5 min)
- Move to SENTENCE dictation (applying spelling in context):
  1. *"The ship went past the rocks."*
  2. *"She had lunch with her friend."*
- Lucas writes the full sentence. Teacher aide checks digraph spellings AND punctuation.

### Spelling Correction (5 min)
- Review Lucas's writing from today's writing segment.
- Together, find and correct any misspelled digraph words.
- Lucas rewrites the corrected words three times each.

## Differentiation

| If struggling | If progressing well |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Provide sentence starters: "Once upon a time, there was a ___." | Add a hook or question in the opening: "Have you ever been lost?" |
| Scribe for Lucas; he re-reads and copies | Write 5+ sentences for the beginning with varied sentence starters |
| Focus on sh and ch digraphs only | Add sentence dictation with digraphs + long vowels combined |
