## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to identify how authors write satisfying endings — solving the problem and showing how the character feels
- **Writing:** I am learning to draft the ending of my narrative with a solution and a closing sentence
- **Spelling:** I am revising all long vowel spelling patterns from Weeks 3 onwards

## Success Criteria

- I can describe how today's book ended and explain whether the ending was satisfying
- I can draft an ending for my story that solves the problem and shows the character's feelings
- I can spell long vowel words (er, ow, ie, oo) with 80%+ accuracy

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Introduction (5 min)
- "Today we're focusing on ENDINGS. A good ending solves the problem and shows us how the character feels."
- Show the book. "Let's read to find out how this story ends."

### Guided Reading (10 min)
- Lucas reads the book aloud.
- Focus reading attention on the final pages: "Here's where the problem gets solved."
- After reading, discuss: "Was this a good ending? Did it feel finished?"

### Comprehension (5 min)
- **Solution:** "How was the problem solved? Who solved it?"
- **Character feelings:** "How does the character feel at the end? How is that different from how they felt in the middle?"
- **Satisfying endings:** "Does the ending feel complete? Or do you have more questions?"
- **Comparison:** "How did the Alba books end? Was it similar?"

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Explicit Teaching: Story Endings (5 min)
- Explain: "A good ending does THREE things:"
  1. **Solves the problem** — tell the reader how it got fixed
  2. **Shows feelings** — how does the character feel now?
  3. **Closes the story** — a final sentence that feels like "the end"
- Model for the Milo story:
  - *"The dog got bored and walked away. Milo climbed down from the tree. He felt brave and happy. Milo walked home under the bright stars."*
- Point out: problem solved (dog left), feeling (brave and happy), closing image (walking home).

### Guided Drafting (10 min)
- Lucas retrieves his draft from Lessons 14–15.
- Re-read the whole story so far together. "You've got a great beginning and middle. Now let's finish the story."
- Refer to the story map: "How did you plan for the problem to be solved?"
- Together, draft 3–4 sentences for the ending:
  - **Sentence 1:** How the problem is solved
  - **Sentence 2:** How the character feels
  - **Sentence 3–4:** A closing sentence or image
- Lucas writes with support.

### Independent Drafting and First Read-Through (10 min)
- Lucas adds any final sentences to his ending.
- Then: **Read the whole story aloud** from beginning to end.
- "Does it make sense? Is anything missing? Does the ending feel finished?"
- Teacher aide takes notes on areas for revision in Week 5.
- **Celebrate:** "You've written a whole story! Beginning, middle, and end!"

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

### Long Vowel Revision — All Patterns (5 min)
- Quick-fire identification: Teacher aide writes a word, Lucas identifies the long vowel sound and spelling:
  - *bird* (er sound, ir spelling)
  - *shout* (ow sound, ou spelling)
  - *night* (ie sound, igh spelling)
  - *moon* (oo sound, oo spelling)

### Mixed Dictation (5 min)
- Dictate 6 words from the er, ow, ie, and oo patterns:
  1. turn (ur)
  2. howl (ow)
  3. flight (igh)
  4. moon (oo)
  5. loud (ou)
  6. new (ew)
- **Target:** 5/6 correct.

### Spelling Audit of Draft (5 min)
- Go through Lucas's complete story draft.
- Underline any words that might be misspelled (Lucas identifies them, teacher aide confirms).
- List these words for targeted revision in Week 5's spelling segments.
