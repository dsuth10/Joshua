## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to identify how authors build tension and describe problems in the middle of a story
- **Writing:** I am learning to draft the middle of my narrative — the problem and what happens because of it
- **Spelling:** I am revising long vowel spelling patterns (ae, ee, oe)

## Success Criteria

- I can identify how the author creates a problem in today's book
- I can draft the middle of my story with a clear problem, character reaction, and at least one compound sentence
- I can spell long vowel words (ae, ee, oe) with 80%+ accuracy

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Introduction (5 min)
- "Today we're focusing on the MIDDLE of a story — where the problem happens."
- Show the book. Read the first few pages together to establish the beginning, then focus on where the problem starts.

### Guided Reading (10 min)
- Lucas reads the book aloud.
- At the problem moment, pause: "This is where things go wrong! What happened?"
- Continue reading through the middle: "How does the character react? What do they try?"
- Notice how the author builds tension: "The problem got WORSE here — how?"

### Comprehension (5 min)
- **Problem identification:** "What exactly went wrong? Can you describe it in one sentence?"
- **Character reaction:** "How did the character feel? What did they do?"
- **Tension:** "Did the problem get worse before it got better? How?"

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Modelled Writing: Story Middle (5 min)
- Continue the Milo example from Lesson 14:
  - Beginning (already written): *"Milo was a small, brave cat..."*
  - Middle draft: *"Suddenly, Milo heard a loud growl. A big dog was standing on the path! Milo felt scared, but he did not run away. He climbed up a tall tree and waited."*
- Point out:
  - "Suddenly" signals the problem starting
  - An exclamation mark shows surprise
  - A compound sentence with "but" shows contrast
  - The character REACTS — they feel something and do something

### Guided Drafting (10 min)
- Lucas retrieves his draft from Lesson 14.
- Re-read the beginning together. "Now let's write what happens next — the problem."
- Refer to the story map: "What problem did you plan?"
- Together, draft 3–4 sentences for the middle:
  - **Sentence 1:** The problem starts (use "Suddenly" or "Then" or "Without warning")
  - **Sentence 2:** Describe the problem
  - **Sentence 3:** How the character feels (feeling word)
  - **Sentence 4:** What they try to do about it
- Lucas writes with support.

### Independent Drafting (10 min)
- Lucas adds 1–2 more sentences to the middle of his story.
- Encourage compound sentences: "Can you join two ideas with 'and', 'but', or 'so'?"
- **Prompt:** "Make the problem feel exciting! What else goes wrong? What does the character try?"
- Save the draft for Lesson 16.

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

### Long Vowel Revision Game (5 min)
- "Sound Snap" — Teacher aide says a word, Lucas writes the long vowel spelling quickly on the whiteboard:
  - *rain* (ai), *play* (ay), *brave* (a-e)
  - *tree* (ee), *eat* (ea), *happy* (y)
  - *home* (o-e), *road* (oa), *slow* (ow)
- Speed challenge: "Can you beat your time from last week?"

### Mixed Dictation (5 min)
- Dictate 6 words mixing ae, ee, and oe patterns:
  1. brave (a-e)
  2. tree (ee)
  3. road (oa)
  4. play (ay)
  5. green (ee)
  6. home (o-e)
- **Target:** 5/6 correct.

### Spelling in Context (5 min)
- Review Lucas's writing from today. Together, find any long vowel words and check spelling.
- If misspelled, write the word correctly and identify the pattern: "This word has the /eɪ/ sound — which spelling should it be?"

## Differentiation

| If struggling | If progressing well |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Provide problem sentences pre-written; Lucas adds the character's reaction | Make the problem get worse (complication + escalation) before the solution |
| Use a sentence frame: "Suddenly, ___! ___ felt ___." | Add dialogue: What does the character SAY when the problem happens? |
| Focus on ae and ee revision; save oe for next lesson | Include er, ow, ie, oo words in the spelling revision |
