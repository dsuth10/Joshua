# Lesson 4 — Week 1

| | |
|---|---|
| **Book** | *Hot Chips for Lunch* (Alba Series, Book 4) |
| **Phonics Focus** | Consonant digraphs — sh, ch, th, tch |
| **Writing Focus** | Compound sentences using "and" and "but" |
| **Week Theme** | Foundations: Sentence Writing |

---

## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to read words with consonant digraphs (sh, ch, th, tch)
- **Writing:** I am learning to join two ideas together using "and" or "but" to make a longer sentence
- **Spelling:** I am learning that two letters can make one sound (digraphs)

## Success Criteria

- I can read words with sh, ch, th, and tch in the story
- I can write at least one compound sentence using "and" or "but"
- I can spell at least 4 words with consonant digraphs correctly

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Review and Introduction (5 min)
- Quick review of the Alba story so far: "What's happened to Alba across the three books?"
- Show the cover of *Hot Chips for Lunch*. "What do you think happens in this book?"
- **Digraph introduction:** Write on the whiteboard: **sh  ch  th  tch**
- Explain: "These two letters work together to make one sound." Model each sound.
- Preview words from the book: *chips, shop, lunch, thin, catch*

### Guided Reading (10 min)
- Lucas reads *Hot Chips for Lunch* aloud.
- When encountering a digraph word, prompt: "Those two letters make one sound together. What sound do they make?"
- Highlight digraphs as they appear: underline sh, ch, th, tch in the text (use a sticky note or point).
- After reading, count together: "How many digraph words did we find?"

### Comprehension (5 min)
- **Literal:** "Where did Alba go? What did she eat?"
- **Inferential:** "Why do you think Alba needed to eat? What problem might this cause?"
- **Story structure:** "What was the problem in this story?" (Begin identifying story problems — preview for later weeks.)

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Explicit Teaching: Compound Sentences (5 min)
- Show two simple sentences:
  - *"Alba was hungry."* and *"She found some chips."*
- Model combining them: *"Alba was hungry and she found some chips."*
- Explain: "We used 'and' to join two ideas. The sentence is now longer and more interesting."
- Show "but": *"Alba was hungry but she could not find food."*
- Explain: "'But' joins two ideas when the second one is different or surprising."

### Guided Writing (10 min)
- Together, write compound sentences about the story:
  - Teacher aide provides two simple sentences. Lucas suggests which word to join them with.
  - *"Alba went to the shop."* + *"She got some chips."* → *"Alba went to the shop and she got some chips."*
  - *"The chips were hot."* + *"Alba ate them."* → *"The chips were hot but Alba ate them."*
- Discuss: "Did we use 'and' or 'but'? Why?"
- Remind: Only one full stop at the end of the compound sentence, not in the middle.

### Independent Writing (10 min)
- Lucas writes 2–3 sentences about *Hot Chips for Lunch*. At least one must be a compound sentence.
- **Prompt card:**
  - Simple: Who? What happened?
  - Compound: ___ **and** ___ or ___ **but** ___
- Teacher aide observes punctuation: capital at start, full stop at end, no full stop before "and"/"but".

---

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

### Digraph Sort (5 min)
- Prepare word cards (or write on whiteboard). Lucas sorts words by their digraph:

| sh | ch | th | tch |
|----|----|----|-----|
| shop | chips | thin | catch |
| ship | much | this | match |
| fish | lunch | with | fetch |

- Lucas reads each word aloud as he sorts it.
- Ask: "What's the same about all the words in this column?"

### Word Building (5 min)
- Using letter tiles, build digraph words:
  - **shop → ship → chip → chin → thin → this**
  - Emphasise that sh, ch, th are one sound each, even though they use two letters
- Lucas reads and writes each word after building.

### Dictation (5 min)
- Dictate 5 words:
  1. chips
  2. shop
  3. thin
  4. catch
  5. lunch
- After writing, Lucas underlines the digraph in each word.

## End-of-Week Check-In

This is the last lesson of Week 1. Teacher aide briefly notes:
- [ ] Can Lucas decode CVC and CCVC words with reasonable accuracy?
- [ ] Can Lucas write a simple sentence with a capital letter and full stop?
- [ ] Has Lucas attempted a compound sentence?
- [ ] Can Lucas hear and spell consonant digraphs?
- [ ] Any specific sounds or patterns Lucas is finding particularly difficult?

---

## Differentiation

| If struggling | If progressing well |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Focus on sh and ch only (reduce cognitive load) | Include "so" and "because" as additional connectives |
| Provide sentence frames: "Alba ___ and ___." | Challenge: write 3 compound sentences about different events |
| Reduce dictation to sh and ch words only | Add tch vs ch discrimination: "When do we use tch? When do we use ch?" |
