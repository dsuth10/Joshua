# Lesson 19 — Week 5

| | |
|---|---|
| **Book** | *Second Series — Book 7* |

| **Week Theme** | Refining: Editing, Publishing, and Assessment |

---

## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to read aloud with fluency and expression, using punctuation to guide my phrasing
- **Writing:** I am learning to publish my story by writing a neat, legible final copy
- **Spelling:** I am doing a final spelling check on my published piece

## Success Criteria

- I can read a text aloud with appropriate pauses and expression
- I can write my published story using consistent, legible unjoined letters
- I can spell all words in my published piece correctly (with assistance for tricky words)

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Introduction (5 min)
- "Today we're going to read with our BEST expression — like a storyteller!"
- Show the book. "Listen to me read the first page. Notice how I change my voice for different characters and pause at full stops."

### Shared Reading — Fluency Focus (10 min)
- Teacher aide reads a page with expression, then Lucas re-reads the same page, mimicking the expression.
- Focus on:
  - Pausing at full stops
  - Voice going up for questions
  - Emphasis for exclamation marks
  - Changing voice for different characters
- Choose 2–3 pages for repeated reading to build fluency and confidence.

### Comprehension (5 min)
- "How did reading with expression make the story better?"
- "When you read your OWN story aloud tomorrow, how will you make it sound interesting?"
- Practice: Lucas reads one paragraph from his own story aloud with expression.

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Explicit Teaching: Publishing (5 min)
- Explain: "Publishing means writing your FINAL copy. This is the version people will read."
- Handwriting reminders:
  - Letters sit on the line
  - All letters are the same size (except capitals and tall letters)
  - Use consistent unjoined letters (not cursive)
  - Leave clear finger spaces
  - Write neatly — take your time
- Show an example of a well-presented published piece vs a messy draft. "Which one would YOU want to read?"

### Publishing (20 min)
- Lucas rewrites his edited story as a neat final copy.
- Use **publishing paper** (special lined paper or good-quality writing paper) to make it feel like a real published piece.
- Teacher aide supports:
  - Reading each sentence from the draft before Lucas writes it in his published copy
  - Reminding about letter formation and spacing
  - Checking spelling as Lucas writes (this is the FINAL copy — spelling should be correct)
- If Lucas finishes the text, he can add an illustration to accompany the story.
- **If time is short:** Focus on getting the text written neatly. The illustration can be completed at the start of Lesson 20.

---

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

> **Note:** Spelling is integrated into the publishing segment today. The formal spelling time is used for a final check.

### Final Spelling Check (5 min)
- After publishing, Lucas reads through his final copy word by word.
- Teacher aide checks: are all words spelled correctly?
- Any remaining errors are corrected neatly (teacher aide provides the correct spelling; Lucas writes it correctly).

### Personal Spelling List — Final Test (5 min)
- Teacher aide dictates all words from Lucas's personal spelling list (5–8 words).
- Lucas writes each word.
- Celebrate improvements: "Last time you wrote ___ but now you wrote it correctly!"

### Phonics Recap Game (5 min)
- Quick game: Teacher aide says a phonics pattern, Lucas writes a word with that pattern:
  - "CVC" → *got*
  - "sh digraph" → *shop*
  - "long a" → *brave*
  - "long e" → *tree*
  - "igh" → *night*
- Fun and fast-paced — this is revision, not testing.

---

## Differentiation

| If struggling | If progressing well |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Teacher aide scribes difficult words; Lucas copies them carefully | Lucas publishes independently with minimal support |
| Publish one paragraph at a time, with breaks | Add a title page with a border and illustration |
| Focus on letter formation for lowercase letters only | Practise writing the title in decorative letters |
