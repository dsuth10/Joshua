# Lesson 11 — Week 3

| | |
|---|---|
| **Book** | *River Flight* (Alba Series, Book 11) |
| **Phonics Focus** | 'ie' sound — igh, i-e, y, i |
| **Writing Focus** | Story events: Writing a problem for a character |
| **Week Theme** | Narrative Elements: Settings and Combining |

---

## Learning Intentions

- **Reading:** I am learning to read words where the long 'i' sound is spelled igh, i-e, or y
- **Writing:** I am learning to write about a problem that happens to a character in a story
- **Spelling:** I am learning the different ways to spell the long 'i' sound

## Success Criteria

- I can read words with igh, i-e, and y (as long 'i') in the story
- I can write a problem for a character that includes what happened and how the character reacted
- I can spell at least 3 words with long 'i' patterns correctly

---

## Reading (≈20 minutes)

### Introduction (5 min)
- Show *River Flight*. "What does 'flight' mean here — flying, or running away?"
- Discuss: "Alba is near a river. What problems could a tiny person have near a river?"
- **Phonics preview:**

| igh | i-e | y | i |
|-----|------|---|---|
| flight | ride | by | find |
| night | side | my | kind |
| right | time | fly | mind |

### Guided Reading (10 min)
- Lucas reads *River Flight* aloud.
- Prompt for long 'i' words: "What spelling is making the /aɪ/ sound?"
- **Problem focus:** "What goes wrong for Alba in this story? When does the problem start? How does she react?"
- Mark the "problem moment" in the text — where does the trouble begin?

### Comprehension (5 min)
- **Story structure:** "Every good story has a problem. What is the problem in *River Flight*?"
- **Character response:** "What does Alba DO about the problem? Is she brave, scared, clever?"
- **Evaluation:** "Was the problem big or small? Why do you think the author chose this problem?"

---

## Writing (≈25 minutes)

### Explicit Teaching: The Story Problem (5 min)
- Explain: "In every narrative, something goes WRONG. This is called the problem or complication."
- Show the pattern on the whiteboard:
  1. **What happened:** *"The river started to flood."*
  2. **How the character reacted:** *"Alba gasped. She looked around for somewhere to hide."*
  3. **What the character did:** *"She grabbed a leaf and jumped on it like a boat."*
- "A good story problem has THREE parts: what happened, how the character felt, and what they tried to do."

### Guided Writing (10 min)
- Together, use a **Problem Planner:**

| What went wrong? | How did the character feel? | What did she try to do? |
|------------------|----------------------------|-------------------------|
| | | |

- Fill in for *River Flight* first (known story).
- Then create a NEW problem for Alba together:
  - e.g., "Alba gets trapped in a bottle" or "A bird tries to eat her"
- Write 2–3 sentences about the new problem together.

### Independent Writing (10 min)
- Lucas invents a new problem for Alba and writes 3–4 sentences about it.
- **Requirements:**
  - Say what went wrong
  - Include how Alba felt (using a feeling word)
  - Describe what she tried to do
- **Prompt:** "Make up a problem for Alba. Something goes wrong! What is it?"
- Teacher aide encourages imaginative thinking and supports sentence structure.

---

## Spelling (≈15 minutes)

### Long 'i' Pattern Hunt (5 min)
- Look through *River Flight* together. Find and list every long 'i' word.
- Sort by spelling pattern: igh, i-e, y, i
- Discuss: "igh is usually at the end or before t; i-e is a split digraph; y says /aɪ/ at the end of short words"

### Word Building (5 min)
- Build word chains:
  - **night → light → flight → fright → right**
  - **ride → hide → side → slide → slime → time**
  - **my → by → fly → cry → try**

### Dictation (5 min)
- Dictate 5 words:
  1. flight
  2. ride
  3. night
  4. time
  5. fly
- Lucas writes, then circles the long 'i' spelling in each word.

---

## Differentiation

| If struggling | If progressing well |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Focus on igh and i-e; save y for revision | Include the less common "i" spelling (e.g., *find*, *kind*, *wild*) |
| Provide the problem; Lucas writes how the character reacted | Write the problem AND a solution |
| Use sentence frames: "Something bad happened. ___ felt ___." | Add setting detail to the problem scene: WHERE did it happen? |
