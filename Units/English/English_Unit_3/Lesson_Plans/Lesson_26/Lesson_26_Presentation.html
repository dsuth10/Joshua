Your idea can change something.

First, make it clear enough for a real audience to act.

Assessment launch: persuasive writing and speaking
ENTRY

Which proposal deserves a hearing?

4 MIN

Choose the proposal that gives an audience a clearest starting point. Be ready to explain why.

PURPOSE

The task: build an opinion people can understand

4 MIN

Part A — Write

Create and edit a persuasive text for an identified audience.

Suggested length: 200–400 words

Part B — Speak

Present and justify your opinion to an identified audience, using features of voice.

Suggested length: 1–3 minutes

You will negotiate a school or community topic, research it, plan, draft, improve, then adapt it for speaking.

MAP

A persuasive text has a job in every section

5 MIN

Which sections help a reader understand what you want? Which sections make the reader believe you?

MODEL

Read the whole model before taking it apart

5 MIN

Our School Needs a Storytime Dog

Imagine walking into the library and being greeted by a friendly dog who listens carefully as students read. A Storytime Dog program should be introduced at our school because it would improve reading confidence, support student wellbeing and create a more positive learning environment.

Firstly, a Storytime Dog would help students become more confident readers. Many students feel nervous when reading aloud in front of adults or their classmates. However, dogs do not judge mistakes. The model then develops this reason with research language and an explanation.

Some people may argue that a Storytime Dog could be distracting. However, trained education dogs follow strict routines and are supervised at all times.

Our school should introduce a Storytime Dog because it would build confidence, improve wellbeing and encourage a love of reading.

Verified model response supplied with this unit. The quoted research wording in the full model is a model feature—not evidence students can reuse as a source.

ANNOTATE

What job is each sentence doing?

7 MIN
Choose a sentence, then its job.
EVIDENCE

Evidence must be traceable

4 MIN

Reason

“It would improve reading confidence.”

Evidence to find

A fact, quotation, expert view, survey, interview or reliable source that directly helps test that reason.

Not enough

“Everyone knows it is good.”
“A website said …”

Later, you will identify your source and explain how its information supports an argument. You do not invent statistics, organisations, experts or quotations.

AUDIENCE

The same idea changes when the audience changes

5 MIN
Choose an audience. What would that audience need to know before agreeing?
STUDIO

Topic → audience → opinion → reasons

10 MIN

Choose a topic that can be negotiated with the teacher and researched responsibly.

REHEARSE

The “so what?” test

4 MIN

Speaker: “I think __________ should happen for __________.”

Listener: “I understand your opinion. I need to know __________ before I agree.”

Speaker: Add one reason, then revise your opinion if it is too broad.
