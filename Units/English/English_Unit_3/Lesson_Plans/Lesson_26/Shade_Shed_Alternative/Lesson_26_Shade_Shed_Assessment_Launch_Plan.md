# Lesson 26 Alternative: Assessment Launch and Model Deconstruction

### Pedagogical Contemplation

The cognitive goal is for students to turn a topic into an arguable, audience-aware claim and to see how a persuasive model is deliberately built: hook, thesis, topic sentences, developed reasons, fair acknowledgement and conclusion. The entry makes the difference between a topic and a proposal visible; the annotation asks students to identify each sentence's job before its label is supplied; and the comparison activity requires a reasoned judgement rather than a guessed match. Thinking becomes visible through a model map, an audience-and-claim card, a spoken rehearsal and an exit statement. The fictional Shade Shed feature gives the class a common, safe text for structural analysis. Its courtyard and characters are engagement context, not a substitute for the later research process.

## Lesson purpose

Students launch the written and spoken persuasive assessment by analysing an original fictional model, **The Shade Shed**, then selecting a suitable school or community proposal, audience and research questions. The lesson retains the assessment destination without relying on *Berani*.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LY06:** plan, create and edit written persuasive texts.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LY06:** plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs and elaborated ideas.

## Learning intention

We are learning to build a clear persuasive opinion for a real audience and explain the job of each part of a model text.

## Success criteria

- I can distinguish a topic from a proposed action and a clear opinion.
- I can identify a hook, thesis, topic sentence, developed reason, counterargument and conclusion.
- I can explain how one model sentence helps persuade its audience.
- I can name an appropriate audience, claim and two questions I need to research.

## Assessment requirements preserved

- **Part A:** an individual written persuasive text for an identified audience; suggested length **200-400 words**.
- **Part B:** an individual spoken presentation that justifies an opinion for an identified audience; suggested length **1-3 minutes**.
- Students negotiate an appropriate school or community topic, locate differing ideas and supporting information, identify their sources, then adapt rather than merely read the written text aloud.

## Preparation

- Open `Lesson_26_Shade_Shed_Assessment_Launch_Presentation.html` and choose fullscreen.
- Print the Model Map and Assessment Direction Organiser, or the Concise Access Pack.
- Print or distribute the original reading article. It is fictional and is for structural analysis only.
- Make the assessment requirements available. Research does not occur in this lesson; Lesson 27 establishes traceable evidence.

## Core sequence (50 minutes)

1. **Proposal or topic? - 4 min.** Compare broad topics with an arguable action and explain the difference.
2. **Assessment map - 4 min.** Establish the connected written and spoken task.
3. **Read the whole model - 6 min.** Read *The Shade Shed* before naming its parts.
4. **Notice the jobs - 6 min.** Mark hook, thesis, reason, other view and conclusion in the reading pack.
5. **Explicit model - 6 min.** Deconstruct one paragraph: claim, reason, development and link to audience.
6. **CFU: thesis or topic? - 5 min.** Choose and justify the statement that gives an audience a position and action.
7. **Audience lens - 4 min.** Predict what a principal, student and duty teacher each need to understand.
8. **Assessment direction - 8 min.** Complete an audience, claim and research-question card.
9. **Oral rehearsal - 4 min.** Test the claim with a partner and revise one vague word.
10. **Exit evidence - 3 min.** Submit a claim, audience and one question that needs research.

## Interaction matrix

| Interaction | Cognitive demand | Evidence / misconception | Teacher signal | Tier 2 hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal sort | Compare and justify | Topic mistaken for a position | Student names action and reason | “Who should do what?” |
| Model annotation | Classify by rhetorical job | Hook confused with thesis | Accurate labels with a purpose explanation | “Which line tells us what should happen?” |
| Thesis check | Apply criteria | Vague preference mistaken for claim | Student selects a specific action and audience | “Find the option that could be researched and debated.” |
| Audience lens | Transfer | Same wording used for every listener | Student adjusts the reason/detail for listener | “What responsibility does this listener have?” |
| Claim rehearsal | Revise | Unresearchable promise or unsupported evidence | Student replaces vague or absolute language | “Which word would you need to prove?” |

## Model and evidence boundary

The Shade Shed, its school, people and details are fictional. Students analyse its structure and language, not its factual reliability. It contains no evidence that students may reuse as research. In Lesson 27 students gather and identify their own traceable sources; they do not invent statistics, expert opinions, organisations, quotations or URLs.

## Differentiation

**Concise access route:** green language shortens directions, preserves the same model jobs, allows pointing, speaking, copying or scribing, and asks for one claim plus one research question.

**Support:** offer the sentence launches “The school should ... because ...” and “I need to find out ...”; co-construct an audience before students choose their own.

**Extension:** adapt the same claim for two audiences, add a qualified counterargument, and identify what evidence would count against the proposal.

## Formative assessment and next step

The annotation and CFU identify students who cannot yet distinguish a topic, hook and thesis. The final direction card identifies students whose claim is vague, unsafe, already decided by a fact claim, or not researchable. Use it to conference topics and build the approved source set for Lesson 27.
