# Lesson 26: Assessment Launch and Model Deconstruction

## Lesson purpose

Students launch Assessment Task 3 by understanding its written and spoken components, deconstructing the verified Storytime Dog model response, and selecting a negotiated persuasive topic with an identified audience and possible reasons to research.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** plan, create and edit written persuasive texts.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs.

## Learning intention

We are learning to turn a school or community idea into a clear persuasive opinion for a real audience.

## Success criteria

- I can explain the written and spoken parts of the assessment.
- I can identify the thesis, paragraph purpose, evidence need and conclusion in a verified model.
- I can name a suitable topic, target audience and opinion.
- I can identify reasons I will need to research rather than invent evidence for.

## Assessment requirements preserved

- **Part A:** individual written persuasive text, planned, created and edited for an identified audience; suggested length **200–400 words**.
- **Part B:** individual presentation that justifies an opinion to an identified audience, using features of voice; suggested length **1–3 minutes**.
- Students negotiate an appropriate school/community topic, research differing ideas and supporting information, including authoritative sources, and identify their sources. The written text supports—but is not simply read as—the spoken presentation.

## Preparation

- Open Lesson_26_Presentation.html in a modern browser. Teacher logistics are in the Notes popup only.
- Print the standard or Lucas organiser and have the supplied Year 5/Year 6 Storytime Dog model responses available.
- Have the assessment sheet available for reference. This 50-minute lesson is the topic-launch conference; research occurs in Lesson 27.

## Core sequence (50 min)

1. **Proposal precision — 4 min.** Compare vague topics with a specific proposed action.
2. **Assessment map — 4 min.** Establish the connected written and spoken task.
3. **Structural jobs — 5 min.** Identify what an introduction, arguments, other view and conclusion do.
4. **Whole model — 5 min.** Read selected sections of the verified Storytime Dog model.
5. **Annotated model — 7 min.** Match hook, thesis, topic sentence, other view and conclusion to their jobs.
6. **Traceable evidence — 4 min.** Distinguish a researchable reason from unsupported assertion.
7. **Audience lens — 5 min.** Choose and justify a target audience.
8. **Topic studio — 10 min.** Negotiate/record topic, audience, opinion and possible research reasons.
9. **So-what rehearsal — 4 min.** Use partner feedback to refine an opinion.
10. **Exit ticket — 2 min.** Submit audience, opinion and one research question.

## Model and evidence boundary

The supplied Storytime Dog models are used to analyse persuasive structure and language choices. They contain generic research language for modelling; students must not copy it or present it as verified evidence. In Lesson 27 they locate, evaluate and identify their own sources. Do not invent statistics, organisations, experts or quotations.

## Differentiation

**Support:** provide the supplied model, colour-code the five labelled structural jobs, offer a small approved topic menu, rehearse opinion statements orally, and conference early.

**Lucas (ICP):** use the low-writing organiser; the student may point, speak, copy or use a scribe to choose a school idea, audience and a simple “should” opinion. This preserves the core destination: expressing an opinion to someone who can act.

**Extend:** require a more complex audience with a plausible concern, articulate a counter-position, and identify what kind of authoritative evidence would best test each reason.

## Formative assessment

The annotation interaction reveals confusion between hook, thesis and topic sentence. The conference and exit ticket reveal whether each student has a researchable, audience-aware proposal before Lesson 27.
