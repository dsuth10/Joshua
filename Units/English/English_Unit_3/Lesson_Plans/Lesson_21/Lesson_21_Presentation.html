Evaluating
Authoritative Sources

How do we decide whether a source is useful, limited or still needs checking?

ENTRY • SOURCE DETECTIVES

Would you trust it yet?

CORE · 4 MIN
CLAIM A“Palm oil is harming orangutans.”

Who says it? What evidence do they use? What might they want readers to do?

CLAIM B“We tried our best.”

Who says it? What evidence would you need before accepting it?

Trust is not a feeling. It is a reasoned judgement.

WHAT HE FINDSOnline searches show rescue organisations and different orangutan species.

A careful researcher names both what is known and what is still uncertain.

SOURCE LENS

Four questions before you use a source

CORE · 5 MIN
1Who created it?

What expertise, role or perspective do they bring?

2What supports it?

Is there evidence, method or a traceable basis?

3Why this source?

Is it current and relevant to this exact claim? What is its purpose?

4What else confirms it?

Can another credible source check the claim?

Turn a judgement into a checklist item

CORE · 5 MIN

Claim: “This source is useful because it is about orangutan rescue.”

Improve it: “Before I use this source, I will check who created it, what evidence it gives and whether another credible source confirms the claim.”

BUILD STUDIO

Create your evidence-source checklist

CORE · 10 MIN

Your checklist needs four checks

  • Creator: Who made this and what is their role or perspective?
  • Evidence: What supports the claim?
  • Fit: Is it current and relevant to my exact claim? What is its purpose?
  • Cross-check: What other credible source could confirm or complicate it?

Write each as a question or a “Before I use this source…” instruction.
