# Lesson 21 Alternative: Evaluating Biased and Balanced Persuasive Texts

## Lesson purpose

Students analyze a series of short, contrasting persuasive reading passages on engaging contemporary issues (School Uniforms, E-Scooters on Footpaths, Zoos & Wildlife Conservation, and AI Homework Tools) to evaluate how authors construct **bias** or maintain **balance**. Students identify specific linguistic indicators—emotive vocabulary, selective evidence, loaded verbs, modal strength, and omitted perspectives. The lesson culminates in students composing two contrasting paragraphs on a selected topic: a **Biased Paragraph** (deliberately employing persuasive bias techniques) and a **Balanced Paragraph** (presenting a fair, multi-sided, evidence-grounded overview).

This is a supplemental alternative lesson for Unit 3. It does not overwrite the original literature-focused Lesson 21.

## Curriculum alignment

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LA02:** Recognise that differing ideas and opinions exist and analyze how text structures and language choices represent these views.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LA02:** Identify bias in persuasive texts and examine how language choices influence reader interpretation.
- **Year 5 & 6 — AC9E5LY06 / AC9E6LY06:** Create structured persuasive texts using evidence, modal language, and audience-appropriate framing.

## Learning intention

We are learning to identify bias and balance in persuasive texts and use these techniques deliberately in our own writing to convey one-sided or balanced arguments.

## Success criteria

- I can define bias and balance using concrete linguistic evidence.
- I can identify 4 indicators of bias in a reading text: emotive words, loaded verbs, selective evidence, and absolute claims.
- I can identify 4 indicators of balance in a reading text: neutral tone, multi-sided perspectives, transparent limitations, and modal qualifiers.
- I can write a 4–5 sentence **Biased Paragraph** that intentionally uses bias techniques to persuade the reader.
- I can write a 4–5 sentence **Balanced Paragraph** that uses fair representation, neutral tone, and modal verbs.
- I can visibly revise one sentence in my writing after peer feedback.

## Finished workbook response

A two-part written response in English workbooks consisting of:
1. **Biased Paragraph (4–5 sentences):** Presents an exaggerated, single-sided argument on a chosen topic using loaded language, modal certainty (*must*, *always*), and single-sided evidence.
2. **Balanced Paragraph (4–5 sentences):** Presents the same topic fairly using neutral tone, acknowledging counter-arguments, modal qualifiers (*may*, *suggests*, *could*), and objective evidence.
3. **Reflective Self-Check:** A margin annotation or table identifying the key language shifts made between the two paragraphs.

## Core sequence — 60 minutes

### 1. Hook & Micro-Bias Test — 4 minutes
Display two contrasting headlines reporting on the exact same event (e.g., *"reckless joyriders menace parkgoers"* vs. *"commuters test eco-friendly electric scooters"*). Students vote on which headline reveals author bias and identify the exact words that signal the author's viewpoint.
- **Student action:** Pair discussion; circle loaded words on the projected slide.
- **Finished when:** Students identify that word choice (*reckless joyriders* vs *eco-friendly commuters*) alters reader reaction before any facts are read.
- **Teacher look-for:** Students focus on word connotations rather than whether they agree with e-scooters.

### 2. Defining Bias vs Balance — 4 minutes
Introduce the **Bias & Balance Toolkit**:
- **Bias Indicators:** Emotive adjectives, loaded verbs (*dumped*, *forced*), selective evidence, absolute claims (*always*, *never*, *everyone knows*).
- **Balance Indicators:** Neutral tone, multi-perspective acknowledgment, transparent limitations, modal qualifiers (*may*, *suggests*, *often*).
- **Student action:** Copy the 4 Spotters into their organiser header.
- **Finished when:** Students can state the difference between an opinion and structural text bias.

### 3. Guided Analysis: Model Reading (Topic A — School Uniforms) — 8 minutes
Read Text 1A (Biased) and Text 1B (Balanced) aloud as a class. Model how to annotate using the two-colour highlighter system.
- **Biased Text 1A:** *"Students are dragged into outdated, suffocating uniforms that crush individuality and drain parents' wallets..."*
- **Balanced Text 1B:** *"While school uniforms can promote equality and reduce social pressure, some families express concern over initial costs..."*
- **Student action:** Annotate Text 1A and 1B in pairs. Highlight loaded language yellow and balanced evidence green.
- **Finished when:** Students complete the 3-column analysis grid on Topic A in their organiser.

### 4. Reading Station / Paired Analysis (Topics B & C) — 15 minutes
Students work in pairs to analyze two contrasting text sets:
- **Topic B:** *E-Scooters on City Footpaths* (Text 2A Biased vs Text 2B Balanced).
- **Topic C:** *Zoos & Wildlife Parks* (Text 3A Biased vs Text 3B Balanced).
- **Student action:** Read, highlight, and complete the Bias Detection Matrix for both topics.
- **Finished when:** Organiser matrix is populated with specific evidence quotes and identified techniques for both topics.
- **Teacher conference priority:** Check student pairs who confuse strong opinion with unsupported bias, or who miss modal qualifiers in balanced texts.

### 5. Spotting the Hidden Bias Techniques — 6 minutes
Display 4 short challenge snippets on the presentation. Conduct a rapid-fire whole-class classification game ("Biased", "Balanced", or "Hidden Omission").
- **Student action:** Whiteboard or hand-signal voting with instant justification.
- **Finished when:** Students correctly spot omission bias (leaving out relevant counter-facts).

### 6. Pre-Writing Planning — 5 minutes
Students select one topic (from Topic A, B, C, or Topic D: *AI Homework Tools*) for their dual-paragraph writing task. They complete the pre-writing planner in their organiser.
- **Student action:** Fill out the word bank: list 3 loaded/emotive words for the biased paragraph, and 3 neutral/modal words for the balanced paragraph.
- **Finished when:** Both word banks are filled before drafting begins.

### 7. Drafting Task 1: The Biased Paragraph — 8 minutes
Students compose a 4–5 sentence paragraph that deliberately uses bias techniques to convince the reader to take a single-sided stance.
- **Student action:** Independent writing in English workbooks.
- **Finished when:** 4–5 sentences drafted including at least 2 loaded words and 1 absolute claim.
- **Sentence launches:**
  - *"It is an absolute outrage that..."*
  - *"Every sensible person knows that..."*
  - *"No one can deny the destructive impact of..."*

### 8. Drafting Task 2: The Balanced Paragraph — 8 minutes
Students rewrite their stance on the exact same topic into a 4–5 sentence balanced, objective paragraph.
- **Student action:** Independent writing in English workbooks.
- **Finished when:** 4–5 sentences drafted including 2 modal qualifiers (*may*, *could*, *suggests*) and an explicit counter-perspective.
- **Sentence launches:**
  - *"While supporters argue that..., opponents raise valid concerns regarding..."*
  - *"Evidence suggests that..., although further research is needed to..."*
  - *"A balanced view considers both the benefits of... and the potential risks of..."*

### 9. Peer Analysis & Revision — 5 minutes
Pairs swap workbooks and use the **Bias Checker Criteria**:
- Yellow highlight: Spot any unintended bias remaining in the partner's "Balanced" paragraph.
- Green highlight: Identify the strongest modal qualifier used.
- **Student action:** Read partner text and visibly revise 1 sentence in their own workbook.
- **Finished when:** 1 visible edit made to improve balance or sharpen intentional bias.

### 10. Exit Evidence — 2 minutes
Students complete the exit reflection on a sticky note or in their organiser:
> *"The single most effective word choice that changed my paragraph from biased to balanced was changing [Word A] to [Word B] because..."*
- **Student action:** Independent submission.

## Optional depth

### Depth A — The Media Bias Audit (8 minutes)
Display a short news article excerpt or advertisement. Students conduct a 5-point audit evaluating: Author credentials, Headline tone, Omitted voices, Use of statistics, and Visual framing.

### Depth B — Visual Bias Deconstruction (7 minutes)
Students compare two photographs of the same event taken from different angles with different lighting and crop choices. They analyze how visual media creates bias without using words.

## Model responses

### Model Biased Paragraph (Topic B: E-Scooters)
> It is an absolute nightmare that cities are allowing dangerous e-scooter joyriders to terrorise innocent pedestrians on our footpaths. These silent, high-speed machines are recklessly abandoned everywhere, cluttering our streets and causing total chaos for families trying to enjoy a simple walk. Reckless riders zoom past vulnerable elderly citizens with zero regard for basic safety or common decency. Local councils must immediately ban these menace vehicles before a catastrophic disaster occurs!

### Model Balanced Paragraph (Topic B: E-Scooters)
> Electric scooters offer an efficient, low-emission transport option for city commuters seeking to reduce urban traffic congestion. Proponents highlight that e-scooter sharing schemes provide affordable micro-mobility and support local eco-friendly travel initiatives. However, urban safety groups raise valid concerns regarding footpath clutter, pedestrian safety, and uneven helmet enforcement. To manage these competing needs, many councils are testing designated parking zones and geo-fenced speed limits to evaluate whether e-scooters can safely share public space.

## Support & Lucas (ICP) pathway

- Use `Lesson_21_Lucas_Pack.md` featuring simplified text snippets, color-coded sentence starters, and icon-assisted bias spotters.
- Provide a pre-filled vocabulary bank (e.g. Loaded: *terrible, brilliant, ruined, perfect* vs Neutral: *reported, states, suggests, considers*).
- Allow oral rehearsal or paired sentence composition prior to writing.
- Accept a 3-sentence biased and 3-sentence balanced response for ICP students.

## Accuracy, discussion boundaries & ethical considerations

- Maintain focus on text construction and linguistic analysis rather than debating personal political or social beliefs.
- Ensure all reading passages provide balanced educational context and avoid harmful stereotypes.
- Encourage critical thinking regarding media consumption without promoting total cynicism or distrust of authoritative sources.
