The Text Toolkit

Step 2: Toolkit
⚠️ 4 Spotters of Bias
  • 🔴 1. Emotive Vocabulary: outrage, terrible, heroic, nightmare
  • 🔴 2. Loaded Verbs: forced, dragged, dumped, terrorise
  • 🔴 3. Absolute Claims: everyone knows, always, never, completely
  • 🔴 4. Single-Sided Evidence: Omitting counter-arguments or trade-offs
⚖️ 4 Spotters of Balance
  • 🟢 1. Neutral Tone: states, indicates, reports, considers
  • 🟢 2. Multi-Perspective: Acknowledges counter-arguments fairly
  • 🟢 3. Modal Qualifiers: may, could, suggests, tends to
  • 🟢 4. Transparent Limits: Naming uncertainties or data bounds
⚠️ Angry / One Side
  • 🔴 Uses ANGRY or big words (terrible, ruined, forced)
  • 🔴 Tells ONLY ONE SIDE
  • 🔴 Says "ALWAYS" or "NEVER"
⚖️ Calm / Both Sides
  • 🟢 Uses CALM words (suggests, reports, can, could)
  • 🟢 Shows BOTH SIDES fairly
  • 🟢 Says "SOME" or "MIGHT"

Guided Analysis: Topic A (School Uniforms)

Step 3: Model
Text 1A: Youth Magazine Column
💻 The Teen Voice Online (Opinion Column by Marcus Vance)

"It is an absolute outrage that kids are forced into dull uniforms that completely crush creativity and drain parents' wallets."

• Absolute claim: "completely crush"
• Single-sided: Ignores equity and morning ease.
Text 1B: Educational Research Feature
📚 Australian Education Digest (Feature by Dr. Rostova)

"While uniforms can promote equality, some parents express concern over initial costs. Studies suggest flexible options help."

• Modal verb: "can promote", "suggest"
• Multi-sided: Balances equity vs cost.
Snippet 1A (One Side)

"Strict uniforms are terrible! They force kids into uncomfortable clothes and destroy all fun!"

👉 Angry words: terrible, force, destroy
Snippet 1B (Both Sides)

"Uniforms can help students feel equal, but some parents raise concerns about clothing costs."

👉 Calm words: can help, raise concerns

Dual-Paragraph Writing Task

Steps 6–8: Writing
Paragraph 1: Biased (4–5 sentences)

Goal: Intentionally write a single-sided opinion piece using authentic bias techniques.

  • Use at least 3 loaded/emotive words
  • Include an absolute claim (must, always, everyone)
  • Ignore counter-evidence
Paragraph 2: Balanced (4–5 sentences)

Goal: Rewrite the exact same topic into an objective advisory or journal paragraph.

  • Use neutral, reporting verbs (indicates, states)
  • Include 2 modal qualifiers (may, suggests)
  • Represent both sides fairly
Paragraph 1: One Side (3 sentences)

1. "It is a complete disaster that..."

2. "This is terrible because it ruins..."

3. "Everyone knows we must stop this!"

Paragraph 2: Both Sides (3 sentences)

1. "While some people think that..."

2. "others raise concerns about..."

3. "Therefore, evidence suggests we could..."

Slide 6 Notes

Circulate to ensure students shift vocabulary between Paragraph 1 and Paragraph 2.

Exemplar Writing Contrast

Model Writing
Exemplar Biased (Letter to Editor)

"It is an absolute nightmare that cities allow dangerous joyriders to terrorise footpaths. These machines are recklessly abandoned everywhere, creating total chaos. Councils must immediately ban them before a disaster occurs!"

Exemplar Balanced (Council Report)

"Electric scooters offer an efficient option for city commuters. While advocates highlight reduced traffic, safety reports indicate concerns regarding footpath clutter. Councils are testing speed limiters to evaluate safe sharing."

Model 1: One-Sided (Angry)

"Scooters are a total nightmare on footpaths! Careless riders ruin every walk. Councils must ban them now!"

Model 2: Both Sides (Calm)

"Scooters can help people travel fast. While riders should be careful, evidence suggests safe parking zones could help."

Slide 7 Notes

Highlight the contrast between aggressive verbs and calm modal qualifiers.
