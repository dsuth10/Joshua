# Lesson 20: Character Alliances

## Lesson purpose

Students compare Ari’s and Malia’s separate first-person chapters to explain how shared values can create a plausible alliance, then make an evidence-based prediction about how that connection could affect Ginger Juice’s fate.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LE03: recognise how point of view influences feelings and reader response.**
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LE01: identify responses to characters drawn from social and ethical contexts.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to explain how Ari and Malia’s different experiences can create common ground and to make a careful evidence-based prediction.

## Success criteria

- I can identify an accurate detail from Ari’s chapter and Malia’s chapter.
- I can explain a shared value or concern without claiming the characters are already allies.
- I can explain how first-person narration reveals each character’s changing understanding.
- I can write a cautious prediction using evidence and words such as *could* or *might*.
- I can revise a statement that goes beyond the text.

## Finished workbook response

A 7–9 sentence prediction explaining how a shared concern could connect Ari and Malia, and how that connection could affect Ginger Juice’s fate.

## Agreed delivery model

- Teacher controls the projected HTML presentation; student logistics are held in the Notes popup.
- Students complete the organiser and substantial composition in their English workbooks.
- The chapter is assumed to have been read. Use the fallback only when required.
- Core route: approximately 50 minutes; the two optional depth slides extend the lesson.

## Preparation

- Open Lesson_20_Presentation.html in a modern browser and choose fullscreen.
- Print both organiser versions as needed.
- Students need their English workbooks.

## Core sequence

1. **A connection needs a bridge — 4 minutes.** Pairs predict one shared value or concern.
2. **Trace how a cause becomes an alliance — 2 minutes.** Students set up a cautious prediction pathway.
3. **Notice what Ari cannot ignore — 5 minutes.** Students retrieve harm, promise and obstacle from pp. 109–113.
4. **Notice what Malia begins to understand — 5 minutes.** Students retrieve want, message and change from pp. 113–121.
5. **Same issue? Different doorway? — 5 minutes.** Pairs compare direct witness with reflection.
6. **Sort the bridge by strength — 6 minutes.** Groups distinguish defensible common ground from unsupported claims, then retry if needed.
7. **What does each narrator let us see? — 4 minutes.** Students explain a viewpoint effect for each character.
8. **Predict without pretending you know — 5 minutes.** Teacher model makes evidence, bridge reasoning and cautious language visible.
9. **Build your alliance pathway — 3 minutes.** Students plan claim, two details, shared value and prediction.
10. **Write the Character Alliances prediction — 12 minutes.** Independent 7–9 sentence response.
11. **Make the bridge strong enough to hold — 4 minutes.** Students self-check and revise one overclaim.
12. **One bridge. One possible change. — 2 minutes.** Exit evidence.

## Optional depth

- **Test a competing bridge — 7 minutes:** judge care for Ginger Juice, feeling misunderstood by adults, or learning responsibility using textual support and explanatory power.
- **Predict with a condition — 7 minutes:** write a conditional prediction without confirming later events.

## Support

- Provide the organiser’s evidence boxes before the comparison.
- Reduce the initial bridge choices; allow oral rehearsal and scribing.
- Prompt with: “Both chapters show ___, so they could ___.”

## Lucas (ICP)

- Use the separate large-print organiser.
- Preserve the destination: identify a concern, a feeling and one possible helpful action.
- Students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe; full independent reading is not required.

## Formative assessment

The sort exposes the misconception that any shared feeling proves an alliance. The planning pathway and exit sentence show whether students can connect two accurate details to a cautious, relevant prediction.

## Teacher reading fallback

In Ari’s chapter, Ginger Juice has been harmed overnight. Ari promises to get her out, but Uncle Kus cares about the restaurant’s customers and warns Ari not to interfere. In Malia’s chapter, Oma reminds her that actions and words show who a person is. Seeing a street girl leads Malia to reflect on Papa’s teaching about kindness and her own behaviour.

## Accuracy and privacy boundaries

- Use only verified details from *Berani* and the approved unit sequence.
- Do not ask students to disclose personal experiences of poverty, family conflict or animal harm.
- Accept different predictions when their evidence and cautious reasoning are sound.
