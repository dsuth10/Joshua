# Lesson 24 Alternative: The Signal Lantern

### Pedagogical Contemplation

Students synthesise three strands of a shared fictional text to make a careful, evidence-anchored prediction. The central distinction is deliberately narrow: evidence tells what is known; an inference explains what it may mean; a prediction names a possible next event. The lesson uses three short, interlocking story cards so every student has common evidence without relying on *Berani* or outside knowledge.

The opening asks students to notice what connects three people before teaching the prediction chain. A classification task exposes overconfident leaps, the explicit model makes each reasoning move visible, and the final forecast requires one detail from every strand plus a stated uncertainty. Engagement comes from solving a believable neighbourhood puzzle; it is not a substitute for the disciplined reasoning students must show.

## Lesson purpose

Students read the original fictional feature *The Signal Lantern*, connect evidence across Noor, Eli and the harbour signal, and compose a cautious 8-10 sentence forecast. They learn that a strong prediction is not a spoiler or a wish: it is a possible outcome supported by accurate details and conditions.

This alternative preserves Lesson 24's cognitive destination while using no *Berani* content.

## Curriculum alignment

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA02:** acknowledge differing ideas and opinions.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA02:** identify bias in persuasive texts.

## Learning intention

We are learning to connect evidence across a text and make a careful prediction without treating the future as certain.

## Success criteria

- I can select an accurate detail from each strand.
- I can explain a connection between details.
- I can distinguish evidence, inference and prediction.
- I can use cautious language and name a condition or uncertainty.
- I can write a forecast that does not pretend a future event has happened.

## Finished workbook response

An **8-10 sentence forecast** for a possible resolution of Noor's, Eli's and the Signal Lantern's storylines. It includes one accurate detail from each strand, at least two reasoning links, cautious prediction language and one condition.

- Open `Lesson_24_Signal_Lantern_Synthesis_Presentation.html` and choose fullscreen.
- Print the Reading Pack and Forecast Organiser; offer the Concise Access Pack as appropriate.
- Students need an English workbook or approved writing device.
- The core route is 50 minutes. Teacher logistics are in the Notes drawer.

## Core sequence (50 minutes)

1. **Three clues, one possible change (4 min):** notice an initial connection.
2. **Prediction chain (3 min):** establish evidence -> inference -> cautious prediction.
3. **Read and trace (8 min):** annotate all three strands in the original feature.
4. **What is actually known? (5 min):** sort supported pathways from overclaims.
5. **Model a careful forecast (6 min):** reveal a complete evidence-to-prediction chain.
6. **Build three arrows (5 min):** record one evidence chain per strand.
7. **Write the forecast (11 min):** compose the independent response.
8. **Turn down certainty (4 min):** revise one overconfident sentence.
9. **Exit chain (4 min):** complete one evidence -> possible outcome statement.

## Interaction matrix

| Interaction | Cognitive demand | Observable signal | Hint / feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supported or overclaim | classify against the text boundary | students can explain why an outcome is not yet confirmed | "Has the text shown that event, or only a clue that it could happen?" |
| Model reveal | name evidence, inference and prediction | students identify the job of each sentence | reveal the condition that protects the forecast |
| Forecast map | connect three strands | three evidence-to-possibility arrows | orally rehearse one arrow before writing |
| Revision | qualify certainty | one visible revised sentence | replace *will* with *could/might* only when the evidence supports a possible outcome |

## Support and concise access

- Preteach **evidence, inference, prediction, condition, forecast**.
- Read the feature aloud in sections; students may highlight one clue per strand rather than annotate every paragraph.
- Use sentence launches: "Because ___, ___ could ___." and "This may happen if ___."
- The concise pack retains the destination: choose a clue, say what it might mean, and name one possible next step. Students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe.

## Extension

- Compare two defensible forecasts and explain which has stronger support.
- Identify a missing condition that would make a prediction unreliable.
- Write a second forecast from a different character's point of view while retaining the same evidence boundary.

## Formative assessment

The classification task reveals students who confuse a plausible outcome with a confirmed event. The map and exit sentence show whether students can connect evidence to a possible future outcome and articulate why it remains uncertain. Reteach with one shared evidence chain for students who make unsupported leaps.

## Safety and factual boundary

The setting, characters, objects and events in *The Signal Lantern* are original fictional classroom material. Do not present them as local history, news or advice about real safety procedures. Students are not asked to share personal experiences of storms, emergencies or family conflict.
