# The Signal Lantern: Teacher Guide

## Text overview

At fictional Wattle Bay, Noor finds a torn map in the old signal shed; Eli has repaired the lantern mechanism but is unsure whether to show the council; and the harbour caretaker has postponed the annual lantern walk because the path is unsafe. None of these details confirms the ending. They provide evidence for careful, conditional forecasts.

## Key teaching language

- **Evidence:** what the text directly tells us.
- **Inference:** a sensible explanation suggested by evidence.
- **Prediction:** a possible future outcome.
- **Condition:** what would need to happen for the prediction to be possible.

## Model answer

Evidence: Eli repaired the lantern mechanism and Noor has found a map showing the old path. Inference: together, these clues may help the caretaker decide whether the walk can continue in a safer way. Prediction: the lantern walk could go ahead on a changed route if the council checks the path and accepts Eli's repair. This is not confirmed because the text has not shown the council's decision.

## Anticipated misconceptions

- **"It is my prediction, so it can be anything."** Return to one exact detail and ask what it makes possible.
- **"Could" makes every statement acceptable.** Cautious language softens certainty; it does not replace evidence.
- **A character's plan is an event.** A plan remains evidence of intention until the text shows it happened.

## Answers for the sort

- *Supported:* "Noor's map could help people consider a different route." 
- *Supported:* "Eli's repair may matter if the caretaker decides the lantern can be used."
- *Overclaim:* "The council has approved the walk."
- *Overclaim:* "The lantern walk is definitely happening tomorrow."

## Conferencing prompt

Ask: "Show me the exact evidence. What does it suggest? Which word keeps the future honest? What would have to happen next?"
