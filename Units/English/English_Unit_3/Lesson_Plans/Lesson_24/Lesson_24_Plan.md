# Lesson 24: Synthesis and Transition

## Lesson purpose

Students synthesise the midpoint evidence across Malia, Ari and Ginger Juice to write a careful, evidence-anchored prediction for the possible resolution of the three storylines. They explicitly distinguish prediction from a confirmed plot event.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LA02: acknowledge differing ideas and opinions.**
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LA02: identify bias in persuasive texts.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to connect evidence across storylines and make a careful prediction without treating a future event as certain.

## Success criteria

- I can select an accurate detail from each storyline.
- I can explain how Malia’s, Ari’s and Ginger Juice’s narratives are beginning to connect.
- I can separate evidence, inference and prediction.
- I can use cautious language and name an uncertainty or condition.

## Finished workbook response

A 9–11 sentence prediction for a possible resolution of Malia’s, Ari’s and Ginger Juice’s storylines, anchored in at least one accurate detail per storyline.

## Preparation

- Open Lesson_24_Presentation.html in a modern browser and choose fullscreen.
- Print the appropriate organiser; students need their English workbooks.
- Core route is 50 minutes. Teacher logistics are held only in the Notes popup.

## Core sequence

1. **What is changing? — 4 min.** Identify a possible connection between three active storylines.
2. **Prediction map — 2 min.** Establish evidence → inference → cautious prediction.
3. **Malia evidence — 5 min.** Retrieve reflection, repair and offered support.
4. **Ari evidence — 5 min.** Retrieve risk, research and intended action.
5. **Ginger Juice evidence — 5 min.** Establish urgency without confirming the next plot events.
6. **Supported arrows — 6 min.** Sort evidence-based pathways from unconfirmed claims.
7. **Model uncertainty — 5 min.** Separate evidence, inference and prediction.
8. **Build the forecast — 3 min.** Plan all three evidence chains.
9. **Write the midpoint prediction — 12 min.** Compose the independent 9–11 sentence response.
10. **Separate know from predict — 4 min.** Revise one certainty.
11. **Exit evidence — 2 min.** Complete an evidence-to-forecast chain.

## Support

- Supply evidence boxes and sentence stems: “Because ___, ___ could ___.”
- Rehearse one evidence chain orally before writing.
- Let students use the organiser to group the three storylines before composing.

## Lucas (ICP)

- Use the low-writing organiser; students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe.
- Preserve the destination: identify a person who can help and one possible helpful next step.

## Formative assessment

The arrow sort exposes claims that leap past the text. The exit sentence reveals whether students can anchor a possible future event in evidence and name why it remains uncertain.

## Teacher reading boundary

Use pp. 125–145 as the evidence base. The next Ginger Juice chapter begins pp. 147–149; it may establish urgency but must not be taught as a confirmed resolution. Do not reveal events that occur after this midpoint.

## Accuracy and discussion boundaries

- Use only verified *Berani* details named in the sequence.
- Accept varied predictions where evidence, reasoning and cautious language are sound.
- Do not ask students to disclose personal experience of animal harm, family conflict or activism.
