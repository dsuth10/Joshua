# Lesson 32: Drafting — Counterargument & Conclusion

## Lesson purpose

Students complete their individual persuasive assessment draft by acknowledging a fair opposing view, responding with precise and checked reasoning, and writing a conclusion that returns to the opinion, makes a believable future visible and calls the identified audience to action.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** create and edit persuasive texts developing and expanding ideas with supporting details.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create persuasive texts using paragraphs, complex sentences and expanded noun groups.

## Learning intention

We are learning to respond fairly to a concern and finish a persuasive text with a clear call to action.

## Success criteria

- I can state a reasonable opposing view fairly.
- I can respond with a precise reason, plan or checked information.
- I can avoid inventing evidence and label an unfinished claim **needs checking**.
- I can conclude by returning to my opinion, naming a believable future and requesting an action.
- I can revise immediately after reader feedback.

## Assessment alignment

Part A is an **individual** 200–400 word (suggested) written persuasive text for an identified audience. It uses supporting details, paragraphs and language choices appropriate to purpose. It draws information from sources and takes account of differing views. Part B is an individual 1–3 minute (suggested) presentation, to be prepared from the written draft in later lessons.

## Preparation

- Open the standalone deck; all teacher logistics appear only in its Notes popup.
- Provide organisers, the students’ Lesson 28 plans, current introduction and PEEL paragraphs, and checked research notes.
- Display the supplied Year 5 and Year 6 Storytime Dog model counterarguments as structural examples only.

## Core sequence (50 min)

1. Trust decision — 3 min
2. Drafting route — 3 min
3. Compare responses — 4 min
4. Annotate Year 5 model structure — 6 min
5. Compare Year 5/6 qualification — 5 min
6. Sentence-job sort — 5 min
7. Rebuttal planning — 5 min
8. Conclusion structure — 4 min
9. Independent ending studio — 12 min
10. Reader test and immediate revision — 5 min
11. Exit check — 3 min

## Model and evidence boundary

The Storytime Dog allergies/distractions examples demonstrate the structure of a counterargument and rebuttal only. They are not research sources, and students must not reuse them as evidence or present them as facts. Students rely on their own identified, accurate sources; where evidence is incomplete, they record **needs checking**.

## Differentiation

**Support:** offer counterargument/rebuttal and return/future/action stems; orally rehearse before writing; focus on one fair concern and one clear action.

**Lucas (ICP):** students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe to communicate one concern, one response, one positive future and one action. The core destination remains a purposeful ending for an audience.

**Extend:** acknowledge more than one relevant concern, qualify a response with a carefully chosen conditional clause, and make a precise judgement without overstating certainty.

## Formative assessment

The comparison and sort reveal whether students can distinguish a fair concern from a dismissal, response and conclusion. The underlined action and visible revision provide conference evidence for the next editing and spoken-preparation sequence.

## Safety guidance

Use negotiated, school/community-safe topics. Do not ask for personal disclosure. Do not invent facts, expert views, quotations, statistics, source details or guaranteed outcomes.
