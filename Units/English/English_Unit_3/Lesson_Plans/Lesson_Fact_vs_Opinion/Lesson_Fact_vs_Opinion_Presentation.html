Fact versus Opinion

Persuasion and Truth in *Berani* (Chapters 1–3)

Learning Intention: Distinguish between objective facts and subjective opinions within persuasive texts.

Year 5

Move beyond bare assertions by using authoritative evidence.

Year 6

Identify objective/subjective language and locate bias.

Goal: Set the context of the lesson.

Explain that today we are separating what can be proven from what is felt, and learning how combining both makes writing persuasive without making bare assertions.

Facts vs. Opinions: The Rules

What is a Fact?

A statement that can be proven true or false using evidence, measurement, scientific research, or observation.

  • Does not change based on who says it.
  • "Fossil remains of stegodons have been found on islands here in Indonesia."

What is an Opinion?

A statement of personal belief, feeling, value, or moral judgment that cannot be proven scientifically.

  • Can change from person to person.
  • "A mini-elephant would make the best family pet."
Critical Rule: Opinions explain why we care, but facts provide the proof. Without facts, your argument is just a bare assertion.

Have students brainstorm a quick general fact and opinion. Write them on the whiteboard to demonstrate.

Emphasise: "The sky is blue" is a fact. "Blue is the prettiest colour" is an opinion.

Guided Practice: Exploring *Berani*

Let's examine how the characters in the first three chapters use both facts and opinions:

Malia's Chapter (Persuasive Activism)

"A creature that shares nearly 97 per cent of our human DNA... is living in the treetops."

👉 Fact: DNA percentage is scientifically verifiable. Malia uses this fact to build authority before presenting her opinion that saving them is a duty.

Ari's Chapter (Understanding Bias)

"Uncle says that she is lucky he got her when she was a baby... safe from poachers."

👉 Opinion: Uncle Kus believes the cage is good and she is "lucky" (reflects his financial bias as restaurant owner). Ari sees the cage and holds the opinion that she is trapped.

Ginger Juice's Chapter (Sensory Voice)

"Fat raindrops tap, tap, tap on fingers. The small male human comes to sweep cage."

👉 Subjective Observation: Ginger Juice describes Ari as "mongoose" or "slow loris". This is her subjective perspective, mapping animal imagery to human features.

Point out to students that Ari's uncle's opinion "she is lucky" is a way of hiding the fact that she is in a small cage.

Ask: "How does this create bias?" (The uncle uses his opinion to justify a business interest).

Writing Task: Balanced Persuasion

Your written persuasive text needs to balance facts and opinions. Writing without facts creates bare assertions; writing without opinions has no passion.

Writing Challenge:

Write a short paragraph persuading readers that wild animals should not be kept in cages at restaurants.

  • Include at least 2 facts from Ginger Juice's chapters (strength, treetop nests, cage size).
  • Include at least 2 opinions expressing moral judgements.
  • Underline your facts with a green pen, and highlight opinions in yellow.

Writing Checklist:

"Like a bridge connecting students to learning, the dog help remove emotional barriers..." - See Model Responses for styles.

Walk around the room during the writing task. Check that students are not making bare assertions (e.g. "keeping animals is bad" without giving facts about orangutan needs).

Exit Ticket: Quick CFU

Exit Ticket

Write the correct letter (A, B, C, or D) on your whiteboard!

Which of the following is a subjective OPINION?

A. Stegodons are fossil dwarf elephants found in Indonesia.
B. Orangutans share 97 per cent of our DNA.
C. Keeping Ginger Juice in a cage is a betrayal of a friend.
D. Warung Malang restaurant is owned by Ari's uncle.

Correct Answer: C. This is Ari's subjective moral judgement. A, B, and D are verifiable facts.

Formative check of the entire classroom. Check how many students held up 'C'.
