Open with a reason to listen.

A strong persuasive introduction earns attention, gives just enough context and makes a clear promise: this is what I want you to do.

Introducing a Storytime Dog program at our school would not simply be an exciting initiative; it would be a powerful educational investment that could improve literacy outcomes, strengthen wellbeing and foster a more inclusive learning environment.

THESIS

A thesis is a clear, arguable promise.

4 min
IDENTIFIED AUDIENCE + should / should not + ACTION + because + 2–3 directions

✓ names what should happen

✓ fits the audience

✓ can be supported with checked reasons

✗ is not a vague wish or unsupported fact

BUILD

Build before you draft.

5 min
1. Hook:Question, imagined moment, striking value or relevant contrast2. Background:What does this audience need to know?3. Thesis:What should this audience do, and why?

Choose a hook that suits your audience. A dramatic hook is not automatically persuasive.

STUDIO

Draft your original introduction

12 min

Writing studio • 12 minutes

  1. Write a hook for your real audience.
  2. Add only the background needed to understand the action.
  3. Write a precise thesis with your position and directions.
  4. Underline your thesis. Circle any claim that needs evidence later.

WRITERRevise one word, sentence or missing job.

If your reader cannot name the action, strengthen the thesis.
