# Lesson 19: The Ethics of Capture

## Lesson purpose

Students use Ari’s attempted enrichment activity and Ginger Juice’s first-person experience to write a fair, evidence-based persuasive appeal about whether Uncle Kus has a right to keep Ginger Juice as a restaurant attraction.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LE03:** recognise how point of view influences reader response.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LE01:** identify and explain responses to characters drawn from social and ethical contexts.

## Learning intention

We are learning to make an ethical claim about captivity using precise textual evidence and fair reasoning.

## Success criteria

- I can distinguish care, control and a situation that contains both.
- I can explain what Ari’s attempted enrichment reveals.
- I can use Ginger Juice’s point of view to explain a reader response.
- I can select evidence that supports an ethical claim.
- I can acknowledge a counterview and write a direct persuasive appeal.

## Finished workbook response

An 8–10 sentence persuasive appeal arguing whether Uncle Kus has a right to keep Ginger Juice for business.

## Agreed delivery model

- Teacher controls the projected HTML presentation.
- Students complete the organiser and substantial composition in physical English workbooks.
- The deck contains no student composition fields.
- The chapter is assumed to have been read; use the fallback only when required.
- Core route: approximately 50 minutes. Optional depth can extend the lesson towards 70 minutes.

## Preparation

- Open `Lesson_19_Presentation.html` in a modern browser and select fullscreen.
- Print `Lesson_19_Organiser.docx` and use the Lucas organiser where appropriate.
- Students need their English workbook and pasted organiser.

## Core sequence

1. **Who gets to decide what care looks like? — 3 min.** Students make a provisional ethical question from the tension between food/safety and confinement.
2. **Care, control, or both? — 4 min.** Pairs classify evidence and state a rule.
3. **Build an ethical appeal — 2 min.** Rehearse an arguable position.
4. **Ari tries to help — 5 min.** Map action, intention and limitation from pp. 103–107.
5. **When enrichment is too small an answer — 4 min.** Place Ari’s attempt on a continuum and justify it.
6. **Read for what Ginger Juice cannot do — 5 min.** Select a precise detail from pp. 107–109 and explain the captivity effect.
7. **Why give this scene to Ginger Juice? — 4 min.** Explain the point-of-view effect.
8. **Evidence test — 4 min.** Choose the two details that best carry an ethical claim.
9. **Annotated model — 5 min.** Identify position, evidence/reasoning, fair counterview and appeal.
10. **Plan — 3 min.** Complete the five-move appeal plan.
11. **Write — 13 min.** Compose the 8–10 sentence appeal in the workbook.
12. **Self-check — 3 min.** Revise one sentence that only retells.
13. **Exit evidence — 2 min.** Complete the evidence-to-appeal chain.

## Optional depth

- **Does intention change the ethical judgement?** Distinguish Uncle’s possible intention from the impact on Ginger Juice.
- **Change the audience, change the appeal.** Reframe a claim for Uncle Kus or restaurant patrons.

## Support

Offer the shared claim: “Uncle Kus should not keep Ginger Juice as a restaurant attraction because …”; reduce the initial evidence choices; permit oral rehearsal; conference first with students who name details but do not explain their significance.

## Lucas (ICP)

Use the separate organiser. Preserve the same destination through picture-supported choices, speaking, pointing, copying or scribing. Do not require independent reading of the full extract.

## Extension

Use Depth A and B. Require a more precise distinction between intention and impact, or adaptation for audience, rather than more volume.

## Teacher reading fallback

Ari spends his coffee money on paint supplies and tries an enrichment activity with Ginger Juice. She briefly tears the paper, then loses interest. Uncle laughs, calls her a “dumb creature” and says he knows what is best. That night, visitors splash alcohol over Ginger Juice and burn her hand; her narration describes fear, pain and having nowhere to hide.

## Accuracy and privacy boundaries

Use only verified details from *Berani* pp. 103–109 and the unit sequence. Do not ask students to disclose personal experiences of harm or captivity. Accept defensible ethical judgements grounded in the text.
