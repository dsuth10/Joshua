# English Unit 3 — Lessons 10–14 Structural Blueprint

## Status and purpose

This is a design blueprint for review before lesson files are built. It covers:

- Lesson 10 — Tenses and Sentence Complexity
- Lesson 11 — Specialist and Topic-Specific Terms
- Lesson 12 — Simile, Metaphor and Empathy
- Lesson 13 — Expanded Noun Groups, Verbs and Adverbs
- Lesson 14 — Comparing Contrasting Perspectives

No presentation, lesson plan or organiser should be treated as final until this blueprint is approved.

## Decisions already agreed

- Each lesson is self-contained. Connections across the five lessons come from the full Unit 3 sequence rather than a compulsory cumulative product.
- Delivery is hybrid:
  - the teacher controls the projected HTML presentation;
  - students discuss and make decisions from the projected material;
  - students record, plan, compose and revise in physical English workbooks.
- The presentation does not contain student composition fields.
- Each lesson has:
  - one small printable standard organiser to paste into the workbook;
  - one separate Lucas/ICP organiser;
  - a 50-minute core route;
  - clearly marked optional depth slides that can extend the lesson towards 70 minutes;
  - a visible safe stopping point after the completed response.
- The chapters are assumed to have already been read. Each lesson begins with retrieval and uses a small number of key quotations or accurate paraphrases.
- The teacher notes include a concise reading fallback for absent students or incomplete prior reading.
- Revision is primarily independent. Students use a projected, lesson-specific self-check before selected examples are discussed.
- All five decks use the Unit 3 presentation system:
  - consistent navigation, progress, notes, timer, reset and workbook cues;
  - the same rainforest–orangutan hero image on Slide 1;
  - a distinct internal visual metaphor and accent colour for each lesson.

## Common presentation system

### Hero slide

Use:

`Lesson_Plans/Lesson_5/assets/rainforest-orangutan-hero.png`

Composition:

- preserve the orangutan on the right;
- place lesson number, title and one short student-facing premise on the left;
- apply the established deep-forest gradient behind the title;
- do not place learning intentions, curriculum codes or administrative directions on the hero.

### Visible student instruction panel

Every slide that requires student action displays:

- **DO:** one explicit action;
- **WORK:** class, pair or independent;
- **RECORD:** exact workbook or organiser location;
- **TIME:** visible time allowance;
- **FINISH:** observable completion point;
- **CHECK:** no more than three success conditions.

The copy should be brief enough to read from the back of the room. Longer examples, answers and prompts belong in teacher notes.

### Hidden teacher-note pattern

Each action slide includes:

1. purpose of the activity;
2. suggested teacher language;
3. expected or defensible responses;
4. misconception to watch for;
5. Lucas/support/extension adjustment where relevant;
6. transition to the next slide;
7. what to omit on the 50-minute route;
8. short reading fallback where required.

### Core and optional slide labels

- **CORE** slides are required for the finished lesson response.
- **DEPTH** slides are optional and clearly marked in the progress display.
- The teacher can skip a DEPTH slide without breaking the instructional sequence.
- The final response and self-check are never optional.

### Workbook cue

Use one consistent workbook icon and a short label:

- `ORGANISER` — record on the pasted organiser;
- `WORKBOOK` — write on the facing workbook page;
- `SELF-CHECK` — reread and revise the workbook response.

## Shared lesson architecture

The exact number of slides may vary, but every lesson follows this logic:

1. Compelling hero/entry.
2. Retrieval from prior reading.
3. Student-facing purpose and finished-product preview.
4. Main interactive investigation.
5. Explicit model that exposes a writer’s decisions.
6. Independent workbook construction.
7. Lesson-specific self-check and immediate revision.
8. Selected examples and exit evidence.
9. Optional depth activity where it strengthens rather than interrupts the core route.

# Lesson 10 — Building Bridges Through Time

## Authoritative sequence focus

- Review main and dependent clauses.
- Retrieve Ari, pages 49–51, and compare more subtle past/present movement in Malia, pages 53–57.
- Use subordinating conjunctions such as **although**, **since** and **while**.
- Control past and present tense.

## Finished workbook response

Students write a 5–7 sentence **Then–Now character paragraph** about Ari.

The paragraph must:

- establish Ari’s present recognition that Ginger Juice’s cage is too small;
- connect that recognition to their earlier relationship;
- include at least three complex sentences;
- use **although**, **since** and/or **while** purposefully;
- maintain controlled tense;
- explain rather than merely retell.

## Visual metaphor

**Bridge spans and time banks**

- Past evidence sits on one side of the bridge.
- Present evidence sits on the other.
- Conjunctions function as bridge designs that express contrast, cause or simultaneity.
- Accent colour: river blue with amber bridge markers.

## 50-minute core route

| Slide | Time | Purpose and projected experience | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero — *Building Bridges Through Time* | 2 min | The hero premise asks: “How can one sentence hold what happened then and what Ari understands now?” | Think silently; offer one initial possibility orally. |
| 2. Retrieval — *Then / Now* | 4 min | Display six accurate details from Ari’s chapter, initially without category labels. | In pairs, mark each detail **Then** or **Now** in the organiser. Finish by circling the detail that best explains Ari’s change. |
| 3. Mission — *Build a relationship, not a timeline* | 2 min | Preview the final paragraph and publish the difference between chronology and explanation. | Restate the finished product to a partner: “I will connect ___ from the past to ___ in the present.” |
| 4. Then–Now Bridge Board | 7 min | Project the two time banks. Reveal one detail at a time only after students have placed it. | Complete the organiser’s two-column evidence bank. Draw two possible bridges between related details. |
| 5. Conjunction Bridge Test | 5 min | Test **although**, **since** and **while** between the same clause pair. Show that each creates a different relationship. | Write two versions of one bridge sentence. Underline the conjunction and label its job: **contrast**, **cause/reason** or **time happening together**. |
| 6. Sentence Surgery | 6 min | Reveal three flawed sentences: one fragment, one illogical conjunction and one uncontrolled tense shift. | Diagnose each fault in the organiser, repair the sentence and identify what changed. Teacher reveals and compares defensible revisions. |
| 7. Annotated model — *Ari, then and now* | 5 min | Show the full 5–7 sentence model first. Then reveal present situation, past evidence, clause relationships and controlled tense one at a time. | Predict each sentence’s job before the label is revealed. Star one model decision to imitate. |
| 8. Independent writing | 12 min | Project the exact task, requirements, optional launches and silent-writing timer. | Write the Then–Now paragraph on the facing workbook page. Use the organiser, but do not copy its notes mechanically. |
| 9. Self-check — *Bridge inspection* | 4 min | Display the lesson-specific inspection checklist. | Independently reread, mark and revise at least one sentence. |
| 10. Exit evidence — *Strongest bridge* | 3 min | Invite two selected examples, then collect one diagnostic line. This is the safe stopping point. | Box the strongest complex sentence. Beneath it, write: “This conjunction shows ___.” |

Core total: approximately 50 minutes.

## Optional depth slides

### DEPTH A — Malia’s subtler time bridge (8–10 minutes)

Students compare a present reaction with an earlier identity experience from Malia’s extract. They explain why her time movement is less obvious than Ari’s.

### DEPTH B — One idea, three conjunctions (6–8 minutes)

Students rewrite one Ari clause pair using **although**, **since** and **while**, then explain which version best fits the intended meaning.

## Standard organiser

One compact A4 organiser designed to be pasted into a workbook:

1. **Then bank** — three short evidence spaces.
2. **Now bank** — three short evidence spaces.
3. **Two bridge lines** — clause A + conjunction + clause B.
4. **Sentence Surgery** — three small diagnose/repair rows.
5. **Paragraph plan strip**:
   - present situation;
   - earlier relationship;
   - what changed;
   - what Ari still misunderstands.

The organiser does not contain a full-page writing box.

## Lucas/ICP organiser

Preserve the core concept: the past affects what Ari thinks and does now.

- Three large visual stages:
  1. Ari and young Ginger Juice together;
  2. Ginger Juice grows;
  3. Ari sees the cage is too small.
- Students order the stages and choose **then** or **now** labels.
- Provide two joining choices: **because** and **but**.
- Supported response:
  - “Then Ari played with Ginger Juice.”
  - “Now she is big.”
  - “Ari knows the cage is too small.”
- Accept ordering, pointing, speaking, copying or partner scribing.

## Lesson 10 self-check

Students silently confirm:

- I clearly showed **then** and **now**.
- My conjunction expresses the relationship I intended.
- My verbs do not drift accidentally between past and present.
- I used evidence about Ari rather than inventing an event.
- I revised at least one sentence.

## Teacher reading fallback

Briefly establish:

- Ari remembers Ginger Juice as a playful young orangutan treated like a family member.
- In the present, he recognises that her cage is now much too small.
- He still believes a larger cage may solve the problem.

Use two short quotations or accurate paraphrases from pages 49–51. Do not reread the whole chapter.

# Lesson 11 — Words With Exact Jobs

## Authoritative sequence focus

- Explore how precise, topic-specific and culturally situated vocabulary contributes meaning.
- Use a curated cross-text set:
  - **bule** — cultural identity and positioning;
  - **galvanise** — purposeful mobilisation of support;
  - **strategise** — deliberate planning;
  - **repercussions** — serious consequences that follow an action.

## Finished workbook response

Students complete a **Precision Rewrite**.

They revise a deliberately vague paragraph about Malia’s campaign using the four target terms, making any grammatical changes required. They then justify two choices:

> I replaced ___ with ___ because ___.

The purpose is deliberate selection, not inserting difficult words wherever they fit grammatically.

## Visual metaphor

**Precision lenses and focal rings**

- Broad words appear blurred.
- Precise words bring a specific aspect of meaning into focus.
- Accent colour: teal with sharp coral focus markers.

## 50-minute core route

| Slide | Time | Purpose and projected experience | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero — *Words With Exact Jobs* | 2 min | Premise: “A near-synonym can be close and still be wrong.” | Consider which word matters more: *result* or *repercussion*. Do not decide yet. |
| 2. Retrieval — *Where did these words work?* | 4 min | Present four short contextual clues from earlier Ari and Malia chapters. | Match each clue to a target word in the organiser and underline the evidence that guided the choice. |
| 3. Mission — *Choose for meaning, not difficulty* | 2 min | Preview the Precision Rewrite and publish the four word-choice checks: meaning, tone, context and grammar. | Restate one check to a partner. |
| 4. Word Fit Lab 1 — *bule / galvanise* | 7 min | Conceal the original word in two authentic contexts. Offer several plausible alternatives. Reveal only after justification. | Choose the best fit, record it and complete: “This word adds ___ that the alternative misses.” |
| 5. Word Fit Lab 2 — *strategise / repercussions* | 7 min | Repeat the decision with planning and consequence contexts. Include a plausible near-synonym rather than an obvious wrong answer. | Record and justify both selections. |
| 6. Precision test — *Can the word travel?* | 4 min | Show a new sentence for each word. Some uses are grammatical but contextually wrong. | Mark **fits / does not fit** and give one reason. |
| 7. Annotated model — *From vague to exact* | 5 min | Show a short vague paragraph, then the revised version. Reveal replacements, grammatical changes and meaning gains. | Predict which vague phrases need replacing before the revision appears. Star one change that improves more than vocabulary alone. |
| 8. Independent Precision Rewrite | 10 min | Project the vague paragraph, exact requirements and silent timer. | Rewrite the paragraph on the facing workbook page using all four terms accurately. Make other sentence changes as needed. |
| 9. Justify two choices | 4 min | Display the justification frame and one weak explanation to avoid: “because it is a better word”. | Annotate two replacements with specific contextual reasons. |
| 10. Self-check — *Bring every word into focus* | 3 min | Project the precision checklist. | Silently revise at least one word or surrounding phrase. |
| 11. Exit evidence — *The near-synonym that fails* | 2 min | Discuss one selected example. Safe stopping point. | Write one target word, a near-synonym and why the near-synonym is weaker here. |

Core total: approximately 50 minutes.

## Draft vague paragraph for the organiser

The final built resource may refine the wording, but its logic should remain:

> Some people treated Malia like an outsider. She made a plan to get people to support her campaign. Her actions caused serious results for other people.

The rewrite should naturally support:

- *bule* as a culturally situated label rather than a generic synonym for outsider;
- *strategise* as deliberate planning;
- *galvanise support* as mobilising others;
- *repercussions* as serious consequences.

## Optional depth slides

### DEPTH A — Register shift (8–10 minutes)

Students compare how the four terms operate in an informal message and a formal analytical paragraph. They revise one sentence for a named audience.

### DEPTH B — Cultural word caution (6–8 minutes)

Students explain why translating *bule* simply as “foreigner” loses contextual meaning. The teacher keeps the discussion text-based and does not ask students to disclose personal experiences of identity labels.

## Standard organiser

1. Four **Word Fit Lab** rows:
   - context clue;
   - selected word;
   - plausible alternative;
   - what the selected word adds.
2. The deliberately vague paragraph.
3. Two short annotation frames for justification.
4. A small “word does not travel everywhere” reminder.

## Lucas/ICP organiser

Preserve the core concept: a more exact word helps a reader understand.

- Use large-print picture-supported pairs:
  - **thinks / plans**;
  - **asks / encourages people to help**;
  - **something happened / a serious result followed**.
- Lucas selects the more exact word or phrase for two illustrated situations.
- Supported rewrite:
  - “Malia makes a plan.”
  - “She asks people to help.”
  - “Her choice causes a serious problem.”
- Orally introduce the class target words without requiring independent spelling.
- Accept pointing, circling, speaking, copying or scribing.

## Lesson 11 self-check

- Every target word fits the exact context.
- I changed the surrounding grammar when the new word required it.
- My rewrite still sounds natural.
- My two explanations identify a meaning or tone difference.
- I did more than say “it is a better word”.

## Teacher reading fallback

Briefly establish:

- Malia experiences *bule* as a label that positions her as an outsider.
- She tries to *galvanise support* for her petition.
- Ari *strategises* before approaching Uncle.
- Malia’s choice creates serious *repercussions* for herself and Mrs Harwono.

# Lesson 12 — Crossing Into Danger

## Authoritative sequence focus

- Analyse imagery in Ginger Juice’s perspective, pages 65–69.
- Examine how simile, metaphor/personification, sensory detail and fragmented syntax position the reader.
- Write from an animal’s perspective without adding unsupported human knowledge.

## Finished workbook response

Students write an 8–10 sentence first-person scene titled **Crossing Into Danger**.

The scene begins as Ginger Juice and Ibu leave the treetops, cross burned ground and approach the human food market.

It must include:

- sensory evidence grounded in the extract;
- Ginger Juice’s limited knowledge and animal perspective;
- two purposeful figurative choices;
- a clear movement from safety towards danger;
- an emotional effect created through detail, not a list of feeling words.

## Visual metaphor

**Three danger thresholds**

1. treetop edge;
2. burned ground;
3. human market boundary.

Accent colour shifts from canopy green to ash grey to warning orange.

## 50-minute core route

| Slide | Time | Purpose and projected experience | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero — *Crossing Into Danger* | 2 min | Premise: “Describe only what Ginger Juice can sense, remember and understand.” | Predict which sense would warn her first. |
| 2. Retrieval — *Three thresholds* | 4 min | Show the three locations without explanatory text. | Order the thresholds in the organiser and add one remembered detail to each. |
| 3. Mission — *Make the reader experience the crossing* | 2 min | Preview the final scene and publish the distinction between imagery and decorative comparison. | Restate the writing boundary: “I cannot give Ginger Juice knowledge she does not have.” |
| 4. Perspective Lens Map 1 — *Leaving the treetops* | 5 min | Model the three-part lens: sense → understanding/feeling → possible figurative expression. | Complete the first organiser row with a partner. |
| 5. Perspective Lens Map 2 — *Burned ground* | 4 min | Reveal details of heat, ash, silence and absence. Ask which detail carries the greatest emotional weight. | Complete the second row and justify one selected detail. |
| 6. Perspective Lens Map 3 — *The human market* | 4 min | Reveal food, hunger, urgency and human danger. Keep evidence and inference boundaries visible. | Complete the third row and draft one figurative line. |
| 7. Figurative purpose test | 4 min | Compare literal, clichéd and purposeful versions of one description. Ask what each makes the reader notice. | Select the strongest version and explain its job. Repair the clichéd version. |
| 8. Annotated model — *One threshold, fully realised* | 5 min | Read a short model passage as a whole, then reveal sensory evidence, perspective boundary and figurative choices. | Identify the function of each highlighted choice before labels appear. |
| 9. Independent writing — *Crossing Into Danger* | 14 min | Project exact requirements, optional launches and silent timer. | Write the 8–10 sentence scene on the facing workbook page. |
| 10. Self-check — *Stay inside the perspective* | 4 min | Display the perspective-and-imagery checklist. | Independently revise at least one description or viewpoint slip. |
| 11. Exit evidence — *The line that carries fear* | 2 min | Discuss one or two selected lines. Safe stopping point. | Box the strongest figurative line and label what it makes the reader sense or understand. |

Core total: approximately 50 minutes.

## Optional depth slides

### DEPTH A — Sound and silence (8–10 minutes)

Students revise a section so sound disappears across the scene. They explain how absence can function as sensory evidence.

### DEPTH B — Fragmented syntax, deliberate effect (6–8 minutes)

Students compare a conventional sentence with a short fragmented sequence such as the extract’s ash imagery. They imitate the pacing without caricaturing Ginger Juice’s voice.

## Standard organiser

1. Three-row **Perspective Lens Map**:
   - threshold;
   - sensory evidence;
   - what Ginger Juice understands/feels;
   - figurative possibility.
2. A small evidence-boundary box:
   - **She can know…**
   - **She cannot know…**
3. An 8–10 sentence planning strip:
   - departure;
   - crossing;
   - approach;
   - final warning image.

## Lucas/ICP organiser

Preserve the core concept: a character’s senses and feelings shape description.

- Three large visual stages:
  - safe trees;
  - hot, empty ground;
  - food and dangerous humans.
- For each, choose one sense and one feeling from a picture-supported bank.
- Build or dictate three first-person sentences:
  - “I leave the trees.”
  - “The ground is hot and black.”
  - “My heart beats like a drum.”
- Accept pointing, ordering, speaking, copying or scribing.
- Do not require imitation of Ginger Juice’s non-standard grammar.

## Lesson 12 self-check

- I wrote from Ginger Juice’s first-person perspective.
- My details come from, or are defensibly inferred from, the extract.
- I used at least two figurative choices for a clear purpose.
- I included sensory experience rather than only naming emotions.
- My scene moves from relative safety towards danger.
- I revised one line that was clichéd, vague or outside her knowledge.

## Teacher reading fallback

Briefly establish:

- Fire and habitat loss leave Ginger Juice and Ibu hungry.
- They leave the safety of the treetops and cross burned ground.
- Ginger Juice experiences the ground as dangerous and follows Ibu towards human food.
- Humans and their intentions are understood through animal observation rather than human terminology.

# Lesson 13 — Two Lenses on One Scene

## Authoritative sequence focus

- Year 5: expand noun groups for description.
- Year 6: expand ideas through deliberate verbs and adverbial detail.
- Retrieve Ari, pages 77–83, and Ginger Juice, pages 83–85.
- Examine the moment when Ari practises chess beside the cage while Ginger Juice watches.

## Finished workbook response

Students write a 6–8 sentence third-person descriptive snapshot titled **The Chessboard Beside the Cage**.

Common requirements:

- depict Ari moving the table and chessboard beside the cage;
- include Ginger Juice watching the patterns and responding to his presence;
- establish a restrained emotional atmosphere;
- remain in third person;
- avoid analysing both internal perspectives in depth, which is reserved for Lesson 14.

Year-level evidence:

- **Year 5:** at least three deliberately expanded noun groups.
- **Year 6:** at least three deliberately strengthened verbs and two purposeful adverbial details.

All students may use both lenses; the assessed emphasis differs.

## Visual metaphor

**Two optical lenses**

- Lens 1 focuses the participant or object: expanded noun groups.
- Lens 2 focuses the action and its circumstances: verbs and adverbials.
- A centre merge shows the complete sentence.
- Accent colours: indigo for noun groups and gold for action.

## 50-minute core route

| Slide | Time | Purpose and projected experience | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero — *Two Lenses on One Scene* | 2 min | Premise: “Precision is not the same as adding the most words.” | Decide which is weaker: a vague noun or a weak verb. |
| 2. Retrieval — *What is in the frame?* | 4 min | Project the scene elements: table, chessboard, pieces, bars, hand, eyes and movement. | Record four concrete details from the chapter in the organiser. |
| 3. Mission — *Describe precisely without overloading* | 3 min | Preview the shared snapshot and make both year-level checks visible. | Mark the year-level lens that will be specifically checked. |
| 4. Two-Lens Lab 1 — noun group | 5 min | Begin with “Ari moved the table.” Build the noun group around **table** or **Ari** and test what each addition contributes. | Draft two expanded noun-group alternatives. Cross out any word that adds no useful information. |
| 5. Two-Lens Lab 2 — verb and adverbial detail | 5 min | Replace **moved** with precise actions and test position, manner or time details. | Draft two action alternatives and label what the added detail clarifies. |
| 6. Merge and judge | 4 min | Combine selected noun and action choices. Compare a precise sentence with an overloaded version. | Select the stronger combined sentence and justify one deletion from the overloaded version. |
| 7. Second lab sentence — Ginger Juice watches | 3 min | Repeat the lens routine more quickly with Ginger Juice as the focus. | Independently upgrade the sentence using the appropriate year-level lens. |
| 8. Annotated model — *The chessboard beside the cage* | 5 min | Show a complete descriptive paragraph, then reveal noun-group, verb and adverbial decisions. | Predict which details are essential and which could be cut. |
| 9. Independent writing | 13 min | Project the exact snapshot task, year-level requirements, optional launches and timer. | Write 6–8 sentences on the facing workbook page. |
| 10. Self-check — *Clean both lenses* | 4 min | Show common and year-specific checks. | Mark required features and revise at least one overloaded or vague sentence. |
| 11. Exit evidence — *Most precise sentence* | 2 min | Discuss selected examples. Safe stopping point. | Box one sentence and label the deliberate noun or action choice. |

Core total: approximately 50 minutes.

## Optional depth slides

### DEPTH A — Same scene, different camera distance (8–10 minutes)

Students revise one sentence as a wide shot and another as a close-up. They decide which grammatical details belong at each distance.

### DEPTH B — Precision versus excess (6–8 minutes)

Students edit an intentionally overloaded description, removing details that repeat, contradict or distract.

## Standard organiser

1. Four-item scene evidence bank.
2. **Noun-group lens**:
   - base noun;
   - useful additions;
   - contribution of each addition.
3. **Verb/adverbial lens**:
   - weak verb;
   - precise verb;
   - useful circumstance.
4. Two merge-and-judge sentences.
5. Six-part snapshot plan:
   - table arrives;
   - pieces arranged;
   - Ginger Juice watches;
   - Ari studies;
   - touch through bars;
   - closing atmosphere.

## Lucas/ICP organiser

Preserve the core concept: exact describing and action words make a scene clearer.

- Large visual/word bank:
  - nouns: Ari, table, chess pieces, cage, hand, Ginger Juice;
  - describing words: small, wooden, black-and-white, metal, gentle;
  - action words: drags, places, watches, reaches, holds.
- Lucas selects a noun, one useful describing word and one precise action.
- Build or dictate 3–4 sentences:
  - “Ari drags the small table.”
  - “He places the chess pieces.”
  - “Ginger Juice watches the black-and-white pieces.”
  - “Ari gently holds her hand.”
- Accept pointing, circling, speaking, copying or scribing.

## Lesson 13 self-check

Common:

- Every added word contributes useful meaning.
- My verbs show the exact action.
- My description remains coherent and restrained.
- I kept the scene in third person.
- I revised one vague or overloaded sentence.

Year 5:

- I deliberately expanded at least three noun groups.

Year 6:

- I strengthened at least three verbs and used two purposeful adverbial details.

## Teacher reading fallback

Briefly establish:

- Ari moves a restaurant table and chair beside the cage so he can practise chess.
- He believes Ginger Juice follows the pieces and responds to his strategies.
- Ginger Juice watches patterns and colours and values the warmth of his touch.
- The full difference between their interpretations will be examined in Lesson 14.

# Lesson 14 — Double Vision

## Authoritative sequence focus

- Compare the overlapping Ari, pages 77–83, and Ginger Juice, pages 83–85, perspectives.
- Examine how each character interprets the same chessboard interaction.
- Show both difference and overlap.
- Preserve the communication barrier between them.

## Finished workbook response

Students write a **Dual Internal Monologue** describing the same chessboard moment:

1. Ari’s internal monologue.
2. Ginger Juice’s internal monologue.

Each monologue should be approximately 5–7 sentences.

The response must:

- use accurate evidence from both chapters;
- give each voice a distinct focus and language pattern;
- reveal what Ari assumes;
- reveal what Ginger Juice actually experiences;
- include at least one genuine point of overlap;
- preserve what each character cannot know.

## Visual metaphor

**Double vision / mirrored frames**

- Left frame: **Ari interprets**.
- Right frame: **Ginger Juice experiences**.
- Centre overlap: **Both share**.
- Accent colours: warm amber for Ari, rainforest green for Ginger Juice and pale gold for overlap.

## 50-minute core route

| Slide | Time | Purpose and projected experience | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero — *Double Vision* | 2 min | Premise: “Two characters can share one moment without sharing its meaning.” | Predict one thing Ari may misunderstand. |
| 2. Retrieval — *One moment, two accounts* | 3 min | Present four brief details from the overlapping chapters. | Mark each as **Ari**, **Ginger Juice** or **both** in the organiser. Do not check yet. |
| 3. Mission — *Reveal the gap* | 2 min | Preview the Dual Internal Monologue and define perspective as selected knowledge, attention and interpretation. | Complete: “Ari thinks ___, while Ginger Juice ___.” |
| 4. Double-Vision Board — clear details | 5 min | Project details with relatively clear placement. Students must supply evidence before the teacher places them. | Complete the three organiser columns and note one textual clue for each placement. |
| 5. Difficult detail — watching the chessboard | 4 min | Test Ari’s belief that Ginger Juice follows and approves of his moves against her attention to patterns and colours. | Place the detail in both frames differently and write what each character thinks it means. |
| 6. Difficult detail — touch and companionship | 4 min | Examine the shared comfort alongside unequal understanding. Avoid reducing the moment to “Ari is wrong about everything”. | Record one true overlap and one important difference. |
| 7. Evidence boundary — *What can each know?* | 3 min | Display four claims, including plausible but unsupported mind-reading. | Mark **supported / inferred / impossible for this character to know**. |
| 8. Annotated mirrored model | 5 min | Read both short model monologues in full. Then reveal matched decisions: focus, evidence, assumption, sensory experience and overlap. | Identify where the reader understands more than either character. Star one voice distinction to imitate. |
| 9. Plan both voices | 3 min | Project a mirrored planning frame. | Complete the organiser with Ari’s belief, Ginger Juice’s experience and the shared moment that connects them. |
| 10. Independent writing — Dual Internal Monologue | 13 min | Display exact requirements, optional voice launches and timer. | Write both monologues on the facing workbook page. |
| 11. Self-check — *Clear the double image* | 4 min | Project the perspective checklist. | Silently revise one voice slip, unsupported thought or missing overlap. |
| 12. Exit evidence — *The gap in one sentence* | 2 min | Discuss one selected pairing. Safe stopping point. | Write: “Ari believes ___; Ginger Juice experiences ___; both ___.” |

Core total: approximately 50 minutes.

## Optional depth slides

### DEPTH A — The reader’s third perspective (8–10 minutes)

Add a centre-bottom frame: **The reader understands**. Students explain the dramatic irony created because the reader has access to both narrators.

### DEPTH B — Hinge sentence (6–8 minutes)

Students write one transition between the monologues that preserves the same physical moment while shifting the meaning.

## Standard organiser

1. Three-column **Double-Vision Board**:
   - Ari interprets;
   - Ginger Juice experiences;
   - both share.
