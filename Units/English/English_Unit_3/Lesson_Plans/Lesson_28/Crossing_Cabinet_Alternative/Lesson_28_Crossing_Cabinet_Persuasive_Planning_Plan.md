# Lesson 28 Alternative: Outlining the Persuasive Plan

### Pedagogical Contemplation

The cognitive goal is to turn a position into a draft-ready map: a specific audience and action, sequenced reasons, traceable evidence notes, a fair concern with a response, and a purposeful conclusion. Students first encounter a fictional proposal that is intentionally incomplete as evidence; noticing its plan lets them name the decisions a writer makes before drafting. The sorting check and comparison require judgement about paragraph purpose and evidence quality, while the planning studio produces the actual individual assessment plan. Engagement comes from solving a real writing problem - “would this plan help a reader decide?” - rather than from the fictional setting itself.

## Lesson purpose

Students organise arguments, counterarguments and traceable evidence on an individual persuasive plan. *The Crossing Cabinet* is an original fictional mentor text about trialling a shared outdoor-games cabinet; it replaces *Berani* as the common model without replacing students’ own research.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LY06:** plan, create and edit written persuasive texts.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LY06:** plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs.

## Learning intention

We are learning to organise our opinion, reasons, checked evidence and response to a fair concern before we draft.

## Success criteria

- I can name an audience, an action and a clear opinion.
- I can order two or more reasons so a reader can follow my thinking.
- I can attach an accurate evidence note and source detail to each reason.
- I can plan a fair counterargument and a direct response.
- I can plan a conclusion that asks my audience to act.

## Assessment requirements preserved

- **Part A:** individual written persuasive text for an identified audience, suggested length **200-400 words**, researched from differing ideas and supporting information with sources identified.
- **Part B:** individual **1-3 minute** spoken presentation that justifies the opinion using voice; the written text guides the presentation rather than being read as a script.

## Preparation

- Open `Lesson_28_Crossing_Cabinet_Persuasive_Planning_Presentation.html` in fullscreen.
- Print the Reading Pack and Persuasive Plan Organiser, or the Concise Access Pack.
- Make students’ checked research notes available. This lesson plans from those notes; it does not create evidence.

## Core sequence (50 minutes)

1. **The decision before the draft - 4 min.** Judge whether a position has enough information to become a plan.
2. **Purpose - 2 min.** Establish the plan route.
3. **Read the model - 6 min.** Read *The Crossing Cabinet* without yet using it as evidence.
4. **Notice planning moves - 5 min.** Identify claim, reason, concern, response and measured next step.
5. **Explicit model - 6 min.** Model one reason linked to an evidence note and source detail.
6. **CFU: paragraph jobs - 5 min.** Match a persuasive paragraph’s job to its purpose.
7. **CFU: ready to draft? - 4 min.** Compare plans and justify what needs revision.
8. **Map arguments - 4 min.** Sequence audience, opinion, reasons, evidence, concern and conclusion.
9. **Planning studio - 10 min.** Complete an individual plan from checked notes.
10. **Peer plan audit - 3 min.** Test one plan for clarity and traceability, then revise.
11. **Exit evidence - 1 min.** Submit the reason-source-concern chain for a drafting conference.

## Interaction matrix

| Interaction | Cognitive demand | Evidence / misconception | Teacher signal | Tier 2 hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paragraph-job match | Classify and explain | A reason paragraph mistaken for a conclusion | Student explains the reader’s next need | “What job does this sentence do?” |
| Ready-plan choice | Compare against criteria | Vague opinion or untraceable evidence accepted | Student names the missing field | “Can a reader locate the evidence?” |
| Plan map | Sequence | Evidence placed after drafting or concern treated as an attack | Completed chain with a fair response | “What must be decided before the first sentence?” |
| Peer audit | Evaluate and revise | Peer supplies facts or rewrites work | One writer-owned revision | “Ask about clarity; do not invent proof.” |

## Model and evidence boundary

The school, cabinet, people and details in *The Crossing Cabinet* are fictional. The reading models planning choices only. Its observations, routines and outcomes are **not** evidence students may cite. Students must use their own checked, identified sources; write **needs checking** where a source detail is incomplete; and never invent data, quotations, organisations or URLs.

## Differentiation

**Concise access route:** the green language view shortens directions, keeps the same planning chain, permits pointing, speaking, copying or partner scribing, and begins with one reason plus a source name.

**Support:** provide the planning frame “I think [audience] should [action] because [reason]”; conference the audience and first source note before adding further reasons.

**Extension:** deliberately sequence reasons for the selected audience; include a source that complicates the position; plan a qualified response that does not dismiss a fair concern.

## Formative assessment and next step

The two CFUs reveal structural and source-traceability confusion. The organiser and exit chain identify students needing a conference before drafting in Lesson 30. Keep collaboration to rehearsal and feedback: planning and assessment writing remain individual.

## Discussion guidance

Use negotiated, school- and community-safe topics. Students do not need to disclose personal finances, family decisions, health or hardship. Record a gap honestly rather than converting a feeling into evidence.
