# The Crossing Cabinet - Teacher Guide

## Teaching decision

The model is a planning mentor, not a research source. Keep returning students to the distinction between a **reason** (their thinking), an **evidence note** (what a source says) and **source detail** (how another reader can find it).

## Model answer guidance

| Reading move | Likely answer | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | “Seabrook should trial a Crossing Cabinet for four weeks.” | Names an actor, action and limited timeframe. |
| Fair concern | Equipment could be lost and staff could be burdened. | It is plausible and treated respectfully. |
| Response | Start small, use a sign-out card and review the trial. | Responds to the actual concern rather than changing topic. |
| Measured conclusion | Trial, collect information, then decide. | Calls for action without promising every outcome. |

## Conferencing prompts

- “Who can act on this proposal?”
- “Which words in your source note can another reader trace?”
- “Is this concern fair enough that an undecided reader might ask it?”
- “Does your response answer that concern, or begin a different argument?”

## Watch for

- “Everyone knows” or invented figures: replace with a source note or **needs checking**.
- A topic rather than an action: ask “Who should do what?”
- A counterargument that is a joke or straw person: ask for a reasonable concern.
- A conclusion that adds a new reason: return it to the claim, future and action.

## Exit triage

Ready: audience, specific action, two source-linked reasons and a fair concern. Conference first: no source detail, vague audience, evidence that cannot be checked, or a response that avoids the concern.
