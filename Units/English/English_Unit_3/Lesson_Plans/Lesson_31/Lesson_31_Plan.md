# Lesson 31: Drafting — Body Paragraphs (PEEL)

## Purpose

Students draft the first two body paragraphs of their individual persuasive assessment text. They use **PEEL** — Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link — to turn planned reasons into reader-followable reasoning. The lesson’s central safeguard is that a model can demonstrate structure but does not supply evidence: students use only their own identified, accurate research notes or label an unfinished claim **needs checking**.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** create written persuasive texts, developing and expanding ideas with supporting details from topics or texts.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create persuasive texts using paragraphs, complex sentences and expanded noun groups.

## Learning intention

We are learning to develop a reason into a persuasive paragraph that gives a reader a point, trustworthy support, reasoning and a link to the opinion.

## Success criteria

- I can write a precise point for each paragraph.
- I can use an accurate, traceable evidence note or mark an unfinished claim **needs checking**.
- I can explain how the evidence supports my point rather than repeat it.
- I can link the reason to my audience and opinion.
- I can draft two different body paragraphs.
- **Year 6:** I can use a purposeful complex sentence and expanded noun group to add precision, not unsupported certainty.

## Assessment alignment

Part A is individual work: a 200–400 word (suggested) persuasive text for an identified audience, with supporting details, paragraphs, topic-specific vocabulary and sentence choices appropriate to audience and purpose. Students research differing ideas and supporting information, including authoritative sources, and identify their sources. Part B is a 1–3 minute (suggested) individual presentation; this written draft will later guide the spoken version.

## Preparation

- Open the Lesson 31 HTML deck; logistics are in the Notes popup, not displayed on slides.
- Provide the relevant organiser, the student’s Lesson 28 plan, and identified research notes/sources.
- Establish a neutral, negotiated school/community topic and remind students that evidence must be accurate and traceable.

## Lesson sequence (55 min)

1. Paragraph comparison — 4 min
2. Purpose and PEEL route — 2 min
3. Notice a precise point — 4 min
4. Evidence boundary — 5 min
5. Annotated reasoning model — 6 min
6. Year 6 precision comparison — 5 min
7. PEEL sort — 5 min
8. Two-paragraph planning — 5 min
9. Independent drafting studio — 12 min
10. Reader check and revision — 5 min
11. PEEL exit — 2 min

## Model and source boundary

The Storytime Dog language in the deck is an explicitly labelled structural model drawn from the supplied model responses. It is not a source students may cite. The model’s evidence slot is deliberately bracketed; students replace it only with a checked, identified source note from their own research. *Berani* remains an anchor for ideas about brave, consequential choices, but students do not need to make claims about the novel in their negotiated assessment topic.

## Differentiation

**Support:** provide colour-coded PEEL prompts, oral rehearsal, sentence stems ("One reason is…" / "This matters because…" / "Therefore…") and two developed reasons rather than more paragraphs.

**Lucas (ICP):** students can point, speak, copy or use partner scribing to create a reason, one supportive detail, a simple because statement and a therefore statement. The destination remains a reason that is explained for an audience.

**Extend:** select evidence that needs careful qualification, explain a causal chain, and use precise complex sentences and expanded noun groups only where they clarify the claim.

## Formative assessment

The PEEL sort exposes whether students can distinguish evidence from explanation. The drafting conference checks that evidence is traceable, points are distinct, and explanations show reasoning. The exit ticket groups students for the counterargument and conclusion work in Lesson 32.
