# Lesson 12: Crossing Into Danger

## Lesson purpose

Students map Ginger Juice's sensory experience across three danger thresholds and use purposeful figurative language to write an evidence-grounded first-person scene.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA03: describe how literary language features contribute to meaning.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA03: explain how authors adapt language and structure for purpose and effect.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to create empathy through sensory evidence, perspective and purposeful figurative language.

## Success criteria

- I can identify what Ginger Juice senses at three thresholds.
- I can separate what she can know from human knowledge she cannot access.
- I can explain the job of a figurative comparison.
- I can write an 8-10 sentence first-person scene with two purposeful figurative choices.
- I can revise a line that is clichéd, vague or outside her perspective.

## Finished workbook response

An 8-10 sentence first-person scene titled Crossing Into Danger, moving from the treetops across burned ground towards the human food market.

## Agreed delivery model

- Teacher controls the projected HTML presentation.
- Students complete the organiser and all substantial composition in physical English workbooks.
- The deck contains no student composition fields.
- The chapter is assumed to have been read. Use the fallback summary only when required.
- Core route: approximately 50 minutes.
- Both optional depth slides are included and may extend the lesson towards 70 minutes.
- Revision is primarily independent through the projected self-check.

## Core sequence

### 1. Put the three danger thresholds in order - 4 minutes

Order the thresholds and add one remembered detail to each.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: Perspective Lens Map.
- **Finished when:** Three ordered thresholds and three details.
- **Teacher note:** The chapters are pre-read. Use the fallback summary if necessary, then keep moving.

### 2. Make the reader experience the crossing - 2 minutes

Complete aloud: Ginger Juice can know ___, but she cannot know ___.

- **Student action:** Partner rehearsal; No writing yet.
- **Finished when:** One clear knowledge boundary.
- **Teacher note:** Establish empathy as disciplined perspective-taking, not sentimental invention.

### 3. Leaving the treetops - 5 minutes

Complete this first lens row with the class.

- **Student action:** Whole class model; Organiser: matching threshold row.
- **Finished when:** Sense, understanding and image recorded.
- **Teacher note:** Model the first row. Ask which detail is sensory and which is interpretation.

### 4. Crossing the burned ground - 4 minutes

Complete the lens row and draft one possible figurative line.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: matching threshold row.
- **Finished when:** Sense, understanding and image recorded.
- **Teacher note:** Students complete this row in pairs. Accept different strongest details when justified.

### 5. Approaching the human market - 4 minutes

Complete the lens row and draft one possible figurative line.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: matching threshold row.
- **Finished when:** Sense, understanding and image recorded.
- **Teacher note:** Keep the evidence boundary visible: she does not know commerce, weapons or human motives.

### 6. Purposeful image—or decoration? - 4 minutes

Select the line that makes the reader notice loss, then repair the cliché.

- **Student action:** Independent first; class check; Organiser margin.
- **Finished when:** One choice, reason and repaired line.
- **Teacher note:** The purposeful model is an original teacher model, not a quotation from the novel. Discuss function before naming device.

### 7. One threshold, fully realised - 5 minutes

Read the whole model. Predict each choice's job before clicking.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one decision on the organiser.
- **Finished when:** One selected craft decision.
- **Teacher note:** This model imitates the extract's craft without copying extended text. Do not require students to imitate non-standard grammar.

### 8. Write Crossing Into Danger - 14 minutes

Write the complete scene from Ginger Juice's first-person perspective.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** 8-10 sentences across all three thresholds.
- **Teacher note:** Students may use conventional grammar in their own response. The target is perspective and imagery, not mimicry.

### 9. Stay inside the perspective - 4 minutes

Mark each check and revise one line.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook scene.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Choose examples for discussion only after silent revision.

### 10. Box the line that carries fear - 2 minutes

Box your strongest figurative line and explain its effect.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One boxed line and one effect statement.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Use the explanation as evidence of figurative-language understanding.


## Optional depth slides

### Make sound disappear - 10 minutes

Write a short sound-to-silence sequence and explain its effect.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** 2-3 sentences and one effect statement.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Encourage restraint: one disappearing pattern is stronger than a catalogue.

### Fragments can control the reader's pace - 8 minutes

Create both versions and justify where the fragment belongs.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Two versions and one placement reason.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Connect back to Lesson 10: students should distinguish craft fragments from incomplete sentences.
