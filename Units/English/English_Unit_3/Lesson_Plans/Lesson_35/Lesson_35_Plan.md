# Lesson 35: Speaking Assessment Preparation

## Lesson purpose

Students turn their individual persuasive draft into a speaking guide. They annotate selected words and transitions for purposeful delivery, make concise cue cards rather than a read-aloud script, and rehearse an excerpt for an identified audience.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** create persuasive texts that develop and expand ideas with supporting details for particular purposes and audiences.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create persuasive texts using language features and structures appropriate to audience and purpose.

## Learning intention

We are learning to use our written argument as a guide and make deliberate voice choices for an audience.

## Success criteria

- I can keep my spoken presentation structured: opinion, arguments with support, fair response and action.
- I can use my written work as a guide rather than read it as a script.
- I can mark emphasis, pauses and volume for a purpose.
- I can make concise cue cards with accurate argument and source prompts.
- I can rehearse for my identified audience, use feedback and make one immediate revision.

## Assessment alignment

Part B is an **individual** spoken presentation of an opinion for an identified audience. Suggested length: **1–3 minutes**. Students share, develop and expand on ideas using supporting arguments and features of voice. The written text is a guide for the spoken structure; students may annotate and adapt it, rehearse with peers, respond to feedback and include relevant impromptu ideas. Optional visual/digital elements may support effect.

## Preparation

- Open the standalone HTML deck; all teacher logistics are in the Notes popup.
- Provide each student’s edited written draft, checked research/source notes, organiser and blank cue cards.
- Confirm an intended audience for each student’s presentation.

## Core sequence (55 min)

1. Speaker connection entry — 3 min
2. Assessment route — 3 min
3. Reading versus cues decision — 4 min
4. Annotated delivery model — 6 min
5. Voice-choice sort — 5 min
6. Cue-card design — 5 min
7. Individual speaking-map and card build — 12 min
8. Audience rehearsal and immediate revision — 10 min
9. Timed excerpt rehearsal — 5 min
10. Exit choice — 3 min

## Model and evidence boundary

The short presentation lines in the deck model delivery choices only. They are not research evidence or a speech to copy. Students use their own negotiated topic, their own written argument and only accurate, identified supporting details. A cue card may name a source or a key detail, but it must never invent a statistic, quotation, expert or outcome.

## Differentiation

**Support:** reduce cards to opening / one argument / action; use icons, colour and oral rehearsal before writing; give one delivery choice at a time.

**Lucas (ICP):** students may point, speak, use symbol cues, record a short message or work with a partner scribe. The core destination remains communicating an opinion and one reason to an audience.

**Extend:** adapt a delivery choice to a likely audience question, include a concise fair response, and use pace, pitch or tone with precise purpose rather than dramatic excess.

## Formative assessment

The compare-and-choose and voice sort show whether students understand that delivery serves meaning. Cue cards and the second rehearsal show whether the speaker can sustain individual ownership, accurate details and audience focus. Exit responses set the next voice/formality target for Lesson 36.

## Safety and authorship

Keep topics school/community-safe and do not require personal disclosure. Assessment work remains individual; listeners offer feedback but do not write content, create claims or supply evidence for the speaker.
