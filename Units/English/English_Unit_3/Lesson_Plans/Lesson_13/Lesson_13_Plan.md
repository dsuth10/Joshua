# Lesson 13: Two Lenses on One Scene

## Lesson purpose

Students use a noun-group lens and an action lens to create a restrained third-person snapshot of Ari practising chess beside Ginger Juice's cage.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA06: understand how noun groups can be expanded for description.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA06: understand how ideas can be expanded through choices of verbs and adverbs.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to make description precise through useful noun-group, verb and adverbial choices.

## Success criteria

- I can identify concrete details that belong in the chessboard scene.
- I can expand a noun group without adding empty detail.
- I can replace a weak verb with a precise action.
- I can recognise and edit an overloaded sentence.
- I can write a 6-8 sentence third-person snapshot and independently revise one vague or overloaded sentence.

## Finished workbook response

A 6-8 sentence third-person snapshot titled The Chessboard Beside the Cage. Year 5 evidence: three expanded noun groups. Year 6 evidence: three precise verbs and two purposeful adverbial details.

## Core sequence

### 1. What belongs inside the frame? - 4 minutes

Record four precise scene details.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: scene evidence bank.
- **Finished when:** Four selected details.
- **Teacher note:** The pre-reading assumption applies. Use the fallback summary for students who missed the chapter.

### 2. Describe precisely without overloading - 3 minutes

Mark the year-level lens that will be specifically checked.

- **Student action:** Independent; Organiser heading.
- **Finished when:** One lens marked and explained.
- **Teacher note:** Keep the class together. Assessment emphasis differs; access to both craft tools does not.

### 3. Bring the participant into focus - 5 minutes

Draft two noun-group alternatives. Cross out any addition that earns no place.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: noun-group lens.
- **Finished when:** Two alternatives and one justified deletion.
- **Teacher note:** Do not teach length as quality. Ask what each addition contributes and what the reader already knows.

### 4. Make the action exact - 5 minutes

Draft two action alternatives and label what each clarifies.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: verb/adverbial lens.
- **Finished when:** Two alternatives and two function labels.
- **Teacher note:** This is especially important for Year 6: more dramatic is not automatically more precise.

### 5. Use both lenses. Keep only what earns its place. - 4 minutes

Select the stronger sentence and justify three deletions from the overloaded version.

- **Student action:** Independent, then class comparison; Organiser: merge and judge.
- **Finished when:** One selection and three deletions.
- **Teacher note:** Some detail from the overloaded sentence could be defended in a different purpose. Here the goal is a restrained snapshot.

### 6. Ginger Juice watches - 3 minutes

Upgrade the sentence using your year-level lens.

- **Student action:** Independent; Organiser.
- **Finished when:** One precise revised sentence.
- **Teacher note:** This prepares the perspective boundary for Lesson 14 without doing the full comparison.

### 7. The chessboard beside the cage - 5 minutes

Predict which words carry each lens and which details could be cut.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one decision on the organiser.
- **Finished when:** One decision selected for imitation.
- **Teacher note:** Read the whole model first. Avoid interpreting their internal perspectives in depth.

### 8. Write The Chessboard Beside the Cage - 13 minutes

Write the complete descriptive snapshot.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** 6-8 coherent third-person sentences.
- **Teacher note:** Do not require a full perspective analysis. Lesson 14 owns that cognitive work.

### 9. Clean both lenses - 4 minutes

Mark the common and year-level checks. Revise one sentence.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook snapshot.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Selected examples should show purposeful restraint as well as successful expansion.

### 10. Box your most precise sentence - 2 minutes

Box one sentence and label the deliberate noun or action choice.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One boxed sentence and one explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Collect evidence separately by year-level emphasis.


## Optional depth slides

### Change the camera distance - 10 minutes

Write one wide-shot sentence and one close-up sentence.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Two sentences and one comparison.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. This increases selection and purpose rather than volume.

### Edit precision out of excess - 8 minutes

Edit the sentence down, then justify two removals.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** One clean revision and two reasons.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Accept different clean revisions when students can defend the retained focus.
