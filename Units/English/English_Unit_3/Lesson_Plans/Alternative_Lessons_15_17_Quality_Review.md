# Quality Review: English Unit 3 Alternative Lessons 15 and 17

## Overall judgement

Both alternatives are high-quality, teachable lesson packages. Their strongest shared feature is alignment between reading, explicit modelling, independent writing and revision: students do not merely discuss an issue; they use a curated text to make and improve a persuasive decision.

Lesson 15 is the tighter model for future alternatives. Lesson 17 is intellectually rich and carefully researched, but its core route asks students to control several demanding ideas at once. Future lessons should retain the evidence quality and authentic audiences while usually selecting one persuasive technique as the explicit teaching focus.

## Lesson 15: Living With Flying-Foxes

### What is particularly strong

- The reading has a genuine purpose: students need its facts to audit two competing messages.
- The objective / subjective / mixed distinction is taught precisely. The warning that objective language is not automatically true is excellent.
- Exact words are connected to audience effects through useful verbs such as alarms, reassures, minimises and dismisses.
- The fictional messages prevent advocacy material from being mistaken for neutral evidence.
- The final council brief transfers analysis into responsible persuasion and requires a visible revision.
- Safety and respect boundaries are unusually thorough and practical.

### What could be tightened

- The curriculum connection could more directly name AC9E6LA02 (objective and subjective language and bias) and AC9E5LA02 (moving beyond bare assertions), even if the sequence retains its original endorsed codes.
- Six classifications, two full messages, an evidence bank and a written brief may be too much for some classes in one hour. The support route should be treated as a planned alternate core route, not only an adjustment after students struggle.
- The reading pack is information-rich. Preteaching the vocabulary before independent reading is important for Year 5 readers.

## Lesson 17: Turtle-Safe Nights

### What is particularly strong

- The article is an original synthesis with careful boundaries around local dates, lighting colour, guarantees and cultural consultation.
- The council decision is authentic and avoids a simplistic wildlife-versus-people frame.
- Opportunity, action and consequence are explicitly separated and causally linked.
- Stakeholder viewpoints are represented fairly, and Traditional Owners are correctly distinguished as rights-holders and knowledge-holders rather than a generic interest group.
- Students classify evidence, inference and overclaim before using the distinction in their own recommendation.
- The light pathway is used responsibly as an interpretive model with a stated limitation.

### What could be tightened

- The lesson simultaneously teaches causal chains, stakeholder viewpoint, source boundaries, modal language, metaphor limitations and policy design. That is excellent depth, but it creates a high working-memory load.
- Twelve core phases make transitions important. For a typical mixed Year 5-6 class, the teacher may need to omit the pathway metaphor or complete the planning orally to protect writing time.
- The final response asks for three evidence points, a fair concern, a causal chain, a concrete action, a metaphor boundary and revision in 13 minutes. A two-lesson option would allow more deliberate composition.
- The phrase "light pathway" needs a very quick concrete model before students use it independently; otherwise the visual organiser can become an extra concept rather than a support.

## Design principles carried into Lesson 18

1. Keep the complete package: original reading, organiser, accessible pathway, teacher guidance and standalone presentation.
2. Use one authentic decision with more than one legitimate value at stake.
3. Explicitly teach one main persuasive move rather than layering several new moves.
4. Mark fictional voices and scenarios clearly; use official sources for factual claims.
5. Require exact language analysis, a model, independent construction, feedback and immediate revision.
6. Preserve a simple 60-minute route, with depth work genuinely optional.
