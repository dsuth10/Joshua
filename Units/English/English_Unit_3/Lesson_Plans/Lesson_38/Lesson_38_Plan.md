# Lesson 38: Spoken Presentations — Session 1

## Lesson purpose

Students in the first scheduled group deliver their individual spoken persuasive presentations. The class practises disciplined audience conduct by recording one strong argument and the evidence or persuasive technique that strengthened it.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations using language features and voice for effect.

## Learning intention

We are learning to present our own opinion respectfully and to listen closely enough to identify how another speaker developed an argument.

## Success criteria

- I can present my own prepared opinion for the agreed audience.
- I can maintain respectful, focused audience conduct.
- I can capture one argument accurately, including what supported or strengthened it.
- I can identify a persuasive technique or delivery choice I heard, without judging the speaker as a person.

## Assessment alignment

Part B is an **individual** spoken persuasive presentation, suggested at **1–3 minutes**. Students share a structured opinion with sequenced arguments and supporting details; features of voice and optional multimodal support may strengthen the effect. The teacher records observed evidence using the assessment recording sheet. Audience capture is formative listening evidence, not peer marking.

## Preparation

- Confirm the first group and any approved adjustments privately.
- Have the teacher recording sheet, student capture organisers, timer and approved support materials ready.
- Establish a quiet transition route and agreed access alternatives (small-group delivery, visual/symbol support, assistive technology, a trusted adult or alternative setting) where required.
- Open the presentation; operational details remain in its Notes popup only.

## Core sequence (50–60 min, adaptable to actual presentation length)

1. Welcome and shared purpose — 3 min
2. Explain speaker/audience roles — 4 min
3. Establish conduct and capture fields — 8 min
4. Presentation system and transition rehearsal — 4 min
5. First presentation block — 20–32 min
6. Audience exit evidence and reset for Session 2 — 4 min

## Audience task

For each presentation, students record the speaker’s opinion/action, one reason, and what supported or strengthened it (for example, a fact, source, example, explanation, word choice, voice choice or optional visual). They record only what they heard. Generic praise and peer scores are not the task.

## Differentiation and access

**Support:** use a one-page capture organiser with short labels; permit a visual cue list; preview the listener task; allow one completed capture rather than every field if language processing requires it.

**Lucas (ICP):** use symbol-supported or scribed capture; respond by pointing, selecting a visual cue or using assistive technology. A presenter may communicate a short opinion and reason using their agreed mode or present in an agreed small-group/alternative setting. The destination remains sharing an opinion with an audience.

**Extend:** distinguish a reason from its support, identify the effect of a voice or language choice, and prepare one respectful clarification question only when the speaker has invited questions.

## Formative evidence

Audience organisers show whether students can distinguish a claim, argument and supporting detail. Teacher observation records the scheduled students’ Part B evidence. Use the exit response to re-establish listening expectations for Lesson 39.

## Safety, dignity and ownership

Do not publish a presentation order if it increases anxiety; retain teacher flexibility for timing and approved adjustments. Students retain authorship of individual assessment work. Follow school recording, privacy and audience protocols. Never require forced eye contact, public peer ratings, applause on demand or a whole-class delivery when an agreed access arrangement is appropriate.
