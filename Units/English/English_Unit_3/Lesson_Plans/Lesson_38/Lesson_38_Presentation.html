ENGLISH UNIT 3 • LESSON 38

Every speaker deserves a listening audience.

Session 1 of our spoken persuasive presentations

SPOKEN PRESENTATIONS • SESSION 1
PURPOSE

Today, we make the room safe for ideas.

4 min
SPEAKERSShare an individual 1–3 minute opinion.AUDIENCEListen for one strong argument.EVERYONEName the evidence or technique that made it persuasive.

Listening is part of the learning—not a pause between speeches.

CONDUCT

What makes an audience trustworthy?

3 min
READYFace the speaker. Devices and side conversations away.
CURIOUSListen for the claim, a reason and what supports it.
RESPECTFULLet the speaker finish. Respond to ideas, not the person.

Choose one action you can control before the first speaker begins.

CAPTURE

Capture one strong argument.

5 min

Argument capture

The speaker’s opinion or action: ______________________________

One strong reason: __________________________________________

What supported it? fact / source / example / explanation / other

What made it persuasive? voice / word choice / repetition / question / visual / other

Write what you heard—not what you wish the speaker had said.

QUALITY

Evidence is more useful than applause.

4 min
TOO GENERAL

“Good job!”

SPECIFIC LISTENER RESPONSE

“I heard your reason. The example or source helped me understand why it mattered.”

SYSTEM

A fair, calm presentation system.

4 min
  1. Teacher confirms the next speaker privately. The speaker may use approved prompt cards, visual support or an agreed access arrangement.
  2. Audience prepares one capture box. Quietly choose a seat and focus method that supports listening.
  3. Speaker delivers. The teacher observes against the recording sheet; audience records one argument and its support/technique.
  4. Transition. A quiet pause lets the next speaker prepare and listeners reset.
PRESENT

Presentation block: listen for the argument.

20–28 min

While a classmate presents

1. Listen for their opinion or requested action.

2. Capture one reason.

3. Identify what supported or strengthened it.

4. Keep the page: it is evidence of your listening.

Speakers: share your own prepared, accurate ideas. Audience: show that those ideas reached you.

WAIT

If you are waiting, you still have purposeful work.

Ongoing
PREPARE QUIETLYCheck your own cue card, breathing plan or access tools.
OBSERVE PRECISELYComplete one argument capture for the current speaker.
RESETAfter your turn, return as an audience member with the same care.

Need a different way to participate? Use the agreed quiet seat, visual capture, scribing, assistive technology or teacher check-in.
