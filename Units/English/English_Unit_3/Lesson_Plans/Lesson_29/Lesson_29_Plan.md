# Lesson 29: End of Novel Study — Hope and Conservation

## Lesson purpose

Students use the ending of *Berani* to identify how a hopeful future is made credible, then apply the same craft move to the conclusion of their persuasive assessment text. The novel is a mentor text for conclusion craft; students' assessment claims still require their own checked evidence.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** create written persuasive texts developing and expanding ideas.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create written persuasive texts using complex sentences and expanded verb groups.

## Learning intention

We are learning to end a persuasive text by returning to our opinion, naming a believable hopeful future and calling our audience to action.

## Success criteria

- I can identify visible change, an open future and what matters in the ending of *Berani*.
- I can distinguish a credible possibility from an unsupported promise.
- I can write a conclusion that returns to my opinion, makes a future visible and gives my audience a specific action.

## Text accuracy

Read *Berani* pp. 191–201. Ginger Juice is moved to a rehabilitation centre, grows stronger and develops survival skills; the text presents her as a possible future release candidate, not already released. On pp. 199–201 she recognises Ibu and they reunite. On pp. 193–197, Ari describes Malia's blog about spices, vanilla and honey grown under rainforest canopy and plans to discuss the possibility with his community.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation in fullscreen and print the standard or Lucas organiser.
- Make students' Lesson 28 plans and Lesson 27 checked evidence notes available.
- Core route: 50 minutes. Teacher logistics are available only from the deck's Notes popup.

## Core sequence

1. **Ending choice — 4 min.** Compare a vague reassurance with a credible action-and-future ending.
2. **Purpose — 2 min.** Establish return to claim → future → call to action.
3. **Read — 7 min.** Read selected passages from pp. 191–201 and capture reunion plus future details.
4. **Hope map — 6 min.** Sort visible change, open future and what matters.
5. **Agroforestry possibility — 5 min.** Separate Malia's information from Ari's proposed community action.
6. **Conclusion model — 6 min.** Label return, future and call; contrast conditional possibility with a promise.
7. **Compare conclusions — 4 min.** Identify why a conclusion has persuasive force.
8. **Plan — 5 min.** Make the three conclusion decisions.
9. **Conclusion studio — 8 min.** Draft, then identify the action and future.
10. **Peer rehearsal — 4 min.** Test whether the action and future are clear; revise one phrase.
11. **Exit — 2 min.** Explain how the ending is hopeful and believable.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** provide return/future/action sentence starters; orally rehearse before writing; use a teacher-supplied generic example rather than invented factual evidence.
- **Lucas (ICP):** choose or communicate one opinion, one positive future and one action using point, speech, drawing, copying or partner scribing.
- **Extension:** acknowledge a realistic limitation in the final sentence and use a carefully chosen conditional clause to make the call more precise.

## Formative assessment

The sort surfaces whether students see hope as evidence-based change and a possible future rather than a happy ending. The conclusion plan, peer test and exit show whether writers can make a realistic call to action before Lessons 30–32 drafting and revision.

## Safety and accuracy boundary

Do not present the novel as a research source proving universal agroforestry claims. Do not invent statistics, expert views or outcomes. Keep personal disclosures optional; students can work with a school or community issue that does not require disclosure.
