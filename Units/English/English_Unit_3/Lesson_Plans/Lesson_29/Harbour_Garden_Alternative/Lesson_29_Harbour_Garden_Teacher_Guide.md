# The Harbour Garden - Teacher Guide

## Model conclusion

**Return:** The school should approve a six-week Harbour Garden trial near the library. **Future:** With a small, carefully managed space, the corner could become a place where students can read, draw or talk quietly at lunch. **Action:** The principal can begin by approving the trial and asking the garden team to record what works before a longer decision is made.

## Answer guidance

- A credible future uses possibility language: *could*, *can help*, *may become*.
- An overclaim promises an outcome the writer cannot prove: *will fix every playground problem*.
- A useful call to action names an audience and a feasible first step.

## Likely misconceptions

- Repeating the claim word-for-word: model a restatement with the same action.
- Adding a new reason at the end: remind students that a conclusion gathers rather than starts a new argument.
- Treating hope as certainty: replace *will solve* with *could help create*.
- Vague action: replace *please help* with an audience plus verb.

## Conference prompt

"Read your final sentence. Who can act, what can they do and what future does that step make possible?"

## Fiction boundary

The article is original fiction for structural analysis. It does not establish a real school need or provide research evidence.
