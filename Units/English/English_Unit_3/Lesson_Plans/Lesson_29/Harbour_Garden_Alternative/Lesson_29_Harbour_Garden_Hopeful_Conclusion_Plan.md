### Pedagogical Contemplation

The cognitive goal is to make a persuasive conclusion do three linked jobs: return to a claim, make a credible future visible and invite a specific next action. Students need to judge the difference between a possible outcome and an over-promise, rather than merely add an optimistic final sentence. The sequence uses an original feature article as a concrete mentor text, then makes the conclusion decisions visible through a three-part map, a comparative choice, oral rehearsal and revision. The garden context provides a memorable setting; the learning is the writer's decision-making, not the setting itself.

# Lesson 29 Alternative: The Harbour Garden - End With a Future

## Purpose and curriculum focus

Students use an original fictional proposal to identify how a hopeful future becomes credible, then transfer the craft move to the conclusion of their own persuasive assessment text.

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LY06:** create written persuasive texts developing and expanding ideas.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LY06:** create written persuasive texts using complex sentences and expanded verb groups.

## Learning intention

We are learning to end a persuasive text by returning to our opinion, naming a believable future and calling our audience to action.

## Success criteria

- I can identify a return to the claim, a possible future and a next action in a conclusion.
- I can explain why a possible future is stronger than an unsupported promise.
- I can write and revise a conclusion with all three jobs.

## Preparation

- Open `Lesson_29_Harbour_Garden_Hopeful_Conclusion_Presentation.html` and print the matching organiser or concise-access pack.
- Provide each writer's existing persuasive plan and checked research notes. The fictional reading is a craft model, never evidence for a student's claim.
- The core route is 50 minutes. DO, WORK, RECORD, FINISH and CHECK details are in the presentation Notes drawer.

## Learning sequence

1. **Ending choice - 4 min:** compare a vague promise with a conclusion that asks for a realistic action.
2. **Purpose - 2 min:** establish the return -> future -> action route.
3. **Encounter - 7 min:** read *The Harbour Garden Proposal* and trace the writer's conclusion.
4. **Guided noticing - 6 min:** classify conclusion moves as claim, future or action; justify one placement.
5. **Explicit model - 6 min:** model how conditional language makes a future believable.
6. **Compare - 4 min:** choose the stronger ending and name the missing job.
7. **Plan - 5 min:** make the three conclusion decisions for an individual claim.
8. **Conclusion studio - 9 min:** write 2-4 sentences and mark the three jobs.
9. **Peer rehearsal and revision - 5 min:** listener identifies the action and future; writer revises one vague phrase.
10. **Exit - 2 min:** write one action, one future and the credibility choice that makes it believable.

## Interaction matrix

| Moment | Cognitive demand | Evidence | Teacher signal | Tier 2 hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ending choice | Compare credibility | A reason for the choice | Students distinguish hope from guarantee | Look for a real action and a word such as *could*. |
| Three-job sort | Classify and justify | Each placement plus a rule | Students can locate conclusion jobs | Ask: does it repeat the position, show a future, or tell someone what to do? |
| Peer test | Evaluate and revise | One marked revision | Writer can state what a reader heard | If the reader cannot say who acts, add the audience and a verb. |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence launches, oral rehearsal, shared model and one conclusion job at a time.
- **Concise access route:** point, speak, draw, copy or scribe a claim, possible future and action. The red-green presentation switch changes projected language without naming a pathway.
- **Extension:** add a controlled conditional clause and acknowledge one realistic condition without introducing unsupported evidence.

## Assessment and boundaries

The final conclusion, peer revision and exit are evidence for the success criteria and identify writers needing a conference before drafting. Harbourview and all article details are fictional. Students must use their own checked sources for assessment evidence and must not invent facts, statistics, quotations or outcomes.
