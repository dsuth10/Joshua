ORIGINAL FICTIONAL READING

When the Lantern Went Dark

“The important turning point was not a dramatic leap onto the jetty. It was Nia choosing a safe action that made expert help possible.”“Nia did not jump onto the jetty. She chose a safe action so trained people could help.”

As you read, track: danger → decision → consequence.As you read, track: danger → choice → what changed.

DO Distribute reading pack and read aloud, paired or independently.

WORK Read the original text.

RECORD Underline one precise decision and circle its consequence.

FINISH Students can locate Nia’s decision.

CHECK This text is fictional; do not treat it as real-world emergency advice or evidence.

EXPLICIT MODEL

From a moment in the story to a claim for a readerFrom the story to a big idea

EVENT

Nia calls for trained help.

CONSEQUENCE

The crew can free the dinghy safely.

CLAIM

Courage can mean taking the responsible next step even when you feel afraid.

SO WHAT?

A reader may rethink bravery as safe care, not a dramatic risk.

CFU: Which box stops this becoming a plot summary?

DO Reveal the four boxes one at a time with the show-answer control if needed.

WORK Whole class, then oral rehearsal.

RECORD Label E, C, Claim and So what in the organiser.

FINISH Students identify that the claim makes a broader idea.

CHECK Accept different claims only when they connect to the event and consequence.
