# Lesson 25: Climax and Resolution

## Lesson purpose

Students analyse the climax and early resolution of *Berani* (pp. 147–171): Ginger Juice’s rescue, Ari’s difficult decision and Malia’s emerging blog purpose. They move from a precise event to an evidence-grounded claim about bravery (*berani*) which anticipates assessment writing.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** Plan, create and edit written persuasive texts.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** Plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs.

## Learning intention

We are learning to explain how a turning point changes characters and develops an idea about bravery.

## Success criteria

- I can identify a precise climax event and its consequence.
- I can explain how Ari’s action involves risk as well as care.
- I can connect Malia’s activism and blog idea to the rescue storyline.
- I can make an evidence-grounded claim about bravery / *berani*.
- I can explain why that claim matters to a reader.

## Finished workbook response

A 6–8 sentence climax-to-claim paragraph that connects one event from pp. 147–171 to its consequence, a claim about bravery and a reason a blog reader should care.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation in a modern browser and choose fullscreen.
- Print the appropriate organiser; students need their English workbooks and *Berani*.
- The 50-minute core uses pp. 147–171. Teacher logistics live only in the Notes popup.

## Core sequence

1. Before → turning point → after — 4 min
2. Trace the change — 2 min
3. Ginger Juice: urgency — 5 min
4. Ari: brave does not mean unafraid — 5 min
5. Malia: future voice — 5 min
6. Sequence the climax — 6 min
7. Compare forms of bravery — 4 min
8. Model event to claim — 5 min
9. Plan — 4 min
10. Write — 10 min
11. Revise — 3 min
12. Exit evidence — 2 min

## Reading and accuracy boundary

The package uses only verified details from *Berani*: Ginger Juice’s condition (pp. 147–149), Ari’s report to the rescue organisation (pp. 149–153), Malia’s plan for a blog (pp. 153–155), the rescue (pp. 157–161), Malia’s reflection and intended blog post (pp. 165–169), and Ari’s conversation with Uncle after the rescue (pp. 169–171). Claims about bravery are interpretations, not facts or the novel’s single stated message. Do not imply certainty about later outcomes or assessment results.

## Differentiation

**Support:** supply one event and consequence; orally rehearse “When ___, this changes ___”; offer claim stems.

**Lucas (ICP):** use the low-writing organiser. Students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe to match a person with a caring action and complete “Brave is ___.” The same destination remains: connect action to care.

**Extend:** compare Ari’s private risk with Malia’s public advocacy, and qualify a theme claim with “can” or “may”.

## Formative assessment

The sequence sort shows whether students recognise the causal shape of the climax. The final paragraph and exit sentence show whether they can move from recounting events to an evidence-grounded theme claim, a necessary bridge to the assessment task’s developed persuasive ideas.

## Discussion guidance

Keep discussion anchored in the fictional text; do not ask students to disclose experiences of animal harm, conflict or activism. Accept varied claims when their evidence and reasoning are sound.
