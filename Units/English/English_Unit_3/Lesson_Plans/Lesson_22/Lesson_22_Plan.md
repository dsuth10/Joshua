# Lesson 22: Identifying Bias

## Lesson purpose

Students identify how Uncle Kus’s language positions the reader in his defence of keeping Ginger Juice at Warung Malang, then rewrite a biased classroom paragraph in neutral, objective language.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LA02: acknowledge differing ideas and opinions.**
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LA02: identify bias in persuasive texts.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to recognise how language and omission can position a reader, and to report a viewpoint fairly in neutral language.

## Success criteria

- I can locate a precise word or claim in Uncle Kus’s speech.
- I can explain what his language foregrounds and what it does not address.
- I can distinguish a reported viewpoint from a verifiable detail.
- I can rewrite a biased statement without adding my own unsupported judgement.

## Finished workbook response

A 6–8 sentence neutral account of Uncle Kus’s position, using two accurate details and explaining one positioning choice.

## Preparation

- Open Lesson_22_Presentation.html in a modern browser and choose fullscreen.
- Print the appropriate organiser and provide English workbooks.
- Core route: 50 minutes; teacher logistics remain in the Notes popup.

## Core sequence

1. **Same facts. Different pull. — 4 minutes.** Students recognise that selection and framing can steer a response.
2. **Name the steer, then remove it. — 2 minutes.** Introduce the finished response.
3. **What Uncle Kus says — 5 minutes.** Locate ownership and business concerns in Ari pp. 109–113.
4. **Bias leaves fingerprints — 5 minutes.** Annotate word choice, priority and omission.
5. **A viewpoint is not a fact — 4 minutes.** Separate a reported claim from a verifiable chapter detail.
6. **Sort by the evidence — 6 minutes.** Classify supported readings and claims that go beyond the text.
7. **Model a fair report — 5 minutes.** Compare a teacher-created biased paraphrase with a neutral rewrite.
8. **Use a neutral toolkit — 3 minutes.** Plan speaker, claim and accurate detail.
9. **Write a neutral account — 12 minutes.** Complete the workbook response.
10. **Check precision — 4 minutes.** Revise one loaded or mind-reading phrase.
11. **Exit evidence — 2 minutes.** Explain a positioning choice and supply a neutral version.

## Support

- Provide the exact chapter lines and organiser evidence boxes.
- Permit oral rehearsal using: “Uncle Kus argues that ___ because ___.”
- Use the report-verb bank before independent writing.

## Lucas (ICP)

- Use the large-print organiser; students may point, speak, copy or use a partner scribe.
- Preserve the destination: identify what someone says and create one fair sentence about it.

## Formative assessment

The sort reveals whether students confuse a strong viewpoint with proof. The exit sentence shows whether they can move from a positioning choice to a neutral report.

## Teacher reading fallback

After Ginger Juice has been harmed, Ari challenges Uncle Kus about the cage. Uncle focuses on ownership and the possibility of losing restaurant customers if Ginger Juice leaves. Earlier chapters establish that she is held at Warung Malang and described as an attraction. Keep the distinction between a character’s claim and the narration’s details clear.

## Accuracy boundary

The teacher-created biased sentence in the model is a paraphrase for revision practice, not a quotation from *Berani*. Do not ask students to disclose personal experiences of animal harm or family conflict.
