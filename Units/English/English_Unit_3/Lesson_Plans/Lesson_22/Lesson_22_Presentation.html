BERANI • LESSON 22

Bias has a direction

How can language steer a reader before it gives them the full picture?

LANGUAGE LENS • 50-MINUTE CORE
ENTRY

Same facts. Different pull.

4 MIN
Two writers can select the same event but guide us toward different conclusions. Today we will spot the steering and revise it.
PURPOSE

Our work: name the steer, then remove it.

2 MIN
notice the language → explain the position → write a neutral version

Finished response: a neutral, objective account of Uncle Kus’s position.

TEXT

What Uncle Kus says

5 MIN
“She belongs to me! … What would the restaurant patrons think if they came and she was gone? We would lose customers!”

Ari, pp. 109–113. Read this as Uncle Kus’s position, not as the novel’s final judgement.

NOTICE

Bias leaves fingerprints

5 MIN
WORD CHOICE“belongs to me” makes ownership the centre.
WHAT MATTERSCustomers and lost business are foregrounded.
WHAT IS MISSINGGinger Juice’s welfare and captivity are not addressed.
COMPARE

A viewpoint is not a fact

4 MIN
VIEWPOINT / CLAIMUncle says the restaurant would lose customers if Ginger Juice were gone.
VERIFIABLE DETAILGinger Juice is kept at Warung Malang, where she is described as an attraction.

Which sentence reports a position? Which sentence reports a detail?

SORT

Sort by the evidence, not the volume

6 MIN

SUPPORTED READING

GOES BEYOND THE TEXT

MODEL

Model: keep the speaker; remove the steer

5 MIN

Uncle Kus selfishly keeps his poor mascot trapped because he only cares about money.

Neutral rewrite: Uncle Kus argues that Ginger Juice should remain at the restaurant because her absence could affect customer numbers. His statement focuses on ownership and the restaurant’s business concerns.

Notice: the rewrite reports his position. It does not add a judgement or erase the conflict.

PLAN

Use a neutral toolkit

3 MIN
Report verbs: says • argues • states • focuses on
Keep: who is speaking + what they claim + a precise detail
Remove: mind-reading • loaded labels • claims the text cannot prove
WRITE

Write: a neutral account

12 MIN

6–8 sentences

  • Identify Uncle Kus as the speaker
  • Report his position about Ginger Juice and the restaurant
  • Use two accurate details from the chapter
  • Explain one way his language positions the reader
  • Rewrite without loaded judgement or invented motives
REVISE

Check: precise, fair, objective

4 MIN
□ I named the speaker. □ I reported the claim accurately. □ I used two details. □ I named a positioning choice. □ I removed loaded judgement. □ I did not invent a motive.
EXIT

One sentence, two lenses.

2 MIN
Uncle Kus’s language positions the reader by __________. A neutral version would say __________.
