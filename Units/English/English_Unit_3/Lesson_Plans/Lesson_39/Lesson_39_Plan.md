# Lesson 39: Spoken Presentations (Session 2)

## Lesson purpose

Students in the second presentation group deliver their individual persuasive presentations. Audience members practise active listening by recording one claim, one authoritative source actually cited and the connection between them. A brief, respectful question option extends listening without turning peer feedback into assessment.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** create persuasive texts that develop and expand ideas with supporting details for particular purposes and audiences.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create persuasive texts using language features and structures appropriate to audience and purpose.

## Learning intention

We are learning to present an individual opinion clearly and listen accurately for how evidence supports a claim.

## Success criteria

- I can deliver my individual opinion with a clear structure and purposeful voice choices.
- I can identify a claim that a speaker actually makes.
- I can record an authoritative source only when the speaker identifies it.
- I can explain how a named detail supports the speaker’s claim.
- I can ask, answer or pass on a respectful, connected question.

## Assessment alignment

Part B is an **individual** presentation of an opinion for an identified audience; its suggested length is **1–3 minutes**. Students use supporting details, including research and authoritative sources, and features of voice. Teacher observation remains the assessment evidence; audience records are listening artefacts, not peer marks.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation; teacher logistics are in the Notes popup.
- Have the relevant student presentations, approved access supports and teacher recording sheet/process ready.
- Print the organiser or provide an equivalent claim–source–connection record.
- Confirm presentation order, audience behaviour and whether a short question window will be used.

## Core sequence (55–65 min, adaptable to presentation numbers)

1. Listening entry — 3 min
2. Re-establish active-listening agreements — 4 min
3. Model the claim–source–connection record — 5 min
4. Responsible-listening decision — 4 min
5. Individual presentations: second group — 25–35 min
6. Mid-session reset — 2 min
7. Optional selected respectful questions — 5 min
8. Accurate listening reflection — 5 min
9. Exit — 3 min

## Evidence and authorship boundary

No example student source, quotation, statistic, organisation or claim is supplied in this package. Audience members record only what a speaker actually states. A blank or “not heard” field is more accurate than a guessed detail. Students retain ownership of their own assessed work; peer questions and records do not create content for a speaker.

## Differentiation

**Support:** give a reduced organiser with picture/word cues for *claim*, *source* and *connection*; allow a student to point, dictate or record a single listening observation.

**Lucas (ICP):** use a three-part visual organiser; allow selecting one heard idea, a supported response mode, a partner scribe or a brief break. The shared destination is attending to an opinion and one supporting detail.

**Extend:** ask students to distinguish the source from the speaker’s interpretation, then pose one concise question about how the detail supports the claim.

## Formative assessment

The responsible-listening decision and organiser reveal whether students can distinguish accurate capture from guesswork. Teacher notes during delivery focus on the assessed speaker’s structure, elaboration, sources, language and voice. Exits inform the final unit reflection.

## Safety and teacher decisions

Keep presentation topics school/community-safe. Pre-select questioners and make passing valid. Use local participation and access arrangements; do not require students to disclose their own topic judgments or publicly critique a peer’s opinion.
