# Lesson 15: The Language Balance

## Lesson purpose

Students distinguish objective information from subjective positioning in the principal's meeting, then explain how both kinds of language shape a reader's judgement.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA06: understand how vocabulary choices add precision and shape viewpoint.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA06: understand how language choices expand ideas and position a reader.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to distinguish objective information from subjective positioning and explain the effect of each choice.

## Success criteria

- I can identify language that can be checked independently.
- I can identify judgement, emotion and loaded wording.
- I can explain that a statement may contain both objective and subjective elements.
- I can compare how Mr Ahmad and Malia position the issue.
- I can revise one unsupported classification after checking the wording.

## Finished workbook response

A Language Balance analysis: classify six statements from the meeting, then write 6-8 sentences explaining how objective and subjective language position the reader.

## Core sequence

### 1. Neutral—or only neutral-sounding? - 4 minutes

Classify each statement before revealing: objective, subjective or mixed.

- **Student action:** Independent, then pair; Organiser: first three rows.
- **Finished when:** Three decisions with one reason each.
- **Teacher note:** Expose the misconception that objective means true and subjective means false. Objective claims still require verification.

### 2. Judge the language—not the speaker - 2 minutes

Write one test you can use when a statement is difficult.

- **Student action:** Partner rehearsal; Organiser: decision rule.
- **Finished when:** One usable test.
- **Teacher note:** Keep fact/opinion language available, but introduce objective/subjective/mixed as the more precise continuum.

### 3. One meeting. Competing frames. - 5 minutes

Collect two checkable details and two evaluative word choices.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: evidence bank.
- **Finished when:** Four accurately placed details.
- **Teacher note:** Students should notice that the same meeting contains shared events but different interpretations.

### 4. Place the statement—then defend the boundary - 6 minutes

Classify all six. For the mixed statement, underline the claim and circle the framing word.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: classification table.
- **Finished when:** Six placements and one language annotation.
- **Teacher note:** Treat 'provides prosperity' as mixed because it makes a potentially checkable economic claim through broad positive evaluation.

### 5. What job does the wording perform? - 4 minutes

Choose one word from each frame and explain its effect.

- **Student action:** Independent, then compare; Organiser: effect row.
- **Finished when:** Three word-effect links.
- **Teacher note:** Move beyond spotting: students must explain positioning. Accept other defensible effect verbs.

### 6. A balanced analysis does not become neutral mush - 5 minutes

Find evidence, effect and nuance before revealing the labels.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one sentence move to imitate.
- **Finished when:** Three model decisions identified.
- **Teacher note:** This is an original teacher model, not a quotation. Emphasise that balance means fair analysis, not forced equivalence.

### 7. Build the explanation before the paragraph - 4 minutes

Plan one detail from Mr Ahmad and one from Malia, then write the comparison insight.

- **Student action:** Independent; Organiser: analysis planner.
- **Finished when:** Two evidence-effect chains and one comparison.
- **Teacher note:** Conference first with students who have labels but no explanation of reader positioning.

### 8. Write the Language Balance analysis - 12 minutes

Write the analysis using your organiser.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** One complete evidence-based analysis.
- **Teacher note:** Students may use the model structure but must select and explain their own evidence.

### 9. Rebalance the reasoning - 4 minutes

Mark every check and revise one weak classification or effect explanation.

- **Student action:** Independent; Organiser and paragraph.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Use the revision to diagnose whether students can move beyond fact/opinion spotting.

### 10. One claim. One frame. One effect. - 2 minutes

Complete the sentence with a fresh example from the meeting.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One justified classification and effect.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Sort exit responses by confusion about classification versus confusion about effect.


## Optional depth slides

### Rewrite the frame, preserve the event - 8 minutes

Rewrite two subjective or mixed statements in more objective language.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Two rewrites and one effect comment.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Students should recognise that objective wording can reduce viewpoint but cannot supply missing evidence.

### Source check: objective is not automatically proven - 8 minutes

Audit the claim that the industry provides prosperity.

- **Student action:** Small groups; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Claim, named source and missing evidence.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Keep this as a source-evaluation exercise using only what the chapter identifies.
