# Lesson 10: Building Bridges Through Time

## Lesson purpose

Students connect Ari's earlier relationship with Ginger Juice to his present recognition that her cage is too small. They use controlled tense and purposeful subordinating conjunctions to write a Then-Now character paragraph.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA03: describe how written texts use language features and are organised into stages.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA03: explain how authors adapt text structures and language choices for purpose.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to connect past and present ideas using controlled tense and purposeful complex sentences.

## Success criteria

- I can identify relevant Then and Now evidence about Ari.
- I can choose although, since or while for the relationship I intend.
- I can repair a fragment, an illogical conjunction and an accidental tense shift.
- I can write a 5-7 sentence Then-Now paragraph with at least three complex sentences.
- I can independently revise one sentence after a bridge inspection.

## Finished workbook response

A 5-7 sentence Then-Now character paragraph about Ari, using at least three purposeful complex sentences and controlled tense.

## Agreed delivery model

- Teacher controls the projected HTML presentation.
- Students complete the organiser and all substantial composition in physical English workbooks.
- The deck contains no student composition fields.
- The chapter is assumed to have been read. Use the fallback summary only when required.
- Core route: approximately 50 minutes.
- Both optional depth slides are included and may extend the lesson towards 70 minutes.
- Revision is primarily independent through the projected self-check.

## Core sequence

### 1. Which details belong to Then—and which belong to Now? - 4 minutes

Sort all ten details, then circle the detail that best explains Ari's change.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: Then / Now banks.
- **Finished when:** Ten details placed and one circled.
- **Teacher note:** Students place cards in their organiser before the teacher moves them on screen. Ask which Now detail shows recognition and which shows continuing misunderstanding. Reading fallback: use the three-sentence summary in the plan.

### 2. Build a relationship—not just a timeline - 2 minutes

Complete aloud: I will connect ___ from the past to ___ in the present.

- **Student action:** Partner rehearsal; No writing yet.
- **Finished when:** Both partners can state the relationship.
- **Teacher note:** Keep this brisk. The product is explanation, not a chronological retell. Transition directly to pairing the evidence.

### 3. Pair one Then detail with one Now detail - 7 minutes

Make two pairs. Each pair needs one Then detail, one Now detail and a relationship label.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: two evidence pairs.
- **Finished when:** Two evidence pairs, each with a relationship label.
- **Teacher note:** Invite more than one defensible pairing. A strong pairing connects family-like treatment in the past with confinement in the present. Ask students to name contrast, reason or time together before choosing a conjunction.

### 4. Change the conjunction. Change the relationship. - 5 minutes

Write two versions. Underline each conjunction and label its job.

- **Student action:** Pairs, then class comparison; Organiser: bridge sentences.
- **Finished when:** Two grammatical versions and two job labels.
- **Teacher note:** Although is likely the best relationship for the displayed clauses, but students may restructure for since or while. Do not accept a sentence merely because it is grammatical.

### 5. Diagnose the first fault. Then repair it. - 6 minutes

Name each fault, repair it, then click to compare the diagnosis.

- **Student action:** Independent first; class check; Organiser: Sentence Surgery.
- **Finished when:** Three repaired sentences.
- **Teacher note:** Reveal only after students have written. Accept multiple repairs if meaning and tense are controlled. The important misconception is that a conjunction can be inserted without considering logic.

### 6. Watch one paragraph carry Then into Now - 5 minutes

Read the whole model. Predict each sentence's job before clicking it.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one model decision on the organiser.
- **Finished when:** One decision selected for imitation.
- **Teacher note:** Read fluently before annotating. Ask why the final sentence prevents the paragraph becoming a simple 'Ari has changed' response.

### 7. Write Ari's Then–Now paragraph - 12 minutes

Write the complete paragraph using your bridge evidence.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** 5-7 complete sentences.
- **Teacher note:** Do not type student composition into the deck. Confer first with students whose organiser contains two facts but no relationship.

### 8. Bridge inspection - 4 minutes

Mark each check. Revise one sentence before you stop.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook paragraph.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Quiet self-check. Select two examples only after revision. Avoid turning this into peer feedback.

### 9. Box your strongest bridge - 3 minutes

Box your strongest complex sentence. Explain the conjunction's job beneath it.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One boxed sentence and one explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Use exit explanations to decide whether the next grammar lesson needs a short logic reteach.


## Optional depth slides

### Malia's time bridge is harder to see - 10 minutes

Build one Malia bridge sentence and explain why the link is less obvious.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Workbook margin or organiser back.
- **Finished when:** One sentence plus one explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. This provides transfer without becoming the independent product. Keep discussion grounded in Malia's experience in the text.

### One idea. Three conjunctions. Three meanings. - 8 minutes

Write all three versions, then defend the most accurate.

- **Student action:** Independent, then compare; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Three versions and one defended choice.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Since and while can both work with restructuring; although requires a genuine contrast. Reward explanation, not one predetermined answer.


## Support

- Reduce the number of initial choices without removing the central decision.
- Permit oral rehearsal before workbook writing.
- Provide optional sentence launches on the projected writing brief.
- Conference first with students whose organiser contains details but no relationship or interpretation.
