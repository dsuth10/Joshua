# Lesson 14: Double Vision

## Lesson purpose

Students compare Ari's interpretation of the chessboard interaction with Ginger Juice's sensory experience. They write two evidence-grounded internal monologues that reveal difference, overlap and the communication barrier.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA06: understand how grammatical and vocabulary choices expand description and viewpoint.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA06: understand how verb and adverb choices expand ideas and character perspective.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to represent two contrasting perspectives on the same moment while preserving what each character can and cannot know.

## Success criteria

- I can classify details as Ari's interpretation, Ginger Juice's experience or genuine overlap.
- I can explain the misunderstanding around the chessboard.
- I can distinguish supported thought, reasonable inference and impossible mind-reading.
- I can write two distinct internal monologues grounded in evidence.
- I can include one meaningful overlap and revise one viewpoint slip.

## Finished workbook response

A Dual Internal Monologue: 5-7 sentences from Ari followed by 5-7 sentences from Ginger Juice, both describing the same chessboard moment.

## Core sequence

### 1. One moment. Two accounts. - 3 minutes

Classify all six details. Do not check until your organiser is complete.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: Double-Vision Board.
- **Finished when:** Six classified details.
- **Teacher note:** Some details are simplified for retrieval. The next slides complicate watching and touch. Use the fallback summary when needed.

### 2. Reveal the gap - 2 minutes

Complete: Ari thinks ___, while Ginger Juice ___.

- **Student action:** Partner rehearsal; Organiser opening line.
- **Finished when:** One accurate contrast.
- **Teacher note:** Establish perspective as selected attention, knowledge and interpretation.

### 3. Place the clear details first - 5 minutes

Complete all three columns and add one textual clue for each.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: three-column board.
- **Finished when:** Three columns with evidence clues.
- **Teacher note:** Require evidence before adding class ideas to the projected board.

### 4. They both watch the chessboard—but not the same thing - 4 minutes

Write what each character thinks the watching means.

- **Student action:** Independent, then compare; Organiser: difficult detail row 1.
- **Finished when:** Two different meanings.
- **Teacher note:** This is the central misconception. Avoid saying Ginger Juice has no intelligence; the text shows different attention and communication.

### 5. Touch creates real comfort—and unequal understanding - 4 minutes

Record one honest overlap and one important difference.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: difficult detail row 2.
- **Finished when:** One overlap and one difference.
- **Teacher note:** This nuance prevents a one-right-answer judgement and gives both monologues emotional truth.

### 6. Supported thought, inference—or impossible mind-reading? - 3 minutes

Label each claim supported, inferred or impossible for that character to know.

- **Student action:** Independent first; reveal; Organiser: evidence boundary.
- **Finished when:** Three labels and one explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Keep the distinction between authorial evidence and character knowledge explicit.

### 7. Same moment. Different inner worlds. - 5 minutes

Read both models. Identify belief, experience and what the reader understands.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one voice distinction on the organiser.
- **Finished when:** One selected voice decision.
- **Teacher note:** Read both monologues before revealing labels. Discuss how the reader can recognise misunderstanding and real connection simultaneously.

### 8. Mirror the moment—not the meaning - 3 minutes

Plan each voice and the shared moment that connects them.

- **Student action:** Independent; Organiser: mirrored plan.
- **Finished when:** Four notes per voice and one overlap.
- **Teacher note:** Confer with students whose two plans merely swap pronouns.

### 9. Write the Dual Internal Monologue - 13 minutes

Write both internal monologues.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** Two complete, distinct voices.
- **Teacher note:** Students may use the mirrored model as a structural guide but must create their own monologues.

### 10. Clear the double image - 4 minutes

Mark every check and revise one voice slip or unsupported thought.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook monologues.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Selected examples should include one successful overlap, not only sharp contrast.

### 11. State the gap in one sentence - 2 minutes

Complete the sentence with one difference and one overlap.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One complete three-part sentence.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Use omissions to diagnose whether students understand overlap as well as contrast.


## Optional depth slides

### Add the reader's third perspective - 10 minutes

Write a 3-4 sentence reader-perspective explanation.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** One evidence-based explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. This shifts from creative voice to literary analysis.

### Write the hinge between voices - 8 minutes

Draft two hinge options and choose the cleaner shift.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** Two options and one selected hinge.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. The hinge can be used between the final monologues if it remains unobtrusive.


## Support

- Reduce the number of initial choices without removing the central decision.
- Permit oral rehearsal before workbook writing.
- Provide optional sentence launches on the projected writing brief.
- Conference first with students whose organiser contains details but no relationship or interpretation.

## Teacher reading fallback

Ari believes Ginger Juice follows or approves of his chess play. Ginger Juice watches patterns and colours but does not understand his human words. Ari's touch provides warmth and helps keep the haze away. Both experience comfort, but they interpret the moment differently.

## Accuracy and privacy boundaries
