# Lesson 37: Rehearsal and Active Listening

## Lesson purpose

Students rehearse an excerpt from their individual spoken persuasive presentation with a partner. They practise active listening, provide one specific delivery observation and a clarifying question, then make and test one targeted revision.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations using language features and voice for effect.

## Learning intention

We are learning to rehearse for an audience by listening actively, using useful feedback and making one deliberate delivery revision.

## Success criteria

- I can use my own cue cards to rehearse an opinion and supporting argument.
- I can listen for clarity, voice, audience connection or a question I still have.
- I can give feedback that is specific, kind and useful.
- I can ask one respectful question about an idea, evidence or next step.
- I can make and explain one immediate revision.

## Assessment alignment

Part B is an **individual** spoken persuasive presentation, suggested at **1–3 minutes**. Students use their written persuasive text as a guide, not a script; rehearse with a partner or small group; ask and answer questions; and make purposeful voice choices. This lesson collects rehearsal evidence, not the final assessed delivery.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation; recurring DO, WORK, RECORD, FINISH and CHECK logistics are in the Notes popup only.
- Provide students’ cue cards/speaking guides, a listener-feedback organiser and a visual timer.
- Establish partner locations and a respectful listening protocol. Offer a trusted-adult or small-group option.

## Core sequence (45 min)

1. Active-listening entry — 3 min
2. Purpose and rehearsal route — 3 min
3. Evaluate feedback choices — 4 min
4. Speaker/listener roles — 4 min
5. Listening lenses — 4 min
6. Model question protocol — 4 min
7. Round 1 rehearsal — 8 min
8. Targeted revision planning — 4 min
9. Round 2 immediate retry — 8 min
10. Optional Q&A extension — 3 min
11. Exit promise — 3 min

## Model and evidence boundary

Model lines demonstrate listening language and delivery only. They are not research evidence or speech content to copy. Students use their own negotiated topic, checked evidence and identified audience. A listener must not invent content, statistics, quotations, sources or outcomes for the speaker.

## Differentiation

**Support:** use cue cards with larger type; offer a visual timer; select one listening lens; give oral or scribed feedback; rehearse a shorter opening and one reason.

**Lucas (ICP):** present a two-sentence opinion to a small group of three peers or a trusted adult. Lucas may use symbol cues, pointing, speech, assistive technology or a recording. The listener may respond orally, with a choice card or through a scribe.

**Extend:** facilitate a brief Q&A, defend a stance with already checked evidence and respond responsibly when an answer needs checking.

## Formative assessment

The listener record, question, observation of the second attempt and exit promise show whether students can turn feedback into an observable audience-focused revision. Use these to confirm readiness and support needs before Lesson 38 presentations.

## Safety and ownership

Keep feedback descriptive and kind. Eye contact is an invitation, not a compliance measure: students may use an audience-facing focus point. Follow school procedures for recording. Each speaker remains the author and owner of their individual assessment response.
