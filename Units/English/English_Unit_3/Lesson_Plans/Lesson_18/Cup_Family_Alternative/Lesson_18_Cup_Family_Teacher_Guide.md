| Item | Guidance |

| Audience | Years 5-6 |

| Final product | 9-11 sentence open letter to a fictional cafe family |
| Explicit technique | FAIR bridge: shared goal, fair concern, evidence, workable recommendation |

## Central teaching point

Responsible persuasion is not made weaker by accurately representing a concern. A fair concession can increase credibility because it shows that the writer understands the decision's human cost. The response must then answer that concern with relevant evidence and a proportionate action.

Keep three distinctions visible:

1. **understanding is not agreement**;
2. **acknowledgement is not surrender**;
3. **a trial is evidence-gathering, not proof of a guaranteed outcome**.

## Reading answers

### Values and responsibilities (V)

- Leila: environmental responsibility, avoiding waste, community leadership, being heard;
- Aunt Samira: family livelihood, wages, safe service, queue speed, fairness to staff and customers;
- shared: a successful, welcoming, responsible cafe that can keep serving the community.

### Genuine concerns (C)

- dirty or damaged customer cups;
- cross-contamination and a clear refusal procedure;
- slower handover during the morning rush;
- customers who forget or cannot use a reusable cup;
- setup, cleaning and replacement costs for a mug-return system;
- staff training and workload;
- uncertainty about local participation.

### Evidence (E)

- Queensland guidance suggests incentives for reusable cups or drinking in store;
- Queensland guidance suggests a mug-return program, possibly with a deposit;
- FSANZ says businesses may decide whether to accept customer reusable cups;
- FSANZ says businesses should use a policy and consider cleaning, sanitising and cross-contamination;
- businesses are responsible for keeping food safe and suitable;
- local disposal pathways differ; Brisbane currently directs coffee cups to participating drop-off points, illustrating why students should not assume every cup belongs in every yellow bin.

### Techniques and framing (T)

- "choosing convenience over the planet" uses accusation and a false binary;
- "protecting jobs" foregrounds livelihood and may minimise the environmental issue;
- "we all want" uses inclusive language and frames common ground;
- the rhetorical question in the model invites a testable action;
- **should** makes a firm recommendation; **may/can** controls uncertainty;
- the triad "count disposable cups, staff concerns and customer participation" creates rhythm while specifying measures.

## FAIR bridge answer guidance

| Move | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | What do writer and audience both value? | A welcoming, safe and responsible family cafe |
| Acknowledge | What real concern must be represented? | Safe service and morning workflow cannot be ignored |
| Introduce | Which evidence answers that concern? | Businesses can set a cup policy and manage contamination risk |
| Recommend | What proportionate action follows? | A four-week trial with a handover rule, refusal safeguard and measures |

## Straw-man check

Reject:

- "My family only cares about money."
- "Anyone against my plan wants the planet covered in rubbish."
- "The cafe refuses to change because it is lazy."

Accept as fair acknowledgement:

- "The family must pay staff and keep service moving."
- "A reusable-cup system needs a hygiene policy."
- "Not every customer will arrive with a suitable cup."

## Technique lab guidance

Techniques should be judged against audience and purpose, not counted as decorations.

- **Inclusive language** is useful because it reduces an us-versus-them frame.
- **Should** is appropriate for the main recommendation; **must** should be reserved for genuine safety duties or an intentionally urgent claim.
- A **rhetorical question** is effective when it opens a practical possibility, not when it humiliates the audience.
- **Emotive language** can establish importance, but loaded attacks reduce fairness and credibility.

## Evidence-boundary answers

1. Queensland guidance suggests incentives or mug-return programs. - **Official evidence**
2. A well-designed trial may reduce disposable-cup use without unacceptable delay. - **Reasonable inference**; local evidence is still needed.
3. No cafe cup will ever enter landfill. - **Overclaim**; not every customer, cup or disposal pathway is controlled.

| Sentence | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | shared goal / inclusive language |
| 2 | clear position and trial scope |
| 3 | fair acknowledgement |
| 4 | pivot without dismissing the concern |
| 5-6 | authoritative evidence relevant to action and safety |
| 7 | evidence boundary |
| 8 | rhetorical question plus concrete safeguard |
| 9 | inclusive language plus measurable trial |
| 10 | qualified call to action |

## Feedback and assessment

### Quick conference questions

- Where have you shown what the family values?
- Is that the concern they actually have, or an easier version to attack?
- Which evidence answers that exact concern?
- What does your chosen technique make this audience think or feel?
- What will happen during the four weeks?
- What would the family count or observe?
- Which word prevents your claim from becoming a guarantee?

### Formative coding

- **FAIR+** - all four bridge moves are causally connected;
- **VIEW+** - both viewpoints are represented accurately;
- **EVID+** - evidence supports the exact claim;
- **TECH+** - techniques suit the family audience and purpose;
- **BOUND+** - the trial's limits are explicit;
- **ACT+** - action, safeguard and measure are concrete.

### Exit grouping

- **Secure:** concession is accurate and followed by relevant evidence/action.
- **Developing:** fair concern is present but the response does not answer it.
- **Support needed:** straw man, personal attack or evidence-free promise.

## Pacing fallback

If reading takes longer than nine minutes:

1. complete the viewpoint comparison orally;
2. teach FAIR using the model;
3. omit the full technique lab and require only inclusive language plus modality;
4. preserve at least ten minutes for writing and four minutes for revision.

The non-negotiable learning is the FAIR bridge and visible revision.

## Lucas / ICP facilitation

- Read the shortened scenario aloud while the student tracks.
- Offer two viewpoint cards and allow pointing or eye-gaze selection.
- Build FAIR orally using four large cards.
- Scribe exactly what the student communicates, then offer one accuracy choice such as **will / may**.
- Keep the genuine family concern. Reduced volume must not remove fairness.
- Accept a four-to-six sentence spoken, typed or scribed letter if this matches authoritative adjustments.
