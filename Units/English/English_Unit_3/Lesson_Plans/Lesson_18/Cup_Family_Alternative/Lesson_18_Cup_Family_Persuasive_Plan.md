# Lesson 18 Alternative: One Cup, Two Loyalties

### Pedagogical Contemplation

1. **Cognitive goal:** Students evaluate how a persuasive writer can represent a genuine opposing concern, connect relevant evidence to that concern and recommend an action whose strength matches the evidence.
2. **Interactive alignment:** The fairness choice forces students to distinguish an accurate concession from a straw man. The evidence-boundary classification forces them to separate sourced fact, reasonable inference and overclaim. The staged model reveal slows attention so students identify the job of each sentence before transferring the structure to their own writing.
3. **Surfacing student thinking:** Mini-whiteboard commitments and clickable classifications make misconceptions visible before answers are revealed. Two-tier feedback distinguishes a first error from a repeated misconception, while the teacher override supports immediate reteaching without turning the interaction into guessing.
4. **Pedagogical versus engagement goal:** The pedagogical purpose is accurate viewpoint representation, evidence calibration and deliberate transfer into writing. Colour, reveal and click behaviours make those decisions visible and manageable; they are not the learning goal themselves.

## Lesson purpose

Students read an original fictional feature about a young environmental advocate and her family's cafe. They examine how a strong persuasive response can protect an ethical position without caricaturing family members whose livelihood, workload and food-safety responsibilities are affected. Students explicitly learn the **FAIR bridge** - Frame a shared goal, Acknowledge a fair concern, Introduce relevant evidence and Recommend a workable next step - then write and revise an open letter proposing a four-week reusable-cup trial.

This is a supplemental, non-novel alternative. It preserves Lesson 18's tension between personal ethics, activism and family expectations without using *Berani* or its characters.

## Transfer from the original lesson

The original lesson asks students to understand a decision that is complicated by family loyalty and consequences for other people. This alternative transfers the same thinking to a new fictional situation:

- **ethical position:** Leila wants the family cafe to reduce avoidable disposable-cup waste;
- **family expectation:** she is expected to understand the pressure of a small morning business;
- **competing responsibility:** the cafe must protect food safety, service speed, accessibility and income;
- **persuasive challenge:** Leila must argue firmly without presenting her family as uncaring or promising that one change will solve waste.

## Curriculum alignment

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LE03:** recognise that viewpoint influences how readers interpret and respond to people and events. Students compare how the same cafe decision is framed by Leila and her aunt.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LE01:** identify responses drawn from social and ethical contexts. Students explain why environmental responsibility and family livelihood can create legitimate tension.
- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA02:** move beyond bare assertions by taking account of differing opinions and authoritative sources.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LY03:** analyse how language features work together to influence an audience.

## Learning intention

We are learning to build a fair and forceful persuasive response by acknowledging a genuine concern, using relevant evidence and proposing a workable action.

## Success criteria

- I can identify the values and responsibilities behind two viewpoints.
- I can distinguish a fair concern from a straw-man version of that concern.
- I can identify how inclusive language, modality, rhetorical questions and emotive vocabulary position an audience.
- I can build a FAIR bridge: shared goal, fair concern, evidence and workable action.
- I can avoid overclaiming what a short trial can prove.
- I can write and visibly revise an open letter that is firm, fair and practical.

## Finished workbook response

A **9-11 sentence open letter to the fictional Rahman family**, recommending a four-week reusable-cup trial at Sunrise Corner Cafe.

The letter must:

1. state a clear position;
2. frame one shared family-and-environment goal;
3. acknowledge one genuine concern accurately;
4. use at least two relevant evidence points from the reading;
5. explain how the evidence supports the proposal;
6. use at least two deliberate persuasive techniques;
7. recommend a practical trial with one safeguard and one measure;
8. avoid claiming that reusable cups solve all cafe waste; and
9. include one visible revision after feedback.

Students may recommend a different trial design or argue against the proposal if they use the evidence fairly and offer a workable alternative.

## Explicit teaching: the FAIR bridge

### F - Frame a shared goal

Begin with a value the audience can reasonably share.

> We all want Sunrise Corner to remain welcoming, safe and responsible.

### A - Acknowledge a fair concern

State the concern accurately and proportionately. A concession is not surrender; it demonstrates that the writer understands the decision.

> A morning rush leaves little room for a system that slows staff or creates hygiene risks.

### I - Introduce relevant evidence

Use evidence that answers the exact concern. Do not drop in an impressive fact without explaining its job.

> Food Standards Australia New Zealand says businesses may choose whether to accept customer cups and should use a clear policy to manage contamination risks.

### R - Recommend a workable next step

Match the strength of the action to the strength of the evidence.

> Therefore, the cafe should test a staff-approved cup procedure for four weeks, record delays and refusals, and review the result before making a permanent change.

## Core sequence - approximately 60 minutes

### 1. Same family, two headlines - 4 minutes

Display:

> Our cafe is choosing convenience over the planet.
>
> Our cafe is protecting jobs during the morning rush.

Students identify what each headline makes readers notice and which family member it might unfairly simplify.

- **Student action:** Silent notice, pair explanation.
- **Finished when:** One language-effect comparison.
- **Teacher note:** Do not decide which side is correct. Both headlines select one value and hide another.

### 2. The family meeting is tomorrow - 2 minutes

Introduce the decision and final product.

- **Student action:** Name the audience, decision and purpose.
- **Finished when:** Students can explain that they must persuade family decision-makers, not defeat an enemy.

### 3. Read the feature as an evidence adviser - 9 minutes

Students annotate:

- **V** - a value or responsibility;
- **C** - a genuine concern;
- **E** - checkable evidence;
- **T** - a persuasive technique or framing choice.

- **Student action:** Independent or paired reading; Reading Pack.
- **Finished when:** Two V, two C, three E and two T marks.
- **Teacher note:** The narrative and family voices are fictional. The source panel contains the authoritative evidence.

### 4. Whose viewpoint makes sense - and why? - 5 minutes

Students complete a two-lens comparison for Leila and Aunt Samira, then identify one shared goal.

- **Student action:** FAIR Organiser 1.
- **Finished when:** Two accurately represented viewpoints and one common value.
- **Misconception revealed:** Understanding a viewpoint means agreeing with it.

### 5. Teach the FAIR bridge - 7 minutes

Model the four moves. Contrast a fair concession with a straw man:

> **Straw man:** My family only cares about money.
>
> **Fair acknowledgement:** My family is responsible for wages, safe service and customers who may not carry a reusable cup.

- **Student action:** Label F, A, I and R; explain why the second version is more persuasive.
- **Finished when:** Students can state the job of each move.

### 6. Technique lab: force without distortion - 6 minutes

Students analyse four sentence choices:

- inclusive language: **we, our, together**;
- modality: **could, should, must**;
- rhetorical question;
- emotive or loaded vocabulary.

They decide which choice suits a family audience and justify using the purpose.

- **Student action:** Individual choice, pair defence, class check.
- **Finished when:** Two technique-effect explanations.
- **Teacher note:** A technique is not automatically effective. It must fit audience, evidence and tone.

### 7. Evidence, inference or overclaim? - 5 minutes

Classify:

1. Queensland guidance suggests incentives or mug-return programs to reduce plastic-lined coffee-cup use. - **Evidence**
2. A well-designed trial may reduce disposable-cup use without creating unacceptable delays. - **Reasonable inference**
3. If the cafe accepts reusable cups, none of its cups will ever enter landfill. - **Overclaim**

- **Student action:** Vote, justify and retry.
- **Finished when:** Three classifications and one boundary explanation.

### 8. Read the model as a writer - 5 minutes

Read the complete model, then reveal the FAIR moves and persuasive techniques.

- **Student action:** Star one FAIR move and underline two deliberate techniques.
- **Finished when:** Students can explain the job of one sentence.

### 9. Plan the open letter - 5 minutes

Students complete:

1. position;
2. shared goal;
3. fair concern;
4. two evidence points and reasoning;
5. trial action, safeguard and measure;
6. two techniques;
7. evidence boundary.

- **Student action:** Independent; FAIR Organiser.
- **Finished when:** Every move has a usable note.
- **Teacher conference priority:** Students whose response attacks a person instead of addressing a concern.

### 10. Write the open letter - 12 minutes

Students write 9-11 sentences to the Rahman family.

- **Student action:** Independent; workbook or approved device.
- **Finished when:** Complete FAIR letter.
- **Optional sentence launches:** *We all want...*; *A fair concern is...*; *Official guidance explains...*; *This matters because...*; *Therefore, the cafe should trial...*; *The trial cannot prove..., but it can show...*

### 11. Test the bridge and revise - 4 minutes

Partners use:

> Your letter represents the family's concern fairly when...
>
> Your recommendation would be more convincing or workable if...

Writers immediately revise one sentence.

- **Student action:** Partner feedback and visible revision.
- **Finished when:** One changed sentence and an identifiable improvement.

### 12. Exit evidence - 1 minute

Complete:

> A fair concession strengthens persuasion when ___ because ___; it becomes weak when ___.

- **Use:** Group next teaching by straw-man thinking, evidence relevance or vague action.

## Optional depth

### Depth A: Turn down the heat - 7 minutes

Rewrite the loaded sentence *Our throwaway habit is choking the planet because my family refuses to change* so it retains urgency, removes the personal attack and adds one defensible action.

### Depth B: Design the trial measure - 8 minutes

Choose three measures: number of disposable cups used, service delay, customer participation, staff concerns, contamination refusals or return rate. Explain what result would justify continuing, changing or ending the trial.

## Model open letter

> Dear Rahman family, we all want Sunrise Corner to remain a welcoming cafe and a responsible part of the community. I believe we should run a four-week reusable-cup trial instead of treating disposable cups as the automatic choice. Aunt Samira is right that staff must protect food safety and keep the morning queue moving. However, acknowledging that concern does not mean change is impossible. Queensland guidance suggests incentives for customers who bring reusable cups and mug-return programs as practical ways to reduce plastic-lined coffee-cup use. Food Standards Australia New Zealand also explains that a cafe may set its own policy for accepting customer cups and should manage cleaning and cross-contamination risks. This evidence supports a controlled trial, not a promise that every problem will disappear. Why not test one clearly marked handover point, train staff to refuse dirty or damaged cups and record any delays? Together, we can count disposable cups, staff concerns and customer participation before deciding what comes next. The trial cannot solve all cafe waste, but it can give our family local evidence for a fairer permanent decision.

## Support

- Preteach: **disposable, reusable, contamination, incentive, livelihood, concession, straw man, modality**.
- Allow paired reading and alternate paragraphs.
- Provide the four FAIR cards on the desk.
- Reduce the evidence set to two official points.
- Orally rehearse the FAIR bridge before writing.
- Permit a 6-8 sentence letter while retaining all four FAIR moves.
- Colour-code the concern and the response so the relationship is visible.

## Extension

- Compare a mandatory reusable-cup rule with a voluntary four-week trial.
- Explain how **must**, **should** and **could** change the relationship between writer and family audience.
- Add a rebuttal to the strongest practical objection without claiming the objection disappears.
- Evaluate whether a rhetorical question strengthens the model or pressures the audience unfairly.

## Accuracy, safety and privacy boundaries

- The Rahman family, Sunrise Corner Cafe and all dialogue are fictional.
- Do not imply that every disposable coffee cup has the same materials or disposal pathway.
- Do not imply that ordinary kerbside recycling accepts coffee cups everywhere; local services differ.
- Do not claim that reusable cups are automatically safe. Businesses remain responsible for safe and suitable food service and may refuse customer containers.
- Do not claim that a four-week trial proves a complete environmental benefit or predicts long-term customer behaviour.
- Do not ask students to disclose family financial pressure, family conflict, cultural background or personal environmental practices.
- Accept different recommendations when they represent both viewpoints accurately and use relevant evidence.
