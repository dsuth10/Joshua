# Lesson 40: Unit Reflection and Celebration

## Lesson purpose

Students consolidate their learning about environmental issues, empathy and persuasion through *Berani*. They take part in a structured, choice-based discussion and write a self-evaluation of both their written persuasive text and individual spoken presentation using concrete evidence and one future goal.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations using language features and voice for effect.

## Learning intention

We are learning to reflect honestly on how our thinking, writing and speaking developed, using evidence to choose a next goal.

## Success criteria

- I can connect an idea from *Berani* to learning about environment, empathy or persuasion.
- I can contribute to a discussion, write a response or pass respectfully.
- I can identify one concrete example of growth in my written text.
- I can identify one concrete example of growth in my spoken presentation or rehearsal.
- I can set one specific next goal with an action and a way to notice progress.

## Assessment alignment

The unit assessment includes an individual written persuasive text (suggested **200–400 words**) and an individual spoken persuasive presentation (suggested **1–3 minutes**). This reflection is not a re-marking activity. It gives students and teacher evidence about drafting, revision, research, audience, voice and rehearsal choices across both modes.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation; all recurring logistics remain in the Notes popup.
- Provide the reflection organiser and access to students’ own drafts, cue cards, rehearsal records or approved feedback.
- Arrange groups of 3–4 with a private written/passing option, and choose an authentic low-pressure acknowledgement format.

## Core sequence (50 min)

1. Private entry reflection — 3 min
2. Purpose and product — 3 min
3. Unit lenses — 5 min
4. Evidence-based reflection model — 4 min
5. Choice-based discussion — 8 min
6. *Berani* to persuasive craft transfer — 4 min
7. Written-text self-evaluation — 6 min
8. Spoken-presentation self-evaluation — 6 min
9. Next-goal design — 4 min
10. Authentic celebration — 4 min
11. Exit reflection — 3 min

## Evidence boundary

The deck uses only broad unit themes: environmental responsibility, empathy across viewpoints, and persuasive craft. Students may use their own notes and work. They must not invent quotations, statistics, authorities, sources or outcomes from memory.

## Inclusion, dignity and safety

Students choose whether to share a text idea, a craft move, a written response or pass. Do not require disclosure of confidence, personal views, identity or experience. Speaking evidence can come from an agreed small-group, trusted-adult, AAC, recording or alternative pathway. Do not rank work, require applause or equate eye contact with success.

## Differentiation

**Support:** use sentence stems, a reduced choice of one writing and one speaking lens, partner scribing and access to prior organisers.

**Lucas (ICP):** use symbol/choice-supported reflection, pointing, speech, AAC or a scribe. Lucas can identify an opinion/reason, a voice choice and one next action; the core learning is reflection on communication.

**Extend:** connect a craft decision to a named audience effect, distinguish a source from an interpretation and set a transferable goal for a new persuasive context.

## Formative evidence

The self-evaluation gives concrete evidence of a student’s perceived writing and speaking growth and their next learning need. Use it for future conferencing and unit transition, not peer assessment.
