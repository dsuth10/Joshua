# Lesson 11: Words With Exact Jobs

## Lesson purpose

Students examine how culturally situated and topic-specific vocabulary carries precise meaning. They use bule, galvanise, strategise and repercussions in a deliberate Precision Rewrite.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA03: describe how language features contribute to meaning.**
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA03: explain how vocabulary choices are adapted for purpose and context.**

## Learning intention

We are learning to select vocabulary for exact meaning, tone and context.

## Success criteria

- I can infer a word's meaning from contextual evidence.
- I can explain what a precise word adds that a near-synonym misses.
- I can recognise when a word is grammatical but contextually wrong.
- I can revise a vague paragraph using four target terms accurately.
- I can justify two replacements with specific meaning or tone reasons.

## Finished workbook response

A Precision Rewrite of a vague paragraph using bule, galvanise, strategise and repercussions, followed by two justified vocabulary choices.

- Teacher controls the projected HTML presentation.

## Core sequence

### 1. Where did these words do their work? - 4 minutes

Match each context to a target word. Underline the clue that guided you.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: Word Fit rows.
- **Finished when:** Four matches and four clues.
- **Teacher note:** Do not treat bule as a simple dictionary synonym. Establish that context includes who uses a word, towards whom and with what effect.

### 2. Choose for meaning—not difficulty - 2 minutes

Choose one check and explain it in your own words.

- **Student action:** Partner rehearsal; No writing yet.
- **Finished when:** Both partners can explain one check.
- **Teacher note:** Keep the four checks visible throughout the Word Fit Lab. The independent task depends on all four.

### 3. Which word brings the context into focus? - 7 minutes

Select the best fit. Explain what it adds that the alternatives miss.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: first two Word Fit rows.
- **Finished when:** Two selections and two explanations.
- **Teacher note:** For bule, accept that foreigner is a partial gloss but insufficient for this cultural context. For galvanise, emphasise movement from passive agreement to action.

### 4. Planning and consequences need exact weight - 7 minutes

Select, justify and note any grammar that must change around the word.

- **Student action:** Pairs; Organiser: final two Word Fit rows.
- **Finished when:** Two selections, reasons and grammar notes.
- **Teacher note:** The distinctions are deliberate planning versus general thinking, and serious indirect consequences versus neutral results.

### 5. A word can be grammatical—and still not fit - 4 minutes

Mark fits / does not fit, then explain the contextual boundary.

- **Student action:** Independent first; class reveal; Organiser margin.
- **Finished when:** Three judgements and one spoken reason.
- **Teacher note:** Keep this brief. The point is semantic boundary, not comic wrong answers.

### 6. From vague to exact - 5 minutes

Predict each replacement and the grammar change it requires.

- **Student action:** Whole class; Star one model change on the organiser.
- **Finished when:** One selected change and reason.
- **Teacher note:** Read both versions aloud. Ask why the precise paragraph requires restructuring rather than four one-for-one substitutions.

### 7. Complete the Precision Rewrite - 10 minutes

Rewrite the paragraph using all four target terms accurately.

- **Student action:** Independent and silent; Facing workbook page.
- **Finished when:** One complete revised paragraph.
- **Teacher note:** Students may consult the organiser. Do not display one fixed answer while they compose.

### 8. Prove that two replacements were deliberate - 4 minutes

Annotate two replacements with specific meaning or tone reasons.

- **Student action:** Independent; Below the rewrite.
- **Finished when:** Two complete justifications.
- **Teacher note:** If a student cannot justify a word, ask them to test a near-synonym and compare the loss.

### 9. Bring every word into focus - 3 minutes

Mark each check and revise one word or surrounding phrase.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One visible revision.
- **Teacher note:** Selected examples should compare decisions, not produce a single canonical rewrite.

### 10. Name the near-synonym that fails - 2 minutes

Record one target word, a near-synonym and the contextual difference.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook.
- **Finished when:** One complete comparison.
- **Teacher note:** Safe stopping point. Use these comparisons as vocabulary evidence, not spelling evidence.


## Optional depth slides

### Move the words into a new register - 10 minutes

Rewrite one informal sentence for a formal audience and justify two changes.

- **Student action:** Independent; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** One formal revision and two reasons.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. This is not a thesaurus exercise; changes must suit the named audience.

### Why isn't bule simply 'foreigner'? - 8 minutes

Explain what the simple gloss loses in Malia's context.

- **Student action:** Pairs, then whole class; Workbook margin.
- **Finished when:** One two-sentence explanation.
- **Teacher note:** Optional depth. Maintain a text-based discussion boundary. Do not invite students to repeat labels from their own lives.
