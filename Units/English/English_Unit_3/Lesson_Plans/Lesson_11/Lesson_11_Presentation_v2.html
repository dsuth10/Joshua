STEP 3 • FILL THE GAP

Which word fits the sentence?

CORE

Your job: pick the best word. Then press Check answers.

MUM

Mum worries some kids might feel pulled two ways. She says they might be ______.

Conflicted = pulled between two sides. Not just “unsure.”
ARI

Looking at Ginger Juice, Ari feels like he is ______ a friend.

Betraying = letting a friend down in a serious way.
THE CAGE

The cage is ______ too small for Ginger Juice.

Woefully = so small it feels sad and wrong. Stronger than “a bit.”
UNCLE

Uncle gives Ari an angry stare. The book calls it a ______.

Glower = an angry look that can stop you talking.

STEP 5 • ALMOST THE SAME?

Why is the exact word better?

CORE

Your job: pick the exact word. Then read why the weaker word fails.

PALM OIL

Mum does not only say the topic is “hard.” She calls it ______.

STEP 10 • REMEMBER THIS RULE

When does a word fit?

CORE

Your job: copy this rule into your workbook.

Ask:
Who says it? • To whom? • How serious is it? • Is it the exact feeling?

If the answers don’t match, the word does not fit — even if the sentence sounds okay.

STEP 11 • WATCH A REWRITE

Change vague words into exact words

CORE

Your job: watch how we make the sentence stronger.

Vague: Ari saw that the cage was really too small, and he felt sad about the past.
Problem: “really” and “sad” are okay — but not exact.
Exact: The cage was woefully too small, and the memory filled him with nostalgia.
Why: woefully = sadly too small · nostalgia = missing the past
