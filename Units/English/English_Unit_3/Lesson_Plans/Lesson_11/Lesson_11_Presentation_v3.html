STEP 3 • FILL THE GAP

Which word fits the sentence?

CORE

Your job: pick the best word. Then press Check answers.

PLAYGROUND

The old play space feels ______ — there is nowhere to run.

Cramped = too small and tight. Stronger than “a bit small.”
FRIEND

No one sat with Jess at lunch. She felt ______.

Lonely = missing company — not just “sad.”
LINE

Cutting the line is ______ to the kids who waited.

Unfair = not right for everyone.
TEACHER

The teacher gave a long, hard look. It was a ______.

Stare = a long look that can stop you talking.

STEP 5 • ALMOST THE SAME?

Why is the exact word better?

CORE

Your job: pick the exact word. Then read why the weaker word fails.

PLAY SPACE

The play space is not only “small.” It is ______.

Tiny shows it is much too small. “Little” is weaker.
LINE CUTTING

Sam’s anger about unfair line-cutting is ______.

Furious = very angry because something is wrong. Grumpy is just a mood.

When does a word fit?

CORE

Ask:

If the answers don’t match, the word does not fit — even if the sentence sounds okay.

STEP 11 • WATCH A REWRITE

Change weak words into exact words

CORE

Your job: watch how we make the sentence stronger.

Weak: The play space was really too small, and Sam felt sad about the old house.
Problem: “really” and “sad” are okay — but not exact.
Exact: The play space was tiny, and thinking of home made Sam feel homesick.
Why: tiny = much too small · homesick = missing home

STEP 15 • MODEL

Listen to a persuasive paragraph

CORE

Your job: watch how exact words help persuade the principal.

Ask: Principal, our class needs a larger play space.
Evidence: Our corner is tiny and cramped for kids who love to dash and climb.
Feeling: Waiting with nowhere to play can feel lonely and unfair.
Push: Please do not stay silent — this is your duty.
Close: If we plan carefully now, everyone will enjoy a more active, safer play time.
