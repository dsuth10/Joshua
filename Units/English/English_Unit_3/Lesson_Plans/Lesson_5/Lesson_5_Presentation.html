MISSION BRIEFING

A powerful opening does three jobs

1

Hook attention

Use a deliberate technique—not a tired phrase.

2

Earn trust

Replace a bare assertion with relevant, credible evidence.

3

Build action

Explain why the evidence matters and link it to the message.

SOURCE CHECK

Which source earns the audience’s trust?

Claim: rainforest habitat is being cleared for palm oil production.

Credibility checklist

  • Author / organisation: who created this and can we trace them?
  • Evidence / method: what proof is offered and how was it gathered?
  • Current / dated: is the information recent enough to trust?
  • Relevant to this claim: does it prove habitat clearing—not just general opinion or support?

Before you check: “I trust ___ most because ___.”

💬

An anonymous comment

“Palm oil is terrible. Everyone knows that.”

Opinion without traceable proof.
🎥

An advocacy video

Named organisation, vivid footage and a clear campaign purpose.

Useful, but check its purpose and supporting data.
📊

A current research report

Named authors, dated data and a method explaining how findings were reached.

Strongest match: traceable, current and evidence-based.
📣

A petition total

Shows how many people support change.

Shows support, not proof that the habitat claim is true.
HOOK SHOWDOWN

Which hook would earn your classmates’ attention?

Audience: classmates. Purpose: make them care enough to listen to the claim.

The 3-second test

  • Specific: not a vague cliché
  • Audience-aware: gives listeners a reason to care
  • Purposeful: leads naturally into the claim

A hook opens the door — evidence earns trust.

A
Did you know palm oil is bad?
vague question
B
Every day, choices in our shopping baskets reach all the way into the rainforest.
surprising connection
C
Imagine waking to the sound of bulldozers destroying your only home.
imagery
D
What if your favourite snack came with a hidden cost?
rhetorical question
