# Teacher Guide: Turtle-Safe Nights

## At a glance

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Audience | Years 5–6 |
| Core time | Approximately 60 minutes |
| Final product | 8–10 sentence evidence-based recommendation to a coastal council |
| Central thinking | opportunity → action → consequence; stakeholder viewpoint; evidence boundary; cautious visual metaphor |
| Core misconception | A plausible pathway is not a guarantee, and a stakeholder concern is not automatically evidence |
| Print | Reading Pack + Organiser; Lucas Pack where appropriate |

## Recommended position

The evidence supports strengthening seasonal lighting requirements in **mapped or locally verified turtle-sensitive areas**, while designing necessary lighting to meet human safety and access requirements.

Avoid framing the decision as “wildlife versus people”. The Australian Government guidelines explicitly recognise that artificial light provides safety and amenity and that creative solutions may be needed to meet both human safety and wildlife conservation objectives.

## Reading annotation answers

### Evidence (E)

Accept accurate paraphrases such as:

- most marine turtle nesting and hatching activity happens at night;
- nesting females may avoid or be disturbed by brightly lit beaches;
- hatchlings use light cues and the brighter ocean horizon to orient toward the sea;
- direct artificial light and skyglow can disorient adult and hatchling turtles;
- disoriented hatchlings may move inland, circle, become exhausted, overheat or face greater predation risk;
- Queensland has mapped turtle nesting areas to assist planning;
- national guidance recommends natural darkness as the starting point and purpose-specific lighting that is low, directed, shielded and adaptively controlled;
- human safety requirements remain relevant;
- marine turtle recovery requires collaboration among governments, Indigenous communities and other stakeholders.

### Actions (A)

Accept:

- identify turtle-sensitive areas using state mapping plus local refinement;
- apply seasonal requirements during locally verified nesting and hatching periods;
- turn off unnecessary lights;
- shield and direct lights away from beaches, ocean and sky;
- keep lights low and use the lowest suitable intensity;
- use timers, dimmers or appropriate adaptive controls;
- reduce blue, violet and ultraviolet wavelengths;
- close curtains or use vegetation/light barriers where suitable;
- audit beachward light and monitor turtle behaviour;
- consult Traditional Owners, technical specialists, residents, visitors and businesses.

### Viewpoint/value (V)

Possible values:

- turtle ranger/researcher: reliable horizon cues, nesting success, monitoring;
- beachfront resident: safe access, home use, workable rules, cost;
- business/accommodation operator: guest safety, evening trade, retrofitting clarity and time;
- council officer: legal duties, public safety, enforceability, cost and measurable outcomes;
- Traditional Owner ranger/Sea Country representative: continuing custodianship, cultural responsibilities, local knowledge, monitoring and long-term care for Country.

Do not accept a stereotyped or invented quotation as evidence of a group’s view.

### Questions/boundaries (?)

Strong examples:

- Which beaches in this council area are verified nesting areas?
- What is the local nesting and hatching period?
- Which existing lights are visible from the beach or water?
- What lighting is required for a specific safety task?
- How will the council measure improvement?
- Which Traditional Owner group speaks for this Sea Country?

## Opportunity–action–consequence guidance

### Defensible chain

**Opportunity:** The council reviews planning and operational rules before the next local turtle season.  
**Action:** It adopts targeted requirements for mapped/verified areas: purpose-specific lighting, shielding, low mounting, suitable intensity, adaptive control and monitoring.  
**Immediate consequence:** Some property owners and council assets need audits, adjustments or retrofits.  
**Environmental consequence:** Less direct beachward light and skyglow pressure may preserve clearer natural horizon cues.  
**Possible turtle consequence:** Nesting disturbance and hatchling disorientation risk may be reduced.  
**Boundary:** The chain does not prove an exact number of turtles saved; effects are site-, species- and project-specific and should be monitored.

### Common weak chains

| Weak move | Why it is weak | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| “Stronger rules will save all turtles.” | Guaranteed outcome; ignores other threats and site variation. | Which link can the evidence support, and what modal verb is accurate? |
| “Businesses dislike rules, so the council should do nothing.” | Invented generalisation; concern is not evidence that inaction is best. | What specific concern might be reasonable, and how could design respond? |
| “Use amber lights and the problem is solved.” | Colour alone is not a complete solution. | What else matters: need, intensity, direction, shielding, height, timing and monitoring? |
| “The map proves every light within the area harms turtles.” | Mapping identifies planning relevance; it does not measure each light’s effect. | What local audit or monitoring is still needed? |

## Stakeholder viewpoint protocol

Use these as **questions a stakeholder may reasonably ask**, not as quotations:

| Lens | Reasonable question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Turtle ranger/researcher | Will the rule reduce light visible from nesting beaches and nearshore water? | light audits, nesting and disorientation monitoring |
| Resident | Can I safely reach my home while reducing unnecessary spill? | task-specific safety requirements and lighting design |
| Business/operator | What must be changed, by when, and what support is available? | audit findings, retrofit options, implementation timetable |
| Council officer | Is the rule targeted, enforceable and measurable? | mapped areas, local season, standards, monitoring triggers |
| Traditional Owner ranger/representative | How will the decision respect Sea Country responsibilities and include the people who speak for this place? | culturally appropriate engagement and ongoing co-management |

## Light pathway answer guidance

The recommended pathway is:

> targeted council rule → audit and redesign of relevant lighting → less avoidable light reaching beach/ocean/sky → clearer natural horizon cues → reduced risk of disturbance or disorientation

Students may create a different defensible pathway.

Required limitation:

> The pathway organises a plausible, evidence-supported chain, but it cannot calculate an exact biological outcome or represent every local condition.

## Evidence-boundary interaction answers

1. **Official evidence:** “National guidance says artificial light can disrupt turtle nesting, sea-finding and dispersal behaviour.”  
   Evidence comes from the National Light Pollution Guidelines and Recovery Plan.
2. **Reasonable inference:** “A targeted seasonal rule using shielding and adaptive controls would likely reduce avoidable light reaching a nesting beach.”  
   It follows from the guidance, but local auditing is still required.
3. **Overclaim:** “If the council changes every bulb to amber, no hatchling will become disoriented.”  
   Colour is only one factor, and no measure guarantees a universal outcome.

## Model recommendation annotations

| Sentence | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | clear, qualified recommendation |
| 2–3 | official evidence plus causal explanation |
| 4 | concrete and workable council action |
| 5 | fair stakeholder concern |
| 6 | response that preserves safety and conservation |
| 7 | cautious use and limitation of the light-pathway metaphor |
| 8 | consultation, monitoring and improvement |

## Feedback and assessment

### Quick conference questions

- Which sentence contains your clearest evidence?
- What action causes the next step in your pathway?
- Where have you shown that another stakeholder has a reasonable concern?
- Which word prevents your claim from becoming a guarantee?
- What exactly should the council do?
- How would the council know whether the rule is working?

### Formative coding

- **OAC+** — opportunity, action and consequence are distinct and causally linked.
- **VIEW+** — stakeholder concern is fair and relevant.
- **EVID+** — evidence is accurate and supports the exact claim.
- **BOUND+** — inference/metaphor limitation is explicit.
- **ACT+** — recommendation is concrete and workable.

### Exit grouping

- **Secure:** accurate causal chain plus explicit boundary.
- **Developing:** accurate evidence but missing causal link.
- **Support needed:** consequence is asserted as certain or stakeholder opinion is used as proof.

## Lucas / ICP facilitation

- Read the short text aloud while the student tracks.
- Offer three action cards and allow pointing or eye-gaze selection.
- Build the pathway orally before writing.
- Scribe exactly what the student communicates, then offer one accuracy choice: **will / may / always**.
- Keep the stakeholder step. A reduced workload must not remove fairness or consequence reasoning.
- Accept a six-to-eight-sentence scribed or speech-to-text recommendation when consistent with the student’s authoritative adjustments.

## Teacher accuracy reminders

- **Artificial light can increase risk; it does not create one identical response in every turtle.**
- **A darker beach is an outcome, not a demand to remove every safety light.**
- **Longer-wavelength lighting is generally less disruptive for hatchlings, but it is not harmless and must be combined with appropriate need, intensity, placement, shielding and timing.**
- **Local dates, species, terrain and existing light conditions matter.**
- **Traditional Owners are rights-holders and knowledge-holders, not simply one interest group in a classroom debate.**
