# Lesson 34: Peer Feedback and Spelling Audit

## Lesson purpose

Students use a short, constructive peer conference and a targeted spelling-and-meaning audit to improve one part of their individual persuasive assessment draft. Readers provide precise evidence-based observations; authors retain choice and make one immediate visible revision.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY08:** spell using phonic, morphemic and grammatical knowledge.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY08:** use spelling knowledge to spell technical and complex words.
- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06 / Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** create and edit persuasive texts that develop ideas for an audience.

## Learning intention

We are learning to give useful feedback and use spelling and meaning checks to strengthen our own persuasive writing.

## Success criteria

- I can give one specific, evidence-based notice and one useful question.
- I can audit a draft through spelling, sentence clarity, tense and cohesion lenses.
- I can use phonic, morphemic or grammatical knowledge before seeking help with an uncertain word.
- I can choose and complete one immediate revision while keeping ownership of my writing.

## Assessment alignment

Part A is an individual 200–400 word (suggested) persuasive text for an identified audience. Students edit for spelling, punctuation and meaning, and use paragraphs, cohesive devices, relevant tenses and complex sentences as appropriate. Feedback supports the author’s decisions; it does not change individual authorship.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation; DO/WORK/RECORD/FINISH/CHECK logistics are available only in its Notes popup.
- Provide students’ current drafts, organisers, approved dictionaries/word banks and their checked research notes.
- Use the Year 5/6 Storytime Dog models solely for structural analysis, never as evidence for a student topic.

## Core sequence (50 min)

1. Entry question — 3 min
2. Product and author-choice route — 3 min
3. Compare feedback quality — 4 min
4. Model conference protocol — 4 min
5. Model editing targets — 6 min
6. Spelling strategy audit — 5 min
7. Four-lens audit — 5 min
8. Feedback-lens sort — 5 min
9. Peer conference, swap roles — 12 min
10. Immediate revision — 6 min
11. Exit evidence — 3 min

## Model and evidence boundary

The Storytime Dog text is a structural model. Its content, research references and examples are not evidence students may copy, cite or generalise. Students use their own identified, accurate sources; uncertain information is marked **needs checking**.

## Differentiation

**Support:** use one lens first, provide the stems “I noticed … because …” and “Could you check …?”, allow oral reading and use a personal word bank.

**Lucas (ICP):** students may point, speak, copy, use assistive technology or a partner scribe to share one message, receive one notice, and choose one change. The author remains the decision-maker.

**Extend:** explain how cohesion, sentence control and audience effect interact; check a technical word using its meaningful word parts; give feedback that distinguishes a reader response from a correction.

## Formative assessment

The feedback comparison, lens sort, conference organiser and marked revision show whether students can diagnose an editing priority and act on feedback without losing authorship. The exit response identifies the next conference or small-group teaching need.

## Safety guidance

Use respectful comments about the writing, not the writer. Do not invent facts, quotations, statistics, source authority or spelling rules. Keep negotiated school/community topics and personal information private.
