BERANI • LESSON 34

Feedback that helps the writer decide.

A useful peer conference notices one precise strength, asks one precise question, and leaves the author in charge of one immediate revision.

PERSUASIVE WRITING • PEER CONFERENCE
PURPOSE

Today: conference, audit, revise.

3 min
AUTHOR GOALREADER NOTICESAUTHOR CHOOSESONE VISIBLE REVISION

Your product is an annotated draft with one immediate, author-chosen revision.

NOTICE

Which comment gives the author something usable?

4 min
COMMENT A

Nice work. Make it better.

COMMENT B

Your topic sentence clearly names your reason. I got lost in this long sentence; could you check where the main idea ends?

PROTOCOL

A constructive conference has roles.

4 min
AUTHORNames one goal“Please listen for whether my reason is clear.”
READERNotices with evidence“I noticed … because …”
AUTHORChooses one change“I will revise …”

Readers do not correct every line or replace the author’s ideas.

MODEL

Read the model structurally, not as evidence.

6 min

“Reading aloud can be intimidating, particularly for students who struggle with fluency or confidence. A trained education dog provides a non-judgemental audience … Consequently, students are more likely to take risks …”

COHESION“Consequently” signals a result.
COMPLEXITYThe opening sentence contains a dependent detail.
TENSEPresent tense is controlled.

The Storytime Dog content demonstrates structure only. It is not a source for your topic.

AUDIT

Spelling: use knowledge, not a random hunt.

5 min
PHONICSay the word slowly; check likely sound patterns.
MORPHEMICFind the base word, prefix or suffix.
GRAMMATICALCheck endings match the sentence: tense, plural or possessive.

Circle one uncertain word. Try a strategy, then use an approved reference or ask for support.

EDIT

Four lenses. One manageable pass at a time.

5 min
1 • SPELLINGtechnical and complex words; endings2 • SENTENCE CLARITYcan a reader follow the main idea?3 • TENSEis the time consistent and purposeful?4 • COHESIONdo links guide the reader across sentences and paragraphs?

Mark what you notice. Do not mark everything you would write differently.

CONFERENCE

Peer conference: precise, brief, kind.

12 min

Round 1 • 6 minutes

  1. Author states one goal and reads one paragraph.
  2. Reader gives one “I noticed … because …” comment.
  3. Reader asks one question from a chosen lens.
  4. Author chooses a revision; reader waits while it is made.

Swap roles • 6 minutes

REVISE

6 min

AUTHOR EXPLAINSThis helps because …

Choose one revision that strengthens meaning, clarity, spelling, tense or cohesion.
