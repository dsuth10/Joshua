# Lesson 7: Spaces and Displacement

## Lesson purpose

Students analyze how authors use descriptions of physical setting and social environment to convey feelings of privilege, displacement, and character motivation. Reading Ari’s chapter on pages 29–33 of *Berani*, students compare the physical space and atmosphere of Ari's crowded public school with the manicured gardens, wide walkways, and affluent atmosphere of the private school SMP in Surabaya. Students use specific text evidence to explain how environment shapes a character's perspective and drives their actions in the story.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 - AC9E5LA02:** Move beyond a bare assertion by supporting an explanation with relevant information from the text.
- **Year 6 - AC9E6LA02:** Understand the uses of objective details versus subjective, emotionally charged language, and explain how an author uses setting to create tone and character contrast.

## Learning intention

We are learning to analyze how details of setting create a sense of privilege, displacement, and character perspective in narrative text.

## Success criteria

- I can identify specific text details that describe the contrast between Ari's public school and SMP private school.
- I can explain how physical environment affects how a character feels (e.g. displaced, awed, determined, or advantaged).
- I can support my comparative analysis using precise quotes from pages 29–33.
- I can explain how the author's language choices highlight socio-economic contrast without explicit judgment.
- I can write a 5–7 sentence explanation connecting setting details to character motivation.

- Open `Lesson_7_Presentation.html` in a modern browser and enter fullscreen mode.

## Sequence (approximately 60 minutes)

### 1. Cold open: Two Worlds, One City - 5 minutes

Display two contrasting images or soundscapes: a bustling, narrow, shoulder-bumping hallway versus a quiet, wide, sunlit garden walkway with manicured lawns. Ask: *How does a physical space change how you stand, speak, or feel?* Students write one emotion word for each space on mini-whiteboards.

### 2. Mission and success checks - 3 minutes

Introduce the core concept: **Setting is not just a backdrop—it shapes privilege, power, and choices.** Share learning intentions and success criteria. Emphasize moving from simple description ("It is a big school") to analytical explanation ("The wide walkways signal a luxury of space that contrasts sharply with Ari's daily reality").

### 3. Concrete encounter: Read pages 29–33 - 10 minutes

Read Ari's chapter aloud or in shared pairs. Pause at three key thresholds:
1. **Arrival at SMP (pp. 29–30):** The physical transition from the street and bus to the reception area.
2. **The Walkway & Malia Encounter (pp. 30–31):** Moving through gardens, bumping into Malia, receiving the petition.
3. **The Games Room & Tournament (pp. 31–33):** Playing Hendra, observing the affluent players' relaxed attitudes versus Ari's burning focus.

Students capture one setting detail at each threshold on their handout quote bank.

### 4. Guided noticing: World Shift Sort - 7 minutes

Display the interactive sorting activity on Slide 5. Students classify details into two categories:
- **Public School Reality:** Shoulder-bumping halls, limited space, intense financial pressure.
- **SMP Private School Privilege:** Wide covered walkways, manicured gardens, blazer uniforms, nonchalance toward prize money.

Discuss the ambiguous details (e.g. the petition passed by Malia): *Is Malia part of SMP's privilege, or an outsider pushing against it?* Require students to defend their reasoning using text evidence.

### 5. Explicit model: Connecting Setting to Perspective - 5 minutes

Model a strong analytical paragraph frame:
- **Text Detail:** "At our school you can't walk in the halls without bumping shoulders... Here the wide walkways and gardens give a glimpse of a much different experience."
- **Analysis:** "The author uses physical contrast to reveal Ari's feeling of displacement. The phrase 'luxury of space' emphasizes that comfort and beauty are unequal privileges."
- **Character Impact:** "Rather than intimidating Ari, this contrast fuels his hunger to win the prize money, giving him a psychological edge over the comfortable private school players."

### 6. Setting & Contrast Lab - 8 minutes

In pairs, students analyze three core quotes from pp. 29–33 using the **Space-to-Mind Matrix**:
1. *Quote 1:* "bumping shoulders" vs "wide walkways and gardens" -> Physical space contrast.
2. *Quote 2:* "golden light" / "amber eyes" -> Visual imagery and emotional impression.
3. *Quote 3:* "affluent players do not care so much about the tournament... not hungry for the win" -> Socio-economic perspective and motivation.

Partners complete the quote analysis table on their handout.

### 7. Cause & Perspective Detective - 5 minutes

Display Slide 8 (Multiple Choice Analysis). Students evaluate three explanations for why Ari feels an "advantage" despite being from a less resourced school:
- *Decoy A:* Because he is a taller chess player. (Incorrect - unsupported detail).
- *Decoy B:* Because SMP students are bad at chess. (Incorrect - oversimplification).
- *Target C:* Because his urgent financial need creates an intense focus and hunger that the comfortable, affluent players lack. (Correct - synthesizes setting and motivation).

### 8. Language microscope - 6 minutes

Examine four specific language choices on Slide 9:
1. **"luxury of space and beauty":** Metaphor/Noun choice highlighting inequality.
2. **"bumping shoulders":** Kinesthetic imagery showing density and congestion.
3. **"stepped into a beam of golden light":** Imagery showing validation and confidence shift.
4. **"hungry for the win":** Sensory/figurative language framing economic motivation.

Students unpack the objective facts versus the subjective emotional impact of each choice.

### 9. Independent construction: Comparative Studio - 10 minutes

Students write a 5–7 sentence analytical paragraph on their handout addressing:
**How does the setting of SMP Surabaya create a sense of displacement for Ari, and how does he transform that feeling into a personal advantage?**

Requirements:
- Minimum of 2 direct text quotes from pp. 29–33.
- Causal/comparative connectors (*whereas, in contrast, consequently, this highlights*).
- Explanation of at least one author language choice.

### 10. Peer audit and revision - 3 minutes

Use the 45-second presentation timer (Slide 11). Partner A reads their response aloud. Partner B checks:
- [ ] Are there 2 direct text details?
- [ ] Is there a clear explanation of how setting affects Ari's feelings?
- [ ] Is comparative language used (*in contrast, whereas*)?

The writer immediately makes one revision to strengthen their evidence or analysis.

### 11. Exit evidence - 3 minutes

Students complete the Exit Ticket on mini-whiteboards or handout slip:
*Complete the sentence:* **The contrast in physical space between Ari's public school and SMP Surabaya reveals that privilege is... while Ari's response proves that...**

## Differentiation

### Support

- Provide `Lesson_7_Support_Handout.docx` with pre-filled quote choices and sentence starters (*"When Ari enters SMP, the author describes... This contrasts with his own school because... This makes Ari feel..."*).
- Reduce independent writing requirement to 3–4 targeted sentences focused on one primary quote contrast.

### Extension

- Require students to analyze how Malia's brief appearance (handing Ari the petition) connects setting to the broader unit themes of environmental activism and social justice.
- Challenge students to identify any subtle objective vs subjective language shifts in Ari's internal monologue.
