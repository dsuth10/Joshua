# Lesson 36: Voice and Formality Techniques

## Lesson purpose

Students make purposeful delivery choices for their individual spoken persuasive presentation: pitch, tone, pace, volume, formality and objective delivery. They rehearse or record a short excerpt, self-evaluate it and make one directed revision.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY07:** plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and/or multimodal presentations using language features and voice for effect.

## Learning intention

We are learning to use our voice and formality deliberately so our audience can understand and trust our persuasive message.

## Success criteria

- I can identify a purposeful choice of pitch, tone, pace or volume.
- I can adapt formality and objective delivery to my identified audience.
- I can annotate a short section of my own speaking guide without turning it into a script.
- I can record or rehearse a short excerpt and use the evidence to choose one revision.
- I can explain what became clearer after a second attempt.

## Assessment alignment

Part B is an **individual** spoken persuasive presentation, suggested at **1–3 minutes**. The written text guides the spoken structure; students annotate and adapt it, use features of voice to suit their audience, rehearse, respond to feedback and may include relevant impromptu ideas. The short 20–40 second snippet is rehearsal evidence—not the whole assessed presentation.

1. Entry decision — 3 min

3. Contrast delivery — 4 min

5. Formality and social distance — 5 min
6. Purposeful-choice rehearsal — 5 min

8. Record/rehearse snippet — 10 min
9. Self-evaluate — 5 min
10. Directed second attempt — 4 min
11. Exit target — 3 min

## Model and evidence boundary

All short lines in this presentation demonstrate delivery and register only. They are not research evidence or a speech to copy. Students use their own negotiated topic, accurate checked details and identified audience. Do not invent claims, statistics, quotations, experts or outcomes.

## Differentiation

**Support:** select only one control at first; use an opening/one argument/action excerpt; annotate with icons; rehearse orally before recording; use a partner or teacher alternative.

**Lucas (ICP):** students may communicate a short message using symbol cues, pointing, speech, assistive technology or a recording with a trusted adult. The core destination remains sharing an opinion and reason for an audience.

**Extend:** vary voice only when it changes audience understanding; compare how a formal and less-formal version changes social distance; integrate a concise response to a likely audience question.

## Formative assessment

The before/after snippet and self-evaluation reveal whether students can connect delivery to purpose instead of simply performing louder or faster. Exit targets form groups for Lesson 37 rehearsal.

## Safety and ownership

Follow school recording procedures. Offer a non-recording rehearsal pathway. Students remain authors and speakers of individual assessment work; peer feedback describes the listener experience and does not supply content.
