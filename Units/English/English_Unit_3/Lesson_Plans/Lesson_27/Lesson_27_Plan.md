# Lesson 27: Researching and Gathering Evidence

## Lesson purpose

Students establish a traceable evidence trail for their persuasive assessment. They analyse how Ari’s chess analogy makes an abstract inequity and proposed action understandable, then gather and evaluate evidence for their own selected topic without inventing claims, quotations or statistics.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY06:** plan, create and edit persuasive texts, developing and expanding ideas using supporting details.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY06:** plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs and elaborated ideas.

## Learning intention

We are learning to find, evaluate and cite sources so our persuasive ideas have evidence that another reader can trace.

## Success criteria

- I can explain how Ari’s chess analogy supports his proposal.
- I can judge a source using its author/organisation, accountability, method and fit to my exact claim.
- I can accurately paraphrase or use a short exact quotation with citation details.
- I can gather two traceable evidence notes, or label a source as needing further checking.

## Finished evidence

Two research-organiser rows: a question, source identification, accurate note, citation details and a decision about how each source relates to the student’s claim.

## Preparation

- Open Lesson_27_Presentation.html and select fullscreen.
- Print the standard or Lucas organiser.
- Make an approved source collection available: library texts, teacher-checked webpages or curated printouts. The lesson contains no invented external facts; a teacher-supplied source is required for the live model.
- Read *Berani*, Ari pp. 177–181. Core route: 50 minutes. Teacher logistics appear only in the deck’s Notes popup.

## Core sequence

1. **Trust decision — 4 min.** Name the features that make a source usable.
2. **Evidence mission — 2 min.** Establish question → search terms → source check → note → citation.
3. **Read Ari’s move — 5 min.** Locate pawn, knight and queen in the principal scene.
4. **Analogy map — 6 min.** Sort what each chess piece helps Ari explain.
5. **Model traceability — 6 min.** Distinguish a claim, accurate evidence note and citation from invented evidence.
6. **NAME source check — 5 min.** Evaluate a supplied source: Named, Accountable, Method, Exact fit; also notice date, purpose and corroboration.
7. **Set up research — 3 min.** Form a useful question and two organiser rows.
8. **Evidence studio — 13 min.** Gather two traceable notes using approved resources.
9. **Partner audit — 4 min.** Test whether another person can follow the trail; revise one item.
10. **Exit — 2 min.** Identify one usable source and one honest next check.

## Support and extension

- **Support:** provide a curated, accessible source set; conference on one question; allow oral paraphrase before a scribe records it; offer the sentence stem “According to [organisation], …”.
- **Lucas (ICP):** students select a topic, identify who made one source, and communicate one source idea by pointing, speaking, copying or partner scribing. The core destination—using a traceable source—is retained.
- **Extension:** seek a source that complicates the claim; compare its perspective or method with a second source and plan a respectful counterargument.

## Formative assessment

The chess-piece sort shows whether students can explain how a comparison does persuasive work rather than merely identify it. The organiser and partner audit expose unsupported paraphrase, incomplete citations and sources that do not fit the exact claim. Use the exit to determine source-conferencing needs before Lesson 28’s outline.

## Accuracy and safety boundaries

- Ari’s chess analogy is from *Berani* pp. 177–181; do not overstate it as proof that the administrator will agree.
- Do not invent facts, statistics, expert opinions, quotations, URLs or organisational claims. Record “needs checking” where verification is incomplete.
- Discuss claims and source quality rather than asking students to disclose personal experiences of poverty, gender discrimination or family finances.
