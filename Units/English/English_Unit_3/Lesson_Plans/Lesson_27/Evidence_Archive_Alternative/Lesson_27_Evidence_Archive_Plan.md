# Lesson 27 Alternative: The Evidence Archive

### Pedagogical Contemplation

The cognitive goal is to establish an evidence trail that another reader can follow: a focused question, an identifiable source, an accurate note, citation details and an honest judgement about fit. A fictional student-council archive provides a safe shared context, while students must recognise that its documents are models, not research evidence. The trust decision, trace-the-note check and partner audit make source judgements visible before students gather their own sources. The archive is an engaging investigation frame; the learning is disciplined research, not finding a convenient fact.

## Purpose and curriculum

Students build two traceable evidence notes for a persuasive assessment without relying on *Berani*. This preserves **AC9E5LY06** (plan, create and edit persuasive texts with supporting details) and **AC9E6LY06** (plan, create and edit persuasive texts using paragraphs and elaborated ideas).

## Learning intention

We are learning to find, evaluate and cite sources so another reader can trace our persuasive evidence.

## Success criteria

- I can identify who made a source and what it actually says.
- I can use NAME: Named, Accountable, Method, Exact fit.
- I can paraphrase accurately or use a short exact quotation with citation details.
- I can complete two evidence-trail rows, or honestly mark a source as needing checking.

## Preparation

- Open `Lesson_27_Evidence_Archive_Presentation.html` in fullscreen.
- Print the Reading Pack and Evidence Trail Organiser, or the Concise Access Pack.
- Prepare teacher-approved library texts, webpages or printouts for the research studio. The fictional archive is for analysis only; it supplies **no** facts students may reuse.

## Core sequence (50 minutes)

1. **The missing label - 4 min.** Decide which source card can be traced and justify why.
2. **Read the archive - 6 min.** Read the original article and underline what a source label lets a reader do.
3. **Explicit model - 6 min.** Separate a claim, accurate note, citation and invented addition.
4. **NAME source check - 6 min.** Apply Named, Accountable, Method and Exact fit to a teacher-supplied source.
5. **Trace-the-note check - 5 min.** Test a paraphrase against its source; revise any overclaim.
6. **Set up the trail - 4 min.** Form one useful research question and prepare two rows.
7. **Evidence studio - 13 min.** Gather two traceable notes from approved sources.
8. **Partner audit - 4 min.** Check whether another reader can follow one trail and revise it.
9. **Exit - 2 min.** Name one usable source and one honest next check.

## Interaction matrix

| Interaction | Thinking | Misconception exposed | Teacher signal | Hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trust choice | Evaluate | Polished design equals authority | Student names author and route | “Who made it?” |
| Note check | Compare | Paraphrase may change meaning | Student removes an overclaim | “What exact words allow that?” |
| NAME check | Apply | Fit is confused with agreement | Keep/question decision | “Does it answer this question?” |
| Partner audit | Revise | Citation is incomplete | One improved row | “Could I find it again?” |

## Evidence boundary, differentiation and next step

The Harbour Street Student Council, its archive and all details in the reading are fictional. They model the difference between an evidence trail and an unsupported claim; they are not sources for the assessment. Do not invent a fact, statistic, quotation, expert, organisation or URL. Record **needs checking** where verification is incomplete.

The green pathway uses shorter language, one row first, oral rehearsal, pointing or partner scribing while retaining traceability. Support students with a curated source set and the stem “According to ___, ___.” Extend by locating a source that complicates the claim and explaining whether its method or perspective changes the argument. Use the organiser and exit to conference evidence before Lesson 28 planning.
