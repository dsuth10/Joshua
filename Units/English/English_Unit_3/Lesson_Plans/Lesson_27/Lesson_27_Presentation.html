MODEL

Model the difference: claim, evidence, citation

6 MIN

Claim: Our school should consider [topic].

Evidence note: [Author/organisation] explains that ______. Copy only what the source actually says.

Citation: According to [author/organisation], “[short exact words]” (title, date if shown).

Do not turn a search result, memory or invented statistic into evidence.

CHECK

The source check: NAME it

5 MIN
N — Named?
Who made it?
A — Accountable?
Can we identify their expertise or role?
M — Method?
Does it show how it knows?
E — Exact fit?
Does it answer our claim?

Also notice date, purpose/perspective, and whether another source agrees.

GATHER

Gather without guessing

3 MIN

Your research table

  1. Write a question that could help an argument.
  2. Use specific search terms or a teacher/library collection.
  3. Record the source title, author/organisation and link or publication details.
  4. Paraphrase one relevant idea accurately; use quotation marks only for exact words.
  5. Mark: supports, complicates or challenges my claim.
STUDIO

Evidence studio

13 MIN
Target: two traceable notes

Use the approved source collection, library resources or teacher-guided search. If you cannot verify a claim, write “needs checking” — do not use it.

What does this source actually allow me to say?

REHEARSE

Partner audit: can the trail be followed?

4 MIN
□ I can find who made this source.   □ My note matches the source.   □ My citation has enough detail to find it again.   □ I have not invented a quote, number or expert.

Say: “I can trace this because…” / “Before using this, I need to check…”

EXIT

One useful source. One honest next step.

2 MIN
For my topic, I can use __________ because __________. My citation will include __________. I still need to check __________.
