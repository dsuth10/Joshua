# Lesson 33: Self-Editing & Meaning

## Lesson purpose

Students self-edit their individual persuasive assessment drafts, first for meaning and cohesion and then for sentence flow, precise topic language, punctuation and spelling. They make and explain one visible revision.

## Curriculum focus

- **Year 5 — AC9E5LY08:** spell using phonic, morphemic and grammatical knowledge.
- **Year 6 — AC9E6LY08:** use spelling knowledge to spell technical and complex words.

## Learning intention

We are learning to edit our persuasive draft so that a reader can follow our reasoning and trust our word choices.

## Success criteria

- I can check that each paragraph has a clear point, supporting detail and link.
- I can improve one connector or expanded noun group so my meaning is precise.
- I can read a sentence aloud and repair its flow or punctuation.
- I can check uncertain and topic-specific spellings with a trusted source.
- I can show one before-and-after revision and explain why it helps my reader.

## Assessment alignment

This is editing time for the **individual** 200–400 word (suggested) written persuasive text. It directly supports the assessment requirement to review and edit for spelling, punctuation and meaning, while retaining each student’s ideas, sources and authorship.

## Preparation

- Open the presentation; all DO/WORK/RECORD/FINISH/CHECK logistics are in the Notes popup only.
- Provide current drafts, organiser, a coloured pencil or revision marks, approved dictionaries and checked source notes.
- Use a teacher-created or anonymous practice paragraph for the think-aloud; do not rewrite student assessment work in the model.

## Core sequence (50 min)

1. Purposeful entry — 3 min
2. Two editor lenses — 3 min
3. Cohesion choice — 4 min
4. Meaning-first think-aloud — 6 min
5. Map paragraph jobs — 5 min
6. Precision of noun groups — 5 min
7. Sentence-flow repair — 5 min
8. Conventions check — 4 min
9. Self-editing studio — 12 min
10. Reader check and writer decision — 5 min
11. Exit revision — 3 min

## Differentiation

**Support:** edit one paragraph at a time, use a ruler to track lines, colour-code point/detail/link, orally rehearse sentences, and use approved word banks or dictionaries.

**Lucas (ICP):** read the opinion aloud to a trusted adult; identify one clear idea by pointing or speaking; check up to five high-frequency or personally important words; make one meaning-preserving change using a scribe if required.

**Extend:** map cohesion across the whole text, test whether every expanded noun group is exact and useful, and reflect against the marking guide without adding unsupported claims.

## Formative assessment

The exit “before / after / why” sample distinguishes surface correction from a meaning-led revision. Sort students for subsequent conferences: cohesion, precision, sentence flow or spelling strategy.

## Safeguards

Students revise their own work. Peer feedback describes reader understanding rather than supplying content. Do not invent facts, quotations, expert views, source details or technical vocabulary.
