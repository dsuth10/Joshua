## 🏗️ 1. Text Structure (How your report is organised)

### What You Did Exceptionally Well (Your Stars! ⭐)
*   **Clear Paragraphing:** You have organised your report into five distinct paragraphs, each focusing on a different part of the event (the cause, the weather, the impact, the communications, and the lessons learnt). This makes it very easy to follow!
*   **Wonderful Introduction:** Your first paragraph does a brilliant job introducing the topic of bushfires, the three things they need to burn (heat, fuel, and oxygen), and a sneak peek of what the rest of your report is about.
*   **Expert Sources:** You did an amazing job including the names of scientists and doctors (like *Scientist Keish*, *Dr Locker*, and *Dr Poodle*) to support your facts. This shows you have done great research!

### Action Steps to Improve (Your Wishes! 💫)
*   **Add Subheadings:** Information reports use subheadings to act as signposts for the reader. Adding a bold subheading above each paragraph will help the reader navigate your report.
    *   *Tip:* Try adding these subheadings:
        *   Before Paragraph 2: **What was Ash Wednesday?**
        *   Before Paragraph 3: **The Perfect Recipe for Disaster (Weather Conditions)**
        *   Before Paragraph 4: **The Cruelty of the Flames (Impact on Communities)**
        *   Before Paragraph 5: **A Communications Crisis**
        *   Before Paragraph 6: **Lessons Learnt: Rewriting the Rulebook**
*   **Fix Sentence Fragments (Incomplete Sentences):**
    *   *Look at this sentence:* *"The massive bushfire that swept throughout south Australia and Victoria known as Ash Wednesday."*
    *   *Why edit it?* This sentence is missing a doing-word (verb) to explain what happened.
    *   *Try this instead:* *"The massive bushfires that swept throughout South Australia and Victoria in 1983 became known as Ash Wednesday."*
*   **Fix Run-on Sentences:**
    *   *Look at this sentence:* *"The tragedy didn’t just begin on Wednesday it had been building for months."*
    *   *Why edit it?* This has two separate ideas joined together without a conjunction (joining word) or proper punctuation.
    *   *Try this instead:* *"The tragedy didn’t just begin on Wednesday; in fact, it had been building for months due to a severe drought."*
*   **Connect the Gap:**
    *   Look at the end of Paragraph 5 and the start of Paragraph 6. Paragraph 5 ends with the words *"As a direct"*, and Paragraph 6 begins with *"As a result..."* It looks like a sentence got cut in half!
    *   *Try joining them like this:* *"As a direct result of these communication issues, Australia assembled standardised emergency radio networks."*

*   **Objective Tone:** You kept your writing factual and objective, which is perfect for an information report.

### Action Steps to Improve (Your Wishes! 💫)
*   **Capital Letters for Proper Nouns (Names of places and people):**
    *   We always use capital letters for the names of specific states, famous roads, and people's titles. Let's fix these in your draft:
        *   *"south Australia"* ➡️ *"South Australia"* (both words need capitals)
        *   *"great ocean road"* ➡️ *"Great Ocean Road"* (all three words need capitals)
        *   *"Dr locker"* ➡️ *"Dr Locker"*
        *   *"Dr poodle"* ➡️ *"Dr Poodle"*
        *   *"Adelaide hills"* ➡️ *"Adelaide Hills"*
    *   *Tip:* Don't forget that when you start a sentence with a word like *"unfortunately"*, it needs a capital letter too (*"Unfortunately..."*).
*   **Plurals and Subject-Verb Matching:**
    *   *Look at this:* *"the forest were dry"* ➡️ If you mean one forest, write *"the forest was dry"*. If you mean more than one, write *"the forests were dry"*.
    *   *Look at this:* *"different locality"* ➡️ Because you are talking about more than one, it should be the plural form: *"different localities"*.
    *   *Look at this:* *"there was 75 people lost their lives"* ➡️ Because 75 is a plural number, we use 'were'. Try this: *"there were 75 people who lost their lives..."* or simply *"75 people lost their lives..."*
