Create a spoken text to express a preference for a place or setting to
peers

You are going to create an editorial to persuade your peers to visit
either the Amazon or the Daintree Rainforest.

1.  Plan your editorial. Use the planning sheet to help you. Think
    about:

- how you will introduce your editorial

- the reasons why this rainforest should be visited

- how you will describe the key features of this place or setting

- how you will conclude your editorial

- the images that will support your text

2.  Create your Editorial.

- Organise your opinions using an appropriate structure to persuade your
  peers. Think about how you will:

<!-- -->

- introduce your topic

- sequence your reasons and include details

- end your talk.

<!-- -->

- Choose language features to strengthen your presentation. For example:

<!-- -->

- topic-specific vocabulary

- words that express and explain your preference clearly (for example:
  'liked', 'preferred', 'because')

- adjectives to describe qualities or features of your preferred place
  or setting

- a range of sentence types.

<!-- -->

- Choose the images to support your presentation

3.  Rehearse with a partner. Practise:

- speaking so your partner can hear and understand what you are saying

- using an appropriate pace --- not too fast or too slow

- using expression --- vary your tone and volume to emphasise important
  or interesting details

- using gestures, or actions to communicate

- adjusting your expression --- vary your tone and volume

- listening actively when your partner is speaking

- asking your partner questions about their preferred setting.

4.  Produce your presentation. Your teacher may present to the class as
    an audience.
