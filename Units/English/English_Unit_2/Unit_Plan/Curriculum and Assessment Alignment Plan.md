## Australian Curriculum Version 9: English — Unit 2
### Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts

---

## About This Document

The English curriculum is recursive and cumulative; knowledge, understanding and skills are revisited and mastery develops across Prep to Year 10. The three strands of Australian Curriculum: English — Language, Literature and Literacy — are integrated in this model.

The Curriculum and Assessment Alignment Planner (CAAP) for each unit demonstrates the alignment of the assessment, curriculum teaching and learning as outlined in the K–6CPM English Year level plan for Prep to Year 6. The CAAP shows the alignment of the aspects of the achievement standards being assessed and/or monitored to the relevant content descriptions for Prep to Year 6, and helps to inform unit planning by showing the progression of learning across year levels.

In Unit 2, there is one assessment in Years Prep to 6.

> **Note:** This Curriculum and Assessment Alignment Planner is provided to support planning. Content is cyclically reviewed.

## Information for Teachers

### Text Selection

The AC V9 assessment tasks for P–6 CPM English are designed to be generic with suggested example texts and contexts provided in the advice section. This allows schools to choose texts and contexts that best suit their students and local settings for each unit.

Text complexity describes the features and scope of the text. There are five levels of text complexity: simple (including decodable texts), elementary (including decodable texts), moderately complex, complex and highly complex. Consider the literacy demands of the curriculum and the assessment when choosing your texts.

#### Text Features by Year Level

| | Prep | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | **Year 5** | Year 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Texts** | Decodable and authentic texts with straightforward sequences; simple and compound sentences; high-frequency words | Decodable texts aligned with phonic development; informative texts with illustrations presenting new content about familiar topics | Literary texts spanning several pages; informative texts with new content; varied sentence structures; range of punctuation conventions | Texts supporting independent readers; informative texts with complex and technical content; varied sentence structures; a variety of punctuation conventions | Literary texts describing events developing over chapters; informative texts with increasing complexity; a variety of punctuation conventions | **Literary texts with complex sequences, elaborated events including flashbacks and shifts in time. Informative texts with technical information about a wide range of topics; chapters, headings, subheadings, tables of contents, indexes and glossaries; complex sentences; unfamiliar technical vocabulary; figurative language; information in various images and graphics** | Literary texts with less predictable characters; flashbacks and shifts in time. Informative texts with technical information; headings, subheadings, tables of contents; complex sentences; unfamiliar technical vocabulary; figurative and idiomatic language; varieties of images and graphics |

#### Suggested Features of Informative Texts (Year 5)

For Year 5, informative texts should feature:

- Clear informative text structure
- Cohesive structure
- Specialist or technical terms
- Vocabulary and language features, including **expanded noun groups**, a variety of sentences including **complex sentences**, sentences with strong starting points, and subjective and/or objective language
- Authoritative sources
- Multimodal elements including a variety of images (photographs, diagrams, graphics) including **a sequence of images** (e.g. before and after photos, short comic strips, illustrations to show a procedure)
- Interesting formatting and layout
- A variety of structural features (e.g. headings, subheadings, homepage, subpages and hyperlinks)

### Teaching and Learning Suggestions

#### Prep–Year 2

**Introduction to a text-based unit:**
- Introduce an informative text to engage students. Model reading aloud, highlighting the conventions of print.
- Build background knowledge specific to the informative texts chosen.
- Engage with narrative stories on the same topic as the information texts, discussing features of stories and making comparisons.
- Share the assessment task and learning intentions for the unit.

**Developing phonics, vocabulary and comprehension:**
- Build topic-specific vocabulary appropriate to year level.
- Read imaginative and informative decodable texts that align with phonic development.
- Establish reading routines (accountable reading, choral reading, echo reading and oral cloze).
- Practise monitoring meaning, re-reading and self-correcting.
- Practise using comprehension strategies (visualising, predicting, connecting, summarising and questioning).

**Exploring and comparing texts:**
- Compare an informative text and a narrative on the same topic.
- Discuss the differences in purpose, audience, text structures and layout, language features, and images.

**Creating and sharing (presentation skills):**
- Exchange ideas in pairs and small groups, practising appropriate listening behaviours.
- Practise through shared construction: set out an information report, add a title and subheadings, find information, use topic-specific words and add an appropriate image.
- Give examples of a spoken report and discuss presentation skills.

#### Years 3–6

**Introduction to informative texts:**
- Review students' prior knowledge of informative texts, including the different types (factual recounts, descriptions, procedures, explanations, biographies, online texts, documentaries and news footage).
- Review audience, purpose and main ideas for informative texts.
- Discuss what makes a source authoritative *(Y5)*: consider experience and qualifications of authors, website content, writers and publishers.
- Explore objective and subjective language *(Y4–6)* and bias *(Y6)*.
- Share the assessment task and learning intentions for the unit.

**Text structures and organisation:**
- Describe the characteristic stages and phases of informative texts and how these relate to purpose.
- Discuss text navigation features of print and online texts that enhance readability, including table of contents, glossary, chapters, headings and subheadings, headlines, drop-down menus, links, graphics and layout.
- Use paragraphs with topic sentences to organise texts.

**Sentences (Year 5):**
- Explore how texts can be made cohesive by using the **starting point of a sentence or paragraph** to give prominence to the message.
- Understand that the structure of a complex sentence includes a **main clause and at least one dependent clause**, and how writers can use this structure for effect.
- Know that complex sentences make connections between ideas to provide a reason, state a purpose, express a condition, make a concession or link two ideas in terms of various time relations.

**Language features (Year 5):**
- Explore language features that support meaning (clause- and word-level grammar, vocabulary, punctuation, images).
- Discuss how vocabulary is used to express **greater precision of meaning**, including specialist and technical terms.
- Explore a range of synonyms and antonyms and morphological knowledge to build understanding.

**Visual features (Year 5):**
- Explain how the **sequence of images** in print, digital and film texts has an effect on meaning.
- Explore how sequential events can be represented visually by a series of images, including comic strips, timelines, photo stories, procedure diagrams and flowcharts, life cycle diagrams.

**Creating texts (Year 5):**
- Plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal informative texts.
- Use **visual features, text structure appropriate to the topic and purpose, text connectives, expanded noun groups, specialist and technical vocabulary, and punctuation**.

**Presenting an informative text (Year 5):**
- Explore **multimodal (including digital) elements** that add to meaning.
- Use model spoken texts to strengthen understanding of features of voice.
- Practise delivering short spoken texts, varying **tone, pace, pitch and volume** for effect.
