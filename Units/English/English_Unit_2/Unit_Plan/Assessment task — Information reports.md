# Assessment Task — Information Reports ☼
## English AC V9 — Year 5
### Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts

This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will engage with the task.

The assessment task gathers evidence of student learning for these aspects of the **Year 5 English achievement standard**:

**Reading and viewing**

- They read, view and comprehend texts created to inform, influence and/or engage audiences.
- They explain how characteristic text structures support the purpose of texts.
- They explain how language features including literary devices, and visual features contribute to the effect and meaning of a text.

**Writing and creating**

- They create written and/or multimodal texts, including literary texts, for particular purposes and audiences, developing and expanding on ideas with supporting details from topics or texts.
- They use paragraphs to organise, develop and link ideas.
- They use language features including complex sentences, tenses, topic-specific vocabulary and literary devices, and/or multimodal features.

**Speaking and listening**

- They use different text structures to organise, develop and link ideas.
- They use language features including topic-specific vocabulary and literary devices, and/or multimodal features and features of voice.

The following materials can be used when implementing Unit 2: Examining, creating and sharing informative texts in the K–6 Curriculum Planning Map (K–6CPM).

See the Curriculum and Assessment Alignment Planner (CAAP) to guide unit planning when developing a teaching and learning sequence and implementing the assessment in the unit.

---

## Assessment Conventions and Resources

**Purpose:** To read, view, comprehend and explain text structures and language and visual features of informative texts. To create a written multimodal information report. To present a multimodal information report to an audience.

**Technique:** Short response (Part A); extended response (Part B); observed demonstration (Part C).

### Conditions

Administer the task individually with students.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Length of student responses should be considered in the context of the assessment and appropriate to the year level.

May be completed over multiple lessons.

---

### Implementation Advice

---

## Part A: Reading and Viewing

Students review an informative text and respond to questions, identifying text structures, language features and visual features that support the purpose of the text and contribute to the effect and meaning.

Instructions

Ask students to independently read the informative text.

After students read the text, continue to the questions.

**Note:** Texts should include visual features such as images, illustrations and diagrams that add ideas and information not included in the written text.

---

## Part B: Writing and Creating

Students write a multimodal information report for an audience, developing and expanding ideas using details from learnt topics or texts.

### Instructions

Guide students to select a topic to report on, for example: a topic taught in class, a topic from texts read in class, or any topic of interest to students.

Establish who the audience will be.

Assist students to decide how the information report will be structured.

Provide a planning sheet for students. See **Sheet — Planning (optional)** as an example.

Guide students to gather ideas from authoritative sources that will provide supporting detail and engage the reader.

Students write their text. Guide them to use:

text structures, language features and multimodal features including visual features appropriate to the task.

Prompt students to review and edit their text.

---

## Part C: Speaking and Listening

Students present a multimodal information report for an identified audience using multimodal features and features of voice.

**This could be:**

a presentation with a slideshow or a poster

a presentation in costume using props or artefacts

a video presentation

a ‘roving reporter’ style news presentation

any other mode of presentation negotiated between students and teacher.

Teachers may use **Sheet ****—**** Multimodal presentation****:**** Teacher recording sheet (optional)** to observe and record evidence.

Instructions

Guide students to plan their presentation and sequence their ideas. Their written text will be an important scaffold for their presentation.

Confirm with students that while they may choose to use the same information, topic-specific vocabulary, sequence of ideas and multimodal elements from their written report, their presentation should be more than a reading of their written text. It should be enhanced with introductory and concluding statements, appropriate formality, features of voice and engaging multimodal elements such as visual and digital.

## Part A: Reading and Viewing

Read the informative text provided by your teacher and answer the questions. You may need to re-read or scan parts of the text to respond to the questions.

Main ideas, purpose and audience

What is the topic of the text?

What are some facts from the text?

Identify the main idea/s and include supporting ideas and information from the text.

Who is the audience for this text?

What is the author’s purpose in writing the text? Use examples from the text in your answer.

### Text Structures

- What are the characteristic features of this text?
- Explain how the characteristic features of the text:
  - support the purpose
  - enhance navigation
  - cohesion and build meaning.

### Language and Visual Features

How has the author used language features including vocabulary to build ideas and make the meaning more precise? Provide examples from the text in your answer.

How has the author used visual features (for example: images, photographs, diagrams, graphics) and their sequencing, design, format and layout to create effect and make meaning more precise? Provide example/s from the text in your answer.

---

## Part B: Writing and Creating

You will create a multimodal information report for an audience, developing and expanding ideas using details from learnt topics or texts.

### Plan Your Text

Select a topic to report on, for example: a topic taught in class, a topic from texts read in class, or a topic of interest to you.

Establish who the audience will be.

Decide how the information report will be structured.

You may like to use a planning sheet to organise your ideas. See Sheet — Planning (optional) as an example.

Gather ideas from authoritative sources that will provide supporting detail and engage the reader.

Include differing ideas and opinions on the topic.

### Create Your Text

Use:

text structures appropriate to the task including:

headings and/or subheadings

well-structured paragraphs with topic sentences that expand on details

a range of sentences including compound and complex sentences

fact boxes, dot points, captions and hyperlinks (if appropriate)

language features including:

adverbs to give precise meaning to verbs and more detail

adjectives in expanded noun groups for fuller descriptions and details

topic-specific, specialist and technical vocabulary

cohesive devices, including using the starting point of a sentence or paragraph to give prominence to the message and to guide the reader through the text

objective language to suit the purpose

relevant tense

multimodal features that support meaning:

animations, voiceovers, images, maps, diagrams

costumes and/or props

visual features for meaning and effect:

placement and size

framing

sequencing, for example: before and after images.

Review and edit your text.

---

## Part C: Speaking and Listening

You are going to present a multimodal information report to your audience using features of voice. You can use the information, topic-specific vocabulary, sequence of ideas, and multimodal features from your written text but you will need to adapt any text or language features so that your spoken text flows. You may also include any new ideas, together with any visual and/or digital features that support your text and engage your audience.

Your multimodal information report could be:

a presentation to a slideshow or a poster

a presentation in costume using props or artefacts

a video presentation

a ‘roving reporter’ style news presentation

any other mode of presentation negotiated between you and your teacher that allows you to use your features of voice for effect.

Plan and create your presentation.

Remember to include:

an introduction

logically sequenced information

supporting details and elaborations to expand ideas

references to reliable sources of information, for example: interviews, experts

topic-specific vocabulary and more specific and precise words to replace general words

objective language

multimodal elements (visual and digital features) such as:

graphics, sound and visuals to enhance audience engagement and understanding

costumes and/or props

a conclusion.

Rehearse your presentation with a partner, seeking feedback.

Experiment with features of voice such as controlling tone, volume, pitch and pace to suit the audience.

Ask for feedback and use it to improve your presentation.

Present the report to an audience.
