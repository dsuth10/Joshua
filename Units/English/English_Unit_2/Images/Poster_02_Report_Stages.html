Stages of an
Information Report

A structural guide to organising factual information to inform your audience clearly and effectively.

1

Classification
General Statement

Introduces the topic and defines the scope. Tells the reader exactly what the report is going to be about.

2

Description
Physical Details

Provides the essential factual details. Describes what the subject is, how it works, or how it forms in nature.

3

Factual Elaboration
Evidence & Examples

Expands ideas with evidence, scientific data, and historical examples. Proves points using authoritative sources.

4

Summary
Concluding Statement

Wraps up the key points. Often includes a final thought about human impact, future preparation, or ongoing research.

Understanding Tropical Cyclones

1. CLASSIFY

A tropical cyclone is a rapidly rotating storm system characterised by a low-pressure centre, strong winds, and a spiral arrangement of thunderstorms that produce heavy rain.

2. DESCRIBE

These massive storm systems form over large bodies of relatively warm water. They derive their energy through the evaporation of water from the ocean surface, which ultimately condenses into clouds and rain when moist air rises and cools to saturation.

3. ELABORATE

For example, Cyclone Tracy struck Darwin on Christmas Eve in 1974. It was a Category 4 storm with peak winds recorded at 217 km/h, causing unprecedented destruction and triggering Australia's largest peacetime evacuation.

4. SUMMARISE

In conclusion, tropical cyclones are among the most powerful and destructive forces of nature. Understanding their formation and historical impact is crucial for preparing vulnerable communities for future extreme weather events.
