# Lesson 9: Floods: Digging into Language (Complex Sentences)

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts
**Focus:** Identifying the structure of a complex sentence, including its main clause and dependent clause(s).
**Core Text:** Floods Archive (How Floods Work; The Brisbane River System)
**Duration:** 45-60 minutes

---

## 1. Learning Intention
I can identify the structure of a complex sentence, including its main clause and at least one dependent clause. (AC9E5LA05)

## 2. Success Criteria
- I can define what a simple, compound, and complex sentence is.
- I can identify the main (independent) clause in a complex sentence.
- I can identify the dependent clause in a complex sentence.
- I can explain how a dependent clause adds meaning (e.g., reason, time, condition).
- I can write my own complex sentences about floods.

---

## 3. Introduction (Warm-up)
**Activate (10 mins):**
- **Discuss:** Review simple and compound sentences. Write a simple sentence on the board: *"Floodwaters rose quickly."* 
- Ask: "What makes this a simple sentence?" (It has one main clause, one verb/action, makes sense on its own).
- Now, write a complex sentence next to it: *"Although heavy rainfall had been forecast for days, the floodwaters rose with a speed that caught residents entirely off guard."*
- Ask: "What is the difference? Which sentence gives us more information about *when* or *why*?"
- **Display Slide 1-3:** Introduce simple, compound, and complex sentences. Emphasize that complex sentences allow writers to show reason, time, condition, or concession.

---

## 4. Core Activities

### Part A: Explicit Modelling (15 mins)
- **Model:** Introduce the concept of a main (independent) clause and a dependent clause.
- **Display Slide 4:** Show a sentence from the *How Floods Work* archive: *"At its most basic, a flood occurs when water covers land that is normally dry."*
- Think aloud: "Let's find the main clause—the part that can stand alone as a sentence. 'A flood occurs.' Now, what's the dependent clause? 'when water covers land that is normally dry.' It depends on the main clause to make sense."
- **Display Slides 5-6:** Jointly construct and break down more examples from the Floods archive, identifying the joining words (subordinating conjunctions) like *because, although, when, if, since*.

### Part B: Independent and Guided Practice (20 mins)
- **Goal:** Students will work on the **Lesson 09 Student Worksheet**.
- **Task:** 
  1. Identify whether provided sentences from the Floods archive are simple, compound, or complex.
  2. For the complex sentences, underline the main clause in blue and the dependent clause in red.
  3. Expand simple sentences into complex sentences by adding a dependent clause (using provided conjunctions).
- Circulate the room to provide guidance and ensure students are correctly identifying clauses.

---

## 5. Conclusion (Consolidation)
**Review (5 mins):**
- Bring the class back together.
- Have 2-3 students share a complex sentence they created.
- Ask the class to identify the main clause and the dependent clause in the shared sentences.
- **Discuss:** "Why do authors use complex sentences in informative texts like the Floods Archive?" (To provide detailed explanations, show cause and effect, and make the writing flow better).

---

## 6. Differentiation

### Support (Lucas - Year 2 Pathway)
- **Goal:** Identify a simple sentence and a compound sentence (with 'and', 'but', 'so') from a printed excerpt. Underline the joining word. Write one compound sentence about floods. (AC9E2LA06)
- **Resource:** Provide **Lesson_09_Handout_Lucas.docx**.
- **Action:** Lucas uses the simplified handout. Working with the teacher or SSO, he reads short sentences about floods, circles the joining words ("and", "but", "so"), and practices combining two simple sentences into one compound sentence.

### Extension
- Fast finishers must find two additional complex sentences directly from the *Brisbane River System* sub-page, write them in their exercise books, and label the main clause, dependent clause, and the subordinating conjunction used. They must write a sentence explaining how the dependent clause adds meaning to the main clause.
