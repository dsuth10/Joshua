# Lesson 8: The Language We Choose (Social Context & Register)

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts
**Focus:** Explaining how language is selected for social contexts and signals the relationship between author and audience.
**Core Text:** Cyclone Archive (Cyclone Tracy)
**Duration:** 45-60 minutes

---

## 1. Learning Intention
I can explain how language is selected for social contexts and signals the relationship between author and audience. (AC9E5LA01)

## 2. Success Criteria
- I can understand the difference between objective and subjective language.
- I can identify the difference between a formal and informal register.
- I can sort phrases based on their language features.
- I can identify formal/objective language choices in the Cyclone Archive and explain how they build trust with the reader.

---

## 3. Introduction (Warm-up)
**Activate (10 mins):**
- **Discuss:** "Think about how you talk to your best friend on the playground. Now think about how you would speak to the Principal in their office. Do you use the same words? The same tone? Why not?"
- Establish the concept of **Social Context**: The situation we are in changes the language we choose.
- Display **Slide 1 & 2**: "The Language We Choose". Discuss how authors also change their language depending on who their audience is and what their purpose is.
- Display **Slide 3**: "Objective vs. Subjective". Explain that informative texts rely on *objective* language (facts, measurable data, unbiased) to build trust. Personal accounts rely on *subjective* language (feelings, opinions, personal experiences) to build empathy.
- Display **Slide 4**: "Formal vs. Informal". Explain the difference in register (e.g., "The residence was destroyed" vs "My house got smashed").

---

## 4. Core Activities

### Part A: Exploring Two Voices (15 mins)
- **Activity:** Display two texts on the board.
  - Text 1: The opening paragraph of the Cyclone Tracy archive page.
  - Text 2: A teacher-provided personal blog excerpt from a survivor of Cyclone Tracy.
- Read both texts aloud.
- **Discuss:** "How does the language feel different? What is the author's relationship to us in Text 1? (Expert sharing facts). What is the relationship in Text 2? (A person sharing their story)."
- **Identify:** Work together to highlight objective facts in Text 1, and subjective feelings/opinions in Text 2.

### Part B: Phrase Sorting Challenge (10 mins)
- **Goal:** Students categorise language choices. Provide the **Lesson 08 Phrase Sorting Handout** (or cards if pre-cut).
- Students work in pairs to sort a list of phrases into two categories: "Formal & Objective" or "Informal & Subjective".
- Examples:
  - *The wind speed reached 217 km/h.* (Formal/Objective)
  - *It felt like a freight train was driving through the living room.* (Informal/Subjective)
  - *Significant structural failure occurred.* (Formal/Objective)
  - *Everything we owned was just gone.* (Informal/Subjective)
- **Review:** Display **Slide 5** to check answers as a class.

### Part C: Author's Choices (15 mins)
- **Goal:** Students independently find examples of formal/objective language in the text.
- Instruct students to open the **Cyclone Tracy** archive page.
- Task: Find 3 examples of formal/objective language. For each example, write down the sentence and explain how this choice signals that the author is a reliable expert (e.g., "The author uses precise numbers instead of saying 'it was really windy', which shows they have researched the facts").
- Display **Slide 6** with instructions.

---

## 5. Conclusion (Consolidation)
**Review (5 mins):**
- Bring the class back together.
- Display **Slide 7**.
- Ask 2-3 students to share one formal sentence they found and how it built trust.
- **Discuss:** "If the Cyclone Archive was written using informal, subjective language, would we trust it as an information source? Why or why not?"

---

## 6. Differentiation

### Support (Lucas - Year 2 Pathway)
- **Goal:** Circle two words on a printed excerpt that indicate this is a factual text. Discuss with teacher: "Is this the author's opinion or a fact?" (AC9E2LA09, AC9E2LY03)
- **Resource:** Provide **Lesson_08_Lucas_Worksheet.docx**.
- **Action:** Lucas works with the teacher/SSO. He reads a short, simplified excerpt about Cyclone Tracy. He circles two "fact words" (like a number or a place). He then talks about whether the text is telling a story from someone's head, or telling facts from the real world.

### Extension
- Ask fast finishers to rewrite a formal, objective paragraph from the Cyclone Archive into an informal, subjective paragraph from the perspective of someone living through it.
