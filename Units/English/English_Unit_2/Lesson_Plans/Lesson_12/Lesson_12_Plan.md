# Lesson 12: Controlling the Message (Cohesion)

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts  
**Term:** 2, 2026  
**Sequence:** 2 — Language Features & Text Structure (Week 3)  
**Core Text:** Floods Archive — Brisbane History & Human Cost  

---

## Learning Intention
I can explain how texts are made cohesive by using the starting point of a sentence or paragraph to give prominence to the message. (AC9E5LA04)

## Success Criteria
- I can identify the **Theme** (starting point) and **Rheme** (the rest) of a sentence.
- I can explain how changing the Theme changes what information is emphasised.
- I can rewrite sentences from the Floods Archive with different starting points to achieve different effects.

---

## Differentiation Key
- **Year 5:** Core Year 5 pathway.
- **Lucas (Y2):** Identify what a sentence is mainly about by looking at the opening words.

---

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Activate (10 mins)
- **Display:** Compare two paragraphs on the class screen (from Slide 2).
    - **Paragraph A:** Sentences start with the subject (e.g., "The flood caused...", "Water rose...").
    - **Paragraph B:** Sentences start with circumstantial information (e.g., "In 1974, the Brisbane River...", "Across the city...").
- **Discuss:** Which paragraph feels more "professional"? Which one emphasises *when* and *where* things happened rather than just *what* happened? 
- **Concept:** Introduce the idea that the first few words of a sentence act as a "signpost" for the reader.

### 2. Explore (15 mins)
- **Direct Instruction:** Introduce **Theme** and **Rheme**.
    - **Theme:** The starting point of the message (the "point of departure").
    - **Rheme:** The new information being told about the theme.
- **Examine:** Use Slide 5 and 6 to show screenshots from the *Brisbane History* and *Human Cost* sub-pages.
    - Point out how historical texts often start with **Time** or **Place** to provide context.
    - Point out how scientific or social reports often start with **Nouns/Subjects** to define the topic.

### 3. Model (15 mins)
- **Joint Construction:** Select a "bare assertion" sentence from the archive.
    - *Original:* "Fast-moving water is deceptive and lethal." (Theme = Fast-moving water)
    - *Rewrite:* "Because it is so deceptive, fast-moving water can be lethal." (Theme = Reason/Condition)
- **Discuss:** How does the rewrite change the focus? It focuses on the *reason* for the danger first.
- **Model:** Work through 2 more examples from the handout together.

### 4. Connect (20 mins)
- **Independent Task:** Students complete the **Lesson 12 Handout: Cohesion**.
    - **Part 1:** Identify Themes in archive excerpts.
    - **Part 2:** Rewrite 5 sentences from the Floods Archive to change the emphasis.
- **Lucas (Y2) Task:** Lucas compares simplified sentence pairs and discusses with the teacher/SSO which part of the sentence is the "start" and what it tells us.
