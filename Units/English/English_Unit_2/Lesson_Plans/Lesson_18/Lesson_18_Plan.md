# Lesson 18: Opening Classification Paragraph

## Learning Intention
I can write an opening classification paragraph using objective language, an expanded noun group and a complex sentence. (AC9E5LY06, AC9E5LA05, AC9E5LA06)

## Success Criteria
- I can identify the purpose of an opening paragraph.
- I can write with objective and authoritative language.
- I can use an expanded noun group to give precise detail.
- I can write a complex sentence with a dependent clause.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- Display the opening paragraph of the **Bushfires Archive** hub page:
  > *"Bushfires are severe, uncontrolled outdoor fires that burn through areas of forest, bushland or grassland, which are common across the dry Australian landscape."*
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - What is the classification statement? (*"severe, uncontrolled outdoor fires that burn through areas of forest..."*)
  - What is the expanded noun group? (*"the dry Australian landscape"*)
  - Where is the complex sentence structure? (*"which are common across..."* - a relative dependent clause starting with *which*).
  - Discuss the tone of this opening sentence. Is it formal and objective? How do we know? (*It states facts rather than opinions like "scary" or "awful".*)

### Explore (15 mins)
- Discuss the primary purpose of an **Opening Classification Paragraph** in an information report:
  1. **Identify the Topic:** Clearly state what the natural disaster is (definition/classification).
  2. **Establish the Register:** Use third-person, objective, and factual language to build authority.
  3. **Set the Scope:** Outline what the reader can expect to learn.
- Unpack key language elements:
  - **Objective Language:** Focus on facts (e.g., *"severe, uncontrolled fires"*) rather than subjective opinions (e.g., *"terrible natural disasters"*).
  - **Expanded Noun Groups:** Adding modifiers to nouns to make them more descriptive and precise (e.g., *precipitation* becomes *"extreme, heavy tropical precipitation"*).
  - **Complex Sentences:** Combining an independent main clause with a dependent clause to add circumstantial detail (time, cause, condition, or concession).

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Think Aloud):** Model drafting an opening classification paragraph for the topic **Tornadoes**.
- *Think Aloud:* *"I need to define what a tornado is objectively, using a complex sentence and an expanded noun group."*
- **Modelled Draft:**
  > *"Tornadoes are violently rotating columns of air that extend from a thunderstorm to the ground, which typically develop during severe convective storms."*
- **Unpack the Modelled Draft with the Class:**
  - **Classification:** *"violently rotating columns of air that extend from a thunderstorm to the ground"*
  - **Expanded Noun Group:** *"violently rotating columns of air"* / *"severe convective storms"*
  - **Complex Sentence:** Main clause + dependent clause (*"which typically develop..."*).
  - **Objective Vocabulary:** *convective*, *rotating*, *extend*, *severely*.

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Independent Practice:** Students refer to their structural plans from Lesson 17 and draft their own opening classification paragraph.
- Students must incorporate:
  - One clear classification definition.
  - One expanded noun group (underlined).
  - One complex sentence (bracketed).
  - Precise topic-specific vocabulary.
- **Peer Share:** In the last 3 minutes, students swap drafts with a partner and complete the peer checklist on their worksheet:
  - [ ] Did they classify the disaster?
  - [ ] Did they use objective language?
  - [ ] Can you find an expanded noun group?
  - [ ] Can you find a complex sentence?

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- Lucas will write 2–3 simple sentences introducing a topic (e.g., *"Fires happen in Australia. They are very hot. They can be dangerous."*).
- Teacher or Support Officer supports his vocabulary choice using the pre-prepared word bank (e.g., *hot*, *dry*, *dangerous*, *big*, *safe*). (AC9E2LY06, AC9E2LA09)

### Extension
- Encourage students to write a multi-sentence opening paragraph, incorporating a second complex sentence that outlines the geographical context or seasonal frequency of their chosen disaster.

## Assessment
- **Formative:** Observation of student writing and peer feedback discussions.
- **Summative:** Lesson 18 Microsoft Forms Assessment (10 questions on opening paragraphs, objective language, and grammatical structures).
