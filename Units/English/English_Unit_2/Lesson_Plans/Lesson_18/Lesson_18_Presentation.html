Lesson 18: The Opening Paragraph

Writing an Objective and Precise Classification Paragraph

Learning Intention

I can write an opening classification paragraph using objective language, an expanded noun group and a complex sentence.

Success Criteria:

  • I can identify the purpose of an opening paragraph.
  • I can write with objective and authoritative language.
  • I can use an expanded noun group to add precise detail.
  • I can write a complex sentence with a dependent clause.

I can write two or three simple sentences to introduce my natural disaster topic.

My Success Checklist:

  • I can choose safe, descriptive, and nature words from my word bank.
  • I can write about what my natural disaster is like.
  • I can draw a clear picture of my natural disaster.

Activate: Mentor Text Analysis

Let's examine the opening paragraph of the Bushfires Archive:

"Bushfires are severe, uncontrolled outdoor fires that burn through areas of forest, bushland or grassland, which are common across the dry Australian landscape."

Notice these three critical ingredients:

  • Classification Identifies and defines exactly what a bushfire is.
  • Expanded Noun Group "the dry Australian landscape" adds rich description.
  • Complex Sentence Links the main clause to a dependent relative clause starting with "which...".

Let's look at this simple example about Bushfires:

"Fires happen in Australia. They are very hot and dry. They can be dangerous."

Notice how we use simple, powerful words:

  • Nature Words "Fires" and "Australia" tell us the topic.
  • Describing Words "hot", "dry", and "dangerous" describe the fire.
  • Simple Sentences Each sentence is short and shares one clear fact.

Explore: Purpose of the Opening

An opening classification paragraph serves three essential purposes in an information report:

1. Define & Classify

Clearly introduce the natural disaster topic. Classify it into a broader group (e.g., natural hazards, weather phenomena).

2. Set the Register

Use objective and authoritative language. Speak in the third person to make the writing factual and credible.

3. Establish the Scope

Introduce the main aspects that the rest of the report will explore (e.g., location, causes, impacts, preparation).

Model: Joint Construction (Tornadoes)

Let's examine how a professional writer drafts an opening classification paragraph:

"Tornadoes are violently rotating columns of air that extend from a thunderstorm to the ground, which typically develop during severe convective storms."
Classification defines exactly what tornadoes are in a factual way.
Expanded Noun Group "violently rotating columns of air" adds rich and precise detail.
Complex Sentence combines a main clause with a dependent clause starting with "which typically...".

Let's look at this simple example about Tornadoes:

"Tornadoes are big winds. They are very fast. Firefighters help keep us safe."
Nature Words "Tornadoes" and "winds" tell us what is happening.
Describing Words "fast" and "big" tell us what they feel and look like.
Safety Words "Firefighters", "help", and "safe" tell us how we stay protected.
