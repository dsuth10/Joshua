# Lesson 25: Earthquakes & Assessment Preparation

## Learning Intention
I can read and comprehend an informative text, explaining purpose, audience, text structures and language/visual features. (AC9E5LY03, AC9E5LY04, AC9E5LY05)

## Success Criteria
- I can skim and scan a complex informative text (the Earthquakes Archive) to locate specific facts under time constraints.
- I can identify the characteristic text structures of an information report (classification, description, factual elaboration).
- I can analyze how specific language features (expanded noun groups, complex sentences, circumstantial starting points) contribute to precise informative meaning.
- I can explain how visual features (tectonic cross-section diagrams, labels, and captions) multiply and clarify written information.
- I can formulate structured, two-step evidence-based responses (Claim + Technical Effect) to prepare for the Part A Assessment.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- **Introduction to Earthquakes:** Present Slide 3: *"Earthquakes & Seismology."* Introduce the Earthquakes Archive as the final and most complex core text of the unit. Discuss: *"What makes an informative text 'complex'? How do scientific texts pack so much detailed information into a single screen?"*
- **Skimming and Scanning Challenge:** 
  - Teacher displays the Earthquakes Hub page on the main screen. 
  - Give students exactly 30 seconds to locate three key pieces of information (e.g., the definition of a seismograph, the name of the earth's outermost layer, and the year of the Newcastle earthquake).
  - Discuss: What visual features did your eyes jump to? (*Headings, bold words, sidebars, images, tables.*) Highlight the difference between skimming (reading quickly for overall gist) and scanning (searching for specific words or numbers).
- **The Part A Assessment Target:** Explain that in the next lesson (Lesson 26), they will be sitting the formal Part A Assessment, which tests their reading and viewing comprehension. Today is their opportunity to master the visual and language features of scientific reports.

### Explore (10 mins)
- **Decoding Tectonic Boundaries (Visual & Diagram Analysis):** 
  - Navigate to Slide 4 (Plate Tectonics Interactive Map). 
  - Explain that scientific informative texts rely heavily on cross-sections and diagrams to explain abstract physical processes.
  - Review the three main types of plate boundaries: **Convergent** (colliding), **Divergent** (pulling apart), and **Transform** (sliding past each other).
  - **Class Exploration:** Have students come to the front board to click on the glowing boundary hotspots. For each hotspot:
    - Observe the movement vectors (indicated by dynamic arrow animations).
    - Unpack the geological stress type (compression, tension, shear stress).
    - Read the scientific outcome (e.g., faulting, subduction zones, volcanic island arcs, deep oceanic trenches).
    - Discuss: *"How does this interactive visual multiply the meaning of the written text? What does the diagram show us that the words alone cannot?"*

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Text & Language Analysis):**
  - Project Slide 5: *"Interactive Annotations: Decoding the Text."*
  - Teacher models reading a paragraph from the *Plate Tectonics* page aloud. Think aloud through the process of analyzing the text structure and language features.
  - *Think Aloud:* *"Let's examine this sentence: 'Although tectonic plates appear solid, the underlying asthenosphere flows like plastic, causing the overlying lithosphere to fracture.' First, I notice this is a complex sentence because it starts with a dependent clause ('Although tectonic plates appear solid'). This sets up a contrast. Second, the noun group 'the underlying asthenosphere' is expanded to provide a highly precise scientific description. Third, the verb 'fracture' is a specialist technical term. By clicking on these highlighted elements, let's see how they map to our assessment expectations. The structural stage is Factual Elaboration, and the language feature is a concession-based complex clause structure. We must explain that this choice allows the author to clarify the mechanical cause-and-effect of tectonic movement."*
  - **Shared Practice:** Model answering a practice question using a two-step formula:
    - **Step 1 (The Evidence):** Identify the exact language or visual feature (e.g., *"The author uses the expanded noun group 'highly volatile transform zones'..."*).
    - **Step 2 (The Technical Effect):** Explain how this feature builds precision and informs the audience (e.g., *"...to precisely describe the location of friction, signaling to the reader the extreme instability of the region."*).

### Connect (15 mins)
- **Part A Practice Worksheet:** Students work independently on `Lesson_25_Worksheet.docx` (Part A Practice Sheet). The worksheet mimics the exact question style, grading guidelines, and structural layouts of the formal assessment.
- **Practice Sheet Tasks:**
  - Section 1: Skim and scan a selected excerpt to answer literal comprehension questions.
  - Section 2: Identify and write the purpose and audience of the Earthquakes Archive text.
  - Section 3: Analyze a text excerpt, identifying one complex sentence, one expanded noun group, and explaining their precision effect.
  - Section 4: Explain how the Tectonic Boundaries diagram assists the reader in understanding physical stress.
- **The Comprehension Quiz Show Game:** 
  - In the final 5–7 minutes of the lesson, navigate to Slide 6 to launch the **Comprehension Quiz Show game**.
  - Standard students take turns answering 5 rapid-fire comprehension questions against the 30-second countdown clock.
  - Track score milestones as a class. Highlight how skimming/scanning strategies were essential to beat the clock.

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- **Focus:** Lucas practices identifying the basic structure of a website page (main title, headings, images, captions) and responding to factual questions using a scaffolded writing sheet (AC9E2LY03, AC9E2LA03).
- **Simplified Goals:**
  - I can find and circle the main website title.
  - I can point to and label the section headings.
  - I can describe what the earthquake image shows.
- **Activity:** Lucas completes `Lesson_25_Lucas_Handout.docx`. With teacher or SSO support, Lucas circles the title, the headings, and the explanatory image in a simplified mock-up of the Earthquakes Hub. He uses large-font sentence starters (*"This website is about..."*, *"Under the heading, I see..."*) to record his findings.
- **Interactive Game:** On Slide 7, Lucas plays the **"Heading & Image Finder" game**, clicking cards to identify correct visual page structures and receiving instant checkmarks and mascot praise.

### Extension
- **Advanced Rhetorical Audit:** Extension students analyze the authorial point of view in the *Australian Seismicity* sub-page, explaining how the choice of authoritative historical quotes (e.g., from Geoscience Australia) moves the report beyond bare assertion to objective proof.

## Assessment

### Formative
- Teacher circulates during the worksheet task, reviewing students' capacity to construct complete two-step responses (Evidence + Effect).
- Class participation metrics during the SVG Plate Tectonics exploration and the Quiz Show game.

### Summative (Diagnostic for Lesson 26)
- **Practice Sheet Review:** At the end of the lesson, collect `Lesson_25_Worksheet.docx`. Mark Section 3 and Section 4 using the rubrics to diagnose students who need targeted intervention in sentence structure and visual analysis prior to the formal assessment in Lesson 26.
