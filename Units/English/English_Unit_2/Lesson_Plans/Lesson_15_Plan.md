# Lesson 15: Floods: Point of View (Perspectives)

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts
**Focus:** Recognising that the point of view in a text influences how readers interpret and respond to it.
**Core Text:** Floods Archive (Human Cost sub-page)
**Duration:** 45-60 minutes

---

## 1. Learning Intention
I can recognise that the point of view in a text influences how readers interpret and respond to it. (AC9E5LE03)

## 2. Success Criteria
- I can define "Point of View" in the context of informative texts.
- I can identify the difference between objective (factual) and subjective (emotional) language.
- I can explain how an author's perspective changes what information is included in a text.
- I can compare two texts about the same event and explain how their points of view differ.

---

## 3. Introduction (Warm-up)
**Activate (10 mins):**
- **Fact vs. Opinion Sort:** Display a series of sentences on the board. Students must vote "Fact" or "Feeling" (Opinion/Subjective).
  - *"The Brisbane River peaked at 4.46 metres in 2011."* (Fact)
  - *"The sound of the rushing water was absolutely terrifying."* (Feeling)
  - *"Insurance claims reached $6.4 billion."* (Fact)
  - *"I felt so helpless watching the mud cover my garden."* (Feeling)
- **Discuss:** "Which sentences would you expect to find in a science archive? Which would you find in a diary or a blog?"
- **Introduce POV:** Explain that even "factual" texts have a point of view—the author chooses what to count as "important" (e.g., money vs. memories).

---

## 4. Core Activities

### Part A: Explicit Modelling (15 mins)
- **Model:** Display the *Human Cost* sub-page from the Floods Archive.
- **Analyze:** Identify 3 specific choices that show an **objective** point of view:
  1. **Vocabulary:** Use of technical terms like "macroeconomic shocks" and "capital stock."
  2. **Evidence:** Use of authoritative sources (World Bank, Deloitte, AIHW).
  3. **Images:** Use of data panels and satellite imagery (Moreton Bay sediment).
- **Contrast:** Introduce the "Survivor's Account" snippet (The Morning the River Came In).
- **Discuss:** "How does the survivor's POV change our understanding of the flood? Does it make you *feel* differently compared to the statistics?"

### Part B: Independent Comparison (20 mins)
- **Goal:** Students work on the **Lesson 15 Student Worksheet**.
- **Task:** 
  1. Read the provided side-by-side texts (Survivor vs. Archive).
  2. Complete the comparison table identifying: Tone, Vocabulary, Focus, and Effect on Reader.
  3. Write a short paragraph: *"The author's point of view in the archive is... I can tell because..."*
- **Circulate:** Check that students are moving beyond "it's about a flood" to "it's about the *money/science* of a flood" vs "it's about the *experience* of a flood."

---

## 5. Conclusion (Consolidation)
**Review (5 mins):**
- **The "So What?":** Why does POV matter when we research?
- Ask: "If you only read the scientific archive, what would you miss? If you only read the survivor blog, what would you miss?"
- **Success Criteria Check:** Self-assess against the LI using a "Fist to Five" (5 = I'm an expert, 0 = I need more help).

---

## 6. Differentiation

### Support (Lucas - Year 2 Pathway)
- **Goal:** Read/listen to a short first-person account about a flood. Answer: "Who is telling this story? Is it a fact or the person's opinion?" (AC9E2LY03, AC9E2LY05)
- **Resource:** Provide **Lesson_15_Lucas_Handout.docx**.
- **Action:** Lucas listens to the survivor account. He circles "How they feel" words (scared, sad, worried). He identifies that this is a *person's story*, not a *science book*.

### Extension
- **Perspective Shift:** Choose one fact from the *Human Cost* archive (e.g., the cost of property damage) and rewrite it as a sentence from the perspective of a small business owner who has just lost their shop. Maintain the "feeling" tone while using the "fact."
