# Lesson 23: Multimodal Meaning and Fire Science

## Learning Intention
I can create and interpret visual features (images, diagrams, captions) that support meaning in my text, while demonstrating comprehension of fire science and firehawk raptor adaptations. (AC9E5LA07, AC9E5LY06)

## Success Criteria
- I can explain how visual features (framing, salience, placement, colour) contribute to the purpose and meaning of a text.
- I can pair complex images from the Bushfires website with their communicative purpose and write highly effective captions.
- I can demonstrate comprehension of basic fire science (the Fire Triangle, fuel loads, and fire weather metrics).
- I can infer the intentionality, evolutionary adaptation, and land-management impact of fire-spreading raptors ("arsonist birds").

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- **Visual Warm-Up:** Display `ppe_breakdown_graphic.jpg` and `ember_attack_diagram.png` from the Bushfires website on the class screen (Slide 2). Elicit observations — "What do you notice? What are the key visual features used here? Why does the author use labels and red arrows?"
- **Multimodal Purpose:** Discuss the purpose of visuals in an information report. They are not merely decorative; they carry technical information, illustrate unseen mechanics, and prompt emotional responses.
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - *PPE Graphic:* The use of callouts and detailed labels communicates high-density factual information about firefighter safety.
  - *Ember Attack Diagram:* The red arrows showing wind blowing glowing embers from a distant forest fire directly into a home's leaf-filled gutters illustrate a cause-and-effect hazard mechanism that is difficult to explain through text alone.

### Explore (15 mins)
- **Interactive Visual Match:** Standard students use the **Interactive Visual Matching game** (Slides 3 and 4) on their devices (or together on the smartboard) to click and pair images with their intended purpose.
  - *Black Summer satellite composite:* Shows the immense, continental scale of smoke plumes drifting across the ocean.
  - *Black Saturday aerial destruction:* Uses high-salience, stark colours (blackened scrub against brick chimneys) to represent human loss and structural vulnerability.
- **Literal Fire Science:** Explore the scientific principles of bushfires (Slide 5-8):
  - *The Fire Triangle:* Highlighting that a fire requires **Fuel, Heat, and Oxygen** to exist. Removing any one component extinguishes the blaze.
  - *Fuel Loads & Moisture:* Explaining that dry leaves, bark, and twigs increase flammability, whereas moisture acts as a natural retardant.
  - *Extreme Fire Weather:* Discussing how high winds (above 35 kilometres per hour), high temperatures (above 40 degrees Celsius), and low relative humidity create catastrophic fire conditions.
- **Raptor Adaptations ("Arsonist Birds"):** Introduce the three predatory species in Northern Australia (Slides 9 and 10): the **Black Kite** (*Milvus migrans*), **Whistling Kite** (*Haliastur sphenurus*), and **Brown Falcon** (*Falco berigora*).

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Literal vs. Inferential Comprehension):**
  - **Literal Step-by-Step:** Model how the "arsonist birds" (specifically Whistling Kites and Brown Falcons) grab smouldering twigs in their talons and fly up to 1 kilometre away to drop them into dry grasslands (Slide 11-13).
  - **Inferring Intentionality:** Think aloud to model inferential comprehension. *Why do they do it?* "They aren't trying to destroy the forest; they are masters of hunting. The fire flushes out hidden lizards and grasshoppers, giving the birds an easy feast. This is a deliberate, learned hunting behaviour."
  - **Skeptics vs. Eyewitnesses:** Model balancing the scientific controversy (Slide 15). Western science demands video footage, whereas local Rangers, firefighters, and ancient Indigenous oral traditions have documented this intentional behaviour for centuries.
  - **Land Management Impact:** Model how fire managers must plan for these birds by creating wider firebreaks (often wider than 100 metres) because raptors can easily carry flames across standard barriers (Slide 16).

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Multimodal Capstone & Reflection:** Students complete the interactive sorting grids and cloze passages (Slides 17 and 18) to synthesise firehawk hunting patterns and their wider land management implications.
- **Guided Writing Practice:** Students complete their `Lesson_23_Worksheet.docx`, drafting a multimodal caption for an image of their choice, and answering 2 literal and 2 inferential comprehension questions.
- **Differentiated Pathway (Activated via Toggle):**
  - When the classroom screen or student devices are switched to **Differentiated Mode** (via the toggle in the top toolbar), the slide content shifts to support the Year 2 pathway.
  - Lucas works with the teacher or SSO, using Slide 3 and Slide 10 in differentiated mode.
  - Lucas completes a simplified visual matching activity, pairing large, high-contrast images of firefighters, birds with sticks, and fire trucks to simple captions (e.g., *"This bird is carrying a stick to start a fire,"* *"A brave firefighter fighting the fire"*).
  - Lucas completes his differentiated handout (`Lesson_23_Lucas_Handout.docx`), drawing lines to match photos to simple literal sentences, and completing a 2-sentence description using his structured word bank. (AC9E2LA08, AC9E2LY05)

## Differentiation

### Support: Differentiated Pathway (Toggle Mode)
- **Focus:** Literal visual association and vocabulary enrichment.
- **Scaffolds:** Simplified 1-to-1 matching games, high-salience borders, audio-friendly text cues, and thick tactile buttons.
- **Handout Scaffold:** Pre-printed matching columns and a structured word bank categorized into *Action Words* (flying, burning, spraying, helping), *Nouns* (bird, tree, fire, hose, truck), and *Describing Words* (hot, smoky, yellow, fast).
- **Sentence Frame:** *"The bird carries a _________ stick to get _________."*

### Extension
- Students write a balanced, multi-perspective paragraph analysing the scientific debate surrounding avian firehawk behaviour, citing both Dick Eussen's firefighting reports and the skepticism of Western researchers regarding accidental talon-carrying.

## Assessment
