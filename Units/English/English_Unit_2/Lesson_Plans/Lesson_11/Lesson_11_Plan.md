# Year 5 English — Unit 2: Informative Texts
## Sequence 2, Lesson 11 — Floods: The Precision of Vocabulary

**Time:** 45 minutes
**Core Text:** Floods Archive — All sub-pages (Vocabulary Hunt); Embedded Glossary/Tooltips
**Learning Intention:** I can explain how vocabulary is used to express greater precision of meaning, including specialist and technical terms. (AC9E5LA08)

---

### Differentiation Key

- **Year 5:** Core Year 5 pathway. Focus: Identifying complex technical terms (e.g., inundation, catchment, morphology) and explaining how they provide more precision than everyday synonyms.
- **Lucas (Y2):** Differentiated pathway. Focus: Identifying topic-specific words (e.g., flood, river, rainfall) and using them in simple sentences. (AC9E2LA09)

---

### Lesson Phases

#### 1. Activate (5 mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Brainstorm: "What words would you need to know to write about flooding?"
- **Activity:** Create a T-Chart on the board: **Everyday Words** vs. **Specialist/Technical Words**.
- **Differentiation (Lucas):** Participate in the brainstorm.

#### 2. Deep Read — Website Walkthrough (10 mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Display the **How Floods Work** page from the Floods Archive on the screen.
- **Deep Read:** Use the slides (Screenshots 1-5) to go through each section with the class:
  - Section 1: What is a Flood? (Defining terms)
  - Section 2: Riverine Flooding (Widespread, slow)
  - Section 3: Flash Flooding & Overland Flow (Rapid, dangerous)
  - Section 4: Storm Tides (Ocean impact)
  - Section 5: Dam-Related Flooding (Mitigation vs Prevention)
- **Interaction:** Pause on each slide to read key paragraphs together and point out the **technical vocabulary** in context.

#### 3. Explore (5 mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Introduce **Precision of Meaning**. Explain that authors choose specialist terms to be exact.
- **Explore:** Display the Floods Archive Hub Page. Look at the word **Catchment** (A City Built on a Floodplain section).
- **Discussion:** Why do authors choose "catchment" instead of "area near the river"? (It specifically refers to the whole drainage area).
- **Tool Check:** Model using the archive's **Glossary/Tooltip system** to find definitions.

#### 4. Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Together, identify 5 impactful specialist words from the Floods Archive.
- **Words to Explore:**
  1. **Inundation** (Hub Page / How Floods Work)
  2. **Catchment** (Hub Page / How Floods Work)
  3. **Runoff** (How Floods Work)
  4. **Riverine** (How Floods Work)
  5. **Overland Flow** (How Floods Work)
- **Analysis:** For each word, discuss: What is its origin? Why is it more precise? (e.g., "Riverine" tells us exactly where the flood is coming from—the river).

#### 4. Connect (20 mins)
- **Student Action:** Complete the **Lesson 11 Student Handout**.
  - **Task 1: Vocabulary Hunt.** Find the 5 core terms in the archive. Record their exact location and definition.
  - **Task 2: Precision Upgrade.** Rewrite 3 simple sentences using the technical terms to increase precision.
  - **Task 3: PEEL Paragraph.** Use the **Human Cost** sub-page to write a paragraph about a social impact of flooding.
- **Differentiation (Lucas):** Complete the **Lesson 11 Lucas Handout**.
  - Find 3 simple topic words about floods.
  - Complete the **Sharing a Feeling** task: How do people feel after a flood? (AC9E2LA09)

---

### Formative Assessment
- **Monitor:** Check vocabulary notebooks. Can students explain *why* "inundation" is a more precise choice than "flooding"?
- **Check-in with Lucas:** Ensure he can identify his three topic words in the text and use them correctly in his sentences.
