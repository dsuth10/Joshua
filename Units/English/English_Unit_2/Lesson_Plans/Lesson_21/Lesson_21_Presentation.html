Activate: Sourced Fact vs. Opinion

Read these two statements. Which one is a stronger research statement? Why?

Statement A (Bare Assertion) ⚠️

"Bushfires are getting worse and more dangerous every single year in Australia."

No evidence provided. Represents a general opinion.
Statement B (Sourced Fact) 🛡️

"According to climate records from the Bureau of Meteorology, the frequency of extreme fire weather days has increased by over 40% since 1950."

Backed by a credible scientific agency with precise data.

Remember: Anyone can make a bare assertion. Researched, sourced facts build authority and make your information report professional!

Explore: Evaluating Sources

Not all sources are equal. When conducting digital research, evaluate credibility:

✅ Authoritative (Credible)
  • •Emergency Services: Rural Fire Services (RFS, QFD, CFA)
  • •Science Organisations: CSIRO, University research divisions
  • •Official Investigations: Royal Commission disaster inquiry records
  • •Meteorology: Bureau of Meteorology official records
❌ Personal / Unverified
  • •Personal travel blogs or survival adventure entries
  • •Social media community threads or personal post entries
  • •Unreferenced internet forum boards or public discussions
  • •Subjective, highly emotional, opinion-heavy accounts

Socratic Question: Why do official reports and science journals carry more weight than individual social media posts during disaster planning?

Model: Attributing Sourced Facts

To strengthen academic writing, writers use specific attribution phrases and verbs. These signals tell the reader exactly where information originates, increasing the credibility of your arguments.

Phrase ””

"According to meteorological records published by the BoM, the frequency of extreme events is rising."

i Focuses on the source location or identity.
Verb 💡

"...a CSIRO forestry division study demonstrates that controlled burns reduce long-term risk."

⚡ Uses active language to show the source's action.
⤳

Integrating Differing Perspectives (Balanced Paragraph)

"Although winter controlled hazard reduction burns are essential for removing dry fuel loads as recommended by the Rural Fire Service, reports from Queensland Health indicate that these burns can trigger temporary respiratory challenges for local communities."
