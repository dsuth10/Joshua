# Lesson 21: Digital Research and Sourced Evidence

## Learning Intention
I can research and select information from authoritative sources to develop and expand ideas in my report. (AC9E5LA02, AC9E5LY06)

## Success Criteria
- I can distinguish between authoritative, credible sources and personal opinions or unverified assertions.
- I can select relevant factual evidence from a digital text (specifically the Bushfires Archive - Ash Wednesday & Black Saturday sub-pages).
- I can integrate sourced evidence into an information report body paragraph using precise attribution phrases (e.g., *"According to..."*, *"Evidence from... indicates"*).
- I can acknowledge a differing perspective or alternative viewpoint in my report based on authoritative evidence.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- **Scaffold Audit:** Ask students to open their natural disaster planning scaffolds. "Look at your planned facts. If a reader asks 'How do you know this is true?', can you tell them who or where that information came from?"
- **Assertion vs. Sourced Fact:** Display two contrasting claims on the class screen (Slide 3):
  > **Claim A:** *"Bushfires are getting worse and more dangerous every single year in Australia."*
  > **Claim B:** *"According to climate records from the Bureau of Meteorology, the frequency of extreme fire weather days has increased by over 40% since 1950."*
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - Which claim is more convincing? Why? (*Claim B, because it references an authoritative source—the Bureau of Meteorology—and provides a specific statistic rather than a general feeling.*)
  - What makes a source "authoritative"? (*Expertise, qualifications, scientific research, official government or emergency agency status.*)
  - Explain that professional information reports move beyond bare assertions by using credible evidence.

### Explore (15 mins)
- **Evaluating Source Credibility:**
  - Discuss what makes a source credible versus unreliable (Slide 4).
    - **Credible/Authoritative:** Emergency services (QFD, CFA), research organisations (CSIRO, universities), meteorological agencies (BoM), official government disaster reports.
    - **Unreliable/Opinion-based:** Social media posts, personal travel blogs, unsourced internet forums, emotional accounts.
- **Scanning and Digital Note-Taking:**
  - Demonstrate scanning the Bushfires Archive *Ash Wednesday* and *Black Saturday* sub-pages.
  - Explain that when researching, we do not copy entire pages. We look for **precise facts** (dates, statistics, impacts) and note down the **authoritative source** that provided them.

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Attribution & Perspectives):**
  - Model how to integrate a researched fact into a body paragraph using precise attribution verbs and phrases (Slide 5).
  - *Attribution Phrases:* *"According to official records...", "Research conducted by CSIRO demonstrates...", "Data published in the Black Saturday Royal Commission report indicates..."*
  - **Acknowledging Differing Perspectives:** 
    - Explain that complex informative writing often acknowledges alternative viewpoints.
    - Model writing a paragraph that balances two authoritative sides of a topic: the ecological benefits of controlled hazard reduction burns versus the short-term impact on local air quality.
  - *Modelled Sentence:* *"Although controlled hazard reduction burns are essential for clearing forest fuel loads as recommended by the Rural Fire Service, reports from Queensland Health indicate they can create temporary, hazardous smoke conditions for nearby communities."*

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Guided Research Practice:** Students open their planning scaffolds and use the Bushfires Archive (or their chosen disaster archive) to research and add at least **two** authoritative sources to back up their facts.
- **Interactive Slide Activity:** Standard students complete the **Interactive Source Matching Game** (Slide 6) to pair natural disaster facts with their appropriate credible source.
- **Lucas (Y2 Differentiated Pathway):** 
  - Lucas works with the teacher or classroom SSO.
  - Lucas reads a simplified reading card about "Firefighter Roles" (what firefighters do during and after a bushfire: fighting flames, rescuing wildlife, clearing roads, and teaching fire safety).
  - Lucas completes his differentiated worksheet (`Lesson_21_Lucas_Handout.docx`). He draws an illustration of a firefighter performing one of these roles, adds simple labels, and completes the sentence frame: *"I found out that firefighters..."* using his tailored word bank. (AC9E2LY05, AC9E2LA08)

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- **Topic Focus:** Firefighter Roles in bushfire emergencies.
- **Visual Scaffold:** Large drawing panel with dedicated label lines to connect visual meaning to text.
- **Differentiated Word Bank:** Word list categorised into *Action Words* (put out, help, rescue, clear), *Object Words* (water, hose, truck, animals, trees), and *Describing Words* (brave, fast, hot, safe).
- **Sentence Frame:** *"I found out that firefighters ____________________ to keep us ____________________."*

### Extension
- Students incorporate parenthetical citations (e.g., *"CSIRO, 2024"*) alongside their introductory attribution phrases to emulate professional scientific reports, and write paragraphs that contrast two different scientific viewpoints on forest management.
