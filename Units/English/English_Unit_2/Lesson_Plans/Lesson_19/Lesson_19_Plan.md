# Lesson 19: Writing Body Paragraphs using PEEL

## Learning Intention
I can write a body paragraph with a topic sentence, supporting details and topic-specific vocabulary. (AC9E5LY06, AC9E5LA08)

## Success Criteria
- I can identify the four components of the PEEL paragraph structure (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link).
- I can draft a cohesive body paragraph with a clear topic sentence.
- I can use precise, topic-specific vocabulary rather than everyday language.
- I can write a paragraph that flows smoothly using text connectives.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- Display a sample body paragraph from the **Bushfires Archive** (Fire Science sub-page):
  > *"Fires require three essential elements to ignite and sustain combustion: fuel, oxygen, and heat. Fuel, which consists of dry vegetation, leaves, and bark, accumulates during warm weather. When an ignition source like lightning occurs in extreme heat, a rapid chemical reaction begins. Consequently, this feedback loop releases vast thermal energy and spreads across the landscape."*
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - What is the main point of this paragraph? (*Fires require fuel, oxygen, and heat to ignite.*)
  - Which words are specialist or technical? (*combustion, ignite, chemical reaction, thermal energy, vegetation*).
  - Compare these with everyday words (e.g., *burning* vs *combustion*, *plants* vs *vegetation*). Why does the author choose specialist vocabulary? (*To achieve precision and write in a scientific, professional register.*)

### Explore (15 mins)
- Introduce the **PEEL** paragraph structure:
  - **P - Point (Topic Sentence):** State the main idea of the paragraph clearly.
  - **E - Evidence / Detail:** Provide facts, statistics, or examples from research.
  - **E - Explanation:** Explain how the evidence supports the main point.
  - **L - Link:** Wrap up the paragraph and connect it back to the overall topic or the next point.
- Show how the PEEL structure is highly effective in information reports because it organises factual ideas logically without opinion or bias.
- Explain the role of **Topic-Specific (Specialist) Vocabulary**:
  - Information reports require exact terms to build authority.
  - Avoid vague verbs (e.g., *"gets hot"*) and replace them with precise verbs (e.g., *"accumulates thermal energy"*, *"triggers combustion"*).

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Think Aloud):** Model drafting a PEEL body paragraph about **Tornadoes** (focusing on how they form).
- *Think Aloud:* *"First, I need my Point (P). What is a tornado's formation? It starts with warm, moist air meeting cold, dry air. Next, I need Evidence/Detail (E). Let's explain the rising air. Then, Explanation (E). Why does it spin? Convective updrafts make it rotate. Finally, Link (L). How do I connect this back to its severe nature?"*
- **Modelled Draft:**
  > *"**(P)** Tornadoes develop when warm, humid air collides with cold, dry air in a highly unstable atmosphere. **(E)** As the warm air rapidly rises, it creates strong convective updrafts that begin to rotate. **(E)** This rotation is further accelerated by wind shear, which turns the rising air into a violent spinning funnel. **(L)** Consequently, this fast-spinning vortex descends to the earth, creating a highly destructive windstorm."*
- **Unpack the Modelled Draft with the Class:**
  - Locate each PEEL component.
  - Highlight the specialist vocabulary: *collides*, *unstable atmosphere*, *convective updrafts*, *wind shear*, *vortex*.

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Independent Practice:** Students refer to their Lesson 17 plan and write their first body paragraph about their chosen natural disaster.
- **Constraints:**
  - Paragraph must follow the PEEL structure.
  - Must include at least one complex sentence.
  - Must include at least two instances of precise, specialist vocabulary.
- **Peer Share:** Students swap drafts and identify the **Point**, **Evidence**, **Explanation**, and **Link** in their partner's paragraph, checking off the checklist on their worksheet.

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- Lucas will write one short paragraph using a simplified PEEL sentence frame:
  > *"My topic is [topic]. One fact is [fact]. This shows that it can be [describing/safety word]."*
- Teacher or Support Officer supports his choice of Nature, Describing, and Safety words from his custom word bank (e.g., *storm*, *wet*, *heavy*, *safe*, *shelter*). (AC9E2LY06)

### Extension
- Students incorporate a complex sentence in BOTH the Evidence and Explanation parts, and use an advanced text connective (e.g., *In contrast*, *Subsequent to*, *Predominantly*) to transition into the next paragraph.

## Assessment
- **Formative:** Observation of student draft writing and peer PEEL-tagging.
- **Summative:** Lesson 19 Microsoft Forms Assessment (10 questions focusing on identifying PEEL components and distinguishing everyday vs. specialist vocabulary).
