# Lesson 20: Paragraph Cohesion and Text Connectives

## Learning Intention
I can use cohesive devices (sentence starting points, text connectives) to link ideas across paragraphs. (AC9E5LA04, AC9E5LY06)

## Success Criteria
- I can identify how varied sentence starting points (themes) give prominence to different information.
- I can select appropriate text connectives to show logical relationships between ideas (cause, contrast, sequence, concession).
- I can revise a paragraph to eliminate repetitive sentence starters.
- I can construct smooth transitions to connect paragraphs cohesively.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- **Repetitive Text Demonstration:** Display the following monotonous, repetitive paragraph on the class screen (Slide 3 of the presentation):
  > *"The bushfire spread rapidly across the dry ridge. The bushfire was pushed by strong north-westerly winds. The bushfire quickly jumped the narrow creek bed. The bushfire threatened several local properties by afternoon."*
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - What is the visual and textual effect of starting every single sentence with the exact same noun phrase (*"The bushfire..."*)? (*It feels repetitive, monotonous, and loses the reader's interest. It lacks flow.*)
  - How does this limit what information is given prominence? (*Every sentence focuses on the agent rather than the circumstances, wind forces, or geographical impacts.*)
  - How can we vary the starting points to change what is emphasised and make the text flow more dynamically?

### Explore (15 mins)
- **Varied Sentence Starting Points (Theme/Rheme):**
  - Explain that the starting point (the theme) of a sentence controls what the reader notices first. 
  - Circumstantial starters (e.g., *"Driven by howling north-westerly winds, the bushfire..."*) draw attention to the weather conditions rather than just the fire itself.
- **Introducing Text Connectives:**
  - Display the text connectives categorised by their logical relationships (Slide 4):
    - **Cause & Effect:** *Consequently*, *As a result*, *Therefore*, *For this reason*.
    - **Contrast & Comparison:** *In contrast*, *On the other hand*, *Alternatively*, *Conversely*.
    - **Authoritative & Reference:** *According to*, *Based on research*, *Evidence demonstrates*.
    - **Concession & Obstacle:** *Despite these conditions*, *Although*, *Nevertheless*.
- **Mentor Text Investigation:**
  - Examine excerpts from the **Bushfires Archive** (*Elemental Magic* and *The Frontline* sub-pages) to see how professional writers employ these exact cohesive devices. (Slide 5).

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Think Aloud):** Model taking the simple PEEL paragraph drafted in Lesson 19 and revising it to enhance cohesion.
- *Think Aloud:* *"In Lesson 19, we wrote a paragraph about fire formation. Looking at it now, three sentences start with 'Fire...' or 'Fuel...'. I want to make the transition into our next paragraph—which is about fire prevention—feel smooth. First, I will introduce a contrast connective like 'In contrast to uncontrolled blazes...' at the start of my paragraph to link it. Next, I will replace a basic starter with a circumstantial starter to emphasise the high winds."*
- **Modelled Revision:**
  - *Before:* *"Bushfires are dangerous. Bushfires spread quickly. Wind shear pushes the embers. Embers cause new spot fires. Firefighters try to contain the front."*
  - *After:* *"**(Circumstantial Starter)** Driven by extreme atmospheric conditions, bushfires spread with incredible speed. **(Text Connective)** Consequently, wind shear pushes hot embers kilometres ahead of the main front. **(Paragraph Transition)** Despite these severe containment obstacles, firefighting crews work tirelessly to establish critical containment lines before temperatures peak."*
- **Unpack the Modelled Revision:** Highlight the use of the circumstantial starter (*"Driven by extreme..."*), the cause/effect connective (*"Consequently"*), and the paragraph transition (*"Despite these severe..."*).

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Independent Practice:** Students open their writing books and review their Lesson 19 PEEL body paragraph draft.
- **Revision Tasks:**
  - Vary at least **two** sentence starting points (themes) to highlight different pieces of information.
  - Insert at least **one** precise text connective to show cause, contrast, or reference.
  - Write a cohesive transition sentence at the end of the paragraph to link into their next planned paragraph.
- **Interactive Slide Activity:** Standard students can also complete the **HTML Drag-and-Drop Sequencing Activity** (Slide 6) to practice arranging scrambled, connective-rich sentences into a perfectly cohesive paragraph.

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- **Evacuation Planning Scenario:** Lucas practices using the time-sequencing connectives **First**, **Then**, and **Finally** to organise a simple three-step bushfire safety plan.
- **Sentence Frames:**
  - *"**First**, we check our family bushfire plan."*
  - *"**Then**, we pack our emergency bags."*
  - *"**Finally**, we go to a safe shelter."*
- **Activity:** Lucas completes his custom differentiated worksheet (`Lesson_20_Lucas_Handout.docx`). He draws an illustration in the boxes for each step of the safety plan, and completes his sentences using his customized word bank (comprising Nature, Describing, and Safety words). (AC9E2LY06)

### Extension
- Students incorporate complex sentences that begin with dependent clauses (e.g., *"Although containment efforts were initiated immediately,..."*) to act as both circumstantial starters and paragraph transitions simultaneously.
