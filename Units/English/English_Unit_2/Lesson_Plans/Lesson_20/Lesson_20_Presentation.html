Lesson 20: Paragraph Cohesion & Connectives

Varying Sentence Starting Points and Structuring Logical Paragraph Flows

Learning Intention

I can use cohesive devices (sentence starting points, text connectives) to link ideas across paragraphs.

Success Criteria:

  • I can identify how varied sentence starting points (themes) give prominence to different information.
  • I can select appropriate text connectives to show logical relationships between ideas (cause, contrast, sequence, concession).
  • I can revise a paragraph to eliminate repetitive sentence starters.
  • I can construct smooth transitions to connect paragraphs cohesively.

I can sequence sentences using time connectives (First, Then, Finally) to make a simple plan.

My Success Checklist:

  • I can put my plan sentences in the correct order.
  • I can match time connectives with safety plan steps.
  • I can select nature, describing, and safety words from my word bank.

Activate: Paragraph Flow

Read this paragraph. What do you notice about how the sentences start?

"**The bushfire** spread rapidly across the dry ridge. **The bushfire** was pushed by strong north-westerly winds. **The bushfire** quickly jumped the narrow creek bed. **The bushfire** threatened several local properties by afternoon."

Analyse the effect:

  • ⚠️ Monotonous starting points: Beginning every sentence with the exact same noun phrase (*"The bushfire"*) makes the writing feel repetitive and boring.
  • ⚠️ Loss of prominence: It fails to highlight important atmospheric conditions (winds) or geographical facts (creek bed, ridge) by hiding them at the end of clauses.

Look at these safety steps. Are they in the correct order?

"Then, we pack our emergency bags."
"Finally, we go to a safe shelter."
"First, we check our family bushfire plan."

Let's learn how to order these steps perfectly!

Explore: Cohesive Devices

To create professional, high-cohesion reports, we use two main devices:

1. Varied Sentence Starting Points

Change the **Theme** (starting point) of sentences to give prominence to different information:

  • **Subject Start:** *"Strong winds pushed the blaze."*
  • **Circumstantial Start:** *"Pushed by howling winds, the blaze..."*
  • **Time Start:** *"By late afternoon, the blaze..."*
2. Text Connectives

Use linking words to show logical connections between sentences and paragraphs:

  • **Cause/Effect:** *Consequently*, *As a result*, *Therefore*.
  • **Contrast:** *In contrast*, *On the other hand*, *Conversely*.
  • **Authoritative:** *According to*, *Evidence demonstrates*.
  • **Concession:** *Despite this*, *Although*, *Nevertheless*.

We use Time Connectives to show the exact order of a plan:

First The very beginning step.
Then The middle step that happens next.
Finally The last step to finish the plan.

Model: Excerpts from Bushfire Archive

Observe how cohesive devices are used in two different sub-pages:

Excerpt A: Elemental Magic (Fire Science)

"Driven by extreme solar radiation, dry fuels accumulate vast thermal energy during peak summer months. Consequently, even a single lightning strike in these volatile grasslands can trigger instant combustion. Although firefighting crews respond immediately to these outbreaks, intense heat makes suppression extremely difficult."
  • 🔶 Driven by... = Circumstantial starter (prominence on solar radiation).
  • 🔷 Consequently = Cause & Effect connective linking to impact.
  • 🟢 Although = Concession connective introducing contrast.

Excerpt B: The Frontline (Response)

"According to emergency services records, establishing containment lines is the primary defence against an advancing firefront. In contrast to standard fuel management, active containment requires heavy machinery. Despite these suppression efforts, erratic wind shifts can cause spot fires to breach containment boundaries."
  • 🔷 According to... = Authoritative reference starter.
  • 🔶 In contrast to... = Contrast connective linking techniques.
  • 🟢 Despite these... = Concession connective acting as a transition.
