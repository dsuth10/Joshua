# Lesson 5: Comparing Perspectives — Cyclone Yasi

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts  
**Core Text:** Cyclone Yasi (Archive vs. Radio Broadcast)  
**Duration:** 60 Minutes

---

## 🎯 Learning Intention
I can compare how the same topic (Cyclone Yasi) is presented in different types of texts.

## ✅ Success Criteria
- I can identify the **purpose** and **audience** for a website and a radio broadcast.
- I can list at least three **differences in language** between the two texts.
- I can explain how **multimodal features** (images, sound) change how I understand the information.
- I can use **comparative language** (e.g., *whereas*, *in contrast*, *similarly*) to discuss the texts.

---

## 📐 Curriculum Mapping (AC v9)
- **Primary**: AC9E5LY03 (Identify and explain the characteristic stages and language features of different types of texts)
- **Secondary**: AC9E5LY01 (Identify how similar topics are presented in different types of texts)
- **Lucas (Y2)**: AC9E2LY01 (Identify how similar topics and information are presented in different types of texts)

## 🕒 Teaching & Learning Sequence

### 1. Activate (10 Mins)
- **Hook**: Play a 30-second wind sound effect. Ask: "If you heard this on the radio in the middle of the night, how would you feel? How is that different from reading a fact in a book?"
- **Discussion**: Show the header of the **Cyclone Yasi Website** and the title of the **Radio Transcript**. Brainstorm: What do we expect to be different about a website we read today versus a radio show from the night it happened?

### 2. Explore (15 Mins)
- **Jigsaw Reading**:
    - Group A reads the **Cyclone Yasi Website** (focus on "A Storm of Record Force" and "Fast Facts").
    - Group B reads the **Radio Transcript**.
- **Observation**: Students use a highlighter to find:
    - Factual numbers (dates, wind speeds).
    - Emotive words (scary, monster, screaming).
    - Who is speaking/writing.

### 3. Model (15 Mins)
- **Teacher Modelling**: Use the **Comparison Table** on the class screen. 
- Compare the **Opening Sentences**:
    - *Web*: "On the night of 1 February 2011, residents across Far North Queensland..." (Formal, Third-person).
    - *Radio*: "You’re listening to ABC North Queensland. It’s quarter past eight on Wednesday night." (Urgent, First-person).
- Model using a **Venn Diagram** to show what is the same (Topic: Yasi, Facts: Cat 5, Location: Mission Beach) and what is different (Register, Mode, Timing).

### 4. Connect (20 Mins)
- **Student Task**: Students complete the **Lesson 5 Handout**. 
    - Write 3–4 sentences comparing the two texts using the "Comparative Language Word Bank" (e.g., "The website is objective, **whereas** the radio broadcast is subjective").
- **Differentiated Task (Lucas)**: Lucas uses his **Venn Diagram Handout** to find one thing that is the same (The Cyclone) and one thing that is different (The Website has pictures, the Radio has sound/voices).

---

## 📋 Differentiation

### Support (Lucas - Year 2)
- Focus on the **visual vs. auditory** difference.
- Use the **Simplified Venn Diagram** with image cues.
- Teacher to scribe his oral comparisons: "The radio man sounds worried, but the website is just giving facts."

### Extension
- Challenge students to explain *why* the author chose different language features (e.g., "The radio uses second-person 'you' to create a sense of immediate danger for the listener").
