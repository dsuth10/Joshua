Lesson 24: Reviewing & Editing Drafts

Systematic Revision for Clarity, Cohesion, and Precise Language Features

Learning Intention

I can review and edit my draft for clarity, cohesion and language features. (AC9E5LY06)

Success Criteria:

  • I can use an editing checklist to systematically evaluate and refine my written work.
  • I can identify and replace general vocabulary with precise, topic-specific specialist terms.
  • I can vary sentence starting points (themes) to improve cohesion and prominence.
  • I can construct expanded noun groups and complex sentences to elevate structural complexity.
  • I can use precise text connectives to establish smooth logical transitions between paragraphs.

I can edit my sentences for capital letters, full stops, and correct topic words. (AC9E2LY06, AC9E2LA10)

My Success Checklist:

  • I can identify and fix lower-case letters at the start of sentences.
  • I can identify and add missing full stops at the end of sentences.
  • I can select and spell correct topic words from my word bank.

Activate: Writing is Rewriting

A first draft is just you telling yourself the story. **Editing is where you make it work for your reader.**

Draft A: Repetitive & Basic

"Some brown falcons fly near fires. The birds carry burning sticks. The birds fly across roads and wide rivers. The birds drop the sticks in dry grass to start new fires. The birds do this to hunt lizards running from the fire."

Draft B: Cohesive & Precise

"Flying high above active conflagrations, these highly intelligent raptors search for smouldering twigs. Specifically, they transport burning embers across containment boundaries. Consequently, by dropping these embers into unburnt fuel loads, they flush out lizards fleeing the smoke."

Look at the before and after. Notice how the sentence starters change and the vocabulary shifts to precise terms!

Read these sentences. Are they ready for publication? What is missing?

1) ⚠️ firehawks are very clever birds

2) ⚠️ they carry hot twigs in their sharp claws

3) ⚠️ dry grass burns quickly

Every complete sentence needs a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end! Let's find and patrol them.

Explore: The Editing Checklist

Use these four pillars of the editing checklist to audit your draft systematically:

1. Sentence Variety

Ensure a mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences. Use dependent clauses to add detail.

"Although fuel was wet, the extreme heat..."

2. Vocabulary Precision

Replace basic, general words with precise, topic-specific specialist vocabulary.

bird -> raptor | fire -> conflagration

3. Theme & Cohesion

Vary sentence starting points (themes). Use circumstantial starters to emphasize time or place.

"Driven by wind shear, hot embers flew..."

4. Paragraph transitions

Establish smooth paragraph links by deploying precise logical text connectives.

Consequently | In contrast | Specifically

Model: Upgrading Sentences

Let's watch how a teacher models upgrading a basic draft sentence step by step:

❌ Basic Draft:

"The birds carry hot sticks in their claws to start fires."

✅ Cohesive & Precise Upgrade:

"To capture fleeing prey, Expanded Noun Group these highly intelligent raptors carry Specialist Vocab smouldering twigs in their claws."

**What changed?** 1. We started with a dependent clause (*"To capture fleeing prey"*) to vary sentence starting point. 2. We swapped the general noun *"birds"* for an expanded noun group (*"these highly intelligent raptors"*). 3. We swapped general words *"hot sticks"* for precise specialist terms (*"smouldering twigs"*).

Let's see how we patrol and fix a sentence with lowercase errors and missing full stops:

❌ Bad Sentence:

"the firehawks carry twigs"

✅ Patrolled & Fixed Sentence:

"The firehawks carry twigs."

See? We changed the lower-case 't' to capital **T** and added a **full stop** at the end. Excellent patrolling!
