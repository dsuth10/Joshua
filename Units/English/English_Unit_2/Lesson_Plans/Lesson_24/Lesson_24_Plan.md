# Lesson 24: Reviewing and Editing Information Report Drafts

## Learning Intention
I can review and edit my draft for clarity, cohesion and language features. (AC9E5LY06)

## Success Criteria
- I can use an editing checklist to systematically evaluate and refine my written work.
- I can identify and replace general vocabulary with precise, topic-specific specialist terms.
- I can vary sentence starting points (themes) to improve cohesion and prominence.
- I can construct expanded noun groups and complex sentences to elevate structural complexity.
- I can use precise text connectives to establish smooth logical transitions between paragraphs.

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- **Introduction to Revision:** Display Slide 3: *"Writing is rewriting."* Discuss the quote: *"A first draft is just you telling yourself the story. Editing is where you make it work for your reader."*
- **Monotonous vs. Cohesive Draft Demonstration:** Display two short excerpts about Northern Firehawks on Slide 3:
  - **Draft A (Repetitive and Basic):**
    > *"Some brown falcons fly near fires. The birds carry burning sticks. The birds fly across roads and rivers. The birds drop the sticks in dry grass to start new fires. The birds do this to hunt lizards running from the fire."*
  - **Draft B (Cohesive and Precise):**
    > *"**(Circumstantial Starter)** Flying high above active bushfires, brown falcons search for smouldering twigs. **(Text Connective)** Specifically, these avian predators transport burning embers across containment boundaries. **(Paragraph Transition)** Consequently, by dropping these embers into unburnt fuel loads, they flush out lizards and insects fleeing the flames."*
- **Discuss & Analyse:**
  - What makes Draft B more engaging and informative than Draft A? (*Draft B varies its sentence starting points, uses precise specialist vocabulary like 'avian predators' and 'fuel loads', and links ideas using text connectives like 'Specifically' and 'Consequently'.*)
  - Highlight that editing is not just fixing spelling mistakes—it is about improving sentence structure, precision, and cohesion.

### Explore (10 mins)
- **Introducing the Editing Checklist:** Present the Year 5 Standard Editing Checklist (Slide 4 and inside `Lesson_24_Worksheet.docx`). Explain the four core pillars of the audit:
  1. **Sentence Variety:** Have I used a mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences?
  2. **Vocabulary Precision:** Have I replaced general words (e.g., *bird*, *stick*, *fire*, *eat*) with specialist terms (e.g., *avian predator*, *smouldering twig*, *conflagration*, *forage*)?
  3. **Theme & Cohesion:** Have I varied the starting points of my sentences to avoid monotony?
  4. **Paragraph Transitions:** Have I used text connectives (e.g., *Consequently*, *In contrast*, *Furthermore*) to link ideas smoothly?
- **Shared Editing Exercise:** Using Slide 4, guide the class in performing a quick visual audit of a short sample paragraph. Highlight where simple nouns can be expanded and where repetitive sentence starters can be combined into complex structures.

### Model (10 mins)
- **Teacher Modelling (Think Aloud):** Model taking a repetitive draft sentence and applying specific checklist improvements (Slide 5).
- *Think Aloud:* *"Let's look at this sentence: 'The birds carry hot sticks in their claws to start fires.' This sentence is simple and uses general vocabulary. I want to elevate it. First, I will replace the general noun 'birds' with an expanded noun group that adds precision: 'These highly intelligent raptors'. Second, I will swap the general adjective 'hot' and noun 'sticks' for specialist terms: 'smouldering twigs'. Third, I will make this a complex sentence by starting with a dependent clause to add a circumstantial starter: 'To capture fleeing prey, these highly intelligent raptors carry smouldering twigs in their claws.' Now, the sentence has visual texture and academic register."*
- **Write the Revised Sentence:** Display the before/after change on the screen, labelling the expanded noun group, the specialist vocabulary, and the dependent clause starter.

### Connect (15 mins)
- **Independent/Peer Editing:** Students open their writing books and retrieve their drafts completed during Week 5 and Week 6.
- **Revision Task:** Students work through their own drafts systematically using the editing checklist in `Lesson_24_Worksheet.docx`.
- **Minimum Requirements:** Every student must make at least:
  - **One** sentence structure improvement (e.g., creating a complex sentence or combining clauses).
  - **One** vocabulary choice upgrade (replacing a basic word with a term from their vocabulary notebook).
  - **One** paragraph transition connective addition (improving paragraph flow).
- **Interactive Slide Activity:** Standard students can also take turns or work in pairs at the front screen on the **HTML Drag-and-Drop "Draft Doctor" Activity** (Slide 6) to practice slotting precise connectives and specialist terms into a fragmented firehawk draft.

## Differentiation

### Support: Lucas (Y2 Pathway)
- **Focus:** Lucas practices editing simple sentences for basic mechanics (capitalisation, full stops) and topic-specific vocabulary (AC9E2LY06, AC9E2LA10).
- **Simplified Editing Checklist:**
  - *Does my sentence start with a capital letter?* (e.g., **F**irehawks...)
  - *Does my sentence end with a full stop?* (e.g., ...in the grass**.**)
  - *Did I use a correct topic word from my word bank?* (e.g., *twig*, *claws*, *smoke*)
- **Activity:** Lucas completes `Lesson_24_Lucas_Handout.docx`. He reviews three simple sentences, identifies and circles capitalisation/punctuation errors, and rewrites them correctly. He then matches three topic-specific words to complete a simple, sequenced description of firehawk behaviour.
- **Interactive Game:** On Slide 7, Lucas plays the **"Capital & Full Stop Patrol" game**, clicking cards with lower-case beginnings or missing punctuation to reveal the corrected punctuation in bold green.

### Extension
- Students incorporate advanced transitional phrases (e.g., *In direct contrast to typical avian behaviours...*, *Despite severe weather obstacles...*) to establish complex logical concessions and paragraph-level transitions.

## Assessment

### Formative
- Teacher observation during independent editing, monitoring students' capacity to identify repetitive starters and deploy specialist vocabulary in their drafts.
- Student performance and interaction logs during the "Draft Doctor" drag-and-drop slide task.

### Summative
- **Lesson 24 Microsoft Forms Assessment** (`Lesson_24_Assessment.docx`). A 10-question multiple-choice quiz administered at the end of the lesson (restricted to the Year 5 pathway). Questions focus on diagnosing run-on sentences, classifying connectives, converting subjective register into objective register, and identifying correct Australian spelling and punctuation.
