# Lesson 16: Differing Ideas and Authoritative Sources

## Learning Intention
I can use differing ideas and authoritative sources in a text, moving beyond bare assertions. (AC9E5LA02)

## Materials
- Lesson_16_Presentation.pptx
- Lesson_16_Worksheet.docx (Student Handout)
- Lesson_16_Lucas_Handout.docx (Differentiated Handout)
- Lesson_16_Assessment.docx
- Floods Archive (all sub-pages)

## Teaching & Learning Sequence

### Activate (10 mins)
- Review the difference between an assertion and a supported claim.
- **Display:** "Floods are dangerous." (Assertion) vs. "Floods claimed 35 lives in Queensland in 2011, according to the Queensland Government Flood Commission." (Supported Claim).
- **Discuss:** What makes the second sentence stronger and more reliable? How does providing a source change the reader's perception?

### Explore (15 mins)
- Introduce the concept of **authoritative sources**.
- **Discuss:** What makes a source credible? Discuss experience, qualifications, and publication status.
- **Examine:** Look at the Floods Archive and identify how the author references and attributes sources (e.g., quotes from experts, statistics from official commissions).
- **Activity:** Have students locate two different sources referenced within the Floods Archive and discuss why they are considered authoritative.

### Model (10 mins)
- **Joint Construction:** As a class, write a paragraph that includes a fact supported by a source.
- Model how to acknowledge a differing viewpoint or perspective within the same paragraph.
- Think aloud to demonstrate the decision-making process: *“I want to show that not everyone agrees, so I will use a phrase like ‘While some believe...’”*

### Connect (10 mins)
- **Independent Practice:** Students write two paragraphs on their worksheet.
  - Paragraph 1: A bare assertion about floods.
  - Paragraph 2: The same idea rewritten as a supported claim using an authoritative source and acknowledging a differing viewpoint.
- Peer review: Students share their rewritten claims with a partner.

## Differentiation
- **Lucas (Y2):** Practise the difference between an opinion ("I think floods are bad") and a fact ("Floods caused damage to 100 homes."). Complete the modified worksheet to write one opinion sentence and one fact sentence about floods. (AC9E2LY06)

## Assessment
- Formative: Observation during the joint construction.
- Formative: Review of the student worksheets (assertions vs. supported claims).
- Summative: Lesson 16 Microsoft Forms Assessment (10 questions on authoritative sources and supported claims).
