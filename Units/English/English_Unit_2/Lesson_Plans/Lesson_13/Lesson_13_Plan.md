# Lesson 13 — Reading Between the Frames: Image Sequencing

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts  
**Week:** 4 · **Lesson:** 1 of 4 · **Duration:** 60 minutes  
**Core Text:** Floods Archive — Brisbane History sub-page (timeline + image gallery)

---

## Learning Intention

> **I can explain how the sequence of images in a text has an effect on meaning.** *(AC9E5LA07)*

### Success Criteria

- I can identify the type of image sequence used (chronological, cause/effect, before/after).  
- I can describe what changes between images in a sequence.  
- I can write 3 sentences explaining how an image sequence builds the reader's understanding.

## Lesson Sequence

### Phase 1 — Activate (10 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Display three Brisbane flood images on the board in *scrambled* order (1974, 2022, 2011). Ask students:  
*"These three images all show flooding in Brisbane. Can you work out the correct chronological order? How do you know?"*

Allow think-pair-share (2 minutes). Take responses. Reveal the correct order: 1974 → 2011 → 2022.

**Discussion prompts:**
- "Why does the *order* of these images matter?"
- "What would change if we looked at them backwards?"
- "How does knowing the sequence help us understand what happened to Brisbane over time?"

**Key idea to establish:** The *order* in which we view images is a deliberate authorial choice. It shapes what we understand and how we feel.

---

### Phase 2 — Explore (15 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Using the presentation (Slide 5), introduce the concept of **image sequencing** and the types authors use:

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronological** | Images ordered by time | 1974 → 2011 → 2022 flood photos |
| **Cause and effect** | First image shows cause; subsequent images show effects | Storm clouds → rising water → flooded streets |
| **Before and after** | Two images comparing a scene pre- and post-event | House before flood / same house after |
| **Procedure / steps** | Images showing how to do something in sequence | Emergency sandbag construction |
| **Life cycle** | Images showing stages of a repeating process | Flood → recede → rebuild → flood again |

Navigate to the Floods Archive Brisbane History sub-page together. Draw students' attention to the timeline and photo gallery.

**Guided observation questions:**
- "What type of sequence is this?"
- "What does the first image tell us? What about the last?"
- "How does the gap between the images (time, place, damage) tell a story?"

---

### Phase 3 — Model (10 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Project a 3-image sequence from the Floods Archive. Think aloud through the following annotation process:

1. **Name** what is shown in each image (literal).
2. **Identify** what changes between images (critical).
3. **Explain** the effect on the reader's understanding (interpretive).

*"Image 1 shows the Brisbane CBD in normal conditions. Image 2 shows the same area partially inundated in 2011. Image 3 shows the same area under deeper water. The sequence moves from normal → disaster → extreme disaster. This builds a sense of escalation — the reader starts to understand that flood events are getting worse, not better."*

Write a modelled response on the board:

> *The image sequence moves from normal conditions to increasing inundation. The author chose this chronological order to show the reader how flooding escalates. By the third image, the severity of the impact is clear, creating a sense of urgency and scale.*

Point out: this is exactly what students will do in their 3 written sentences.

---

### Phase 4 — Connect (20 min)

**Task:** Students complete the Lesson 13 Handout (Image Sequencing Analysis Template).

- **Part 1** — Vocabulary matching (5 terms)
- **Part 2** — Graphic organiser: analyse a 3-image sequence from the Brisbane History sub-page
- **Part 3** — Written response: 3 sentences explaining how the sequence builds meaning

**Sentence starters provided on handout:**
- *"The image sequence shows..."*
- *"The author chose to arrange the images this way because..."*
- *"By the [second/third] image, the reader understands that..."*

Students work independently. Teacher circulates for conferencing.

---

### Phase 5 — Consolidation (5 min)

**Share and reflect:**  
Select 2–3 students to read their written response sentences aloud. Ask the class:
- "Does this explanation make sense?"
- "Has [student name] explained the *effect* on meaning, or just *described* the images?"

Exit task: **Microsoft Forms assessment** (10 questions, 5–7 min) administered via teacher iPad/device share or individual devices. Students complete independently.

## Differentiation

### Lucas (Y2) — Support Pathway

| Phase | Task |
|---|---|
| **Activate** | With teacher support, look at the same 3 images and answer: "Which one happened first? How do you know?" |
| **Explore** | Listen to class discussion. Lucas receives Lucas Worksheet with images *described* in words. |
| **Model** | Use teacher-modelled language as scaffold. |
| **Connect** | Complete Lucas Worksheet: draw arrows showing image order; answer "What happened first? What happened next?" using sentence starters. Draw a flood picture in the drawing box. |
| **Assessment** | Oral response to teacher — "Tell me what is happening in these flood pictures. What changed?" Scribed if needed. (AC9E2LA08, AC9E2LY05) |

### Extension

Students who finish early: Find a *second* image sequence on a different sub-page of the Floods Archive. Analyse whether it is the same type of sequence (chronological) or a different type. Write one additional sentence justifying their classification.
