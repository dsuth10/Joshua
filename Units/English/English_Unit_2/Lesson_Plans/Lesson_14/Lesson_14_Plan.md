# Lesson 14 — Reading Between the Frames: Visual Features & Effect

**Unit:** Year 5 English — Unit 2: Examining, Creating and Sharing Informative Texts  
**Week:** 4 · **Lesson:** 2 of 4 · **Duration:** 60 minutes  
**Core Text:** Floods Archive — The Human Cost sub-page (photographic evidence and visual layout)

---

## Learning Intention

> **I can explain how visual features contribute to the effect and meaning of an informative text.** *(AC9E5LA07, AC9E5LY03)*

### Success Criteria

- I can identify key visual features in an informative text (framing, placement, salience, layout).  
- I can describe what draws the reader's eye first and how foregrounding directs our attention.  
- I can write a short analysis explaining why an author chose a specific image and how its visual features create an effect on meaning.

## Lesson Sequence

### Phase 1 — Activate (10 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Project the core photograph from *The Human Cost* sub-page: the **Mud Army volunteers cleaning up flood mud** (`mud_army.png`). Ask students to look at the image closely for 30 seconds in complete silence.

**Guiding questions:**
- *"What do you notice immediately?"*
- *"What is foregrounded (placed closest to the front)?"*
- *"What draws your eye first, and where does your eye travel next?"*

Allow think-pair-share (2 minutes). Take select responses. 

**Key idea to establish:** Authors do not choose images purely for decoration. Every image is framed and placed to emphasize specific aspects of the topic and build a targeted response from the audience.

---

### Phase 2 — Explore (15 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Using the presentation slides (Slides 3–4), introduce the core terminology of **visual features**:

| Feature | Definition | Effect in Informative Texts |
|---|---|---|
| **Framing** | The distance and angle of the camera (close-up vs. wide shot) | Close-ups build emotional connection; wide shots show scale and severe context. |
| **Placement** | Where the image sits on the page (left/right, top/bottom) | Controls the reading path and reinforces subheadings or pull quotes. |
| **Salience** | The element that draws the eye first due to colour, size, or contrast | Directs immediate attention to the primary message or focal point. |
| **Layout** | The structural combination of images, borders, captions, and text blocks | Organises complex data cleanly to prevent cognitive overload. |

Navigate together to *The Human Cost* sub-page. Examine how images are placed alongside expert quotes, data callout panels, and primary text sections to multiply meaning.

---

### Phase 3 — Model (10 min)

**Teacher action:**  
Project the **Mud Army photograph** alongside its accompanying paragraph and caption from the website. Model a complete "Think Aloud" annotation connecting written text to visual choices:

1. **Identify** the dominant visual feature (salience/framing).
2. **Connect** the visual element to the written facts.
3. **Explain** the combined effect on the reader.

*"Looking at this photograph, salience is given to the volunteer covered in thick grey mud in the foreground. The mid-shot framing allows us to see both the human expression of exhaustion and the communal scale of the line of workers behind them. The author chose this image to support the text about social impact and community recovery. Rather than just stating statistics, the visual feature of foregrounding human effort creates an immediate sense of shared resilience and highlights the immense physical labor required after the waters recede."*

Write the modelled response structure clearly on the board:
> *The image shows volunteers working together to clear mud from destroyed properties. The author chose this image because it supports the text about community recovery. The visual feature of foregrounding the mud-covered volunteer creates an effect of shared exhaustion and powerful resilience, reinforcing the human focus of the text.*

---

### Phase 4 — Connect (20 min)

**Task:** Students complete the core handout (`Lesson_14_Worksheet.docx`):
- **Part 1**: Core vocabulary mastery (framing, placement, salience, layout).
- **Part 2**: Visual feature identification using graphic organiser boxes.
- **Part 3**: Short analytical response based on a secondary image choice (e.g., the Moreton Bay sediment plume or the Riverine flooding infographic).

**Sentence Starters Provided:**
- *"The image shows..."*
- *"The author chose this image because..."*
- *"The visual feature of [salience/framing/placement] creates an effect of..."*

Students work independently to compose their paragraph analysis. Teacher circulates to conduct targeted desk conferences.

---

### Phase 5 — Consolidation (5 min)

**Share and reflect:**  
Have 2 students read their written analysis aloud to the class. Use peer feedback to confirm understanding:
- *"Did they name a specific visual feature?"*
- *"Did they explain the effect on meaning, or simply describe the photo?"*

**Exit task:** Administer the **Microsoft Forms assessment** (10 questions, 5–7 min) via shared teacher device or 1-to-1 student devices. Ensure independent completion to log tracking metrics.

| **Assessment** | Verbal check-in with teacher/SSO. Provide verbal explanation of a simple image. Scribe responses if required. *(AC9E2LA08)* |

### Extension

Students completing the core activity early may select a third image from any other disaster archive (e.g., Cyclones or Bushfires). They must identify its primary visual feature and write a comparative sentence explaining how its layout differs from the Floods Archive choices to achieve a different impact.
