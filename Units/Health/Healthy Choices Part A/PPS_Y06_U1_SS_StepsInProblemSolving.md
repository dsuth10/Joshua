Steps in problem solving
Objective:

To explain steps in problem solving, an example is given based on a problem experienced by local councils on the Queensland coast.

To solve the problem, the steps are worked through sequentially.

Note: this slideshow is used in conjunction with the sheet Steps in problem solving

### Notes:
Work through the steps with students. Have students work through a problem in pairs - the problem could relate to school issues such as providing lunch time activities, safety on roads around schools or encouraging more students to be active.

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->

Situations
A family is walking along a Queensland beach which is not patrolled.  While wading through shallow water, a child in the family group steps on a stonefish. The child screams and collapses in pain.
Three people are snorkelling off rocks near an open beach. One of them suffers a shallow-water blackout and is found floating unconscious in the water by the others.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->

Step 1: Identify the problem
How to respond to emergency situations on open beaches as quickly as possible. (An emergency could be in the water or on the beach.)

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->

Step 2: Gather information
Who needs to respond to emergencies? Ambulance, lifeguards, fire brigade, SES, council employees
What information do they need to know?  Location of emergency, number of people involved, level of consciousness of casualties, injuries to casualties, treatment of casualties, ease of access: roads, tracks, vegetation.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

Step 3: Ideas for possible solutions
Teach people first aid
Put more lifesaving towers on beaches
Have patrols on open beaches
Have beach access signs with information
Stop people from accessing open beaches
Have special ambulances specifically for beach emergencies
Have water-based responders
Create more access roads to open beaches

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->

Step 4: Consider a solution: evaluate ideas
| Idea | Plus ? what makes this idea work. | Minus ? what could prevent this idea from working | Interesting ? possible outcomes, interesting aspects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Train people to perform first aid | People can respond quickly People willing to learn More people available to respond | Not enough expertise No confidence Not enough appropriate equipment Not enough trainers High cost | People may acquire skills they can use in different situations |
| Beach access signs with information | Easy way to give location of emergency Not costly Can give specific information about a location | People may not know about the sign and not pay attention to it People would need educating | Consistency with design of signs Can put other information such as environmental-protection information on sign |

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Step 5: Select the best idea
What idea is the better one?
Is it the one with more pluses or is it the one that is more interesting?

Make a decision –
 Beach access signs with information

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->

Step 6: Apply and review the idea
Prior to the installation: there were three different numbering systems. Comprehensive consultation with rescue and safety organisations took place before the signs were installed.
Standardised and sequential beach access signs: installed on Sunshine Coast beaches in 2011.
Reason: to make it easier for rescue crews to respond to emergency situations.
Future: Include QR (Quick Response) codes on the  beach access signs to give information in different languages about beach conditions.
