The 3 Non-Negotiables

Tap each card below to unpack the rules your game MUST follow to pass the assessment:

🤝 1. 100% Inclusion Tap to reveal rule
Everyone plays continuously. No one is left sitting out or benched. The game must be easily adapted for different mobility, fitness, and skill levels.

Session 1 • Worked Example

Worked Exemplar

Case Study: Noodle Ball

Respectfully adapted from Dabi (Mabuiag Island, Torres Strait) for 6 players.

The 5 Rules of Noodle Ball

1. Setup: 6 players stand within a netball court third with one pool noodle each. Number off 1 to 6.
2. Start: Player 1 starts with the soft ball and passes to any player using only the noodle.
3. Dribble Limit: A player can trap and dribble for max 5 seconds before passing.
4. Change Cue: At any time, Player 1 calls 'Change!'. All players drop noodles and swap sticks.
5. Continuous Play: Caller rotates ($P_1 \rightarrow P_2 \rightarrow P_3 \dots$). No outs, no scoring!

Session 1 • Health Analysis

The Health Rationale

The 4 Dimensions of Health

Click each dimension to see how your game provides comprehensive health benefits:

Physical Health

Cardiovascular fitness, motor skills & coordination

Continuous running and passing strengthens the heart, lungs, and active muscles while sharpening hand-eye coordination and agility.

Sentence Starter: "Playing this game improves physical health because continuous movement strengthens the cardiovascular system, while trapping/passing develops motor coordination."

Social Health

Communication, teamwork & 100% peer inclusion

Equal turn-taking and cooperative play require players to communicate clearly, encourage teammates, and celebrate shared success.

Sentence Starter: "This activity builds social health because players must communicate and share the ball equally so every peer is actively included in the team."

Emotional Health

Confidence, fun & freedom from fear of mistakes

Removing elimination and scoring creates a safe, low-pressure atmosphere where students feel confident to participate without anxiety.

Sentence Starter: "This supports emotional wellbeing because the non-competitive structure removes the fear of making mistakes or getting 'out', boosting self-confidence."

Environmental Health

Connecting to outdoor spaces & caring for grounds

Playing outdoors connects students to fresh air and natural elements, while fostering responsibility for checking and clearing the play area.

Sentence Starter: "This enhances environmental wellbeing because playing outside connects students to nature, and players take responsibility for keeping school grounds clean and safe."
Session 1 • Valuing Diversity

Booklet Page 5

Adapting for Diversity & Inclusion

How does your game welcome every student in our school?

♿ Mobility Adaptation

• Longer noodles for seated players.
• Widened passing lanes.
• Controlled zone defense rather than chasing.

🎯 Skill Differentiation

• Larger / softer ball options.
• Shorter passing distances.
• Dual roles (passer & tactical caller).

🌏 Cultural Inspiration

• Respectfully adapted from Traditional Games (e.g., Dabi, Edor, Keentan).
• Acknowledges cultural origins accurately.

Session 1 • Final Check

Pre-Testing Audit

The 2-Minute Pair Pitch

Pitch your game to a partner. Run the 3-Point Safety Audit before Session 2's Festival!

1. Collision Check

Are players running head-on into each other, or is spatial movement spread out?

Session 2 • Activity Festival

Live Playtesting

The Peer Activity Festival

Teach, Play, Referee & Refine

Today your game comes to life! You will lead a group of 6 peers, observe how your rules work in real time, and collect feedback for refinement.

1. Lead & Referee

Deliver a 60-second clear rule briefing and ensure safety at your station.

2. Observe & Test

Watch player movement: Is everyone included? Are rules smooth?

3. Collect Feedback

Peers score your game and give 'Two Stars and a Safety Wish'.

Session 2 • Referee Protocol

Leadership Guidelines

The 3 Golden Rules for Game Leaders

As the designer and referee, you are responsible for the fun and safety of your 6 players:

1. Clear 60s Briefing

Explain the goal, show the boundaries, and demonstrate one movement before starting.

2. Active Encouragement

Call out positive praise, guide player rotations, and keep energy high.

3. Safety First

Blow the whistle / call 'Freeze' immediately if players get too close to obstacles.
