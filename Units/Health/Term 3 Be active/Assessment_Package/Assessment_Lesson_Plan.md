# Let's All Be Active — Summative Assessment Lesson Plan (2-Session Sequence)

**Unit:** Let's All Be Active (Year 6 Health, C2C Unit 2)  
**Year level:** Year 6  
**Format:** 2 × ~60 min Health Lessons (Session 1: Design Studio; Session 2: Peer Activity Festival & Reflection)  
**Summative Task:** Inclusive 6-Player Physical Activity Design Portfolio & Live Playtest  
**Curriculum Alignment:** ACPPS054, ACPPS059, ACPPS060, ACPMP064  

---

## Unit & Assessment Overview

### Assessment Destination
Students demonstrate their understanding of health, wellbeing, diversity, and safety by designing a non-eliminating, 100% inclusive physical activity for a group of 6 Year 6 peers, analysing its impact across 4 dimensions of health (Physical, Social, Emotional, Environmental), playtesting it with peers in a structured festival, and executing a justified design refinement.

### Key Non-Negotiables for Student Game Designs
1. **100% Inclusion & Zero Elimination:** No player is ever "out" or waiting on the sidelines; active engagement is continuous for all 6 players.
2. **Safety by Design:** Strict spatial bounds, soft/minimal equipment (foam noodles, soft balls, cones), non-contact rules, and safe court/field surfaces.
3. **Cultural / Activity Inspiration:** Mechanically inspired by or respectfully adapted from Traditional Indigenous Games (e.g. *Dabi*, *Edor*, *Keentan*, *Wana Wana*), multicultural activities, or cooperative fitness games.

## Session 1: Inclusive Game Design & Health Dimension Analysis (60 min)

### Learning Intentions & Success Criteria
* **Learning Intention:** We are learning to design a safe, inclusive 6-player physical activity and analyse its health benefits across the 4 Dimensions of Health.
* **Success Criteria:**
  * I can apply the 3 Non-Negotiables (100% Inclusion, Zero Elimination, Safety) to invent a 6-player game.
  * I can write 5 clear, numbered step-by-step rules and draw an accurate spatial court diagram.
  * I can explain how my game provides specific benefits for Physical, Social, Emotional, and Environmental health using *because* links.
  * I can plan modifications for diverse mobility needs (e.g. wheelchair users) and skill ranges.

### Session 1 Teaching Sequence

| Time | Phase | Teacher Moves & Prompts | Evidence to Notice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0–10 min** | **Launch & Challenge Deconstruction** | Project Slide 2 (*The Design Challenge*). Review the non-negotiables. Remind students of cohort survey data gathered in Weeks 1–3 (students prefer outdoor, fun, non-eliminating activities). | Watch for students who default to knockout games (e.g., dodgeball). Prompt: *"How does your rule ensure everyone moves 100% of the time?"* |
| **10–25 min** | **Game Blueprint & Spatial Diagram** | Walk through the worked model: *Noodle Ball* (adapted from *Dabi*). Guide students to complete Booklet pp. 2–3 (Title, safe location, equipment list, court diagram, 5 numbered rules). | Check diagram accuracy: clear starting positions ($P_1$–$P_6$), equipment zones, boundaries, and movement direction arrows. |
| **25–45 min** | **4 Dimensions of Health & Diversity Matrix** | Project the 4 Dimensions matrix. Model detailed justifications explaining physiological, social, and emotional mechanisms. Students complete Booklet pp. 4–5. | Ensure students avoid vague phrases like *"it is fun"* and instead write *"The non-competitive format removes fear of mistakes, building emotional confidence."* |
| **45–60 min** | **Pair Safety Pitch & Peer Audit** | Pairs pitch their game mechanics to a partner. Partner uses the 3-point Safety Audit: (1) Collision risk low? (2) Equipment soft? (3) Continuous action? Students adjust rules. | Listen for immediate adjustments made to prevent bottlenecking or standing still. |

## Session 2: Peer Playtest Festival, Refinement & Submission (60 min)

### Learning Intentions & Success Criteria
* **Learning Intention:** We are learning to teach and referee our physical activity for peers, collect structured feedback, and execute a justified design refinement.
* **Success Criteria:**
  * I can clearly explain and referee my 6-player game outdoors.
  * I can maintain safety and 100% active participation during live playtesting.
  * I can give and receive constructive feedback using "Two Stars and a Safety Wish".
  * I can justify at least one design change made after playtesting and complete my GTMJ self-assessment.

### Session 2 Teaching Sequence

| Time | Phase | Teacher Moves & Prompts | Evidence to Notice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0–10 min** | **Safety Briefing & Station Setup** | Establish 4–6 outdoor game stations (netball court thirds, shaded grass zones). Hand out equipment packs (cones, noodles, soft balls). Set whistle cues. | Ensure all students have hats, appropriate footwear, and water bottles. |
| **10–35 min** | **The Peer Activity Festival (Live Playtesting)** | **Round 1 (12 min):** Group A designers teach & referee their game for 5 peers. Group B plays.<br>**Round 2 (12 min):** Group B designers teach & referee. Group A plays. | Observe student leadership: Are instructions clear? Do all 6 players participate actively? Is safety maintained? |
| **35–45 min** | **Peer Feedback & Iterative Redesign** | Return to classroom. Peer testers complete Booklet p. 6 (*Two Stars and a Safety Wish*). Designers document one explicit rule or spatial change made based on testing. | Check that designers use the formula: *"I changed [Rule/Setup] because [Peer observation]."* |
| **45–60 min** | **Final Reflection & GTMJ Self-Assessment** | Students write their school community wellbeing reflection and complete the A–E GTMJ self-assessment rubric. Collect completed booklets. | Check student self-ratings against their submitted evidence. |

## Teacher Worked Model: Noodle Ball (Adapted from Dabi)

* **Game Title:** Noodle Ball (Adapted from *Dabi*, Mabuiag Island, Torres Strait)
* **Safe Location:** Two-thirds of a netball court (open boundaries, padded goalposts, asphalt/court surface).
* **Equipment Needed:** 6 foam pool noodles, 1 small soft foam ball, 8 boundary cones.
* **Rules Summary:**
  1. Players stand within marked third with one noodle each. Players number off 1 to 6.
  2. Player 1 starts with the soft ball and passes to any player using only the noodle.
  3. Players can dribble for a maximum count of 5 seconds before passing.
  4. At any time, Player 1 calls *"Change!"* and all players drop their noodles and swap to an adjacent stick.
  5. The call rotates in sequence ($P_1 \rightarrow P_2 \rightarrow P_3 \dots$). No scoring or outs; continuous passing.
* **4 Dimensions of Health Rationale:**
  * **Physical:** Cardiorespiratory fitness from constant movement; hand-eye coordination from controlling and trapping the ball with pool noodles.
  * **Social:** Team cooperation and communication; all players must receive the ball to maintain the rotation.
  * **Emotional:** Low-pressure, non-competitive environment builds self-efficacy and eliminates anxiety of elimination.
  * **Environmental:** Conducted outdoors in fresh air; players clean up cones and check grounds before and after play.
* **Diversity & Accessibility Adaptations:**
  * Longer noodles allow seated or wheelchair players to reach the ground easily; boundaries expand or contract based on group mobility.

## Differentiation Matrix

* **Support Pathway:**
  * Pre-drawn court boundary templates.
  * Sentence starters for 4 Dimensions: *"This activity supports [Dimension] because [Rule/Action], which leads to [Health Benefit]."*
  * Bank of inclusive mechanics: passing loops, cooperative tag, synchronized relays.
* **Core Expectation:**
  * Complete all 7 booklet sections independently with clear diagrams and 5 numbered rules.
  * Comprehensive rationales across all 4 dimensions of health.
  * Active leadership during the outdoor festival with a justified post-testing redesign.
* **Extension Pathway:**
  * Multi-tier rule scaling (e.g. progressive spatial constraints, variable tempo, dual-role challenges).
  * Whole-school adaptation plan: how the activity could be scheduled for junior primary buddies at lunchtime.

## Guide to Making Judgments (GTMJ) Marking Rubric

| Grade | Knowledge & Understanding (ACPPS054, ACPPS059, ACPPS060) | Investigating & Applying (ACPMP064) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Comprehensive description of the significance of physical activity across all 4 health dimensions. Creates an exceptionally detailed, safe, and 100% inclusive physical activity matching all guidelines. | Insightful explanation of how the activity accounts for diversity, connects to the environment, and enhances community wellbeing. Executes a purposeful design refinement. |
| **B** | Detailed description of the significance of physical activity across multiple health dimensions. Creates a well-structured, safe, and inclusive physical activity that clearly matches guidelines. | Clear explanation of how the activity accounts for diversity and supports peer wellbeing. Describes a relevant design refinement. |
| **C** | Describes the significance of physical activity to health and wellbeing. Creates a safe physical activity matching key guidelines. | Describes how the activity accounts for student diversity and supports peer participation. Notes an adjustment made during testing. |
| **D** | Identifies basic benefits of physical activity. Creates a simple physical activity with some guidelines omitted or safety/inclusion gaps. | States basic connections between physical activity and wellbeing. Identifies a simple change without clear justification. |
| **E** | Lists fragmented facts about physical activity. Outlines an incomplete activity that does not meet safety or inclusion criteria. | Makes isolated statements about personal physical activity without linking to diversity or community wellbeing. |
