## Lesson concepts

#### Note: Teachers should be aware that students may have experienced some of the geological or extreme weather events learned about in this unit. Consider the needs of these students when discussing relevant events.

---

## Example learning sequence

Explore features of synoptic charts
- Identify the features of a synoptic weather chart.
- Understand that isobars are the lines on a synoptic chart and the different isobars represent different levels of pressure.
- Define high and low pressure systems.
- Identify cold and warm fronts.
- Identify these systems on a synoptic chart.
Interpret information on synoptic charts
- Use synoptic charts to predict the effect that high and low pressure systems have on wind speed and direction.
- Explore the movement of weather systems over Australia from west to east.
- Predict weather conditions based on synoptic charts.
- Explain how additional research could help create a more accurate prediction.

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Understand the relationship between high and low pressure systems and the weather experienced.
Understand that synoptic charts are representations of weather data.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Relate weather conditions to high and low pressure?
Interpret information on a synoptic chart?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Distinguish between high and low pressure systems.
Relate weather conditions to high and low pressure.
Interpret synoptic charts.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that weather conditions are simple to predict.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that the prediction of weather conditions is a complicated skill that requires knowledge of patterns and interpretation of data gathered from a wide range of sources.

---

## Ideas for differentiation

## Support

Ask students to work in small groups to construct synoptic charts.

## Extension

Locate synoptic charts from other sources and use these to predict the weather in the local area.
