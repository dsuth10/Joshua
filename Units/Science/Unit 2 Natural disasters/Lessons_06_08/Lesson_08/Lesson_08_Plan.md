# Lesson 8 — Modelling Cyclone Formation

> **Safety Note:** Cyclone in a bottle uses water and requires careful handling. Students must complete a risk assessment before beginning. Ensure towels are available for spills.

## Lesson Objectives

1. Explain why cyclones rotate in a clockwise direction in the southern hemisphere.
2. Predict the outcome of the cyclone in a bottle model.
3. Conduct the investigation, draw an annotated diagram of the vortex, and compare results with predictions.
4. Compare the features of the model with real cyclone formation.

## Evidence of Learning

- Explain why convection currents contribute to cyclone formation (with reference to Lesson 7)
- Relate the vortex in the bottle to the rotation of a real cyclone
- Identify at least 2 ways the model differs from a real cyclone

## Equipment Required

*(See `Sci_Y06_U3_SLR_MaterialsEquipmentList.docx`)*
- 2 × 2-litre plastic bottles
- Water, food colouring (optional)
- Tornado tube connector (or tape and a washer)
- Towels/tray for spills

### Phase 4: Why Do Cyclones Spin? (5 min)

**Slide 5: Rotation and the Coriolis Effect**

Simple explanation (no complex physics):

> "As air rushes inward toward the low-pressure centre, the Earth is rotating beneath it. This rotation deflects the air — pushing it to the left in the southern hemisphere. This is called the Coriolis effect. The result: cyclones always spin clockwise in the southern hemisphere, anti-clockwise in the northern hemisphere."

Show diagram on slide — two cyclones, one in each hemisphere, arrows showing rotation direction.

Ask: *"In your cyclone diagram from Lesson 7 — are your rotation arrows correct?"* Students check and correct if needed.

### Phase 6: Practical — Cyclone in a Bottle (20 min)

*(Based on `Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U3_SH_InvestCycloneBot.docx`)*

**Step 1 — Risk Assessment (Section C):**
Students identify 2 risks and control measures (spills, bottle instability).

**Step 2 — Method:**
1. Fill one bottle approximately ¾ full with water (add food colouring if available)
2. Connect the two bottles using the tornado tube connector
3. Invert the bottles so the full one is on top
4. Hold firmly and swirl the top bottle in a circular motion
5. Observe the vortex that forms as water drains

**Step 3 — Draw Annotated Diagram (Section D):**
Students draw and annotate the vortex:
- Label: vortex, direction of spin (arrows), water movement pattern, air rising through centre
- Add notes explaining what each feature represents in a real cyclone

**Step 4 — Compare Observations with Predictions (Section E):**
- Did the water spin the direction you predicted?
- Describe the shape of the vortex
- Was this consistent every time you repeated it?

### Phase 8: Model vs Reality Consolidation (4 min)

**Slide 9: Model vs Reality**

Students complete the "Model vs Reality" table (Section F):

| Feature | Our Model | Real Cyclone |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| Size | Bottle-sized | 500–1000 km wide |
| Heat source | Gravity (water falling) | Warm ocean (26.5°C+) |
| Rotation | Manual swirl | Coriolis effect over days |
| Duration | Seconds | Days to weeks |

Discussion: *"What does the model help us understand? What does it not show us?"*

Key vocabulary box (Section G): Students write own definitions for vortex, Coriolis effect, model.

## Differentiation

**Support** — Use `Lesson_08_Handout_Support.docx`:
- George/Mahina reading with multiple-choice questions and highlighted sentences
- Rotation diagram — circle the correct direction arrow
- Prediction scaffold: "I think the water will spin... because..."
- Risk assessment as a tick-box checklist
- Diagram template with pre-labelled features — students add direction arrows only
- Model vs Reality with 2 rows pre-filled

**Extension:**
- Research the Coriolis effect in more depth using a scientific source
- Compare Cyclone George's path data (`tc_george_bom_best_track.csv`) — plot coordinates on a map
- Investigate: Why did Cyclone Larry have zero fatalities despite being Category 5?

---

## Learning Alerts

- Students assuming the model perfectly represents the real cyclone — emphasise model limitations
- Students confused about rotation direction (clockwise = southern hemisphere — confirm with diagram)
- Students who have difficulty swirling — partner support, demonstrate technique first
