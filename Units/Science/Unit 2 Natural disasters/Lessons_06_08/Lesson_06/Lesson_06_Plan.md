# Lesson 6 — Introducing Tropical Cyclones

> **Wellbeing Note:** Be aware that some students may have personal experience with cyclones or other extreme weather events. Approach all discussions with sensitivity and check-in with students before showing footage.

---

## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Describe the conditions required for the formation of a tropical cyclone.
2. Distinguish between cyclones, hurricanes, typhoons, and tornadoes.
3. Identify cyclone categories and relate them to wind speed.
4. Describe the effects of tropical cyclones on Earth's surface and communities.

---

## Evidence of Learning

Can the student:

- Explain why cyclones only form over warm ocean water?
- Identify what makes a cyclone different from a tornado?
- Correctly classify a cyclone by category using wind speed?

---

## Learning Sequence (60 minutes)

### Phase 1: Hook (5 min)

**🎬 VIDEO — Cyclone Yasi Satellite Loop**
`Resources/Video/Sci_Y06_U3_BOM_CycloneYasiSatLoop.mp4`

- Play the BOM satellite loop of Tropical Cyclone Yasi with no prior context.
- Students watch, then respond to 3 rapid questions (show on slide):
  1. *What is happening in this image?*
  2. *Which direction is the storm spinning?*
  3. *What do you think causes this rotation?*
- Take 3–4 quick verbal responses. Accept all answers. Record key words on the board.

**Teacher Tip:** Students often think cyclones spin anti-clockwise (they've seen hurricanes in northern hemisphere news). Note both directions — come back to this.

---

### Phase 2: Video Block 1 — The Science of Cyclones (10 min)

**🎬 VIDEO — Tropical Cyclones**
`Resources/Video/Sci_Y06_U3_tropicalcyclones.mp4`

- Before playing: Students open **Lesson_06_Handout.docx** to the **Watch & Record table** (Section A).
- Task: *As you watch, record:*

  - The minimum sea surface temperature needed
  - What happens to warm, moist air
  - The wind speed that classifies a storm as a cyclone
  - One effect of cyclones on Earth's surface
- After video: Brief 2-min pair-share. Students confirm their answers with a partner.

**Key teaching point:** Sea surface temperature must be at or above **26.5°C**. Wind speed must exceed **62 km/h** to be classified as a cyclone.

---

### Phase 3: Terminology — Cyclones, Hurricanes, Typhoons, Tornadoes (5 min)

**📊 SLIDES — Slide 4: Cyclone vs Hurricane vs Typhoon vs Tornado**

- Teacher-led direct instruction. Display hemisphere map showing where each name is used.
- Students complete the **Terminology Comparison Table** in Section B of the handout.

| Term      | Where Used            | Hemisphere                    |
| --------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Cyclone   | Australia, South Asia | Southern & North Indian Ocean |
| Hurricane | Americas, Caribbean   | Northern                      |
| Typhoon   | Western Pacific, Asia | Northern                      |
| Tornado   | Over land             | Both                          |

**Learning Alert:** Students commonly think all four terms describe completely different weather events. Clarify: cyclone, hurricane, and typhoon are the **same type** of storm — different names by location. Tornadoes are fundamentally different (land-based, short-lived).

---

### Phase 4: Video Block 2 — BTN Overview (8 min)

**🎬 VIDEO — Cyclone: Behind The News**
`Resources/Video/Cyclones/Cyclone - Behind The News.mp4`

- Task: Students use Section C of the handout (**BTN Key Facts**) to record:

  - 3 facts about how cyclones affect communities
  - 1 question the video raised for them
- After video: Cold call 3–4 students to share one fact each. Build class list on the board.

---

### Phase 5: Real Story — Kerry Alcock Audio (5 min)

**🎧 AUDIO INTERACTIVE — Kerry Alcock describes his cyclone Larry experience**
`Resources/Interactives/Start_Kerry_Alcock_describes_his/index.html`

- Open the interactive on the class display. Play the full audio account.
- Discussion questions (verbal — no writing):
  - *How did Kerry feel during the cyclone?*
  - *What surprised you about his experience?*
  - *How does a personal account like this differ from a satellite image or news report?*

**Teacher Note:** This is a primary source — a real personal account from 2008. It offers emotional authenticity that satellite data cannot.

---

### Phase 6: Video Block 3 — Eye Spy (5 min)

**🎬 VIDEO — Eye Spy**
`Resources/Video/Sci_Y06_U3_eyespy.mp4`

- Focus question (on slide): *What is the job of emergency services during a cyclone?*
- Students write one sentence response in Section D of the handout.
- Briefly discuss cyclone categories displayed on slide (Category 1–5 wind speed table).

---

### Phase 7: Reading Task (10 min)

Students select **one** reading text:

- **Lesson_06_Reading_Yasi.docx** — Cyclone Yasi (2011, Category 5, Mission Beach QLD)
- **Lesson_06_Reading_Larry.docx** — Cyclone Larry (2006, Category 5, Innisfail QLD)

*(Teacher may assign based on reading level — Yasi is slightly shorter and more accessible.)*

Students complete Section E of the handout:

- 4 comprehension questions drawn from their chosen text
- Highlight: 1 cause, 1 effect, 1 statistic, 1 surprising fact

---

### Phase 8: Case Study — Cyclone Katryn Warning Message (7 min)

**📄 DOCUMENTS:**

- `Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U3_SH_TCSampleWarning.docx` — Sample BOM warning message
- `Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U3_SH_TCAdviceQuest.docx` — Analysis questions

Students read the sample BOM warning message for the fictitious Severe Tropical Cyclone Katryn and answer the analysis questions in Section F of the handout:

1. *What is the name of the severe tropical cyclone?*
2. *How fast is the severe tropical cyclone moving?*
3. *Where could very destructive winds be expected?*
4. *How do the people of Marrow Island need to prepare? Explain.*
5. *Where could you access more information to ensure the forecast is accurate?*

---

### Phase 9: Video Block 4 — Larry Wind Gusts (5 min)

**🎬 VIDEO — Cyclone Larry Wind Gusts**
`Resources/Video/Sci_Y06_U3_CycloneLArryWindGusts.mp4`

- Students watch footage of actual cyclone wind gusts.
- Task: *Look at the destruction. What category do you think this cyclone was? Use the category table to justify your answer.*
- Students write a 1-sentence justification in Section G.

---

### Phase 10: Wrap-Up — Vocabulary Consolidation (5 min)

**📊 SLIDES — Slide 10: Key Vocabulary**

- Display key vocabulary on the slide.
- Students complete the vocabulary box at the bottom of the handout: write a definition in their own words for:
  - cyclone
  - eye
  - storm surge
  - low pressure system
  - category

**Exit check:** "Thumbs up if you can explain to someone what a cyclone is and how it differs from a tornado."

## Differentiation

### Support

- Use **Lesson_06_Handout_Support.docx** which includes:
  - Pre-filled Watch & Record table with a word bank
  - Multiple-choice format for reading comprehension
  - Sentence starters for all short-answer sections
  - Visual comparison chart for terminology (pre-labelled)
- Seat support students near the front during video segments
- Pause videos at key moments to narrate key facts

### Extension

- Research Cyclone Tracy (1974) and compare with Cyclone Yasi:
  - Same category? Same wind speed? Different impacts — why?
- Investigate why Cyclone Larry (Cat 5, 240 km/h) had 0 fatalities — what does this tell us about preparation?
- Read the full Cyclone Archive website page for their chosen cyclone

---

## Ideas for Monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:

- Correctly categorise a cyclone by wind speed (can use the table independently)
- Distinguish cyclone from tornado without prompting
- Extract specific scientific information from video content (Watch & Record)

---

## Learning Alerts

Be aware of:

- Students confusing the **eye** (calm centre) with the most dangerous part of the cyclone
- Students thinking tornadoes and cyclones are the same (they form differently, behave differently)
- Students from cyclone-affected communities who may have personal experiences
