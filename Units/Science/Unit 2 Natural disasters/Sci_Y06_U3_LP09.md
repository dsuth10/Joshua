## Lesson concepts

## Example learning sequence

Review convection currents in gases
- Review movements in fluids caused by differences in temperature.
- Explain that movement in water and air can be related to differences in temperatures on the surface of Earth.
Explore the effects of temperature differences between the ocean and the land
- Explore (e.g. view a slideshow) the development of onshore and offshore breezes and winds from convection currents.
- Identify onshore and offshore breezes.
- Explain that movement in water and air is related to differences in temperatures on the surface of Earth (both land and sea).
Interpret a thermograph
- Identify methods for recording temperature differences at Earth's surface.
- Explain that a thermograph represents the different surface temperatures of the ocean in different areas as bands.
- Compare ocean temperatures at different locations in the sea.
- Interpret the thermograph and explain how the information provided could be useful.
- Discuss limitations of a thermograph.
- Explore how differences in temperature of Earth's surface can generate convection currents and, therefore, winds and weather patterns around the world.
- Discuss how this data allows more accurate weather forecasting or predictions.

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Understand the effects of convection currents in the atmosphere.
Understand how thermographs are used.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Explain how convection currents in the atmosphere cause winds?
Relate thermographs to ocean currents and weather?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Identify causes of winds.
Describe the relationship between thermographs and weather.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that the entire ocean is at the same temperature.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that ocean temperature varies with depth as well as at the surface due to ocean currents moving warm and cool water between the equator and the poles.
