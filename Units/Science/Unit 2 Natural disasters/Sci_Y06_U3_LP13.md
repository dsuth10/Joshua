Weather measurement --- Analysing evidence using the Beaufort wind scale

## Lesson concepts

#### Note: Teachers should be aware that students may have experienced some of the geological or extreme weather events learned about in this unit. Consider the needs of these students when discussing relevant events.

---

## Example learning sequence

Record barometer and anemometer readings to add to class data
- Record wind speed and air pressure measurements using constructed instruments.
- Identify whether the air pressure or wind speeds have changed since the last measurement.
- Explore whether these changes are reflected in the weather conditions.
Explore and discuss the Beaufort wind scale
- Explain that wind speed cannot always be measured directly, but can be determined by the effects of the wind.
- Identify and examine the use of the Beaufort scale to report wind speeds.
- Explore how only the maximum wind speed can be recorded after the event, but images taken during the event can indicate the wind speed at different stages of the event.
- Identify limitations of the Beaufort wind scale.
Examine photos for evidence to rate estimated wind speed according to the Beaufort wind scale
- Use a slideshow to view evidence from different weather events and complete an activity estimating wind speeds using the Beaufort wind scale.
- Compose or collect photos displaying evidence of the effects of the wind and share them on a digital space.

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Use the Beaufort wind scale to identify wind speed.
Understand that the Beaufort wind scale has limitations.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Draw conclusions based on evidence using the Beaufort wind scale?
Identify the limitations of the Beaufort wind scale?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Use evidence to determine wind speeds after the weather events have passed.
Critique the usefulness of the Beaufort wind scale.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that wind speeds can only be measured directly.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that wind speeds can be measured by comparing the effect that the wind has on Earth's surface, with an established scale.

---
