## Lesson concepts

#### Note: Teachers should be aware that students may have experienced some of the geological or extreme weather events learned about in this unit. Consider the needs of these students when discussing relevant events.

---

## Example learning sequence

Introduce cyclones
- Show, using a video, the movement and rotation of Tropical Cyclone Yasi.
- Understand how tropical cyclones move and rotate.
- Distinguish between cyclones (in the southern hemisphere) and hurricanes and typhoons (in the northern hemisphere).
- Describe the differences between cyclones (and hurricanes and typhoons) and tornadoes.
- View a video about tropical cyclones to explore the requirements for the formation of cyclones with respect to location and sea temperature.
- Identify cyclones as low pressure systems with a wind speed of greater than 62 km/h and relate cyclone categories to wind speed.
- Discuss personal accounts of the effects of tropical cyclones on Earth's surface and communities.
Identify the main features of a cyclone
- Recall the difference between a labelled and an annotated diagram.
- Label an annotated diagram of a tropical cyclone identifying the processes involved in the formation of the cyclone.
- Discuss how the model of the tropical cyclone differs from the real event.
Investigate convection currents in water
- Conduct an investigation that demonstrates the role of heat in the development of convection currents in water (and air).
- Predict the outcome of the investigation.
- Assess risk involved in the procedure.
- Follow a method to investigate movement of water due to heating.
- Draw an annotated diagram showing the convection currents in water.
- Discuss observations and investigation results.
- Discuss how the movement of water in this investigation explains convection current.
- Recognise that convection currents also form in air through the same process of warm air rising and cool air sinking.
- Define convection currents as vertical movements of hot and cold air or water.
Link convection currents to cyclone formation
- Compare the movement of water and air due to heat differences.
- Explain that convection currents in air contribute to the formation of cyclones as the cool air falls and warm air rises referring to the annotated drawing of a tropical cyclone.
Investigate how cyclones are formed using a model
- Explore the rotational direction of a tropical cyclone.
- Model the formation of a tropical cyclone.
- Predict the outcomes of the model.
- Identify potential safety risks associated with the activity.
- Use equipment to model the formation of a tropical cyclone.
- Draw an annotated diagram showing the vortex and direction the water spins in the model.
- Compare observations made while modelling with predictions.

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Understand the structure of a tropical cyclone.
Understand the role of heat and convection currents in cyclone formation.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Explain why convection currents form?
Describe how convection currents affect the formation of a cyclone?
Relate features of the model to the features of tropical cyclones?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Explain the role of heat in the formation of convection currents.
Relate the features and movement of a cyclone to a model.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that tropical cyclones, hurricanes, typhoons and tornadoes are the same things.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that hurricanes, typhoons and cyclones are different names for the same kind of weather system in different hemispheres.
Explain to students that tropical cyclones are much larger and last longer than tornadoes. Tornadoes typically form over land, while tropical cyclones form over water.
