## Lesson concepts

**Note:** Teachers should be aware that students may have experienced some of the geological or extreme weather events learned about in this unit. Consider the needs of these students when discussing relevant events.

---

## Example learning sequence

Define natural disasters
- Discuss ideas and knowledge of natural disasters.
- Define a natural disaster as a natural event that has an impact which causes loss of life and/or economic damage. (Loss of life includes humans and other living things).
- Identify a range of extreme natural events and determine if they qualify as natural disasters.
Introduce volcanoes and define dormant, active and extinct volcanoes
- Locate local volcanoes using internet satellite image searches.
- Define dormant, active and extinct volcanoes.
- Label the parts of a volcano:
- crater
- lava
- magma
- main vent
- vent
- volcanic ash.
Explore volcanic eruptions and their effects
- View videos showing active volcanoes in the process of erupting.
- Describe the effects that need to occur for a volcanic eruption to be classified as a natural disaster.
- Discuss volcanoes on the Australian mainland and the Australian Antarctic Territories.

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Define natural disasters.
Understand why volcanic eruptions may or may not be natural disasters.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Identify different kinds of natural disasters?
Identify where volcanic eruptions are natural disasters?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Identify different kinds of natural disasters including volcanic eruption.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that 'dormant' and 'extinct' are the same.
Students thinking that 'lava' and 'magma' are the same.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that extinct volcanoes are not likely to ever become active, whereas dormant volcanoes are inactive and may erupt again.
Explain to students that magma is molten rock beneath the surface and that lava is magma which has reached the surface.

---

## Support
