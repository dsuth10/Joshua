Weather measurement --- Comparing the measuring scales of weather events

## Lesson concepts

#### Note: Teachers should be aware that students may have experienced some of the geological or extreme weather events learned about in this unit. Consider the needs of these students when discussing relevant events.

---

## Example learning sequence

Identify instruments used to record and report weather conditions
- Discuss daily weather observations in the local area.
- View a picture of a weather instrument and predict what it may be used for.
- Identify other scientific instruments used to measure weather conditions.
- Show (e.g. view a slideshow) images of instruments that measure weather data.
- Predict and discuss what each instrument may be used for.
- Define the units of measure for wind speed (kilometres per hour --- km/h) and air pressure (hectopascals --- hPa).
- Explain how recording wind speed and air pressure can help predict weather events.
- Understand that scientists use the data collected by weather instruments to better understand the weather and predict future weather events.
Construct an anemometer and a barometer
- Construct weather measuring instruments.
- Record barometer and anemometer readings for class data.
Explore how an anemometer and barometer work in forecasting weather events
- Conduct activities that demonstrate how these instruments are used to gather data.
- Explain that many factors, not just air pressure and wind speed, contribute to the formation of weather patterns (e.g. humidity, surface temperature, altitude).

## Lesson objectives

Students will:
Understand that weather data can be collected using different instruments.
Understand that weather data is used to predict weather events.

---

## Evidence of learning

Can the student:
Explain how weather data can be collected?
Identify data used to make predictions?

---

## Ideas for monitoring

Monitor students' ability to:
Describe how weather data can be collected.
Relate data to predictions.

---

## Learning alerts

Be aware of:
Students thinking that weather conditions are simple to predict.

---

## Suggested next steps for learning

Explain to students that the prediction of weather conditions is a complicated skill that requires knowledge of patterns and interpretation of data gathered from a wide range of sources.

---

## Support
