# Lesson R2 — Seismic Engineers (Revision)

### Pedagogical Contemplation

1. **Cognitive Goal**: Students will analyze structural behaviors under seismic stress, compare traditional/modern building techniques, and evaluate how cultural narratives preserve empirical geological observations.
2. **Interactive Alignment**:
   - A **Two-Column Sort** will classify building elements (e.g., rigid bricks, flexible timber beams, diagonal braces, mud mortar) into "Rigid/Vulnerable" vs. "Flexible/Seismic Resistant" to reinforce engineering concepts.
   - A **Highlight/Hotspot** activity will have students identify empirical observations (e.g., shaking ground, rising sea levels, eruptions) hidden within traditional Indigenous accounts of natural hazards.
3. **Surfacing Student Thinking**: The rebuilding recommendation forces students to explain *why* timber frames and mud mortar prevent collapse, showing if they grasp the core physics concept (flexibility allows structures to dissipate energy, whereas rigid structures crack under stress).
4. **Pedagogical vs. Engagement Goal**: The engagement layer is the "Seismic Engineer Academy". The pedagogical layer is understanding cultural contributions to science (Science as a Human Endeavour) and seismic engineering physics.

---

## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. Explain how the Kashmiri Taq and Dhajji-Dewari building systems provide earthquake resistance.
2. Formulate engineering recommendations to adapt traditional flexible designs to modern building envelopes.
3. Describe how Indigenous oral histories contribute to modern scientific records of natural events.

## Evidence of Learning

Can the student:
- Identify the role of diagonal cross beams and lean mud mortar in earthquake-resistant walls?
- Compare how rigid brick walls and flexible timber-braced walls react to seismic waves?
- Explain how a specific Indigenous story matches geological evidence of a natural disaster?

## Learning Sequence (60 minutes)

### Phase 1: Hook — Earthquakes in Australia (5 min)

- Introduce the session:trainee **Seismic Engineers** at the Disaster Response Academy.
- Present a scenario: A coastal town in New South Wales has suffered damage from a moderate earthquake. Modern brick-and-mortar homes cracked and collapsed, while simple wooden sheds survived.
- Ask:
  1. *Why did the rigid, strong brick houses fail while the weaker, flexible structures survived?*
  2. *How can we design buildings to move with the earth instead of resisting it?*

---

### Phase 2: Kashmir Traditional Systems (15 min)

**📄 READ & DISCUSS — Traditional Kashmiri Earthquake Architecture**
- Introduce the Taq and Dhajji-Dewari systems of northern India.
  - **Taq system**: Large horizontal wood beams embedded in brick walls at floor and window levels. The entire structure ties together, moving as one unit and preventing wall cracks.
  - **Dhajji-Dewari system**: A timber frame dividing brick walls into small panels with diagonal cross beams and lean mud mortar. The wooden frames absorb vibrations, and mud mortar allows tiny movements without collapse.
- **💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Structural Sort**
  - Sort structural elements into "Fails under Seismic Stress" and "Survives Seismic Stress".
  - Students complete **Section A (Traditional Architecture)** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 3: Application to Australian Rebuilding (15 min)

- Imagine we are writing a report to the town council of the damaged NSW town.
- Students write a recommendation explaining how the council can use wood-frame construction, diagonal braces, or horizontal supports to rebuild safer, earthquake-resistant houses.
- Encourage students to use scientific vocabulary: *seismic waves*, *flexibility*, *structural integrity*, *stress distribution*.
- Students complete **Section B (Engineering Report)** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 4: Indigenous Hazard Stories (15 min)

- Discuss how Indigenous cultures documented natural disasters long before modern seismographs.
- Read an account (e.g., volcanic eruptions at Mount Gambier or earthquake stories of the Pacific/Australian coast).
- **💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Story Highlighting**
  - Highlight sentences in the story that describe actual geological observations (e.g., ground rising, red hot rocks, sea receding).
  - Discuss how these stories contribute to scientific understanding (they preserve long-term data over thousands of years).
  - Students complete **Section C (Indigenous Stories)** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 5: Consolidation & Exit Quiz (10 min)

- Wrap up the session: Traditional engineering emphasizes flexibility over rigidity.
- Students complete the 5-question multiple-choice review in **Section D (Exit Quiz)** of their handout.
- Teacher reviews answers using the interactive presentation slide.

---

## Differentiation

### Support
- Provide labeled diagrams of Taq and Dhajji-Dewari walls showing where wood is placed.
- Provide a sentence starter list for the engineering report.
- Simplify the Indigenous stories text.

### Extension
- Research the Japanese "Shinbashira" (pagoda central pillar) and compare its seismic damping effect to the Kashmiri Taq system.
- Write a paragraph explaining the molecular difference between rigid concrete mortar and flexible mud mortar.
