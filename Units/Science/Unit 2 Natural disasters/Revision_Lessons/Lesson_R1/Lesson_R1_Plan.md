# Lesson R1 — Cyclone Trackers (Revision)

### Pedagogical Contemplation

1. **Cognitive Goal**: Students will interpret graph data, extract cyclone categories, evaluate prediction accuracy, and justify research methods using scientific reasoning.
2. **Interactive Alignment**: 
   - A **Matching** interaction will review weather instruments and wind scale levels to ensure basic recall.
   - A **Cloze** interaction will force students to translate graph wind speeds into cyclone categories and damage levels.
3. **Surfacing Student Thinking**: The prediction critique requires students to write a short-answer justification showing whether they understand that predictions are probabilities requiring multiple data sources (radar, pressure, temperature) rather than single data points.
4. **Pedagogical vs. Engagement Goal**: The engagement goal is roleplaying as "Cyclone Trackers" at the Disaster Response Academy. The pedagogical goal is to assess and refine students' graph literacy and scientific justification skills under the Australian Curriculum Inquiry strand.

---

## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. Interpret a cyclone wind speed graph to identify categories and predict damage.
2. Identify limitations of the Beaufort wind scale.
3. Critique a cyclone path prediction using a tracking map and justify improvements using meteorological instruments.

---

## Evidence of Learning

Can the student:
- Retrieve a cyclone category from a wind speed table based on a graph coordinate?
- Justify why a cyclone prediction is accurate or inaccurate?
- List at least three meteorological data sources to improve forecast accuracy?

## Learning Sequence (60 minutes)

### Phase 1: Hook — Disaster Response Academy (5 min)

- Welcome students to the **Disaster Response Academy**. Today, they are trainee **Cyclone Trackers**.
- Introduce the scenario: A severe tropical cyclone, **Tropical Cyclone Neo**, is forming off the Queensland coast. Emergency services need our tracking data to coordinate evacuations.
- Display the slide showing the satellite tracker. Ask:
  1. *What information do we need to determine how dangerous TC Neo is?*
  2. *How do emergency services categorise these storms?*

---

### Phase 2: Weather Instruments & Scale Match (15 min)

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Weather Instrument Matching**
- Students match weather instruments and scales (anemometer, barometer, Beaufort scale, synoptic chart) to their scientific purposes.
- Class runs through the matching widget on the smartboard.
- **CFU (Whiteboard Option)**: Write down the unit used to measure wind speed in Australia (km/h) and show it.
- Students complete **Section A (Instrument Match)** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 3: Wind Graph Analysis — TC Neo (15 min)

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Wind Graph Cloze**
- Display the wind speed graph of Tropical Cyclone Neo recorded over 12 days.
- Show the standard Australian Cyclone Category Table (Category 1 to 5).
- Students complete the Cloze activity on the slide, filling in blanks regarding TC Neo's category when it crossed the coast on Day 8 (wind speed: 175 km/h, Category 4) and the corresponding damage (e.g., roof failures, structural damage).
- **Teacher Tip**: Remind students to read both average wind speeds and maximum gusts to make accurate category decisions.
- Students complete **Section B (Graph Analysis)** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 4: Prediction & Mapping (15 min)

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Cyclone Track Critique**
- Display a synoptic map showing the path of TC Neo over the Coral Sea.
- Read the "Friend's Prediction": *"TC Neo will cross the coast near Townsville at exactly 10:00 am on Wednesday."*
- Ask students: *Is this prediction reliable? Why or why not?*
- Lead a class discussion on the limits of single-coordinate predictions. Introduce additional research methods: satellite radar, ocean pressure sensors, barometric pressure trends.
- Students write their critique and list three improvement methods in **Section C (Prediction Critique)** of the handout.

---

### Phase 5: Consolidation & Exit Quiz (10 min)

- Summarise key concepts: Beaufort wind scale limitations (it is subjective and based on visual observations of land/sea, not direct measurements).
- Students complete the 5-question multiple-choice review in **Section D (Exit Quiz)** of their handout.
- Teacher uses the "Show Answer" button on the interactive slide to review.

---

## Differentiation

### Support
- Provide a simplified wind speed graph with clear horizontal category gridlines.
- Include a word bank for the prediction critique (e.g., *probability*, *path*, *radar*, *uncertainty*).
- Pre-fill parts of the Beaufort scale comparison.

### Extension
- Ask students to explain why extending the Beaufort wind scale (introducing higher categories) is necessary for modern climate records.
- Challenge students to calculate the rate of wind speed increase between Day 3 and Day 7.
