# Lesson R3 — Geologists (Revision)

### Pedagogical Contemplation

1. **Cognitive Goal**: Students will identify and explain rapid changes to Earth's surface using geologic evidence, formulate predictions of shear deformation on man-made structures, and compile scientific explanations with precise domain vocabulary.
2. **Interactive Alignment**:
   - A **Sequencing/Rank Order** widget will order the progression of a landslide dam formation (shaking → landslide → river blocked → lake formed).
   - A **Highlight/Hotspot** widget will have students tag specific evidence of lateral vs. vertical displacements in aerial photographs.
3. **Surfacing Student Thinking**: The train track and parallel fence prediction surfaces whether students understand that fault movements are relative offsets (the line cuts clean across both and shifts them by the exact same amount in opposite directions).
4. **Pedagogical vs. Engagement Goal**: The engagement layer is the "Forensic Geology Team". The pedagogical layer is the spatial-geological reasoning and structured scientific explanation of tectonic plate movements.

---

## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. Explain how earthquakes cause rapid changes to Earth's surface (faulting, landslides, scarps).
2. Predict and sketch the effects of lateral strike-slip faulting on continuous linear structures (train tracks, fences).
3. Identify and justify non-visual geological evidence that confirms earthquake occurrences (seismograph records, ground fractures, liquefaction).

---

## Evidence of Learning

Can the student:
- Distinguish between lateral (horizontal) and vertical displacement on a fault line?
- Predict that a parallel fence will warp or break with the same relative offset as adjacent tracks?
- Formulate a written explanation using *tectonic plates*, *fault line*, *shear stress*, and *displacement*?

## Learning Sequence (60 minutes)

### Phase 1: Hook — Forensic Geology Team (5 min)

- Welcome students: they are **Forensic Geologists** at the Disaster Response Academy.
- Present a mystery: A series of before-and-after aerial photographs shows that a road has split, a fence line is offset by 2 metres, and a new lake has suddenly appeared in a valley.
- Ask:
  1. *What natural force could make these changes happen in less than a minute?*
  2. *How can we prove that tectonic movements caused these specific surface features?*

---

### Phase 2: Analyzing Earth's Surface Changes & Fault Mechanics (20 min)

**📄 READ & EXPLAIN — Three Cases of Rapid Change**
- Detail three geological case studies:
  - **Case A: The Displaced Fence** (Horizontal strike-slip fault offset where land on either side of a fault line slides past each other horizontally).
  - **Case B: The Valley Landslide** (Severe shaking triggers a massive landslide, which blocks a river channel and creates a "landslide dam" and new lake upstream).
  - **Case C: The Road Rupture** (A vertical dip-slip fault scarp where land on one side drops or rises relative to the other side).
- **💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Landslide Sequence**
  - Sequence the steps of a landslide dam event.
  - Students complete **Section A: Identifying Landform Changes** on their handouts.

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDES — Mechanics of Tectonic Faulting**
- Guide students through the animated block diagrams illustrating the three ways fault lines move:
  - **Slide 3: Normal Faults**: Horizontal **tensional stress** (pulling apart) at divergent boundaries causes the hanging wall to slip **downward** relative to the footwall, creating scarps/valleys.
  - **Slide 4: Reverse Faults**: Horizontal **compressional stress** (pushing together) at convergent boundaries pushes the hanging wall **upward** and over the footwall, forming folded mountain belts.
  - **Slide 5: Strike-Slip Faults**: Horizontal **shear stress** (sliding past) at transform boundaries slides crust blocks horizontally past each other, offseting linear surface features.
- Students complete **Section B: Mechanics of Fault Line Movements** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 3: Track & Fence Deformation Prediction (12 min)

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Fault Line Shift Prediction (Slide 6)**
- Display a diagram of straight train tracks crossed perpendicularly by a fault line.
- Show a parallel fence running beside the tracks.
- Ask students to predict what the track and fence will look like after a horizontal (lateral) earthquake shift.
- Discuss **relative displacement**: both the tracks and the fence must shift by the same distance and direction because the entire block of land moves.
- Students sketch the shifted tracks and fence in **Section C: Track & Fence Deformation Prediction** of the handout.

---

### Phase 4: Geological Evidence & Scientific Writing (18 min)

**💻 INTERACTIVE SLIDE — Identifying Earth Evidence**
- What other sources of evidence would suggest these changes were caused by an earthquake?
- Seismograph readings, ground crack maps, soil liquefaction signs, eyewitness logs.
- Students draft a formal scientific explanation using the vocabulary bank: *tectonic plates*, *fault line*, *shear stress*, *lateral displacement*.
- Students complete **Section D: Scientific Justification of Seismic Evidence** on their handouts.

---

### Phase 5: Consolidation & Exit Quiz (10 min)

- Wrap up the session: Earthquakes create rapid, measurable visual changes on both natural landforms and man-made structures.
- Students complete the 8-question multiple-choice review (from `Lesson_R3_Assessment.docx` or interactive Slide 7) to check their understanding.
- Teacher reviews answers using the interactive presentation slide.

---

## Differentiation

### Support
- Provide dotted lines in the sketch box showing the direction of relative fault movement.
- Provide a structured cloze-style template for the scientific explanation.
- Pair students to share observations of vertical vs. horizontal displacement.

### Extension
- Research how scientists calculate the slip rate of a fault line using historical displacements.
- Explain how soil liquefaction happens at a microscopic level during shaking.
