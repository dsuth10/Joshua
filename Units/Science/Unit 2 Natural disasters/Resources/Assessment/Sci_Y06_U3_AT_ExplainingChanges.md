| **Part B: Coping with natural disasters** |
| --- |

- During the course of exploring natural events you have discovered how people from across the world have developed methods to cope with different natural events.

The following passage explains a building technique used in an earthquake-prone area in northern India:

| The Kashmir region in northern India experiences frequent earthquakes. The people who live in this area have developed two unique construction practices for making their houses safer during an earthquake. These techniques, known as the Taq and Dhajji-Dewari systems, have been shown to provide earthquake-resistant qualities to buildings. In the Taq system, large pieces of wood are used as horizontal supports embedded into the walls. These supports are located at floor level and at the top of windows. They tie together all of the elements of the building, allowing the entire structure to move during the earthquake. This prevents spreading and cracking of the brick walls. In the Dhajji-Dewari system, timber frames for separating the brick and mortar walls into small parcels are used. The timber frames not only have vertical and horizontal supports, but also have diagonal cross beams, which divide the walls into various small panels. The most important characteristic of this type of construction is the use of lean mud mortar. A common practice in the region is to use the Dhajji-Dewari system in the upper storey walls, especially below peaked roofs. |
| --- |

Reference: *Indigenous knowledge for disaster risk reduction: good practices and lessons learned from experiences in the Asia- Pacific region (UNISDR)* by Myers, K & Watson, P, pp 5-8. © 2008 United Nations. http://www.unisdr.org/files/3646_IndigenousKnowledgeDRR.pdf
