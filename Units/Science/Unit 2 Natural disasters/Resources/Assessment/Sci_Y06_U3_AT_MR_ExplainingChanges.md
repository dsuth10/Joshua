- What is a limitation of the Beaufort wind scale?

The Beaufort wind scale was originally determined for seagoing vessels. Wind strength is determined by looking at the environment, either the sea or land, to estimate what the wind strength would be and therefore doesn’t provide an accurate reading and or the crucial elements of the wind condition, such as direction, that other instruments may provide.

Winds may be influenced by the local environment and this could greatly affect the estimated compared to the actual wind speeds in a given area.

The scale finishes at 12 (118km/h) and a storm may have much stronger winds and be a lot more damaging and the scale does not provide accurate data at this end. This means that cyclones must have their own categories for classification.

- Why may extending the Beaufort wind scale be useful?

As winds can be a lot stronger than 118 km/h, extending the scale would provide more differentiation between wind strength so more accurate forecasting could be provided.

- During the course of exploring natural events you have discovered how people from across the world have developed methods to cope with different natural events.

The following passage explains a building technique used in an earthquake-prone area in northern India:

| The Kashmir region in northern India experiences frequent earthquakes. The people who live in this area have developed two unique construction practices for making their houses safer during an earthquake. These techniques, known as the Taq and Dhajji-Dewari systems, have been shown to provide earthquake-resistant qualities to buildings. In the Taq system, large pieces of wood are used as horizontal supports embedded into the walls. These supports are located at floor level and at the top of windows. They tie together all of the elements of the building, allowing the entire structure to move during the earthquake. This prevents spreading and cracking of the brick walls. In the Dhajji-Dewari system, timber frames for separating the brick and mortar walls into small parcels are used. The timber frames not only have vertical and horizontal supports, but also have diagonal cross beams, which divide the walls into various small panels. The most important characteristic of this type of construction is the use of lean mud mortar. A common practice in the region is to use the Dhajji-Dewari system in the upper storey walls, especially below peaked roofs. |
| --- |

Reference: *Indigenous knowledge for disaster risk reduction: good practices and lessons learned from experiences in the Asia- Pacific region (UNISDR)* by Myers, K & Watson, P, pp 5-8. © 2008 United Nations. http://www.unisdr.org/files/3646_IndigenousKnowledgeDRR.pdf

A town on the central coast of New South Wales has recently suffered an earthquake and experienced a large amount of damage to houses that were constructed from modern-style bricks and mortar.

- Describe how the residents of the New South Wales town could use the knowledge of the Taq or Dhajji-Dewari building techniques to rebuild their houses with more earthquake-resistant designs.

Student answers should indicate that the Taq and Dhajji-Dewari building techniques would improve the modern designs. Answers should indicate that these techniques, which allow the people of northern India to suffer minimal damage during an earthquake, can be used (modified or not) to improve our own building designs.

For example: Most Australian houses are made of brick and by using the Taq system it would allow the entire building to move with the earthquake and reduce the spreading and cracking of brick walls.

The Dhajji-Dewari system could also be used on two-storey brick homes where the upper storey walls would be built in small panels to reduce the damage to the building in an earthquake.

- Explain how indigenous stories contribute to the scientific understanding of natural disasters.

Indigenous stories, including those of Aboriginal peoples and Torres Strait Islander peoples as well as the native inhabitants of Asia and the Pacific, demonstrate to scientists that geological and weather events have been occurring with some regularity in a region as the stories were recorded a long time ago.

They also give indications of the magnitude of the events in the description. Some stories also provide warnings and information about preparing for the disaster and how to avoid loss of life. These stories can be shared as a valuable source of information for disaster risk reduction.
