**Fossil fuels: Teacher background information**

Fossil fuels are formed through geological processes (high temperatures and extreme pressure) over millions of years from the organic remains of living things (animals and plants). They are referred to as non-renewable energy sources because they are used faster than it is possible to replace them.

There are three types of fossil fuels:

- Coal —formed from plant remains

- Crude oil — formed from the remains of marine organisms

- Natural gas — formed from the remains of marine organisms

**Coal**

Coal is mostly used for generating electricity. It is burned in furnaces in power plants and the heat energy generated is then used to boil water. The steam from the boiling turns giant turbines. The movement energy generated from the spinning turbines is converted into electrical energy in generators.

Coal is also very important in the production of steel and in the manufacturing of cement.

Coal can undergo a process called coal liquefaction to make liquid fuel that can be used as an alternative to oil.

**Oil**

Crude oil may undergo different processes to form a range of different products. Below are the range of products and their uses:

- LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) — bottled gas

- Petrol — car fuel

- Paraffin — aircraft fuel

- Diesel — car, truck and bus fuel

- Heating oil — central heating fuel

- Fuel oil — ship and power-station fuel

**Natural gas**

Natural gas is odourless and mainly composed of methane. It is the cleanest-burning fossil fuel. It emits fewer greenhouse gases when burned in power stations than coal.

Natural gas is used in a variety of ways:

- Residential — cooking, hot water, heating, clothes dryer

- Commercial — cooking, hot water, heating

- Industrial — production of plastics, fertilisers, antifreeze and fabrics
