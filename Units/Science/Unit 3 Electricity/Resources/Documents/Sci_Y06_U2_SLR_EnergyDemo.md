**Energy demonstrations**

**Energy** is the ability to make things happen or cause changes. There are many different forms of energy, e.g. movement (kinetic) energy, chemical energy, heat energy, light energy, sound energy, and (stored) potential energy.

An **energy source** provides energy that can be transferred to other objects or transformed into different forms of energy, e.g. the battery in a torch transforms stored chemical energy into light energy and heat energy.

Forms of energy and sources can be demonstrated in a number of different ways. The following is a list of some ways that different forms of energy and sources can be demonstrated in the classroom.

Many of these energy sources generate a number of different forms of energy, e.g. a lit match is a source of light energy and heat energy.

**Heat**

Heat energy can be demonstrated using sources including:

- a hotplate*

- an electric heater*

- a lit candle or match

- rubbing hands together.

**Note:** It is difficult to directly observe heat energy, but easy to observe its effects. Use a frying pan on a hotplate and melt some butter or heat water in order to show the effect of heat energy on the butter or water.

**Light**

Light energy can be demonstrated using sources including:

- turning on a torch*

- turning on a TV, computer monitor or interactive whiteboard*

- lighting a match or candle.

**Sound**

Sound energy can be demonstrated using sources including:

- playing a radio*

- clapping your hands

- clicking your fingers

- talking.

**Movement**

The energy of movement can be demonstrated using sources including:

- turning on a fan to move air*

- rolling a marble down an inclined plane

- dropping a ball from a height

- walking.

* Sources marked with an asterisk transform electrical energy into other forms of energy. Electrical energy is a later focus of the unit.
