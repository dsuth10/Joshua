**Types of bioenergy**

**Pellets**

What are pellets?

Pellets are a compacted type of bioenergy. They are typically made of timber waste, sawdust and agricultural waste that is dried and processed into a densely compressed cylindrical pellet measuring about 1cm long, resembling rabbit and chicken food. Because they have a low moisture and ash content pellets burn very efficiently to generate heat and electricity. As a result, pellets are generally used in direct burning devices such as domestic wood heaters and commercial furnaces and boilers.

Pellets are mainly used for residential heating, although there are an increasing number of commercial scale users - such as hospitals, swimming pools, retirement homes etc - using pellets to generate electricity. Pellets are also increasingly being used in large-scale coal power plants to help generate electricity in a more environmentally-friendly manner.

Benefits of pellets

Pellets offer many unique benefits, such as:

- Pellets can provide a use for wood by-products - such as sawdust - that would otherwise have no market value. As Australia is one of the top 20 producers of sawdust excess from forestry in the world, this presents opportunities for Australia to use sawdust for domestic renewable energy generation or for export. (A large global export market exists for wood pellets, with some 9 million tonnes of wood pellets traded annually.)

- Because pellets have consistent quality and size they are easy to transport, store and handle. This allows cost-efficient transportation and automatic operation for heat and power, for residential homes and large-scale power plants.

- Existing coal power plants can be modified to produce a percentage (typically 3 and 15%) of its electricity from pellets rather than coal (this is known as ‘co-firing’)

Does pellet production cause deforestation?

Provided that wood pellets are supplied from forest resources that are managed and harvested in a sustainable manner – such as from the use of wood by-products from plantation timber – wood pellets offer a low-carbon, renewable energy source. It is estimated there is enough wood by-product from forest industry activities in Australia to supply 3000 gigawatt-hours of renewable energy per year without harvesting a single extra tree.

**Landfill waste (biogas)**

What is a landfill site?

When we throw our rubbish away, it usually ends up at a waste transfer facility where some of it is recycled. Any rubbish that isn’t re-used or recycled is placed in large holes dug in the ground. This is called a landfill site. It can also be called a dump or tip.

Over time, the organic matter in the landfill decomposes and produces gases which are harmful to the environment. Carbon dioxide and methane gas make up about 90–98 per cent of the gases produced by landfills. Methane gas is 21 times more environmentally damaging than carbon dioxide. These gases are called biogas or landfill gas.

A network of pipes are laid throughout the landfill to collect the biogas. The biogas is then extracted and cleaned to isolate the methane gas. Methane gas collected from landfill can be used as an energy source. The methane gas is burned off to generate electricity.

If biogas isn’t collected and used as an energy source then the gases escape the landfill and enter the atmosphere. A landfill can produce gases for 50 years or more. Biogas is a renewable energy source because there will always be waste that will produce biogas. In addition to landfills, agricultural waste, industrial waste and energy crops also produce biogas.
