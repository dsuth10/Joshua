# TA04: Teacher Guide — Switches and representations

## Expected Answers / Solutions

### Section 3: Note Organiser (Switch States)
- **Open Switch (OFF):** The metal contacts inside the switch are **separated** (or **apart**). This makes the circuit path **incomplete** (there is an air gap). The electricity **stops** (or **cannot flow**).
- **Closed Switch (ON):** The metal contacts inside the switch **touch** (or **connect**). This makes the circuit path **complete** (no gaps). The electricity **flows** (or **transfers energy**).

### Section 4: Practical (Switch Comparison Table)
- **Slide / Rocker Switch:** Stays closed (ON) or open (OFF) after being clicked once. (Maintained control).
- **Push-Button Switch:** Requires constant finger pressure to stay closed (ON). Turns OFF the moment pressure is released. (Momentary control).

### Section 4: Circuit Diagram
- Look for:
  - Standard symbols used (Battery: long/short parallel lines; Bulb: circle with X; Switch: open slanted line with circles or closed flat line with circles).
  - Wires drawn as straight horizontal/vertical lines with clear 90-degree corners.
  - A single loop showing the components connected in series.

### Section 5: Scientific Explanation (CER)
- **Claim:** A switch controls a bulb by completing or breaking the conducting path in the circuit.
- **Evidence:** When the switch was closed (turned ON), the bulb lit up. When the switch was open (turned OFF), the bulb went out.
- **Reasoning:** In an open switch, there is an air gap between metal contacts. Since air is an electrical insulator, electricity cannot flow across it, leaving the path incomplete. Closing the switch brings the metal contacts together. Since metal is a conductor, it completes the path, allowing electrical energy to flow from the battery to the bulb.

### Section 6: Exit Reflection
- **Answer:** No, a circuit diagram does not need to show exact lengths or distance. Its purpose is to show the *logical connections* between components, not its physical scale or appearance. This helps scientists focus on how electricity flows rather than the shape of the wires.

## Misconception Alerts
1. **"Circuit diagrams are drawings of physical shapes."**
   - *Issue:* Students try to draw wire loops, circular battery bodies, or bulb filaments.
   - *Teacher Action:* Emphasize that symbols are abstract and universal. Contrast a layout diagram (showing realistic wires) with a schematic diagram (using right angles and symbols).
2. **"Switches generate or add electricity."**
   - *Issue:* Students think turning the switch ON creates the electrical energy.
   - *Teacher Action:* Clarify that the battery stores chemical energy which is transformed into electrical energy. The switch is simply a gate or a bridge that allows or blocks the flow.
3. **"Wires must be curvy to work."**
   - *Issue:* Students think straight lines in diagrams mean physical wires must be perfectly straight.
   - *Teacher Action:* Remind students that diagrams represent the logical connections, not physical geometry.

## Safety Notes & Risk Controls
- **Short Circuit Prevention:** If a student connects the positive battery terminal directly to the negative battery terminal without a bulb/load, the wire and battery pack will get very hot, potentially melting insulation. If a student's wire gets hot, immediately disconnect the battery pack.
- **Low-Voltage Only:** Restrict all building to approved 1.5V/3V battery packs. Explicitly remind students that household electricity (240V mains) is extremely dangerous and must only be touched by a qualified electrician.
- **Teacher Checkpoint:** Perform a visual check of each group's physical circuit before they switch the battery pack to ON to ensure no short circuits exist.

## Conferencing Prompts (During Practical)
- **Terminal Mapping:** "Show me where the wire connects to the switch. Why did you use the middle pin and an outer pin? What would happen if we used the two outer pins?" *(Answer: The switch would not connect/disconnect properly).*
- **Momentary vs Maintained:** "Why would you want a slide switch for a bedroom light but a push-button for a doorbell? What would happen if doorbells used slide switches?" *(Answer: The doorbell would ring forever unless clicked off).*
- **Bridge Concept:** "Look at the open switch symbol. Why does it look like a tilted line? How does that represent what is happening physically inside the switch?" *(Answer: It represents the open metal contacts that are separated).*
