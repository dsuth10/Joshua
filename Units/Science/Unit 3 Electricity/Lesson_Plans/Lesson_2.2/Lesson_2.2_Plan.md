## Pedagogical Contemplation
1. **Cognitive goal:** Students progress from describing physical circuits visually to representing them abstractly. The transition from physical layout to schematic diagram is a significant conceptual leap that helps them focus on *logical connectivity* over *physical appearance*.
2. **Interactive alignment:** The *Circuit Symbol Studio* (WS04) interactive tool directly addresses the layout-to-diagram translation, allowing students to match symbols, test switch actions, and complete diagram paths with instant feedback.
3. **Visible thinking:** Using interactive smartboard slide activities and mini-whiteboard diagram challenges, the teacher can instantly see if students are drawing lines that cross or using correct symbols.
4. **Pedagogical vs engagement purpose:** Comparing rocker (maintained) and push-button (momentary) switches exposes how mechanical contacts physically close/open gaps.
5. **Simulation/physical relationship:** Students build the circuit in Tinkercad first to verify their switch placement works safely, then build it with physical parts (comparing push-button, slide, or rocker switches).
6. **Retained evidence:** Students paste screenshots of their Tinkercad builds, draw a standard circuit diagram in their journal (JN04), and record reflections explaining open vs. closed states.

## Lesson Sequence

### 1. Launch (10 mins)
- **Hook:** Display a slide showing a bedside lamp with a rocker switch. Turn a physical lamp on and off (or toggle slide theme).
- **Question:** *What happens inside the cable and switch when I click it? Where does the electricity go when it is off?*
- Introduce the Learning Intention and Success Criteria (Standard and Support pathways).

### 2. Teach: The Switch as a Bridge (10 mins)
- Show the cutaway animation of a switch.
- Define **Open Switch** (air gap, incomplete path, no flow) and **Closed Switch** (contacts touch, complete path, electricity flows).
- Introduce different physical switch types: rocker/slide (maintained) and push-button (momentary). Compare their behaviors.

### 3. CFU 1: Switch States (5 mins)
- Quick whiteboard check: Draw a circuit path showing a switch.
- *Ask: If the light is ON, is the switch open or closed? If the path has a gap, what is the state?*

### 4. Teach: Scientific Representations (15 mins)
- Explain why scientists draw diagrams instead of physical pictures (speed, clarity, international standards).
- Introduce circuit symbols: Cell/Battery, Wire, Bulb, Switch (Open/Closed).
- **Worked Example (Teacher Think-Aloud):** Model drawing a circuit diagram on a grid. Show how to draw straight wires with 90-degree corners, and place symbols correctly. Emphasize that distance and scale do not matter on the diagram.

### 5. CFU 2: Diagram Check (5 mins)
- Display a layout photo. Students draw the corresponding circuit diagram on their mini-whiteboards.
- Prompt: *Check your corners. Are they right angles? Are your symbols correct?*

### 6. Practical & Journal: Challenge 3 - Take Control (15 mins)
- Students open **TC04** and **JN04**.
- **Tinkercad Phase:** Add a slide/slide-switch to control a light bulb in Tinkercad. Verify correct operation. Take a screenshot.
- **Physical Phase:** Construct the switched circuit physically. Test two switch types (slide/rocker vs push-button).
- **Journal Phase:** Students complete the switch comparison table, draw their circuit diagram on the grid, and write their explanations.

### 7. Exit & Reflection (5 mins)
- Reflection prompt: *Why do we draw circuit diagrams using straight lines and symbols rather than drawing the actual components?*
- Collect exit responses.
