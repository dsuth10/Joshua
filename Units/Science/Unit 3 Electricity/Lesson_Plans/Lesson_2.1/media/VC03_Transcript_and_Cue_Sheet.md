# VC03: Transcript and Cue Sheet (A Complete Circuit)

**Focus Question (Before viewing):** 
Watch carefully to see what path the electric current takes from the battery, through the circuit, and back.

## Storyboard & Transcript

| Timestamp | Visual | Audio Transcript |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 - 0:15 | Animation: A low-voltage battery, wire, and a bulb in a closed loop. Tiny glowing particles flow in a continuous line from the positive (+) terminal, through the lamp filament, and back to the negative (-) terminal. The lamp glows. | **Narrator**: For electricity to do work, it must travel. It needs a path. A closed loop made of conducting materials, connecting one terminal of the battery all the way to the other, is called a complete circuit. |
| 0:15 - 0:35 | Animation: A pair of scissors cuts one of the wires. A visible gap appears. The glowing flow stops instantly, and the lamp goes dark. | **Narrator**: What happens when we break that path? The flow of electricity stops immediately. It doesn't slowly leak out or pool at the cut—it stops everywhere in the circuit. This is an incomplete circuit. |
| 0:35 - 0:55 | Close up: A slide switch is connected into the gap. We see a metal contact sliding back and forth. Closed switch = metal touches metal; open switch = metal is lifted, creating an air gap. | **Narrator**: A switch is just a controlled gate. When the switch is closed, the metal parts touch, completing the conducting path. When open, it creates an air gap. Since air is an insulator, the electricity stops flowing. |
| 0:55 - 1:15 | Video close up: A real alligator clip clamped onto a wire, but the clip is gripping the plastic insulation sleeve instead of the metal copper core. The lamp doesn't light. | **Narrator**: Sometimes we build a circuit, but a connection is faulty. If an alligator clip grips the plastic insulation instead of the metal wire, the path is broken because plastic is an insulator. We must verify metal-to-metal contact to complete our loop. |
| 1:15 - 1:30 | Diagram: A simple schematic diagram overlays the physical layout, emphasizing the loop. | **Narrator**: Remember, to make any component work, electricity must be able to trace a path from the energy source, through the component, and all the way back to the start. Check your loops, and happy building! |

**Follow-up Journal Prompts (After viewing):**
1. Why did the bulb go out the instant the wire was cut?
2. What happens if a wire clip grips the plastic insulation of a wire instead of the metal copper inside?
