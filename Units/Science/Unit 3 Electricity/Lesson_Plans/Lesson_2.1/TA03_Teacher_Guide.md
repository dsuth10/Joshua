# TA03: Teacher Guide — Complete and Incomplete Circuits

## Expected Answers / Solutions

### Section 3: Note Organiser (Loop or Gap)
1. A **complete circuit** is a **closed** loop that has no gaps.
2. The path must be made of **metal** (conducting materials) like copper wire.
3. Electricity leaves the battery from the **positive** (+) terminal, travels through the components, and must get all the way back to the **negative** (-) terminal.
4. When the loop is complete, electricity **flows**.
5. An **incomplete circuit** is a path that has a **gap** or break in it.
6. The gap could be an **open** switch, a disconnected wire, or an **insulator** (like plastic or air) blocking the path.
7. If there is a break anywhere in the loop, electricity **stops** flowing instantly throughout the whole circuit.

### Section 4: Practical (Challenge 2 Table)
- **Case A (Unconnected Battery):** *Does it light?* No. *Fault:* Positive wire is not connected to the terminal. *Repair:* Drag wire to the battery terminal.
- **Case B (Trapped Clip):** *Does it light?* No. *Fault:* Alligator clip is touching the plastic sleeve of the bulb socket terminal rather than the metal contact screw. *Repair:* Connect clip to the metal contact screw.
- **Case C (Floating Wire):** *Does it light?* No. *Fault:* Wire tip is resting in the air gap without contacting the bulb terminal. *Repair:* Align wire tip to touch the bulb terminal contact securely.
- **Case D (Open Switch):** *Does it light?* No. *Fault:* The switch is open (OFF state). *Repair:* Close the switch (turn it ON).

### Section 5: Scientific Explanation (CER)
- **Claim:** The trapped plastic sleeve in Case B stops the bulb from lighting because it blocks the conducting path.
- **Evidence:** When the clip touched the plastic sleeve, the bulb remained dark. When the clip touched the metal screw terminal, the bulb lit up.
- **Reasoning:** Electricity requires a continuous, complete loop of conductors to flow from the positive to the negative terminal of the battery. Metal is a conductor, which allows electrical energy to flow. Plastic is an insulator, which blocks electricity. The plastic sleeve acted as a gap in the conducting path, making the circuit incomplete. Bypassing the plastic and touching metal completed the loop.

### Section 6: Exit Reflection
- **Answer:** If there is a gap anywhere in the circuit, the path is incomplete and the electrical energy cannot flow. Electricity cannot jump across air gaps or flow through insulators under these low voltages, so a single break halts the transfer of energy in the entire loop.

## Misconception Alerts
1. **"Electricity gets used up by the output device (bulb)."**
   - *Issue:* Students think current flows out of the positive terminal, gets consumed by the bulb, and nothing goes back to the negative terminal.
   - *Teacher Action:* Emphasize that the same current flows throughout the entire loop. Use the metaphor of a bicycle chain: the chain must go all the way around a loop to move. If you cut the chain anywhere, the whole chain stops moving immediately.
2. **"Electricity flows from both terminals and collides at the bulb."**
   - *Issue:* Students think current exits both terminals simultaneously and meets in the middle.
   - *Teacher Action:* Have students trace the path in a single direction (from positive, through bulb, back to negative) to establish the concept of continuous flow in a loop.
3. **"Only metals are conductors."**
   - *Issue:* Students think non-metal materials cannot complete a loop.
   - *Teacher Action:* Prepare a graphite pencil mark on paper to demonstrate that carbon (graphite) is a non-metal conductor during the extension or upcoming conductor lessons.

---

## Safety Notes & Risk Controls
- **Short Circuit Prevention:** If students bypass the bulb and connect red directly to black, the alligator leads will get hot and the battery will drain quickly. Teach students to always feel their wires. If a wire feels warm, disconnect it immediately.
- **Low Voltage Safety:** Limit all physical building to 1.5V and 3V battery boxes. Explicitly remind students that 240V mains electricity is extremely dangerous and must only be handled by licensed professionals.

## Conferencing Prompts (During Practical)
- **Tracing loops:** "Put your finger on the positive terminal and trace the wire. Where is the gap? What material is in that gap?"
- **Contact inspection:** "Is your alligator clip biting onto the copper metal, or is it biting onto the plastic coating?"
- **Switch concept:** "When the switch is open, what is inside the gap between the metal contacts? Why can't electricity flow through that air?" *(Answer: Air is an insulator).*
