## Pedagogical Contemplation & Design Architecture

1. **Cognitive Goal:** Students transition from identifying basic energy forms to analyzing complex systems. They must trace sequential energy transformations (e.g., $Chemical \rightarrow Thermal \rightarrow Kinetic \rightarrow Electrical$) and weigh trade-offs (reliability vs environmental impact) to construct balanced scientific evaluations.
2. **Assessment Criteria Alignment:**
   - **a. Energy source:** Identifying renewable vs non-renewable origin.
   - **b. Why is it a good choice?:** Explaining suitability, availability, energy density, or sustainability.
   - **c. Flow diagram:** Visualizing energy transformations from primary storage to generator output.
   - **d. Explanation of sequence:** Describing *how* each energy form converts to the next.
   - **e. Advantages and disadvantages:** Evaluating environmental, economic, and operational factors.
3. **Interactive Alignment:** The classroom deck features a 5-source station carousel (Solar PV, Hydroelectric, Coal-Fired Thermal, Wind Power, Nuclear Fission) allowing whole-class modeling, guided station rotation, or individual inquiry.
4. **Presenter Notes Architecture:** All logistical directives (`DO`, `WORK`, `RECORD`, `FINISH`, `CHECK`) are strictly housed within the hidden off-canvas Notes Drawer to maximize slide canvas area for full-screen visual learning.
5. **Dual-Level Pathways:** Every instructional slide provides a `.standard-only` pathway (full Year 6 academic vocabulary and structural scaffolding) and a `.lucas-only` support pathway (simplified syntax, visual bulleting, and scaffolded sentence stems).

## Detailed Step-by-Step Lesson Sequence

### 1. Launch & Hook (5 Mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Project Slide 1. Display contrast images of a solar farm at sunrise and a coal power plant at dusk. Ask: *"When you flick a light switch in Queensland, where did that electrical energy actually begin its journey?"*
- **Student Action:** Quick pair-share.
- **Notes Drawer Logistics:**
  - `DO`: Prompt students to think beyond the power outlet.
  - `WORK`: Think-Pair-Share on power origins.
  - `RECORD`: None required yet.
  - `FINISH`: Transition to Learning Intention.
  - `CHECK`: Listen for student awareness of renewable vs non-renewable sources.

### 2. Explicit Instruction: Assessment Criteria Breakdown (10 Mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Walk through Slides 2–4. Break down the 5 assessment criteria (a–e). Model an exemplar breakdown using **Hydroelectric Power**.
  - *Source (a):* Water stored in high reservoirs.
  - *Suitability (b):* High annual rainfall, natural topography, instant dispatchability for peak demand.
  - *Flow Diagram (c):* $Gravitational\ Potential\ Energy \rightarrow Kinetic\ Energy\ (falling\ water) \rightarrow Mechanical\ Kinetic\ Energy\ (turbine\ rotation) \rightarrow Electrical\ Energy\ (generator)$.
  - *Sequence Explanation (d):* Gravity pulls stored water down penstocks; kinetic energy turns turbine blades; spinning magnets in generator induce electrical current.
  - *Advantages & Disadvantages (e):* Rapid response time & zero greenhouse gas emissions during operation vs high construction costs & ecosystem flooding.
- **Notes Drawer Logistics:**
  - `DO`: Unpack criteria a–e step-by-step.
  - `WORK`: Follow exemplar breakdown on main board.
  - `RECORD`: Note key key terms ($Potential$, $Kinetic$, $Mechanical$, $Electrical$).
  - `FINISH`: Transition to Multi-Source Carousel.
  - `CHECK`: CFU via mini-whiteboards: *"What form of energy is stored in dam water behind a wall?"* ($Gravitational\ Potential$).

### 3. Guided Practice: Multi-Source Station Carousel (15 Mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Project Slide 5. Cycle through the 5 source tabs (Solar PV, Hydroelectric, Coal, Wind, Nuclear).
- **Student Action:** In small groups or individually, choose or receive an assigned energy source. Use the interactive slide data to examine energy flows, suitability factors, and trade-offs.
- **Notes Drawer Logistics:**
  - `DO`: Facilitate station exploration. Guide support students to Solar or Wind; challenge extension students with Nuclear or Coal transformation chains.
  - `WORK`: Examine source details and draft flow diagrams.
  - `RECORD`: Draft section a, b, and c on worksheet/journal.
  - `FINISH`: Call time for flow diagram checks.
  - `CHECK`: Ensure students differentiate between Photovoltaic (direct light to electricity) and Thermal (heat to steam to turbine).

### 4. Independent Application & Journal Drafting (15 Mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Display Slide 9 & 10. Distribute `JN_Energy_Sources_Assessment_Journal.md` / printed task sheets.
- **Student Action:** Complete all five criteria (a–e) for their selected energy source:
  1. State energy source.
  2. Explain why it is a good choice.
  3. Draw neat flow diagram with arrows and labeled energy forms.
  4. Write sequential paragraphs describing each energy change.
  5. Fill in Advantages vs Disadvantages table (minimum 2 of each).
- **Notes Drawer Logistics:**
  - `DO`: Circulate around tables, providing targeted prompts for criterion d (explanations) and e (advantages/disadvantages).
  - `WORK`: Silent independent assessment drafting.
  - `RECORD`: Finalize assessment response in Student Journal / Task Sheet.
  - `FINISH`: Give 2-minute wrap-up warning.
  - `CHECK`: Review support pathway students' sentence stems.

### 5. Review & Exit Ticket (5 Mins)
- **Teacher Action:** Project Slide 11.
- **Student Action:** Complete the Exit Ticket: *"Identify one advantage and one disadvantage for an energy source different from the one you researched today."*
- **Notes Drawer Logistics:**
  - `DO`: Collect exit tickets for formative assessment review.
  - `WORK`: Complete digital/paper exit ticket.
  - `RECORD`: Record peer takeaway.
  - `FINISH`: Dismiss class.
  - `CHECK`: Evaluate mastery of Criterion E trade-off analysis.

## Assessment Rubric (Year 6 AC9S6U03)

| Criterion | Developing (Level 1-2) | Proficient (Level 3-4) | Advanced (Level 5) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **a. Energy Source** | Names an energy source but misclassifies type. | Correctly names energy source and identifies as renewable or non-renewable. | Accurately classifies source and describes its natural origin and availability. |
| **b. Suitability Rationale** | Gives vague reason (e.g., "it is good"). | Explains why the source is suitable using basic environmental or economic context. | Provides a compelling, evidence-based justification considering local geography and grid needs. |
| **c. Flow Diagram** | Missing steps or uses incorrect energy form labels. | Draws a clear sequence of energy transformations using arrows and correct form names. | Illustrates a complete, highly accurate flow diagram including primary storage, intermediate forms, and final electrical output. |
| **d. Sequence Explanation** | List of forms with minimal explanation of conversion. | Explains how energy changes from one form to the next across the sequence. | Articulates precise mechanisms (e.g., combustion, steam expansion, turbine induction, photovoltaic excitation) for each transformation. |
| **e. Advantages & Disadvantages** | Identifies only 1 basic point or confuses pros/cons. | Identifies 2 distinct advantages and 2 disadvantages accurately. | Delivers a nuanced evaluation balancing reliability, cost, environmental impact, and energy density. |
