Connect: Source vs. Form Refresher

Before building flow diagrams, let's lock in the core difference between a source and a form:

🌱 Energy Source

Where energy originates or is stored in nature before extraction.

Examples: Coal, Sun, Water in reservoir, Wind, Uranium

⚡ Energy Form

How energy manifests, travels, or does work in a system.

Examples: Chemical, Thermal, Kinetic, Electrical, Light
⚠️ Common Misconception: "Electricity is an energy source."
Electricity is a secondary form of energy generated from primary sources!

Remember the key difference:

🌱 Energy Source

Where energy comes from or is stored.

Sun, Water, Wind, Coal

⚡ Energy Form

How energy looks or moves.

Heat, Light, Movement, Electricity

Step 1 ➔ 2 (Combustion):

Coal stores chemical potential energy. When burned inside the furnace, combustion converts chemical energy into thermal energy, heating water into high-pressure steam.

Step 3 ➔ 4 (Generation):

Expanding steam rushes past turbine blades, turning thermal energy into mechanical kinetic energy. The spinning turbine turns copper coils inside magnetic fields within the generator, inducing electrical energy.

Criterion E: Advantages vs. Disadvantages Evaluation

Every energy source has technical and environmental trade-offs. Compare key factors below:

✅ Key Advantages Factors

  • Zero Carbon Emissions: Solar, wind, hydro, & nuclear emit zero CO2 during generation.
  • Infinite Supply: Solar and wind energy are continuously renewed by natural processes.
  • Baseline Reliability: Coal and nuclear provide steady 24/7 power regardless of weather.
  • Low Operating Cost: Fuel costs for wind and solar are completely free.

❌ Key Disadvantages Factors

  • Intermittency: Solar only generates in sunlight; wind power stops when calm.
  • High Initial Capital: Building hydro dams or nuclear plants requires billions in upfront cost.
  • Greenhouse Gas Emissions: Burning coal releases heavy CO2 and particulate pollution.
  • Waste & Ecosystem Impact: Hydro dams flood habitats; nuclear creates long-lived radioactive waste.

👍 Advantages (Good Points)

  • Clean air (no smoke from solar/wind).
  • Free sunlight and wind fuel.
  • Reliable 24/7 power from coal/nuclear.

👎 Disadvantages (Drawbacks)

  • No solar power at night time.
  • High cost to build big dams or plants.
  • Smoke and carbon pollution from coal.

Exemplar Analysis: Top-Mark Response

Sample Student Response: Solar Photovoltaic Level 5 Exemplar

a. Source: Solar energy from sunlight (Renewable).

b. Good Choice: Australia receives world-leading solar irradiance, making rooftop solar highly suitable to reduce peak household grid demand during hot summer days.

c. Flow Diagram: Sunlight (Light Energy) ➔ Semiconductor Absorption (Photovoltaic) ➔ Inverter (Direct Current to Alternating Current) ➔ Electrical Output

d. Sequence Explanation: Photons from sunlight strike silicon photovoltaic cells, exciting valence electrons. This photovoltaic effect transforms light energy directly into electrical energy without needing spinning turbines.

e. Evaluation: Advantages: Zero greenhouse gas emissions during generation; free unlimited fuel. Disadvantages: Intermittent output requiring battery storage; high upfront installation cost.

Writing Scaffold (Solar Example):

a. Source: The Sun (Renewable).

b. Good Choice: Australia has lots of sunny days.

c. Diagram: Sunlight ➔ Solar Panel ➔ Electricity

d. Explanation: Light hits the solar panel. The panel turns light into electricity.

e. Pros/Cons: Good: No smoke, free sun. Bad: No power at night, panels cost money.
