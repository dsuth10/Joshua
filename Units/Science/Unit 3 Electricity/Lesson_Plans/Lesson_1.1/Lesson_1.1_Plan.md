## Pedagogical Contemplation
1. **Cognitive goal:** Students are practicing the skill of *distinguishing* between a source (where the energy is stored/originates) and a form (how the energy is observed/doing work). They are also *sequencing* how energy changes.
2. **Interactive alignment:** The Source-Form Sorter interaction forces students to make a hard categorisation choice, exposing the common misconception that "electricity" is a source. 
3. **Visible thinking:** The teacher will see the class's understanding via the mini-whiteboard CFU before revealing the sorter answers.
4. **Pedagogical vs engagement purpose:** The drag-and-drop or tap-to-select sorter isn't just for fun; it provides immediate two-tier feedback on common distractors.
5. **Simulation/physical relationship:** The "Energy Hunt" is a physical classroom observation task where students apply the theoretical distinction to real objects around them.
6. **Retained evidence:** Every student records a categorisation table and a real-world energy chain in their OneNote journal.

## Lesson Sequence
1. **Launch (5 mins)**: Hook with an image of a lit-up city. Ask: *What makes all of this work?*
2. **Teach (10 mins)**: Define Energy Source vs. Energy Form. Introduce the vocabulary.
3. **CFU 1 (5 mins)**: Mini-whiteboard response. *Is a battery a source or a form?*
4. **Interactive (10 mins)**: Run the Source-Form Sorter (TP01). 
5. **Teach (10 mins)**: Transformation Chains. Watch the short video (VC01) and model a chain: Sun -> Solar Panel (Electrical) -> Light.
6. **Practical Briefing (5 mins)**: Explain the Classroom Energy Hunt (TC01). 
7. **Practical/Journal (10 mins)**: Students hunt for objects, fill in their tables (JN01).
8. **Exit (5 mins)**: Write one complete energy chain for an object they found.
