Energy All Around Us

What makes all of this work?

Look at a city at night. Lights glow. Cars move. Homes stay warm.

Where does all this energy come from?

Launch & Hook

Hook students with the city image. Ask: What powers the lights, cars, and buildings?

Misconception alert: Students may say "electricity". Prompt deeper: "Where did the electricity come from before it reached the light bulb?"

Learning Intention

Today we will distinguish an energy source from an energy form, and trace simple energy transformation chains.

  • Identify at least three energy sources and three energy forms.
  • Categorise examples correctly into sources or forms.
  • Trace a simple energy chain (e.g. Coal → Electrical → Light).

Today we learn where energy comes from and how we see it working.

  • Name three places energy comes from.
  • Sort words into "source" or "form".
  • Make a simple energy chain with three steps.

Source vs Form

Energy Source

Where energy is stored or where it originates.

Examples: Coal, The Sun, Wind, Battery

Energy Form

How we observe energy doing work or moving.

Examples: Light, Heat, Sound, Electrical

Energy Source

Where energy comes from (where it is stored).

Examples: Sun, coal, battery

Energy Form

How energy looks or feels when it works.

Examples: light, heat, sound

Direct Instruction

Emphasise: a source stores energy; a form is what it does.

Battery example: the battery stores chemical energy (source); it provides electrical energy (form) to a device.
