  - Column 2: Example learning sequence<br>Explore electrical components<br>Identify electrical components:<br>Power source, e.g. electrical cell or series of cells (battery)<br>Wire<br>Load, e.g. light bulb, buzzer<br>Clarify that what is commonly referred to as a battery is a single electrical cell and that in science ‘battery’ refers to two or more cells.<br>Discuss safety considerations when working with electrical components.<br>Explore simple circuits<br>Explore different ways the components can be configured to make the light bulb and buzzer work.<br>Identify these arrangements of electrical circuits as:<br>simple circuit — consists of the power source, wires and one type of load only<br>series circuit — consists of the power source, wires and two or more types of load where the electricity flows in one path.<br>Describe the role of each component in an electrical circuit:<br>Power source, e.g. electrical cell or battery provides energy and electricity for the circuit<br>Wire — allows electricity to flow around the circuit and transfer electrical energy between components<br>Load — a device or component that slows down the flow of electricity and transforms electrical energy into other forms, e.g.<br>- light bulb transforms electrical energy into light and heat energy<br>- buzzer transforms electrical energy into sound energy.<br>Identify that stored energy in the electrical cell is transformed into electrical energy and transferred to other parts of the circuit.<br>Define the requirements of a complete circuit<br>Discuss the idea that for the light bulb or buzzer to work the circuit must be complete, i.e. have no gaps between any components, so electricity can flow around the entire circuit in one path.<br>Recognise that electricity and electrical energy are not the same thing. While electricity flows around a circuit, electrical energy is transferred from one component to another, where it is transformed into other energy forms. Each time a transformation occurs, there is less electrical energy flowing to subsequent components.<br>**Note:** Any electrical work in a home or work setting should only be carried out by a qualified electrician.<br>Resources<br>Supporting learning resource — [Materials and equipment list](../Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U2_SLR_MaterialsEquipmentList.md)<br>Supporting learning resource — [Science inquiry skills poster Years 3–6](../Resources/Documents/Sci_Y3-6_Lvl3_SLR_ScienceInquirySkillsPoster.pdf)<br>Supporting learning resource — [Electrical circuits: Teacher background information](../Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U2_SLR_ElectricalCircuits.md)<br>Sheet — [Requirements for a complete circuit](../Resources/Documents/Sci_Y06_U2_SH_RequirementsForACompleteCircuit.md)<br>Helpful information<br>Learning object - *Wiring: the simple circuit* (Curriculum Corporation) (TLF L3058) https://learningplace.eq.edu.au/cx/resources/items/f34b548d-06eb-3668-c040-cbba5a2b9d59/0/ViewIMS.jsp<br>Safety<br>Teachers need to:<br>refer to the *Health and Safety* policy http://education.qld.gov.au/health/safety/index.html pertaining to schools<br>consult the *Curriculum Activity Risk Management Guidelines* http://education.qld.gov.au/curriculum/carmg/index.html<br>ensure that students are aware of the risks associated with batteries when working in this unit and do not use any batteries which are leaking or have chemicals outside the case<br>ensure students know the light bulbs are constructed of very thin glass. When handling light bulbs students should be advised that they may break easily.<br>- Avoid working with the electrical circuits in wet areas and near liquids.
