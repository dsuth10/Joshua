**Unit focus:** Designing, testing and improving circuits with Tinkercad, physical components and optional Arduino-compatible microcontroller activities  
**Year level:** Year 6  
**Suggested use:** Student workbook, digital journal, OneNote section, printed booklet or Google/Word template  
**Connected teacher unit plan:** *Energy and Electricity: Designing, Testing and Improving Circuits with Tinkercad and Arduino*  

---

## 1. Purpose of the Engineering Journal

The Student Engineering Journal is the main evidence book for the practical side of the unit. It helps students record their thinking before, during and after each circuit investigation.

The journal should not simply be a place for finished answers. It should show the full engineering and science process:

**Predict → Build → Simulate → Test → Record → Explain → Troubleshoot → Improve → Reflect**

Students use the journal to collect:

- predictions
- screenshots of Tinkercad circuits
- hand-drawn or digital circuit diagrams
- test results
- troubleshooting notes
- explanations of energy transfer and transformation
- safety notes and risk assessments
- conductor and insulator data
- Arduino input/output explanations
- final design challenge evidence
- reflections on improvements

The journal is designed so that each Tinkercad challenge card produces a matching journal entry. This makes student evidence easier to assess because every practical task has a written or diagram-based record.

---

## 2. How the Journal and Challenge Cards Work Together

Each practical lesson should have two linked student resources:

1. **Tinkercad Challenge Card** — tells students what to build, test and explain.
2. **Engineering Journal Page** — gives students a structured place to record evidence.

The challenge card creates the practical experience. The journal page turns that experience into assessable Science evidence.

### Recommended lesson routine

| Lesson stage | Student action | Journal evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Read the challenge question | Write or highlight the investigation question |
| Prediction | Predict what will happen | Complete prediction box |
| Build | Create the circuit in Tinkercad | Insert or sketch screenshot |
| Simulate | Press start simulation and observe | Record result |
| Explain | Describe what happened using Science language | Complete explanation scaffold |
| Troubleshoot | Fix any faults | Complete fault/improvement log |
| Represent | Draw or label the circuit | Add circuit diagram |
| Reflect | Connect to the big idea | Complete reflection prompt |

## 6. Differentiated Journal Response Options

### Support version

Use sentence stems and diagrams.

> My circuit worked because the electricity had a complete path.  
> Electrical energy went from the ______ through the ______ to the ______.  
> The ______ changed electrical energy into ______ energy.  
> When I changed ______, the circuit ______.

### Standard version

Students write a full explanation using scientific vocabulary.

> The circuit worked because the electrical energy could travel through a complete closed path from the cell, through the wires and components, and back to the other side of the cell. The ______ transformed electrical energy into ______ energy. When the switch was open, the circuit was incomplete and the output stopped.

### Extension version

Students include cause-and-effect reasoning.

> The change I made affected the circuit by ______. This happened because ______. The evidence that supports my explanation is ______. A limitation of my model is ______.

# Challenge Card 1 — Make It Glow

**Lesson link:** Lesson 2 — What makes a circuit complete?  
**Difficulty:** Core  
**Main concept:** A circuit must be complete for electricity to flow.

## Your challenge
Build a simple circuit in Tinkercad that makes a bulb or LED glow.

## Components
- 1 battery or cell
- wires
- 1 bulb or LED
- 1 resistor if using an LED

## Build steps
1. Place the battery/cell in the workspace.
2. Add a bulb or LED.
3. Connect the components with wires to make a complete loop.
4. If using an LED, make sure it is connected the correct way.
5. Start the simulation.
6. Change one thing to break the circuit on purpose.
7. Explain why the circuit stopped working.

## Prediction
What do you think must happen for the light to turn on?

## Evidence to collect
- screenshot of working circuit
- screenshot or sketch of broken circuit
- one-sentence explanation of why the broken circuit failed

## Science explanation focus
Use these words: **complete circuit, electrical energy, component, open circuit, closed circuit**.

## Extension
Can you make the light turn on again by changing only one wire?

## Journal page
Complete: **Complete Circuit Evidence**.

## 9. Teacher Implementation Notes

### Best way to introduce the journal

Introduce the journal as an engineer’s notebook rather than a worksheet booklet. Emphasise that real engineers record unfinished thinking, mistakes, tests and improvements. The goal is not to make every page neat; the goal is to show thinking clearly.

### Evidence expectations

For most practical lessons, students should produce at least:

- one screenshot or sketch
- one prediction
- one result
- one explanation
- one reflection or improvement idea

For major assessment moments, students should produce:

- a complete labelled circuit representation
- a clear explanation using Science vocabulary
- a safety note or risk assessment
- evidence of troubleshooting or improvement

### Suggested marking focus

Do not over-mark every page. Instead, use selected checkpoints:

| Checkpoint | Suggested pages to collect or inspect |
|---|---|
| Early circuit understanding | Complete Circuit Evidence; Switch Investigation |
| Mid-unit investigation | Conductors and Insulators Investigation Plan and Results |
| Arduino/control extension | Input → Process → Output Diagram |
| Energy generation | Energy Transformation Chain; Energy Decision Brief |
| Final assessment | Final Design Folio pages |

### Practical management

- Use one shared naming convention for screenshots, for example: `Name_Challenge3_SwitchCircuit`.
- Allow students to paste screenshots into OneNote, Word, Google Docs or printed boxes.
- Consider using group journals for practical builds and individual reflection pages for assessment evidence.
- For students who struggle with writing, accept labelled diagrams plus oral explanations recorded by teacher notes.
- Keep Arduino tasks optional or scaffolded heavily if the class needs more time on core circuits.

By the end of the journal, I can:

- build a simple complete circuit

- use a switch to control a circuit

- identify conductors and insulators using evidence
- follow safety rules when using low-voltage circuits
- draw or label a circuit clearly
- troubleshoot a circuit fault
- explain how a microcontroller can control outputs
- describe how electricity can be generated from different energy sources
- compare energy sources using evidence
- design, test and improve an electrical system
