2. Self Cleaning House

Read the passage, then answer all questions using evidence from the text.

Reading

Frances Gabe invented a self-cleaning house, with waterproof ceilings, furniture, walls and sloping floors. Her all-concrete house was designed to appeal to elderly and physically handicapped people – those for whom housework was difficult. It was designed to save time labour and space – and banish housework.

The dishwasher doubled as a cupboard. Clothes were cleaned as they hung in the wardrobe. Shelves, showers, baths and toilets cleaned themselves. Everything could be washed with jets of water and dried with hot air. Even the furniture was washable. While some may consider this innovative residence to be their dream come true, there will be those who are just not ready for the necessarily stark nature of the design and the bold lifestyle changes it would demand.

Why might the self-cleaning house not appeal to some homeowners?

3. Disc Brakes

Read the passage, then answer all questions using evidence from the text.

Reading

In recent years, vehicle brake design has changed significantly. The use of drum brakes has declined. Because of their simpler design, lighter weight and better performance, disc brakes, used for years for front wheel applications, are now a feature on the rear wheels of modern cars.

They are superior in ‘brake fade’ resistance. Brake fade is caused by high temperatures that are generated by repeated hard braking. It occurs when the pads or shoes ‘glaze’ due to the great pressure and heat of hard use. Once they cool, the condition subsides.

Compared to drum brakes, disc brakes have greater air ventilation. Drum brakes would be ineffective if water accumulated in them so they are not internally ventilated. Disc brakes rapidly fling off any water that they are exposed to.

Is brake fade a permanent problem? Why do you say that?

4. The African Continent

Read the passage, then answer all questions using evidence from the text.

Reading

Seventy percent of the Earth’s surface is sea. Even though not always separated by sea, the larger landmasses are regarded as being made up of seven continents. One of the largest of these, with a total area of 3.5 million sq km (one fifth of the earth’s landmass), is Africa. It is surpassed only by Asia. The African continent stretches nearly 8 100 km from around latitude 35° north of the equator to nearly 35° south.

More than 680 million people live in Africa. This is more than double the total population of the United States of America and Canada combined. Apart from some regions with a low population density, the overall population of this continent is rapidly expanding. Regardless of some of the less developed African countries having a high infant mortality rate and limited life expectancy through disease, famine or war, it has a growth rate of 3%. Although the Sahara Desert occupies one fourth of Africa’s landmass, in those areas that can support agriculture, the population density is closer to the world average.

Do you think that Africa is regarded as a densely populated continent? Why do you say that?
