The *P--6 Curriculum Planning Model* (P--6CPM) for Australian Curriculum
V9 English provides a tailored approach to curriculum organisation,
supporting systematic and synchronous curriculum implementation in
multilevel contexts across Prep to Year 6. This flexible model draws on
level descriptions, text types and targeted achievement standard aspects
to streamline content and contextualise units and assessments,
integrating the strands. Its cyclic design helps sustain engagement and
relevance across different contexts.

Unit 1 focuses on 'adapting familiar stories' as a common theme. Unit
contexts across successive years may explore a range of literary texts,
for example:

- Narrative text topics such as family or life-like contexts, imaginary
  creatures, futuristic or fantasy worlds, social issues, historical or
  cultural contexts.

- A selection of classic and contemporary narrative texts.

- Literature of wide-ranging Australian and world authors, including
  that of First Nations Australians and texts from and about Asia.

The P--6CPM includes supplementary resources to support implementation
in a range of schools including small schools and larger schools with
complex and/or multilevel contexts.

This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement
standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform
decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will
engage with the task.

Assessment conventions and resources

**Purpose:** To read, view, comprehend and compare imaginative narrative
texts. To create a written and/or multimodal imaginative adaptation of a
narrative.

**Technique:** Extended response.

Conditions:

- Individual work.

- Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

- Adapt the mode and conditions to suit the diverse learning needs of
  your students and school context.

- Suggested length: Length of student responses should be considered in
  the context of the assessment (informed by QCAA guidelines).

Part A: Reading and viewing

- Students independently read an excerpt (unread) from an imaginative
  text.

- Students provide short written responses.

Part C: Writing and creating

- This assessment may take place over multiple lessons.

Implementation advice

- Adapt this assessment task to suit the diverse needs of the students,
  school and local context.

- When adapting the assessment, maintain alignment to the targeted
  achievement standard aspects and aligned curriculum elements and
  consider ways to make the assessment meaningful and accessible for
  students.

- Answer spaces have not been included to provide flexibility in how and
  where students will respond.

- Each part of the summative assessment may be conducted as a series of
  smaller tasks contributing to a collection of work to be used to
  gather evidence of student learning against the marking guide.

- Assessment offers tasks across varying language modes -- listening,
  speaking, reading, viewing and writing. Teachers can adapt tasks to
  allow students to demonstrate the full extent of what they know,
  understand and can do in multiple ways. This approach ensures
  accessibility for varied learning needs and supports students' unique
  ways of seeing, hearing, and/or processing language.

Part A: Reading and viewing

Students read, view, comprehend and compare imaginative texts.

Instructions

- Select a written imaginative narrative text with a social, cultural or
  historical context that develops ideas in the story through
  characters, settings and events. This could be a novel or short story.

- Select an appropriate text that students have not read to read
  independently, for example: a chapter of a narrative or a complete
  short story. The selected text should support text comprehension and
  be long enough for students to demonstrate their reading ability.
  Ensure an appropriate level of text complexity.

- If engaging with a shared class novel, before administering the task,
  students read/engage with the narrative up to the selected text
  portion as part of teaching and learning in this unit of work and then
  independently read the selected portion.

- Provide students with individual copies of the text selection and
  questions 1--5 in Part A.

- Students independently read the selected text and answer questions
  1--5.

- For students with mild reading difficulties and who struggle to read
  curriculum text, adjustments to access the assessment task can be
  provided. This might include providing more time, enlarging the text,
  annotating the text or chunking the text into smaller parts.

- For individual students with significant reading disorders who are
  unable to independently read, more significant adjustments may be
  required in consultation with specialists, support staff, students and
  caregivers.

- Provide students with an alternate imaginative narrative text that
  reflects the same context to read or view (as whole class, in a small
  group or independently). This could be another print text or a digital
  text. If using a digital text, consider how students will access for
  repeated viewing.

- After the students read and view both texts, they respond to
  question 6. Remind them to re-read and annotate relevant sections of
  the text(s) to support their responses.

**Note:** Teachers should adapt the task to align with the language used
in the teaching and learning sequence, to ensure continuity between
teaching and assessment.

Part B: Speaking and listening (monitoring strategy)

- Guide students to discuss the narrative and how they could adapt plot
  structure, characters, settings and/or ideas, with a partner or small
  group.

- Guide students to tell their narrative ideas.

- Observe students' interaction skills and students' sharing of a
  narrative experimenting with ideas from the original text.

Part C: Writing and creating

Students adapt the familiar written imaginative narrative text using the
same characters and settings and reflecting author style.

When students are planning their written adaptation, they need to
consider:

- author's purpose (consider if the author had a message or idea they
  wanted to share) and style (consider the author's distinctive language
  choices)

- existing social, cultural and/or historical context, characters and
  settings

- cohesive and innovative elements (for example: rising and falling
  action, plot tension, non-linear plot structure, shifts in time).

Before students begin writing, remind them to use appropriate text
structures, language features and literary devices.

Students may also choose to include multimodal elements as an optional
aspect.

Teachers may choose to provide students with any of the following
optional materials to support planning:

- **Sheet --- Narrative adaptation planning table** to assist students
  in planning their narrative.

- **Sheet --- Narrative adaptation plot profile** to assist students in
  planning their narrative.

Students use their planning to **create** their narrative.

Prompt students to re-read and **edit** their narrative for precision
using negotiated criteria.

Students **publish** their narrative (optional). They may choose to
include multimodal elements.

Part A: Reading and viewing

After you have independently read the narrative text provided by your
teacher, answer questions 1--5.

**Audience and purpose**

1.  Who is the audience for this narrative? Explain why you think this.

2.  What is the author's purpose/s in writing the narrative? (Consider
    if the story is just meant to entertain and tell a good story or if
    the author is trying to teach the audience something or persuade
    them on an issue.)

**Context and setting**

3.  Describe the setting and context (social, historical, cultural) of
    the narrative using examples from the text. (For example: During
    which historical event does the narrative take place? What is the
    social or cultural context?)

**Author style**

4.  What are some characteristics of the author's individual style? (For
    example: Does the author use vocabulary, narrative voice, patterns
    of language, mood, humour or idioms suited to the particular
    context?)

**Characters and events**

5.  a\. Who is a main character in the narrative?

<!-- -->

b.  Summarise their actions during the events of the narrative text
    provided by your teacher.

c.  Why has the character taken these actions?

d.  How do their actions reflect the context and develop ideas of the
    narrative?

**Read, view and comprehend** a second imaginative narrative text and
answer the following question comparing the two texts.

**Similarities and differences**

6.  Compare the texts by identifying similarities and differences.

In your response, you could consider some or all of the following:

- how the texts present and develop ideas about the same context through
  similar or different characters, settings or events

- how the same historical event and/or cultural context is portrayed
  through the point of view of different characters

- how the social relationships between characters are similar or
  different

- how the two authors have similar or different styles of writing or
  presenting

- how the authors' styles, use of language, narrative structure and/or
  point of view (narrative voice) evoke a similar or different response
  from the reader/viewer regarding the character or events.

Part B: Speaking and listening

- Discuss with a partner or small group how you could write your own
  imaginative narrative by adapting plot structure, characters, settings
  and/or ideas and experimenting with literary devices from the
  narrative.

- Tell your narrative.

Part C: Writing and creating

You will plan, create, and edit an adaptation of a familiar written
imaginative narrative text. This might be a prequel, a sequel, an
alternative ending, a short story or a different adventure for the same
characters.

1.  **Plan an adaptation**

Record notes and details about the actions of characters, the setting
and plot for your adaptation. Also consider how you will engage the
author's original audience, support their purpose and reflect their
style.

You might like to use the **Sheet --- Narrative adaptation planning
table (optional)** or **Sheet --- Narrative adaptation plot profile
(optional)** to organise your ideas and assist your planning.

Remember to:

- plan an adaptation in which the main character or characters respond
  differently to events in the novel

- use the existing context, characters and settings from the narrative,
  reflecting the original author's style, purpose and audience

- create a series of events in your adaptation that will lead to a new
  complication and/or resolution that addresses the author's original
  purpose and is directed to the same audience

- experiment with literary devices (for example: narrative structure,
  flashbacks and shifts in time, characterisation and setting,
  rhetorical devices, quoted/reported speech and figurative language)

- consider also using ideas from other texts you have read or viewed.

2.  **Draft the adaptation**

Consider how you could use:

- text structures including:

<!-- -->

- paragraphs with supporting detail to logically sequence ideas

- elaborated events such as flashbacks, flashforwards and shifts in time

- cohesive devices such as repetition, synonyms and antonyms, and word
  associations to link ideas and to alert the reader about how the story
  is unfolding

<!-- -->

- language features, including:

<!-- -->

- a variety of sentence types, including complex sentences with embedded
  clauses

- nouns, expanded noun groups, adjectives and adjective groups for
  fuller descriptions

- expanded verb groups, adverbs and adverb group/phrases, and elaborated
  tenses

- topic-specific and vivid vocabulary, words and phrases suited to the
  context

- punctuated dialogue

<!-- -->

- literary devices, for example:

<!-- -->

- characterisation and setting

- emotive vocabulary, rhetorical devices and figurative language such as
  metaphors, similes, personification, idioms, imagery and hyperbole.

In your adaptation:

- keep the characters, context, purpose, audience and style of your
  adaptation the same as in the narrative

- ensure the events in which the characters are involved are new and
  lead to a new complication and/or conclusion

- experiment with text structures, language features, literary devices
  and narrative point of view to create a cohesive adaptation.

3.  **Review and edit your draft** for precision, including:

- text structure and meaning

- accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation.

4.  **Publish your text** (optional). You may include multimodal
    elements such as illustrations.
