| **Overview of Unit** | Students engage with a variety of texts for enjoyment including picture books, stories and films. They participate in shared reading, viewing and storytelling of texts that expand and reflect their world and involve straightforward sequences of events and everyday happenings. Texts may include traditional oral narratives and literature of First Nations Australians.  Students make connections between characters, settings and events, and link ideas to personal experiences.  Students use these texts as models to retell events from a story and share preferences. | | | | | Students engage with a variety of texts including the oral narrative traditions and literature of First Nations Australians through picture books, stories, short films, animations and texts written by Australian and world authors. Sequences of events and everyday happenings with recognisable characters are evident in these texts.  Students discuss features of stories including plot, characters and settings, and make connections with their own experiences.  Students use these texts as models to adapt a familiar story and share and retell events. | | |

| **Prep** READING & VIEWING (Monitoring Strategy) • The names of letters in the English alphabet.  • The most common sounds (phonemes) represented by letters (basic GPCs).  • That letters make sounds which help us read and write.  • Stories have characters, settings and events.  • Stories follow a sequence: beginning → middle → end.  • Words and images work together to communicate meaning. SPEAKING & LISTENING • Stories can be retold using oral language.  • Story retells must include characters, settings and sequenced events.  • Opinions include likes/dislikes and reasons.  • Listeners follow speaking and listening rules (turn-taking, facing the speaker, speaking clearly).  • Stories can be changed or continued using imagination. WRITING & CREATING • Stories can be retold using drawings and emergent writing.  • Drawings communicate characters, ideas and events.  • Letters represent sounds and can be used in writing attempts.  • Writing moves left to right and uses spaces between words.  • Known words support meaning in texts.  **Year 1** READING & VIEWING (Monitoring Strategy) • Know letter–sound relationships: short vowels, common long vowels, consonants, and familiar digraphs (sh, ch, th, wh).  • Know that blending sounds helps read one-syllable words.  • Know common letter patterns in one- and two-syllable words (-ing, -ack, -est, -ish).  • Know a growing bank of high-frequency words.  • Know sentence boundary punctuation: capital letters, full stops, question marks.  • Know that punctuation helps phrasing and fluency.  • Know and use self-correction strategies when reading. SPEAKING & LISTENING • Know story elements: characters, setting, problem, solution.  • Know how to retell a story in sequence.  • Know words and phrases used in the text to include in retells.  • Know basic interaction skills: listening, taking turns, responding.  • Know that opinions need reasons supported with simple details.  • Know that familiar stories can be adapted or changed. WRITING & CREATING • Know the purpose of a recount (to retell events in order).  • Know that recounts include characters, setting, and events in sequence.  • Know how to plan ideas using drawings or key words.  • Know features of simple sentences.  • Know that sentences start with a capital letter and end with a full stop.  • Know that proper nouns require capital letters.  • Know basic connectives: and, then, next.  • Know that descriptive language adds detail. | | | | | **Prep**  Reading & viewing (Monitoring Strategy  Name letters when shown (upper and lower case).  • Say the common sounds for taught letters.  • Identify the main character, setting, and key events.  • Describe story events in order.  • Use phonic knowledge to recognise familiar words. SPEAKING & LISTENING • Retell a familiar story including beginning, middle and end.  • Share thoughts and preferences with a reason.  • Talk about how a story could change or what might happen next.  • Engage in partner talk using active listening skills.  • Report key ideas to an audience. WRITING & CREATING • Plan a retell using drawings of the beginning, middle and end.  •Sequence drawings to show order of events.  • Create a short retell using images and known words/sound attempts.  • Use letter–sound knowledge in emergent writing.  • ‘Read back’ their text to assign meaning.  **Year 1** READING & VIEWING (Monitoring Strategy) • Blend vowel and consonant sounds to decode one-syllable words.  • Read one- and two-syllable words with familiar patterns.  • Automatically read high-frequency words during connected text reading.  • Use punctuation cues to pause or stop when reading aloud.  • Monitor their reading for meaning and attempt self-corrections.  • Read a decodable text aloud with developing fluency. SPEAKING & LISTENING • Retell a familiar story including characters, setting, events, problem, and solution.  • Share simple opinions using a reason linked to the story.  • Suggest adaptations or what might happen next in the story.  • Use vocabulary from the text during oral retell.  • Participate using active listening and turn-taking skills. WRITING & CREATING • Plan ideas using drawings or a planning sheet.  • Write a recount including beginning, events in order, problem, and solution.  • Write simple sentences using correct punctuation.  • Use story vocabulary, pronouns, and connectives to link ideas.  • Add descriptive details using noun and verb groups.  • Re-read and edit writing for meaning, spelling, and punctuation.  • Add illustrations or multimodal elements if appropriate. | | | |
