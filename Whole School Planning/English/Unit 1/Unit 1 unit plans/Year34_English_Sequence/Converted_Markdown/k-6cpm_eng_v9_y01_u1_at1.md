| Unit 1: Examining and creating imaginative literary texts |
| Assessment task 1 — Responding to literature☼ |

The K–6 Curriculum Planning Model (K–6CPM) for Australian Curriculum V9 English provides a tailored approach to curriculum organisation, supporting systematic and synchronous curriculum implementation in multilevel contexts across Prep to Year 6. This flexible model draws on level descriptions, text types and targeted achievement standard aspects to streamline content and contextualise units and assessments, integrating the strands. Its cyclic design helps sustain engagement and relevance across different contexts.

Unit 1 focuses on ‘adapting familiar stories’ as a common theme. Unit contexts across successive years may explore a range of literary texts, for example:

* narrative text topics such as family or life-like contexts, imaginary creatures, futuristic or fantasy worlds, social issues, historical or cultural contexts
* a selection of classic and contemporary narrative texts
* literature of wide-ranging Australian and world authors, including that of First Nations Australians and texts from and about Asia.

The K–6CPM includes supplementary resources to support implementation in a range of schools including small schools and larger schools with complex and/or multilevel contexts.

This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will engage with the task.

Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To retell or adapt a familiar story using oral language and create a written and/or multimodal recount of a story.

Technique: Observed demonstration (Part B); Extended response (Part C).

Conditions:

* Individual work.
* Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.
* Suggested length for Part B: Up to 45 seconds (informed by QCAA guidelines).
* Suggested length for Part C: Up to 50 words (informed by QCAA guidelines).
* May be completed over multiple lessons (Part B and Part C).

Implementation advice

* Adapt this assessment task to suit the diverse needs of the students, school and local context.
* When adapting the assessment, maintain alignment to the targeted achievement standard aspects and aligned curriculum elements and consider ways to make the assessment meaningful and accessible for students.
* Answer spaces have not been included to provide flexibility in how and where students will respond.
* Each part of the summative assessment may be conducted as a series of smaller tasks contributing to a collection of work to be used to gather evidence of student learning against the marking guide.
* The assessment offers tasks across varying language modes – listening, speaking, reading, viewing and writing. Teachers can adapt tasks to allow students to demonstrate the full extent of what they know, understand and can do in multiple ways. This approach ensures accessibility for varied learning needs and supports students’ unique ways of seeing, hearing, and/or processing language.

Part A: Reading and viewing (monitoring strategy)

Before administering the task, students engage with texts about the topic to build relevant background knowledge. Background knowledge texts can be read to the students.

**Instructions**

* Select a decodable text that aligns with the explicitly taught phonics scope and sequence program and students’ phonic development. For example, consider texts available at [Decodable texts](https://det-school.eq.edu.au/reading/phonics/decodable-texts) on the [Reading through the Australian Curriculum](https://det-school.eq.edu.au/reading) hub.
* Ask students to:
* independently read aloud the decodable text. Observe students:
* blending short vowels, common long vowels, consonants and digraphs to read one-syllable words
* reading one- and two-syllable words with common letter patterns
* reading high-frequency words (high-frequency words will depend on your context)
* using sentence boundary punctuation to support phrasing and fluency
* monitor their own reading and, if needed, use learnt self-correction strategies. Use the **Teacher recording sheet (optional)** (provided) and the monitoring strategy.

Part B: Speaking and listening

Students retell key events in a familiar story using oral language and express opinions about characters and events in the text.

Establish and communicate to students who the audience will be, for example: teacher, peers or younger students.

**Instructions**

* Select an authentic familiar imaginative story that includes a clear main character, setting and events to read to the class.
* Ask students to retell the events in order. Remind students to use words and phrases from the story to talk about characters and plot, including the beginning, how the problem is introduced and how the problem is solved. Students might do this as a puppet show or role-play.
* Provide students with informal opportunities to express their opinion about the text by sharing ideas with a partner or small group. Guide students with **Sheet — Active listening (optional)** (provided) and observe students’ interaction skills (monitoring strategy).
* Ask students to share:
* things they liked and disliked about the story, for example: ‘Did you like or dislike the story? Use details from the story to give a reason.’
* ideas about how they might adapt the story, for example: ‘What would you change?’; ‘What might happen next?’

Part C: Writing and creating

Students create a short recount of a familiar story including events and characters.

Establish and communicate to students who the audience will be, for example: teacher, peers or younger students.

**Instructions**

* Select an authentic familiar imaginative story that includes a clear main character, setting and events to read to the class.
* Invite students to write a recount of the events in the story in order, including the beginning, problem and solution.

Guide students to:

* plan their ideas, optionally using the **Sheet — Story recount (optional)** (provided). Remind students to think about the characters, setting and events
* create their recount, ensuring they:
* write in complete sentences
* start sentences with a capital letter and finish them with a full stop
* use a capital letter for proper nouns
* include ideas and words from the story
* connect ideas with connectives (for example: ‘and’, ‘then’) or use pronouns when sequencing ideas
* use descriptive language such as noun and verb groups
* re-read to edit their sentences and make sure they make sense, add or delete words to improve meaning, and check spelling
* add a multimodal element to their recount (optional).

Part A: Reading and viewing

Read a text aloud to your teacher.

Part B: Speaking and listening

Listen to a story read by your teacher. Your teacher will ask questions about the story.

1. **Retell** the story including details about the characters, setting and events in order.

Remember to talk about:

Who are the characters?

What is the setting? Was there more than one setting?

What was the main problem?

What was the solution?

1. What did you like or dislike about the story? Explain why.

**Talk** with a partner.

1. What do you think might happen next?
2. What would you change in the story?

Part C: Writing and creating

Write a recount of the story you have listened to.

1. Plan your ideas using the story recount planner.
2. Create your recount. Remember to:

use your plan

write in complete sentences

start your sentences with a capital letter and finish them with a full stop

use a capital letter for proper nouns

include ideas and words from the story

connect ideas with connectives (for example: ‘and’, ‘then’) or use pronouns when sequencing ideas

use descriptive language such as noun and verb groups.

1. **Re-read** and **edit** your work. Remember to:

read your sentences to check they make sense

think of any words you could add, remove or change to improve meaning

check your spelling and punctuation.
