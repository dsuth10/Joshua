This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will engage with the task.

The assessment task gathers evidence of student learning for these aspects of the Year 3 English achievement standard:

Reading and viewing

Read, view and comprehend texts, recognising their purpose and audience.

Identify literal meaning and explain inferred meaning.

Describe how stories are developed through characters and/or events.

Read fluently, using phonic, morphemic and grammatical knowledge to read multisyllabic words with more complex letter patterns.*

Speaking and listening

Interact with others, and listen to and create spoken and/or multimodal texts including stories.*

Writing and creating

Create written and/or multimodal texts including stories to inform, narrate, explain or argue for audiences, relating ideas including relevant details from learnt topics, topics of interest or texts.

Use text structures including paragraphs, and language features including compound sentences, topic-specific vocabulary and literary devices, and/or visual features.

*Denotes monitoring strategy within the task.

## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To read, view and comprehend an imaginative text. To create a written and/or multimodal imaginative text using or adapting ideas from a familiar narrative.

Technique: Short response (Part A); Extended response (Part C).

Conditions:

Individual work.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Students independently read a passage (unread) from an imaginative text (Part A).

Suggested length for Part C: Between 100–200 words (informed by QCAA guidelines).

Provide lesson time for drafting with access to teacher feedback and conferencing.

May be completed over multiple lessons (Part B and Part C).

## Implementation advice

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Before administering the task, students engage with texts about the topic to build relevant background knowledge. Background knowledge texts can be read to the students.

Instructions

Select an unfamiliar short story or passage of text that develops ideas in the story through characters, settings and events. Ensure an appropriate level of text complexity.

Ask students to:

independently read aloud the selected passage of text (approximately 1–2 minutes). Observe students:

using 	fluency, phonic, morphemic and grammatical knowledge to read multisyllabic words with more complex letter patterns

monitor their own reading and, if needed, use re-reading and self-correcting.

Record observations, including prompts, using the Teacher recording sheet (optional) (provided) and the monitoring strategy.

Students answer questions provided.

## Part B: Speaking and listening (monitoring strategy)

Provide students time to plan how they could adapt the structures or language features in the story, for example: character/s, setting/s, events or dialogue.

Provide students opportunities to discuss their adaptation ideas with a partner or small group.

Observe students’ interaction skills, for example: using collaborative language, affirming other speakers and building on their comments, asking relevant questions and providing useful feedback.

Students tell their story to support their written text.

## Part C: Writing and creating

Students create a text, using or adapting text structures and/or language features, for example: characters, characters’ dialogue, settings or events from a shared narrative.

Instructions

Guide students to:

plan their ideas, optionally using the Sheet — Story planner (optional) (provided)

make notes about the decisions and actions of their characters, setting/s and plot.

Remind students:

to consider audience and purpose

they can use ideas, context, vocabulary or style from the narrative as a model

to write in simple paragraphs

to 	re-read and edit to improve meaning and structure.

Guide students to add a multimodal element to their story using audio or digital tools, for example: image generator to create characters and settings for their story (optional).

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Read the text provided by your teacher and answer the questions.

Purpose and audience

What is the purpose of this text?

Who is the audience for this text?

Find evidence in the text to support your answer. Consider the words, images and text structure.

Characters, settings and events

Name and describe the main character/s.

What events in the story help us learn more about the characters?

How do the characters’ actions make the story interesting or exciting?

What important things do the characters do that change what happens next in the story?

Describe what happened in the story. Use the author’s words and other details from the text in your descriptions.

Choose an image from the text that you think adds meaning for the reader. Describe how the image gives more information about the characters, setting or events.

Think about the language the author uses to describe the characters and events in the story.

Find two examples and explain how these affect the mood of the story.

For example, ‘The child skipped merrily’ makes the mood playful and happy because skipping is a fun activity and ‘merrily’ means happy. These words make the reader feel happy too.

## Part B: Speaking and listening

Discuss with a partner or small group how you could adapt this story by changing the structure or language features, for example: a character or character’s dialogue, the setting or the events.

Share your story.

## Part C: Writing and creating

Create your own story adapting an idea from the story you read.

You might change the structure of the story and/or language features, for example:

characters

what the characters say to each other (dialogue)

setting

events

new character’s perspective.

- Plan your ideas using a story planner.

What will you change in the story?

What is the problem?

How will you sequence the events?

How will the problem be resolved?

Create your story

Use your planning to help write your story.

When writing be sure to:

engage the audience with interesting details and events

include an orientation, complication and resolution

include interesting words to describe the mood, characters and setting (detailed descriptions, adjectives, noun groups)

use simple and compound sentences to express and combine ideas

group ideas in paragraphs

use consistent tense (happened in the past; is happening now)

link your ideas, sentences and paragraphs using:

pronouns (she/he/it)

conjunctions (and, but, because, so)

time-order words (first, next, then, finally) to sequence events.

Re-read and edit your work. Remember to:

read your sentences to check they make sense

think of any words you could add, remove or change to improve meaning

check your spelling and punctuation.

Publish and illustrate (optional).

Bring your story to life by including an illustration (for example: using digital tools or drawing).
