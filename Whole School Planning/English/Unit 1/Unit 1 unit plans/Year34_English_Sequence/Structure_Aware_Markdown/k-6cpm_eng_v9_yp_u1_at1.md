| Assessment task 1 — Responding to literature☼ |

This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will engage with the task.

The assessment task gathers evidence of student learning for these aspects of the Prep English achievement standard:

Reading and viewing

Name the letters of the English alphabet and know and use the most common sounds (phonemes) represented by these letters (graphs).*

Speaking and listening

Listen to texts, interact with others and create short spoken texts, including retelling stories.*

Share thoughts and preferences, retell events and report information or key ideas to an audience.

Writing and creating

Create short written texts, including retelling stories using words and images where appropriate.

Retell, report information and state their thoughts, feelings and key ideas.

*Denotes monitoring strategy within the task.

The following materials can be used when implementing Unit 1: Examining and creating imaginative texts in the K–6CPM English Year level plan example.

See the English Unit 1 Curriculum and assessment alignment planner (CAAP) to guide unit planning when developing a teaching and learning sequence and implementing the assessment in the unit.

## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To retell a familiar imaginative text using oral language, images and beginning writing knowledge.

Technique: Observed demonstration.

Conditions:

Individual work.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Suggested length for Part B: Length of student responses should be considered in the context of the assessment (informed by QCAA guidelines).

May be completed over multiple lessons (Part B and Part C).

## Part B: Speaking and listening

Students retell a familiar authentic story using oral language and share thoughts and preferences about characters and events in the text.

Establish and communicate to students who the audience will be, for example: teacher, peers or younger students.

Instructions

Select an authentic familiar imaginative story that includes a clear main character, setting and events to read to the class.

Ask students to retell the events in order. Remind students to use words and phrases from the story to talk about characters and key events from the beginning, middle and end. This may be done orally or as a puppet show or role-play.

Provide students with informal opportunities to share ideas about their chosen story with a partner or small group. Guide students with Sheet — Active listening (optional) (provided) and observe students’ interaction skills (monitoring strategy).

Ask students to share:

thoughts and preferences about the story, for example: ‘What did you think of this story?’; ‘Did you like or dislike the story? Use details from the story to give a reason.’

how they might adapt the story, for example: ‘What would you change?’

## Part C: Writing and creating

Students use images and beginning writing knowledge, sound–letter relationships and known words where appropriate to create a short text to retell a familiar story.

Establish and communicate to students who the audience will be, for example: teacher, peers or younger students.

Instructions

Invite students to use sticky notes or separate sheets of paper to draw beginning, middle and end ideas or events of the shared familiar imaginative story read to them.

Guide students to plan the order of events using Sheet — Planning – Sequencing template (optional) (provided) to retell the story, including characters and events. Support students to include key ideas from the beginning, middle and end.

Support students to add any known words or sound–letter relationships to their images.

Invite students to assign a message to their own text by ‘reading back’ their own attempts at writing.

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Your teacher will ask you to name some letters of the alphabet and say the sounds.

## Part B: Speaking and listening

Listen to a story read by your teacher. Your teacher will ask questions about the story.

Retell the story including details about the characters, setting and events in order.

Remember to talk about:

the main character

where was the story set

events at the beginning, the middle and the end of the story.

Did you like or dislike the story? Explain why.

Talk with a partner.

What do you think might happen next?

What would you change in the story?

## Part C: Writing and creating

Create a retell about the story.

- Create your retell by drawing events that happened in the story in order.

Add any words or sounds you know to your drawing.

Share your retell with your teacher.
