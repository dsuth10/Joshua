| Assessment task 1 — Responding to literature ☼ |

This assessment task is aligned to the aspects of the achievement standard being assessed. Individual school contexts will inform decisions about how the task is to be implemented and how students will engage with the task.

The assessment task gathers evidence of student learning for these aspects of the Year 4 English achievement standard:

Reading and viewing

Read, view and comprehend texts created to inform, influence and/or engage audiences.

Describe how ideas are developed including through characters and events, and how texts reflect contexts.

Read fluently and accurately, integrating phonic, morphemic, grammatical and punctuation knowledge.*

Speaking and listening

Interact with others, and listen to and create spoken and/or multimodal texts including stories.*

Writing and creating

Create written and/or multimodal texts including stories for purposes and audiences, where they develop ideas using details from learnt topics, topics of interest or texts.

Use paragraphs to organise and link ideas.

Use language features including complex sentences, topic-specific vocabulary and literary devices, and/or visual features.

*Denotes monitoring strategy within the task.

## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To read, view and comprehend an imaginative text. To create a written and/or multimodal imaginative text developing storylines, characters and settings.

Technique: Short response (Part A); Extended response (Part C).

Conditions:

Individual work.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Students independently read a passage (unread) from an imaginative text (Part A).

Suggested length for Part C: 100 to 200 words (informed by QCAA guidelines).

Provide lesson time for drafting with access to teacher feedback and conferencing (Part C).

Can be completed over multiple sessions (Part B and Part C).

## Implementation advice

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Before administering the task, students engage with texts about the topic to build relevant background knowledge. Background knowledge texts can be read to the students.

Instructions

Select a short story or portion of an unfamiliar passage of narrative text that has a prominent context and engaging plot tensions, characters and settings. Ensure an appropriate level of text complexity.

Ask students to:

independently read aloud from the text for the monitoring strategy (approximately 1–2 minutes). Observe students:

using fluency, integrating phonic, morphemic, grammatical and punctuation knowledge

monitor their own reading and, if needed, use re-reading and self-correcting.

Record observations, including prompts, using the Teacher recording sheet (optional) (provided) and the monitoring strategy.

Students answer questions provided.

## Part B: Speaking and listening (monitoring strategy)

Provide students time to plan how they could adapt the structure and/or language features in the story, for example: character/s, setting/s, events or dialogue.

Provide students opportunities to discuss their adaptation ideas with a partner or small group.

Observe students’ interaction skills, for example: listening for key information and contributing to discussions, acknowledging another opinion, sharing and extending ideas and information.

Students tell their story to support their written text.

## Part C: Writing and creating

Students create a text, using or adapting text structures and/or language features, for example: characters, settings, storylines from the shared narrative.

Instructions

Guide students to

plan their ideas, optionally using the Sheet — Narrative adaption planning table (optional) (provided)

identify where their imaginative adaptation fits within the events of the narrative.

Remind students to:

consider audience and purpose

use paragraphs and a variety of sentences to build tension

include topic-specific and precise vocabulary

to re-read and edit to improve meaning and structure.

Students may wish to add a multimodal element to their story using audio or digital tools, for example: image generator to create characters and settings for their story (optional).

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Read the text provided by your teacher and answer the questions.

Purpose and audience

What is the author’s purpose in writing the narrative?

Who is the audience for this narrative?

Context and settings

a.	What is the context of the text?

How do you know the narrative is set in this context? For example, characters’ clothing, behaviour, speech, setting, historical facts/events.

Characters and events

Name and describe the main character/s.

Summarise the narrative, connecting main events and actions of characters. Include important details from the text.

Identify a moment in the plot when a character is faced with choices. Explain how the author helps the reader care about the characters’ choices and the consequences. Include details from the text.

## Part B: Speaking and listening

- Discuss with a partner or small group how you could adapt this narrative by changing the structure or language features, for example: a character or character’s dialogue, the setting or the events.

- Share your story.

## Part C: Writing and creating

- Plan, create and edit an adaptation of the familiar narrative. This might be a new chapter or a different adventure for the same characters.

Record notes and details about the actions of characters, the setting and the plot for your text.

You might like to use a story planner to organise your ideas.

Remember to think about:

the purpose and audience

the existing context of the story

language choices and use of dialogue (both direct and indirect)

how you will build tension to engage the interest of the reader.

- Create your adaptation.

Use your planning to guide your story development.

- Use text structures to organise your ideas:

- narrative text structure

- paragraphs with logically sequenced ideas and supporting details

- cohesive devices such as text connectives and pronoun referencing.

- Use language features to connect and extend your ideas and create richer descriptions of characters and settings:

- a variety of simple, compound and complex sentences

- noun, verb and adverb groups

- adverbial clauses to add information about an action or event, for example: ‘how?’, ‘when?’, ‘where?’ or ‘why?’

- adjectival clauses to add detail about nouns

- dialogue

- precise and topic-specific vocabulary.

- Use consistent tense and correct punctuation, including quotation marks for dialogue.

Review and edit your writing for:

text structure and meaning

accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation.

Publish your narrative (optional). You may include multimodal elements such as illustrations.
