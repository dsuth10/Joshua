  - Column 2: Statistics: Conduct guided statistical investigations 1.	Consolidate prior knowledge by collecting, recording and representing data. Discuss that data is all around us and explain what data is, why it is collected and how it is gathered. Revise different ways to collect, record and represent categorical data, for example: planning and conducting a range of observations, experiments and surveys; recording responses in tally charts and lists; representing data in one-to-one column graphs, picture graphs, dot plots, sticker charts and bar charts. Access external data sources, for example: weather records in Australia and neighbouring countries or seasonal calendars from Aboriginal peoples and Torres Strait Islander peoples. 2.	Develop knowledge of collecting, recording, representing and interpreting categorical and discrete numerical data. Engage in class discussions to consider a question of interest, pose an investigative question and discuss what kind of data can be used to help answer the question. As a class, discuss methods used to collect data, for example: survey the class with the investigative question and record data in a tally chart; observe the different types of insects in the playground and record as a list. As a class, use chosen methods to collect and record data. Represent data in a data display, for example: one-to-one column graphs, picture graphs, dot plots, sticker charts and bar charts. As a class, interpret the data by answering questions and making simple inferences. As a class, use findings to answer the question posed.
  - Column 3: Statistics: Representing data and shape of distribution 1.	Deepen understanding of data displays by assessing the suitability of displays for representing data. Identify the best methods of representing data to illustrate the results of investigations. Suggest questions that can be answered by a given data display and use the display to answer these questions. Compare different student generated diagrams, tables and graphs, describing their similarities and differences and commenting on the usefulness of each representation for interpreting the data. 2.	Deepen understanding and discuss the shape of distributions and variation in data. Explore different data representations, discussing the shape of distribution and variation in data, for example: the number of peaks and how the data is spread out. Compare the heights of columns in a simple column graph to determine the tallest and recognise this as the most frequent response. Read the values and interpret the representations, noting key features and any patterns or trends.
  - Column 4: Statistics: Line graphs 1.	Deepen understanding and interpret line graphs. Introduce the method of displaying different data types using line graphs. Understand that line graphs are used to show changes that happen over a period of time. Discuss the relationships that are represented, for example: how the horizontal axis represents measures of time such as days of the week or times of the day, and the vertical axis represents numerical quantities or ordinal categorical variables such as percentages, money, measurements or ratings such as fire hazard ratings. Interpret real-life data represented in a line graph, making inferences. 2.	Deepen understanding and discuss the relationships that are represented and conclusions that can be made from line graphs. Match unlabelled line graphs to the context they represent.
  - Column 5: Statistics: Arguments presented in the media 1.	Identify statistically informed arguments presented in the media. Investigate data representations in the media and discuss what they illustrate. Identify potentially misleading data representations in the media, for example: a scale not starting at zero, graphs with broken axes or non-linear scales, not using uniform intervals on the axes, graphics not drawn to scale, data not related to the population about which the claims are made and pie charts in which the whole pie does not represent the entire population about which the claims are made. 2.	Critique data representations and arguments presented in the media. Scrutinise reports, graphs and secondary data presented in the media, for example: evaluate different data representations, both useful and those that misrepresent information, mislead or are inaccurate. Analyse statistical information and describe the positive and negative; consider why the statistical data was used in the media.
