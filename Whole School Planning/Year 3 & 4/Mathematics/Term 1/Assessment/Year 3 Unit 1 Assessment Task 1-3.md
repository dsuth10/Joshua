## Part A: Collect, represent and interpret data about the number of colours

	**Discrete numerical dat****a** involves numbers or amounts with a count, for example: how many pets people own. 

	If required, use an alternative question for the guided statistical investigation about discrete numerical data to suit your context and make it an authentic task for students, for example: ‘How many pets do you have: none, 1, 2, 3, 4 or more?’; ‘How many sports do you play: none, 1, 2, 3, 4 or more?’; ‘How many days of the week do you ride your bike: none, 1, 2, 3, 4 or more?’

## Part B: Collect, represent and interpret data about choice of colour

	**Categorical data** involves values or observations that can be sorted into groups or categories, for example: eye colour, choices of sandwich fillings. 

	If required, use an alternative question for the guided statistical investigation about categorical data to suit your context and make it an authentic task for students, for example: ‘What type of pets do you own: dog, cat, bird, fish or other?’; ‘What sports do you play: tennis, swimming, soccer, netball or other?’; ‘Where do you ride your bike: at home, in the park, at the bike track or other?’

	In Question 1, remind students to include the same number of colours (categories) as identified in the results of the statistical investigation in Part A. For example, if five colours was chosen the most in Part A, then five colours should be included in Question 1.

## Part A: Collect, represent and interpret data about the number of colours

## UNDERSTAND

Your class is going to create a mural for your school. A mural is a colourful artwork. 

You need to find out how many colours your classmates want to use in the mural. 

## PLAN

You are going to survey at least 15 people and ask them the question: ‘How many colours should we use to create our mural: 2, 3, 4 or 5?’

	You are going to record your data using tally marks in the table on **Sheet ****—**** Data table 1**. Record a **title** for your table.

## DO

Ask **at least 15** people the question and use tally marks (||||) to **record** the data in the table: 

	Count the tally marks and record the total number.

	Make a column graph to show your data on **Sheet ****—**** Column graph**. Remember to include a **title and**** ****labels**.

	Look at your graph to help answer the following questions.

	How many people want to use three colours?

	What number of colours was chosen the most?

	What number of colours was chosen the least?

## CONSIDER and COMMUNICATE

My data shows that we should use ____ colours for the mural.

## Part B: Collect, represent and interpret data about choice of colour

## UNDERSTAND

In Part A, you conducted a survey to find out the number of colours people want to use to create the mural.

Now, you need to find out what colours people want to use in the mural.

## PLAN

You are going to survey at least 15 people and ask them your question.

- Complete the survey question:

You are going to record your data using tally marks in the table on **Sheet ****—**** Data table 2**. Record a **title** for your table.

## DO

Ask **at least 15** people your survey question and use tally marks (||||) to **record** the data in the table.

Count the tally marks and record the total number.

Make a picture graph to show your data on **Sheet — Picture graph**. Remember to include a **title and**** ****labels**.

	Look at your graph to help answer the following questions.

- 		How many people chose (blue)?

- 	What is the most popular colour?

- 	What is the least popular colour?

- 	How many more people chose the most popular colour compared to the least popular?

## CONSIDER and COMMUNICATE

My data shows that we should use the colour _______ the most in the mural.

If another Year 3 class conducted the same statistical investigation, would the data they collected be the same as your data? Explain your answer.

## Part C: Compare different data displays

Look at your column graph from Part A and your picture graph from Part B.

- 	What is the same about a column graph and a picture graph?

Answers will vary, for example:

	They both have a title.

	They both have categories.

	They both show collected data.

	They are both used to answer a question.

- 	What is different about a column graph and a picture graph?

Answers will vary, for example:

	The column graph uses numbers on the vertical (y-) axis to show how many for each category.

	The picture graph uses pictures to show how many for each category.

	You have to count the pictures to find out how many in each category in the picture graph. In the column graph, you can easily find out how many by looking at the number at the top of the column on the vertical (y-) axis.
