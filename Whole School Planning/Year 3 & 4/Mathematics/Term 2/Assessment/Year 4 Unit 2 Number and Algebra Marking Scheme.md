| Unit 2: Number and Algebra |

Use a monitoring strategy to collect evidence of how students are progressing against the targeted Year 4 Mathematics achievement standard aspect.

The targeted achievement standard aspect for this monitoring strategy is:

.

Ideas for collecting evidence

	The following activity examples are ideas that could be used to collect evidence towards the targeted achievement standard aspect. 

Students use strategies, diagrams and materials to record multiplication facts in different ways, for example: doubling and halving, and commutative, distributive and associative properties.

Students choose efficient calculation strategies when solving addition and subtraction problems, for example: using place value partitioning, inverse relationship, compatible numbers, jump strategies, bridging tens, splitting one or more numbers, extensions to basic facts, algorithms.

Students choose efficient calculation strategies when solving multiplication and division problems (no remainder), for example: doubling, halving, using known multiplication facts and commutative, distributive and associative properties.

Students represent multiplication facts using arrays to explain related division facts.

Play games involving the recall, recognition and explanation of multiplication and division facts.

Play games to show proficiency with addition and multiplication facts, for example: play Buzz with facts up to 10 × 10, or target number games where students take turns to apply an addition or multiplication rule to the number/s on rolled dice, trying to reach the target number first.

Students use online tools to check proficiency with addition and multiplication facts.

Supporting information	

	Monitoring strategies are flexible so teachers can identify the best way/s to implement as part of the teaching and learning sequence.

	Evidence of how a student is progressing against the targeted achievement standard aspect can be collected in a variety of ways during the teaching and learning sequence, such as observations, multimodal evidence, checklists and journaling.
