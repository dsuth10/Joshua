## Part B: Use mathematical modelling to create a design using arrays

	In Unit 2, students use the process of mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving multiplication (2s, 5s and 10s) when they:

understand the problem by making statements and wonderings (predictions and assumptions) and identifying information needed to help find the solution

plan how to solve the problem by representing the problem with diagrams, arrays, physical or virtual materials and formulating the problem using number sentences

do (solve) the problem using a range of chosen calculation strategies and showing all working including number sentences 

consider and communicate solutions by interpreting and sharing their results.

	For more information about the process of Mathematical modelling, see:

 (Mathematical modelling in multilevel contexts section)

## Implementing the mathematical modelling process

Understand the problem

Provide students with a ‘messy’ real-world problem involving single-digit multiplication and recalling multiplication facts for 2s, 5s, and 10s. For example:

| Our school is having a ‘Colourful flower garden’ competition. Design a flower garden and include: 	at least five different-coloured flowers 	flowers of the same colour arranged in arrays of 2s, 5s or 10s 	the number of flowers in each array using multiplication 	the number of flowers there are altogether in the garden design. How many flowers are needed for a garden design using arrays of 2s, 5s and 10s? |
| --- |

Consider your cohort and change the topic of the problem if needed. For example:

How many of each individual item do we need in gift bags for 2, 5 and 10 people? 

How many food and drink items are needed to cater for a picnic with 2, 5 and 10 people?

What is the best way to arrange students in rows of 2, 5 or 10 for a choir performance?

As a class, discuss the problem (not assessed), including statements and wonderings (predictions and assumptions). Support students to brainstorm questions and/or statements about the problem. Example prompt questions and sentence starters may include: 

What do you know about the problem? I know … I notice … 

What do you wonder about the problem? I wonder … 

What do you need to find out to solve the problem? If I knew …, then I could work out …

What maths can you use to solve this problem? What strategies can you use? I can use maths to …

Optional: Students may use digital tools to help plan and solve the problem. Opportunities to access and use digital tools may include: using a word-processing program or online drawing program; using virtual materials, for example: counters.

Plan how to solve the problem

Students plan how they will solve the problem, including making decisions, for example: what colour flowers, how big the garden will be, what the garden design will look like, how they will represent the flower arrays, how they could use digital tools (where appropriate).

Provide students with resources to support planning arrays and solving the problem. Physical or virtual resources may include grid paper, dot paper, counters, blocks, flower images.

Students develop their plan by representing the problem, for example: as diagrams, arrays, using materials (physical or virtual) and number sentences.

Students formulate and solve the problem using number sentences.

Do (solve) the problem

Encourage students to use a range of calculation strategies when solving the problem, showing all working.

After the assessment (not assessed)

When students have completed the assessment, extend the mathematical modelling process by having students share their solution with others.

Have a class discussion about the solutions the students arrived at and how mathematical modelling is a useful process for solving problems that have more than one answer. For example:

What were your solutions?

How did you work out your solutions? What maths did you use?

What do you notice about everyone’s solutions? Are there any similarities or differences?

How did your plan help you to work out your solution?

Is there another way you could have worked out your solution?

If you could plan another garden, what else would you include?
