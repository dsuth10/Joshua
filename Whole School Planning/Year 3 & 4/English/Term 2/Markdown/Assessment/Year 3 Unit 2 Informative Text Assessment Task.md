## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To read, view and comprehend an informative text and describe the text structures and language features and how visual features extend meaning. To create a written multimodal information report. To present an information report with visual features to an audience.

Technique: Short response (Part A); extended response (Part B); observed demonstration (Part C).

Conditions:

Administer the task individually with students.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Length of student responses should be considered in the context of the assessment and appropriate to the year level.

May be completed over multiple lessons.

## Implementation advice

## Part A: Reading and viewing

# Students identify the text structures, language and visual features of informative texts and how images extend the meaning of the text. They describe how these texts use language features and structures relevant to the purpose and audience.

Instructions

Ask students to:

independently read an informative text

answer questions about the informative text.

## Part B: Writing and creating

# Students create a written multimodal information report relating details from learnt topics or texts. They use appropriate text structures, language features and visual features that extend meaning.

Instructions

Establish the audience with students and guide them to select a topic.

Discuss how the information report will be structured.

Guide students to plan by gathering information about their topic from online and digital sources.

Students write their text. 	Guide them to use:

appropriate text structure and layout (spatial arrangement of print and graphics on a page or screen including size of font, positioning of illustrations, captions, labels, headings, dot points, borders and text boxes)

	facts and relevant details related to the topic grouped in simple paragraphs with topic sentences

	simple and compound sentences with a broader range of conjunctions (and, but, so, because, when)

	sequenced sentences

	adjectives to build description in noun groups/phrases

	mostly correct tense

	cohesive devices, for example: text connectives 

		topic-specific and technical vocabulary, for example: millimetre.

Guide students to select appropriate images, photographs and/or diagrams to extend meaning and information.

Prompt students to review and edit their text for meaning and spelling.

Students may use the Sheet — Planning (optional) to organise their ideas.

## Part C: Speaking and listening

	Confirm with students that while they may choose to use the same information, topic-specific vocabulary, sequence of ideas and visual elements from their written report, their presentation should be more than a reading of their written text. It should be enhanced with introductory and concluding statements, appropriate formality, features of voice and engaging visual features.

Instructions

Guide students to plan and create their presentation including:

an introduction

a clear structure with sequenced facts about the topic researched from sources

details to elaborate ideas and information

topic-specific and precise vocabulary

visual features (for example: images, diagrams, maps, props and costumes)

a conclusion.

Guide students to practise their presentation using features of voice to suit the audience and purpose, such as:

appropriate pace, pitch and pausing

varied volume and intonation to suit purpose and audience.

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Read the informative text provided by your teacher and answer the questions. You may need to re-read or scan parts of the text to respond to the questions.

Main ideas, purpose and audience

	What is the topic?

	Why might someone read this informative text?

	Who is the audience?

	What are the important ideas in the text? Include details in your answer.

Text structures, language features and visual features

	How is the informative text organised? What are the text structures?

	How do these structures help the reader find information? Give examples from the text.

	What are some language features and visual features that tell you this is an informative text?

	Describe how the author uses language features to give more information about the topic. (For example: noun groups and topic-specific vocabulary.) Give examples from the text.

	How do the visual features (for example: colour, shape and size of images) add meaning or give extra details about the topic? Give examples from the text.

## Part B: Writing and creating

You are going to plan, write and edit a multimodal information report for an identified audience on a topic you have learnt about.

- 	**Plan** your multimodal information report.

		Choose a topic.

		Gather facts from sources (for example: internet search, interview, observations, texts).

		Consider how you will publish the report and the layout (for example: arrangement of print and images, illustrations, captions, labels, headings, dot points, borders and text boxes).

		Consider the images you will include that extend the meaning of your text.

**Create** your text. Remember to use your planning to:

		use an appropriate text structure (for example: heading and subheadings)

	organise your facts in paragraphs that begin with a topic sentence

		write using simple and compound sentences (use conjunctions such as: and, but, so, because, when)

		use adjectives in noun groups/phrases to build descriptions

use 	correct tense

use 	text connectives (for example: and, then)

	include topic-specific and technical vocabulary

		include images that extend meaning and add information to your text.

**Review and edit** your work to improve spelling, meaning and organisation.

## Part C: Speaking and listening

You are going to present an information report to an audience using features of voice. You can use the information, sequence of ideas and visual features from your written text, but you will need to adapt any text or language features so that your spoken text flows. You may also include any new ideas, together with visual features that support your text and engage your audience.

- **Plan** and **create** your presentation.

Remember to include:

a clear structure with sequenced facts about the topic (for example: introduction, body and conclusion)

researched facts from sources

details to elaborate ideas and information

topic-specific and precise vocabulary

visual features (for example: video, images, slides, diagrams, maps, costumes and/or props).

**Practise** presenting your report to a partner using appropriate tone, pace, pitch and volume that will suit the audience and purpose.

**Present** the report to an audience.
