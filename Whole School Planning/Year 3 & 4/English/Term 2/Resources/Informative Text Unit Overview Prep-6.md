**Informative Text Unit – Teaching & Learning Sequence****
One Page Per Year Level (Prep–Year 6)**

This document organises the unit into separate pages for each year level. Each page can be copied directly into planning documents or used as a standalone teacher guide.

**Prep**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Introduction to informative texts: share an authentic informative text; model reading aloud; identify title, author, cover and basic print conventions; build background knowledge linked to the topic.

- Compare texts: engage with narrative stories on the same topic and discuss what is real vs imagined; group texts by type and topic using covers and visuals.

- Oral language: discuss likes/dislikes using speech and gesture; make simple connections to personal experiences; recount events through role‑play, sequencing pictures, drawing or digital tools.

- Phonics and reading: use decodable texts aligned to the school phonics scope and sequence; establish reading routines (choral, echo, oral cloze); practise monitoring meaning and simple comprehension strategies (predicting, visualising, connecting).

- Sentence and word building: explore simple sentences that express one idea; identify naming words and action words in shared texts; begin writing through labelling and simple factual statements.

- Creating and sharing: jointly construct simple information reports with teacher support; add a title and images; practise speaking clearly to share information with peers.

**Year 1**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Introduction to informative texts: revisit print conventions; identify features such as title, author, contents page and simple headings; build topic knowledge.

- Compare informative and narrative texts on the same topic; discuss purpose and how images support meaning.

- Oral language: make connections and share opinions based on experiences; practise turn-taking and listening behaviours in pairs and small groups.

- Phonics and reading: continue decodable texts aligned to phonics; practise re-reading and self-correcting; use comprehension strategies (predicting, questioning, summarising).

- Grammar and vocabulary: understand simple sentence structure (single independent clause); identify nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs in context; build topic-specific vocabulary.

- Creating and sharing: shared construction of information reports using title, simple subheadings and images; practise short spoken reports using greeting and clear voice.

**Year 2**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Introduction to informative texts: review purpose and audience; explore non-fiction texts, reports, signs and digital texts.

- Oral language: use comparative words and verbs to express degrees of preference; recount events and explain ideas in sequence.

- Reading and comprehension: maintain phonics/spelling routines as required; apply comprehension strategies independently with support.

- Text features: explore layout features including headings, simple navigation and webpages; discuss how images add meaning.

- Grammar and vocabulary: identify parts of speech and use topic-specific words; begin organising ideas into simple sections.

- Creating and sharing: plan and create simple information reports with subheadings, images and topic vocabulary; practise presentation skills and audience awareness.

**Year 3**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Introduction to informative texts: review text types (reports, explanations, biographies, online texts); identify audience, purpose and main ideas.

- Build background knowledge and explicitly teach technical vocabulary; introduce note-taking and discussion protocols (think-pair-share).

- Text structures: describe stages of informative texts; use paragraphs with topic sentences; discuss layout and navigation features (contents, headings, links).

- Sentences and grammar: understand clauses with subject–verb agreement; use verbs for different processes; maintain consistent tense.

- Language features: explore objective language and topic-specific vocabulary; discuss how language choices vary by purpose and audience.

- Visual features: identify how images extend meaning.

- Creating and presenting: plan, draft, edit and publish informative texts using simple paragraphs, correct tense and visual features; practise spoken delivery varying tone, pace, pitch and volume.

**Year 4**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Introduction to informative texts: deepen understanding of informative and media texts; review audience, purpose and main ideas.

- Text organisation: strengthen paragraph cohesion and use text connectives; explore navigation features in print and digital texts.

- Language features: explore objective and subjective language; build precision through synonyms and antonyms; maintain appropriate tense.

- Visual features: explore framing, placement and salience of images and how these influence meaning.

- Creating texts: develop linked ideas using complex sentences, visual features, spelling and punctuation appropriate to year level.

- Speaking and listening: use model spoken texts to develop features of voice and fluency when presenting information.

**Year 5**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Critical reading: discuss authoritative sources and reliability; provide context for reading; explicitly teach technical vocabulary and note-taking skills.

- Text organisation and cohesion: use paragraphs and topic sentences; explore text connectives and sentence openers to guide readers.

- Complex sentences: understand main and dependent clauses; use complex sentences to express reason, purpose, condition and time; recognise tense relationships; expand noun groups.

- Language features: explore clause- and word-level grammar, vocabulary and punctuation; use specialist and technical terms for precision; discuss choices by mode/medium.

- Visual and multimodal features: explain how image sequences and multimodal elements contribute to meaning.

- Creating and presenting: create informative texts with appropriate structure, expanded noun groups and punctuation; present multimodal informative texts using effective voice features.

**Year 6**

**Teaching and Learning Focus**

- Critical analysis: examine media texts for context, point of view and bias; compare informative and imaginative text structures and purposes.

- Advanced cohesion and grammar: use embedded clauses to elaborate ideas; expand verb groups; refine tense control; apply commas for lists and dependent clauses.

- Language precision: develop vivid, topic-specific vocabulary; distinguish objective language from bias in written and multimodal texts.

- Visual literacy: explain how images, diagrams, tables, graphs and maps represent information and relationships.

- Creating and presenting: plan, draft, edit and publish informative texts using varied complex sentences, strong paragraphing, accurate punctuation and purposeful visual features; deliver fluent spoken presentations with expression.
