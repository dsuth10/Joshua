## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To read, view and comprehend an informative text and describe how text structures, language and visual features shape meaning. To create a written multimodal information report. To present an information report with visual features to an audience.

Technique: Short response (Part A); extended response (Part B); observed demonstration (Part C).

Conditions

Administer the task individually with students.

Provide the task to students in a way that is age-appropriate.

Length of student responses should be considered in the context of the assessment and appropriate to the year level.

May be completed over multiple lessons.

## Implementation advice

## Part A: Reading and viewing

# Students describe the text structures of informative texts and how language and visual features shape meaning and support the purpose of the text.

Instructions

Ask students to:

independently read an informative text

answer questions about the informative text.

## Part B: Writing and creating

# Students create a written multimodal information report for an audience in which they develop ideas using details from learnt topics or texts. They organise their text, use language features to suit the purpose and consider the choice and placement of visual features.

Instructions

Establish the audience with students and provide guidance on topic selection.

Discuss how the information report will be structured.

Guide students to use research to gather ideas from a range of reliable sources including online and digital sources.

Students write their text. Guide them to use:

appropriate text structure and layout (spatial arrangement of print and graphics on a page or screen including size of font, positioning of illustrations, captions, labels, headings, dot points, borders and text boxes)

facts and details from learnt topics and a range of sources

complex sentences to create more specific and detailed descriptions

paragraphs with topic sentences to organise, link ideas and sequence content

topic-specific, precise and varied vocabulary

visual features such as framing, salience, composition, background, size and colour, to shape meaning.

Prompt students to review and edit their text for meaning, spelling and punctuation.

Students may use the Sheet — Planning (optional) to organise their ideas.

## Part C: Speaking and listening

Confirm with students that while they may choose to use the same information and sequence of ideas from their written report, their presentation should be more than a reading of their written text. It should include topic-specific vocabulary, features of voice and visual features.

Instructions

Guide students to plan and create their presentation including:

an introduction, body and conclusion

appropriate structure

an organised sequence of researched facts

descriptive details

references to reliable sources of information (for example: interviews)

more specific and precise words

objective language

visual features.

Guide students to practise presenting their report to a partner including controlling tone, pace, pitch and volume, and to seek and respond to feedback.

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Read the informative text provided by your teacher and answer the questions. You may need to re-read or scan parts of the text to respond to the questions.

Main ideas, purpose and audience

	What is the topic of this text? 

	What are some facts from the text?

	What is the purpose of this text?

	Who is the audience?

	What is the main idea/s of the text? Include supporting details in your answer.

Text structures, language features and visual features

	Describe the characteristic features (text structures, techniques, language and visual features) of this text.

	How has the author used these features to meet the purpose and help the reader navigate the text? Provide examples from the text.

	Describe how language features and vocabulary shape meaning in the text. Give examples from the text. For example: What technical vocabulary, objective language and quotes have been used and why?

	Describe how the author has used visual features to help readers build meaning. For example: Consider framing, composition and salience (placement, size and contrast) of the image/s.

## Part B: Writing and creating

You are going to plan, write and edit a multimodal information report on a topic you have learnt about.

- Plan your multimodal information report.

			Choose a topic you have learnt or read about in class.

		Gather information about this topic from a range of sources.

		Consider which facts and descriptive details you will use.

		Consider what text structures, language features and visual features you will use to organise information logically, connect ideas and support meaning.

	Consider how you will publish the report and the layout (for example: arrangement of print and images, illustrations, captions, labels, headings, dot points, borders and text boxes).

Create your text. Remember to use your planning.

	Use an information report text structure, including:

		paragraphs with topic sentences

		a variety of sentence types including complex sentences.

		Use language features to provide fuller descriptions, extend ideas and express greater precision of meaning. For example:

		noun groups, verb groups and adverb groups/phrases for descriptions and details

			cohesive devices such as text connectives, for example: however, therefore, firstly, then, next

		topic-specific vocabulary and precise words found in research.

		Consider visual features such as:

		framing, salience (foreground, size, tone and colour) and composition.

Review and edit your work to improve spelling, meaning and organisation.

## Part C: Speaking and listening

You are going to present an information report to an audience using features of voice. You can use the information, sequence of ideas and visual features from your written text, but you will need to adapt any text or language features so that your spoken text flows. You may also include any new ideas, together with any visual and/or digital features that support your text and engage your audience.

- Plan and create your presentation.

Remember to include:

	an introduction, body and conclusion

	an organised sequence of researched facts and information

	descriptive details

	references to reliable sources of information (for example: interviews, experts)

	more specific and precise words about the topic

	objective language

	visual features

	technology or audio and visual features to enhance spoken text (for example: videos a spoken presentation with music, sound effect enhancements) images, maps, diagrams

	costumes and/or props.

	Practise presenting your report to a partner including controlling tone, pace, pitch and volume. Ask for some feedback and use it to improve your presentation.

	Present the report to an audience.
