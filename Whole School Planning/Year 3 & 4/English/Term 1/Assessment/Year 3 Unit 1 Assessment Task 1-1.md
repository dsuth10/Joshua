## Assessment conventions and resources

Purpose: To read, view and comprehend an imaginative text. To create a written and/or multimodal imaginative text using or adapting ideas from a familiar narrative.

Technique: Extended response.

Conditions:

Individual work.

Adapt the mode and conditions to suit the diverse learning needs of your students and school context.

- Part A: Reading and viewing:

Students independently read an unfamiliar passage of an imaginative text.

Students provide short written responses.

- Part C: Writing and creating:

May be completed over multiple lessons.

Provide lesson time for drafting with access to teacher feedback and conferencing.

## Implementation advice

## Part A: Reading and viewing

Students read, view and comprehend an unfamiliar passage of text.

**Instructions**

	Select a short story or portion of an unfamiliar passage of text that develops ideas in the story through characters, settings and events. Ensure an appropriate level of text complexity.

	Ask students to:

	independently read aloud the selected passage of text. Observe student:

	fluency, re-reading and self-correcting when required

	monitor their own reading and, if needed, use learnt self-correction strategies. Use the **Teacher recording sheet (optional)** (provided) and the monitoring strategy.

	Students answer questions provided.

## Part B: Speaking and listening (monitoring strategy)

	Provide students time to think about how they could adapt the character/s, setting/s or events from a favourite narrative.

	Provide students opportunities to discuss their adaptation ideas with a partner or small group.

	Observe students’ interaction skills and creation of spoken stories.

	Students tell their story.

## Part C: Writing and creating

Students will create a text inspired by their favourite narrative.

**Instructions**

Guide students to:

	plan their ideas, optionally using the **Sheet — Story planner (optional)** (provided)

make notes about the decisions and actions of their characters, setting/s and plot.

Remind students:

	to consider audience and purpose

	their story may be told from the perspective of a new or existing character, or set in a similar or different setting

	they can use ideas, context, vocabulary or style from the narrative as a model

	to write in simple paragraphs

to 	re-read and edit to improve meaning and structure.

**Note:** Teachers should adapt the task to align with the language used in the teaching and learning sequence, to ensure continuity between teaching and assessment.

## Part A: Reading and viewing

**Purpose and audience**

	What is the purpose of this text?

	Who is the audience for this text?

Find evidence in the text to support your answer. Consider the words, images and text structure.

**Characters, settings and events**

	What is the title?

	Name and describe the main character/s.

	Describe what happened. Use the author’s words and other details from the text in your descriptions.

	What is main idea of the story? Give evidence from the text to support your thinking.

	Choose an image from the text that you think adds meaning for the reader. Describe how the image gives more information about the **characters, setting or events**.

	Think about the language the author uses to describe the **characters, settings or events** in the story.

Find two examples and explain how these affect the **mood** of the story.

For example: ‘The child skipped merrily.’ makes the mood playful and happy because skipping is a fun activity and ‘merrily’ means happy. These words make the reader feel happy too.

## Part B: Speaking and Listening

	Discuss with a partner or small group how you could adapt a favourite story by changing a character/s, setting/s or events.

	Tell your story.

## Part C: Writing and creating

You will write your own story inspired by your favourite story.

You can adapt the character/s, setting/s or events and use words from the narrative. Your adaptation may be told from the perspective of a new or existing character, or set in a similar or different setting.

You might like to use the **Sheet —** **Story planner (optional)** to record your ideas as notes, dot points or list important words.

- 	**Plan** your ideas using the Story planner.

	What will you change in the story?

	Identify the purpose and audience.

	How will you sequence the events (orientation, complication and resolution)	?

**Create**

	Use your story planner to help you write your story.

When writing be sure to:

	engage the audience with interesting details and events

	include an orientation, complication and resolution

		include interesting words to describe the mood, characters and setting (detailed descriptions, adjectives, noun groups)

		use simple and compound sentences to express and combine ideas

		group ideas in paragraphs

		use consistent tense (happened in the past; is happening now)

		link your ideas, sentences and paragraphs using:

		pronouns (she/he/it),

		conjunctions (and, but, because, so),

	time-order words (first, next, then, finally) to sequence events.

**Re-read** and **edit** your work. Remember to:

	check that you have used accurate spelling and punctuation (for example: capital letters, commas, apostrophes in contractions [*couldn’t, didn’t*], full stops, exclamation marks, question marks)

	re-read your story or share it with another person and make any changes to improve meaning and structure.

	**Publish** and illustrate (optional).

	Bring your story to life by including an illustration (for example: using digital tools or drawing).
